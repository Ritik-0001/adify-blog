## What "Ergonomic" Actually Means

Every chair on Amazon is labelled "ergonomic." Most are not. An ergonomic chair needs to do three specific things:

1. **Support your lumbar curve** — the lower back has a natural inward curve (lordotic curve). A chair that pushes your lower back outward or doesn't support it at all flattens this curve, which compresses spinal discs. You want adjustable lumbar support that pushes into your lower back, not just a padded backrest.

2. **Allow armrest adjustment** — arms should rest naturally with shoulders relaxed, elbows at roughly 90°. Fixed armrests that are too high force your shoulders up. Too low and you hunch forward. Adjustable height armrests (4D armrests = up/down + forward/back + rotate + width) are the standard in proper ergonomic chairs.

3. **Seat depth adjustment** — your thighs should be fully supported with 2–3 fingers of space between the seat edge and the back of your knee. Too shallow and your thighs are unsupported (pressure on lower back). Too deep and the edge cuts off circulation.

Mesh backrests are also generally better for Indian climate — fabric builds heat and sweat in 8+ hour sessions in a country where 35°C summers are normal.

## What to Skip

**Gaming chairs under ₹20,000** — bucket seat design, fixed high lumbar bolster, no proper lower back support. Designed for aesthetics, not ergonomics. Racing-seat style promotes a reclined posture which is fine for gaming (2 hours) but terrible for work (8 hours).

**Any chair with "orthopedic" in the name but no adjustable lumbar** — marketing term, not a specification. Adjust the lumbar, check the armrests, ignore the label.

**Boss chairs / executive chairs** (the big leather high-backs) — look impressive, provide no lumbar support, force you to perch at the front of the seat. Common in Indian offices, universally bad for backs.

## Setup Matters as Much as the Chair

The best chair in the world won't help if your monitor is at the wrong height or your keyboard forces your wrists to bend upward. Spend 30 minutes setting up your workstation correctly:

- **Monitor height:** Top of screen at eye level or slightly below. Eyes look naturally downward at about 15°.
- **Keyboard height:** Elbows at 90°, forearms parallel to floor, wrists neutral (not bent up).
- **Chair height:** Feet flat on floor (or footrest), thighs parallel to floor.
- **Lumbar position:** The chair's lumbar support should press into the curve of your lower back, not your mid-back.

An ₹8,000 monitor stand and a ₹15,000 Green Soul chair beats a ₹50,000 Herman Miller in a badly configured setup.
