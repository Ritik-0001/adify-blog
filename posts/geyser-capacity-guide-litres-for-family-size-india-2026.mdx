A 25 litre storage geyser is the right size for most families of three to four people with one or two bathrooms, a 35 to 50 litre geyser suits five or more people or a bathroom with a bathtub, and an instant geyser of 3 litres suits a single kitchen or basin tap. Premium glass-lined or stainless steel storage models from Racold, AO Smith, Bajaj, Havells and similar brands generally start around ₹15,000 once you add larger capacity, five-star ratings, smart controls or better anode protection.

This guide is researched, not hands-on tested. Tank litres are brand-stated figures, so what you actually get at the shower depends on the thermostat setting, how cold your inlet water runs in January and how much cold you add at the mixer.

## 1. 15 Litre Geysers: One or Two People

Usually below ₹15,000, so this size sits under this guide's premium focus and is best for a kitchen or a single basin. A 15 litre tank is enough for one or two people who bathe quickly, or for a kitchen-plus-washbasin setup. It heats fast and uses little space, but a long shower for two people can run out.

## How to Choose the Right Capacity

Count peak use, not average use. If three people bathe in the same hour on a winter morning, you need tank size for three showers plus a little margin. Add kitchen use if the same heater feeds the kitchen.

Check the tank material. Glass-lined and stainless steel tanks are common, and the anode, warranty on the tank and thermostat quality matter more than the capacity number alone.

Match power and wiring. Most storage geysers draw around 2,000 watts, so a dedicated 16 amp point and good wiring are important.

## Sizing Mistakes That Cost Money Later

Buying too small to save a few thousand rupees is the classic error, because a tank that runs cold on a December morning is a daily irritation. Vertical and horizontal models are not interchangeable on the same bracket, so decide the orientation before you drill. If you live on a high floor with a booster pump, check the pressure rating on the listing.

## Matching the Tank to the Household

A couple in a 1BHK rarely needs more than 15 litres, and 25 is plenty for comfort. A family of four in a 2BHK is better served by one 25 litre five-star unit per bathroom, or a single 35 litre unit if the bathrooms share a wall. Only joint families of six or more, or homes with a bathtub, should look at 50 litres and above. A bigger tank also costs more to keep hot, so size to your peak use.

## Recommendation

For most families, start with a 25 litre five-star storage geyser from a reputable brand, and move up to 35 or 50 litres if you are five or more or live somewhere cold. Choose brand-level ranges that fit your bathroom, confirm the model on the listing, and keep the installer's wiring checklist handy.
