Most camera buying guides are written for photographers. YouTube creators need something different — autofocus that tracks faces during a monologue, good audio input options, a flip screen you can actually see in daylight, and a camera that can run for 2+ hours without overheating.

I've tested cameras with Indian content creators in Delhi and Bangalore. Here's what actually works.

---

## What a YouTube Camera Needs (That Photography Cameras Don't)

**Flip screen:** A screen that flips 180° so you can see yourself while recording is not optional for solo creators. Tilt screens (that only tilt up/down) are a compromise. Full flip-out screens are what you want.

**Eye/face tracking autofocus:** You'll be moving, gesturing, walking around frame. Camera needs to lock onto your face and keep it sharp throughout. Not all cameras do this well.

**Overheating:** Entry-level mirrorless cameras frequently overheat after 20–30 minutes of 4K recording. For talking-head videos this is fine. For vlogs or travel content with long continuous recording, it's a deal-breaker.

**No recording time limit:** Some cameras (especially older Canon models) have a 29:59 minute recording limit due to EU import tax regulations. Newer cameras have removed this. Check before buying.

**Good audio input:** Built-in mics are terrible on all cameras. You need a 3.5mm audio input for an external mic. Some budget cameras skip this.

**Price:** ₹65,000–₹72,000 (body only)  
**Sensor:** 26MP APS-C  
**Video:** 4K 60fps (no crop), 1080p 120fps  
**Autofocus:** Real-time Eye AF + Subject Recognition

**Audio input:** 3.5mm + Multi-Interface Shoe

Sony built the ZV-E10 II specifically for video creators, and it shows in every decision. The autofocus uses Sony's latest tracking system — it locks on faces from across the room, follows them through obstacles, and barely loses track. For monologue videos and vlogs, this is the most reliable AF system under ₹80,000.

4K 60fps without crop means you can shoot slow-mo B-roll and talking heads at the same focal length, which matters for consistent visual style. Most APS-C cameras crop 4K footage (effectively narrowing your field of view), requiring a wider lens to compensate.

The E-mount lens ecosystem is the biggest in mirrorless — the 16mm f/1.4 (₹35,000), Tamron 17-28mm f/2.8 (₹55,000), and Sony 18-135mm kit (₹30,000 with camera) all work brilliantly.

**The catch:** Body-only at ₹65,000+ and then lens costs add up quickly. Budget ₹80,000–₹1,00,000 total for a proper setup.

### 2. Canon EOS M50 Mark II — Best for Beginners

**Price:** ₹48,000–₹55,000 (with 15-45mm kit lens)  
**Sensor:** 24.1MP APS-C  
**Video:** 4K (heavy crop), 1080p 60fps  
**Autofocus:** Dual Pixel CMOS AF (excellent for faces)  
**Flip screen:** Yes, fully articulating  
**Audio input:** 3.5mm  

Canon's Dual Pixel autofocus is legendary — it's smooth, fast, and rarely hunts. For talking-head YouTube videos, the M50 Mark II's AF is as good as anything in this price range.

The honest caveat: 4K is heavily cropped (1.6× APS-C crop + additional 4K crop = roughly 2.56× total), which makes your lenses effectively much longer. Most M50 Mark II YouTube creators shoot in 1080p 60fps instead, which looks excellent and has no crop.

**Why Indian creators choose it:** Canon's colours straight out of camera are warm and flattering for Indian skin tones. Less post-processing needed. Footage from the M50 II looks good even without a colour grade.

**The M-mount problem:** Canon's EF-M lens mount is being discontinued in favour of the RF mount (full frame). The M50 II still works perfectly, but you won't get new native lenses in the future.

## What to Buy With Your Camera

**Microphone:** Rode VideoMicro (₹8,000) for on-camera audio. DJI Mic 2 wireless (₹18,000) for interview-style or distance recording.

**SD Card:** Minimum Sony V30-rated 128GB. For 4K 60fps, get a V60 card. Don't cheap out here — recording failures mid-video are the worst.

**Extra battery:** Always. All these cameras eat batteries fast during 4K recording. Get two OEM batteries minimum.

**Lens:** If budget is limited after the camera body, the Sony 16-50mm kit, Canon 15-45mm kit, or Fujifilm 18-55mm kit are all good starters. Buy fast primes (35mm f/1.8 range) when budget allows.
