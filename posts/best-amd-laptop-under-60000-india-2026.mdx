## AMD vs Intel Under ₹60,000: The Real Differences

**Multi-core performance:** Ryzen 5 7530U and Ryzen 7 7730U outperform Core i5-1235U and Core i7-1255U in multi-core workloads by 15–30%. For tasks that use multiple cores (compilation, video export, parallel processing), AMD wins at the same price.

**Single-core performance:** Intel 12th/13th gen Core i7 P-series laptops have slightly better single-core performance. For responsiveness (UI, single-threaded apps), Intel is marginally faster.

**Integrated graphics:** AMD Radeon 680M (in Ryzen 7 7730U) is significantly more capable than Intel Iris Xe. Light gaming (CS2, Valorant at low settings), video editing preview, and GPU-accelerated tasks work better with Radeon 680M.

**Linux compatibility:** AMD's amdgpu driver is open-source and maintained by AMD. Intel Iris Xe has good Linux support. Both are better than NVIDIA discrete GPUs on Linux.

**Battery life:** Ryzen 7 U-series laptops typically get 9–12 hours vs Intel U-series at 7–10 hours — AMD's power efficiency is better in this tier.

## When to Choose AMD Over Intel

**Choose AMD Ryzen if:**
- You run Linux (AMD's open-source drivers work better)
- You need maximum multi-core performance at budget price
- Battery life is a priority
- You do light gaming or GPU-accelerated tasks on integrated graphics

**Choose Intel Core if:**
- You need maximum single-core responsiveness (Ultrabooks)
- You use Thunderbolt 4 accessories (Thunderbolt is Intel-exclusive in most laptops)
- Corporate IT department mandates Intel vPro
- You prefer the Intel ecosystem

**Recommendation:** Lenovo IdeaPad Slim 5 Ryzen 7 for developers and students who want maximum performance under ₹60,000. ASUS VivoBook 15 Ryzen 5 for budget buyers under ₹45,000.
