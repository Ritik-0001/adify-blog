## How to Choose a 65 Inch TV

Check the room. A 65-inch TV suits seating distances of about 8 to 12 feet, and needs a wall or stand that can take its weight.

Look at panel and processing. At this size, upscaling quality matters, since lower-resolution content is stretched across a larger screen. Better processors handle it more cleanly.

Check the sound and ports. A larger TV benefits from a soundbar, and HDMI eARC makes setup easier.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Ignoring the stand.** Check the width of the feet.

**Buying without measuring.** Check the space.

**Skipping wall-mount planning.** Confirm the mounting pattern.

**Overlooking service.** Check the brand's local coverage.

## What to Check on the Listing

Confirm the panel type, refresh rate, HDMI ports, weight and warranty. See whether installation and a wall mount are included.

## Quick FAQ

**Is 65 inches too big?** Not for a larger room, but it can overwhelm a small one.

**Do I need 120Hz?** For gaming, yes. For general viewing, 60Hz is fine.

**Is QLED better than LED?** It gives stronger colour and brightness.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A large living room with a sofa about ten feet away.** A 65-inch 4K TV is comfortable, and a QLED panel helps in a bright room.

**A home theatre room.** Consider a step up to OLED or a high-end Mini-LED for better contrast, plus a soundbar or a surround system.

**A budget-limited buyer.** A 65-inch 4K from Xiaomi, Hisense or TCL gives the big screen at a lower price, though service coverage is worth checking.

**A cricket and news household.** Motion handling and brightness matter more than deep blacks, so a good LED or QLED is a sensible pick.

## Setup and Mounting

A 65-inch TV is heavy, so check the wall mount pattern and the weight limit of the wall or stand. Plan the location of power and antenna or set-top connections before mounting. Ask whether installation is included, and keep the packaging for the first weeks in case of transport damage.

## Adding a Soundbar

A 65-inch TV has a lot of screen and very little speaker, so budget for a soundbar. Look for HDMI eARC on the TV so that one cable carries audio to the bar, and match brands if you want features such as speaker pairing. Even an entry-level soundbar improves dialogue clarity noticeably over built-in speakers.
