## How to Choose for Your Boss

First, check your company policy. Many organisations cap the value of gifts to managers or discourage them entirely. If a policy exists, follow it. If not, keep the value modest and the message respectful.

Second, avoid anything too personal. Grooming items, clothing and health products can be awkward. Stationery, dry fruits, chocolates and small desk accessories are widely accepted and do not create obligations.

Third, consider a team gift. A shared present from the whole department looks generous and spreads the cost, and it avoids the appearance of one person seeking favour. Keep the card simple and sincere.

## Quick FAQ

**How much should I spend on a boss's gift?** Keep it modest, usually a few hundred to a couple of thousand rupees for an individual gift. Spending too much can make both of you uncomfortable, and many workplaces set a ceiling on the value of gifts.

**Is it appropriate to gift a boss at all?** In many Indian workplaces, a Diwali gift is a normal courtesy, but the culture varies. If your team leader or HR has shared a policy, follow it. When in doubt, a box of sweets from the team is a safe gesture.

**Should I include a card?** Yes. A short, sincere note wishing them and their family a happy Diwali costs nothing and makes the gift feel considered rather than transactional.

**What should I avoid?** Skip alcohol, clothing, perfume and anything that could be read as personal or expensive. Keep the gift clearly festive and professional.
