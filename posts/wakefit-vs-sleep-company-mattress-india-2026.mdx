## Feel and Support

Wakefit's popular orthopaedic memory foam mattresses are built as layered foam with a firm base and a memory foam comfort layer, giving a medium-firm feel. The Sleep Company's SmartGRID uses a patterned grid layer that gives a more responsive, bouncier feel than standard memory foam.

If you like the classic slow-sinking feel, Wakefit is closer to it. If you like a feel that pushes back gently, SmartGRID is different.

## Cooling and Temperature

Memory foam can hold warmth. The Sleep Company promotes the grid design for airflow and a cooler feel, while Wakefit offers memory foam with breathable covers and some cooling variants.

Hot sleepers should read reviews for the specific model, since climate and bedding also affect temperature.

## Trial and Warranty

Both brands typically offer a home trial period and a multi-year warranty. Terms vary by model and change over time, so read the trial length, the return process and the warranty exclusions on the listing.

## Price and Value

Wakefit is generally more affordable for comparable sizes. The Sleep Company's SmartGRID range costs more, which reflects its design and brand positioning.

Compare thickness and layers when comparing price, since a thicker mattress usually costs more.

## Service and Availability

Both sell widely online, with delivery across major cities. Check delivery timelines and the process for trial returns in your area.

## How to Choose Between Wakefit and Sleep Company

Start with your sleeping position and temperature. If you sleep warm or want a responsive feel, look at SmartGRID. If you want a traditional memory foam feel at a lower price, choose Wakefit.

Read recent buyer reviews on sagging and comfort after several months. Test the mattress using the trial fully.

## Durability and Long-Term Care

Both brands stand behind their mattresses with multi-year warranties, but warranties usually cover manufacturing defects such as deep sagging, not normal softening. To get the most from either, use a supportive base, rotate the mattress head to foot every few months if the manufacturer allows it, and protect it with a washable mattress protector.

Keep the invoice and the warranty card. If you need to claim, photographs of the sag and the bed base are usually required, and a bed frame with slats spaced too far apart can void the claim.

## What to Check on the Listing

Compare the thickness, the density of the foam layers, the cover fabric, the trial length and the return pickup process. Read the lowest-rated recent reviews as well as the top ones, since they show what goes wrong in practice.

## Comfort for Different Body Types

Lighter sleepers, typically under 60 kilograms, often prefer softer, more contouring surfaces because they do not sink deeply into firmer foam. Heavier sleepers, above 90 kilograms, tend to need denser support layers and thicker mattresses so that the spine stays aligned. Compare the density and layer details on each listing rather than relying on the firmness label alone, and look for reviews from people of similar build.

Couples with a large weight difference should pay extra attention to edge support and motion isolation, and use the trial period to test the mattress together.
