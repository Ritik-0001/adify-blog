A storage geyser is the better choice for bathrooms and for families of three or more, because it keeps a tank of hot water ready, while an instant geyser is better for a kitchen sink or a single basin where you need a small amount of hot water quickly. For a main bathroom, premium storage models from brands such as Racold, AO Smith, Bajaj and Havells typically start around ₹15,000 once you add capacity and safety features.

This comparison is researched, not hands-on tested, so the points that decide the purchase are the ones to verify yourself: the heater's rated wattage against your home's wiring, the tank material and the warranty split between tank and element.

## 1. Storage Geysers: Best for Bathrooms

Roughly ₹15,000–30,000 for premium models depending on capacity and brand, while basic units cost less. A storage geyser heats a tank and holds the water hot, so several showers can happen without waiting. Thermostats cut power once the set temperature is reached. You pay for that comfort with a heating wait at the start, some standing heat loss and a larger wall footprint.

## 2. Instant Geysers: Best for Kitchens and Basins

Usually below ₹15,000, so instant geysers sit under this guide's premium focus and suit kitchens and basins. An instant geyser heats water as it flows, usually in a 1 to 3 litre chamber. It is compact and gives hot water in seconds, which suits washing utensils or a basin. Flow rate is limited, so most models are not meant for a full bathroom shower.

## How to Choose Between Instant and Storage

Start with usage. For bathing, storage is almost always the answer, because a shower needs several litres per minute. For a sink, an instant unit saves you from heating a whole tank.

Check electrical capacity. Instant geysers draw high power, often around 3,000 watts, so wiring and the MCB must support it. Storage geysers draw about 2,000 watts for longer.

Look at running cost. A well-insulated storage geyser holds heat for hours, while an instant geyser only draws power while you use it. For short, frequent draws instant units can be cheaper, and for long baths storage units usually win.

## Who Should Skip the Storage Tank

If your only hot-water need is a kitchen sink, or you rent a flat with weak walls, a tank is the wrong tool. Filling a wall bracket with a full tank asks a lot of an old partition, and heating a whole tank for a few litres of hot water at the sink wastes power. In those homes a compact instant unit, with wiring that can carry its higher load, is the more sensible spend.
