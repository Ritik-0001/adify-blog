## Weight stacks and the 2:1 ratio

Many functional trainers use a 2:1 pulley ratio, meaning the handle moves twice as far as the stack. A 50 kg stack then gives about 25 kg at the handle. That is fine for flyes and curls but weak for rows and pulldowns by stronger users. A 1:1 ratio gives the full weight but moves with more friction.

Always check the stack weight, the ratio and the increments. A 5 kg step is comfortable. 10 kg steps jump too far for isolation work. Add-on micro plates cost little and help.

These have two adjustable towers, each with its own stack. You can do flyes, presses, face pulls and single-arm movements. Expect roughly ₹80,000-1,50,000 and about 1.2 m by 2.4 m of floor. ## Pulleys, cable and build quality

Look for steel pulleys with sealed bearings instead of plastic. Cable should be coated steel, ideally 5 mm or thicker, and the machine should use a swivel at the handle. Check frame tube thickness, with 2 to 3 mm steel common. Reviews mentioning squeaks, fraying cable or plastic pulley cracks are warnings.

Adjustable pulley height with a pop-pin and many holes gives more exercise options. Check the user weight rating and whether the bench or seat adjusts.

## Footprint and floor

Even compact units need around 2 m by 2 m once you account for movement. Ceilings matter too, as tall towers can reach 2.2 m. Place on concrete and use a mat to prevent floor damage. Check the delivery path through doors and lifts, as the machine ships in heavy boxes and needs assembly with several people. ## Brands and service

Indian brands sell cable machines online, and commercial gym makers like Cosco and others supply bigger units through dealers. Because specific model availability varies, look for brands with a stated warranty, spare parts for cables and pulleys, and an assembly service. Ask whether installation is free.

## Who should skip a cable machine

Beginners who only want to build general strength may do better with a bench, dumbbells and a pull-up bar for far less. If you have a small room under 100 sq ft, a resistance band set and a door anchor can approximate cable work. ## Recommendation

For a serious home gym, a dual-stack functional trainer with a 2:1 ratio, steel pulleys and a 70 kg or heavier stack is the best investment. For back training only, buy a dedicated lat pulldown and low row. Measure doors and floor space before ordering.
