## Bowl capacity and what it means

Bowl size is quoted in litres but your real limit is the batter or dough. A 4.5 to 5 litre bowl typically handles around 700 g to 1 kg of flour in dough, enough for two loaves of bread or a double batch of cookies. A larger 6 to 7 litre bowl-lift model suits families who bake for gatherings, though it takes more counter space and costs more.

Small compact mixers around 3 litres are tidy but struggle with stiff bread dough. If you plan to bake sourdough or pizza, size up.

## Tilt-head versus bowl-lift

### 1. Tilt-head

The head tilts back for access to the bowl. It is lighter, more compact and easier to use for cakes, cookies and light doughs. Most home bakers choose this style. Heavier doughs can make the head wobble if the machine is light.

### 2. Bowl-lift

The bowl raises and lowers on arms. It is sturdier, with bigger motors, better for heavy doughs, but also larger and costlier. Suits serious bread bakers. See [bowl lift stand mixer](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=bowl+lift+stand+mixer&tag=adifystore-21).

## Motor and dough ability

Check watts, but also the gearing. A 300 to 500 watt motor in a well-geared mixer does more than a higher rated one with weak gears. Indian atta-based dough is dense and sticky; a mixer that handles it without overheating is a good sign. Avoid running a mixer at full load for more than the manual allows, and work in batches if needed.

Kenwood's kMix and Chef ranges and Bosch's MUM range are well known for dough ability. Search [Kenwood stand mixer](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=kenwood+stand+mixer&tag=adifystore-21) and [Bosch stand mixer](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=bosch+stand+mixer&tag=adifystore-21).

## Attachments

The three standard tools are a flat beater for batters, a wire whisk for cream and meringue, and a dough hook for bread. Optional extras include pasta rollers, a meat grinder, a food processor, a spiralizer and an ice cream bowl. Most are sold separately and add up quickly.

Check whether the mixer has a power hub, which is a front port where attachments connect. Not every model does. Search [stand mixer pasta attachment](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=stand+mixer+pasta+attachment&tag=adifystore-21).

## Bowl material

Stainless steel bowls are durable and easy to clean. Glass bowls allow you to see the mix but are heavier and can crack. Ceramic bowls look nice but chip. For daily use, steel is practical.

## Counter space and weight

A stand mixer is heavy, often 8 to 12 kg. If you will keep it on the counter, measure the height under your cabinet, including the tilted head. If you need to store it in a cupboard, you will use it less often.

## Noise and vibration

Dough work is noisier than whipping cream. A solid base and rubber feet stop walking across the counter. Run it on a stable surface and avoid sliding it while it is running.

## Service in India

Spare parts like gears, bowls and attachments matter in the long run. Buy from authorised sellers and check warranty terms. KitchenAid, Kenwood and Bosch have service networks in major cities; smaller cities may be limited.

## Who should skip a stand mixer

- Occasional bakers who make a cake a few times a year
- Small kitchens with no counter or cupboard space
- People who knead bread by hand and enjoy it
- Buyers on a tight budget; a quality [hand mixer](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=hand+mixer+for+baking&tag=adifystore-21) handles most cakes and whipped cream

## Honest trade-off

A premium stand mixer is a lasting appliance, often kept for a decade, but a cheaper mixer with weak gears will struggle with dough and may fail early. It is better to buy once at a good level than twice at a low one.
