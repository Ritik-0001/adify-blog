## Why crop sensors suit wildlife

An APS-C sensor crops the image by about 1.5x or 1.6x compared with full-frame, so a 300 mm lens frames like roughly 450 to 480 mm. For small, distant birds this extra reach is valuable and cheaper than buying a longer lens. A crop body also tends to be lighter, helping on long safari days. The trade-off is slightly weaker low-light performance, which matters at dawn and dusk when animals are most active.

## Autofocus and burst speed

Look for subject-detection autofocus that recognises birds and animals and tracks eyes. Bodies such as the Canon R7, Sony A6700 and Nikon Z8 offer this. Burst rate matters for flying birds: electronic shutters on modern bodies reach around 20 to 30 frames per second, though rolling shutter can distort fast wing motion. A mechanical shutter at around 10 to 15 frames per second is often enough. Check the buffer size to see how many frames you can shoot before the camera slows.

## Weight and handling

A big telephoto can weigh two kilograms or more, and holding it through a jeep safari is tiring. A beanbag on the jeep window, a monopod or a lightweight gimbal head helps. Weather sealing matters in the monsoon and dusty parks, and a rain cover costs little. [Browse monopods](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=carbon+fiber+monopod&tag=adifystore-21).

## Memory cards and storage

Burst shooting generates large files. Use fast UHS-II SD cards or CFexpress for bodies that support them, and carry a laptop or drive for daily backups. [Browse UHS-II SD cards](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=UHS-II+SD+card+256GB&tag=adifystore-21).

## Who should skip

If you shoot mostly landscapes and occasionally visit a park, a rented telephoto lens makes more sense than buying one. If your budget is under ₹1,50,000 in total, a body plus a basic 70-300 mm zoom will not match wildlife-grade results.

## Recommendation

For most beginners and enthusiasts, buy a crop-sensor body such as the Canon R7 or Sony A6700 and put the bigger part of the budget into a 300-500 mm class lens. Move to full-frame only if you often shoot in poor light. Check lens compatibility, weight and weather sealing before ordering.
