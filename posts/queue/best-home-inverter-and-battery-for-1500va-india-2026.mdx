## Start with load planning

Add up wattage. Ceiling fans use 70-80 watts each, LED lights 9-12 watts, a TV 80-150 watts, a Wi-Fi router 10-20 watts, and a laptop around 60. Four fans, six lights, a TV and a router come to roughly 550-650 watts. A 1500VA inverter delivers roughly 1,000-1,200 watts of real power depending on its power factor, so this load fits with margin.

Do not plan on running an AC, a geyser or a mixer-grinder from a 1500VA inverter. An AC demands far more than the unit can deliver, and attempting to power one will trip the overload protection or drain the batteries in under an hour.

## Voltage and battery count

A 1500VA inverter generally works on a 24V system, so it uses two 12V batteries in series. That determines the minimum setup, and the capacity of those batteries decides your backup hours. Smaller 850VA to 1100VA units use a single 12V battery, which limits backup to short periods.

A plain 12V one-battery arrangement is cheaper but gives less runtime and less margin for heavier loads, so step up to 24V when the load exceeds roughly 500 watts.

## Battery sizes and backup hours

Two 150Ah batteries hold roughly 3.6 kWh of nominal energy. After taking about 70 percent as usable and around 85 percent inverter efficiency, you get roughly 2.1 kWh of delivered energy. At a 500-watt load, that is about 4 hours, and at 300 watts it stretches to 6-7 hours.

Two 200Ah batteries lift this to roughly 2.8 kWh usable, which is 5-6 hours at 500 watts, but costs more and takes more space. Look at [200Ah tubular battery](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=200ah+tubular+battery&tag=adifystore-21) listings if your area has long cuts. A single tubular battery of 150Ah costs roughly ₹14,000-18,000, and 200Ah roughly ₹18,000-24,000, so a pair adds up fast.

## Sine wave, always

Buy pure sine wave. Square or modified wave units cause humming in fans, damage some electronics and make LED lights flicker. Sine wave output behaves like the grid and is safe for sensitive devices such as a laptop charger or a modem.

Check that the inverter has a good efficiency rating, overload and short-circuit protection, and a display showing battery level and load. Many models offer a smart charge mode that protects battery life.

## Space, ventilation and installation

Batteries are heavy, around 40-50 kg each for tubular models, so a sturdy [inverter battery trolley](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=inverter+battery+trolley&tag=adifystore-21) helps. Put them in a ventilated spot, away from living areas, since lead-acid batteries release gas while charging. Check distilled water levels every 2-3 months, as tubular batteries need topping up.

Use thick copper cable of the right gauge for the battery connections, and keep the cables short. A cheap installer using thin wire creates a fire risk and wastes power.

## Running cost and life

Expect tubular batteries to last roughly 4-6 years with proper care. Frequent deep discharge and poor maintenance shorten that. Inverter charging adds to the monthly bill, roughly 20-40 units extra in places with frequent cuts.

## Who should skip a 1500VA system

Flats that rarely face cuts may be fine with a [UPS for Wi-Fi and fans](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=1kva+ups+for+home&tag=adifystore-21) or a small 850VA unit. Those wanting AC backup need a much bigger inverter or a solar hybrid, which is a different category.
