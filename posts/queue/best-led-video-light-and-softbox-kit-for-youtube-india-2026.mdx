## Wattage: how much light do you really need?

Wattage is a rough guide and not comparable between brands, but as a rule of thumb, a 60 W LED with a softbox is enough for a desk talking-head setup in a small room. A 100 W lamp gives headroom for larger softboxes or shooting in a bright room.

If your room has a big window and you shoot in the daytime, you need more power to balance the daylight, so go 100 W or higher. For a dark room at night, 60 W per light is usually plenty.

More power also means more heat and fan noise. Check whether the fan can be turned down or off, because mic pickup of fan noise ruins good video.

## Colour temperature and CRI

Bi-colour lights let you adjust from roughly 2700K warm to 6500K daylight. This matters in Indian homes where tube lights and yellow bulbs add cast. You can match the light to a window or deliberately make it warmer for a cosy look.

CRI (colour rendering index) tells you how faithfully the light renders colours. For skin tones, aim for CRI 95 or higher; some budget lights claim 95 but measure lower. TLCI is another measure that matters for video.

## Softbox sizes and placement

Bigger softboxes mean softer light, but only when they are close to your face.

- 60 cm softbox: good for a desk talking-head setup, easy to place.
- 90 cm softbox or octabox: softer, but needs more space.
- 120 cm parabolic: studio look, but needs a big room.

A classic setup is a key light about 45 degrees to one side, slightly above eye level, a fill at lower power on the other side, and a small LED behind you for separation. Keep the key light close, within a metre, so the softbox acts as a big soft source.

## Power, heat and room reality

Indian power cuts and voltage fluctuations affect LED panels less than tungsten, but a surge protector or small UPS is wise. Check the power adapter, because some lights use a heavy external brick.

In a small room, high wattage means heat. LED panels run cooler than old halogen lights but still warm a room. Turn off the AC or fan only when recording, and consider a lower power setting to reduce heat.

## Small accessories that matter

A diffusion layer inside the softbox gives softer light. Barn doors or grids prevent light spill onto the background. A small RGB tube light adds colour accents for gaming or tech channels. ## Who should skip a full kit

If you shoot only on a phone for short vertical videos, a ring light or small panel may be enough. If you shoot in a bright, window-lit room during the day, a reflector and good window positioning might be better than adding lamps.

Also skip the cheapest ₹3,000 ring-light kits; they have poor colour accuracy and flat lighting.
