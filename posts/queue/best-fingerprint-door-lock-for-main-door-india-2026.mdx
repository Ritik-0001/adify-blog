## How to Choose a Fingerprint Lock for a Main Door

Check door and frame. Measure the door thickness, check whether the door is wood or metal and choose a lock body that fits.

Look at security features. A solid metal build, a tamper alarm and a mechanical key backup improve security and resilience.

Think about the environment. Dust, humidity and sun can wear sensors, so choose a lock with a good reputation and warranty.

## Fingers, Dust and Monsoon Humidity

Fingerprint readers struggle when fingers are wet, oily or covered in construction dust, so a main door with heavy foot traffic from gardening or workshops needs the PIN as a regular alternative. Register two or three fingers for each family member, including a spare hand, and wipe the sensor with a soft dry cloth. Ask how the lock copes with monsoon humidity before you buy it for an exposed doorway.

## Choosing Between Fingerprint, PIN and Card

Fingerprint is fastest for daily use, but a PIN is useful for visitors and for wet or dusty hands. Cards or tags suit domestic help. A good lock offers all of these so that one failed method never locks you out, and the mechanical key remains the last resort.

## Security Habits Beyond the Lock

A strong lock is only part of home security. Keep the key backup somewhere safe, remove old fingerprints when people leave and avoid easily guessed PINs. Add a video door phone or camera if you want to see who is at the door, and book a yearly check of the latch and bolt.

## Who Should Skip This

If your main door is a sliding or folding grille, or an older door with a warped frame, a fingerprint lock will not seat properly and you should fix the door first. Skip it too if the doorway has no shelter at all and the listing offers no weather protection, since sensors in direct sun and rain age faster.
