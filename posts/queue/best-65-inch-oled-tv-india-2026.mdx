## How to Choose an OLED TV

Understand what OLED offers. Each pixel makes its own light, so blacks are perfect and contrast is excellent, which suits dim rooms and film viewing. OLED is less bright than the best LED TVs, so bright rooms may reduce its advantage.

Check gaming and ports. Four HDMI 2.1 ports, 120Hz and VRR suit modern consoles and PCs.

Consider burn-in care. Modern OLEDs include protections, but avoid static images for hours.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Placing it opposite a bright window.** Reflections hurt contrast.

**Ignoring the model year.** Check features.

**Skipping the sound.** Plan for a soundbar.

**Overlooking installation.** OLED panels are delicate.

## What to Check on the Listing

Confirm the model, panel type, refresh rate, HDMI ports and warranty. See whether installation and a wall mount are included.

## Quick FAQ

**Is OLED worth the price?** For film and gaming in dim rooms, yes.

**Will it burn in?** It is possible with heavy static content, but protections reduce the risk.

**How long does it last?** Many last seven to ten years.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A dedicated movie room.** OLED's contrast is at its best in a dim room, so a C-series or Sony OLED is a strong fit.

**A living room with daytime viewing.** Consider a brighter QD-OLED or a high-end Mini-LED if you watch a lot with windows open.

**A console gamer.** Look for four HDMI 2.1 ports, 120Hz and VRR, which the C-series covers well.

**A buyer who wants value.** A previous-generation OLED such as the C3 can offer excellent picture quality for less, subject to availability.

## Care and Longevity

Vary your content, avoid leaving static logos on screen for hours, and let the TV run its automatic pixel refresh cycles when idle. Use the standard or filmmaker mode rather than maximum brightness for everyday viewing. Keep the TV ventilated and clean the screen with a soft, dry microfibre cloth.

## OLED vs QD-OLED vs Mini-LED

Standard OLED, as used in LG's C series, lights each pixel individually and offers perfect blacks. QD-OLED, used by Samsung and some Sony models, adds a quantum dot layer for stronger colour volume and brightness. High-end Mini-LED TVs are the alternative for bright rooms, since they reach higher peak brightness, though contrast is not as pixel-perfect as OLED. Match the technology to your room lighting and viewing habits.
