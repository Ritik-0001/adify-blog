## How to Build a Home Theatre System

Start with the room and budget. Measure seating distance and room size, then split your budget between the receiver, speakers and subwoofer, with speakers deserving a large share.

Choose a receiver with room to grow. HDMI 2.1, Dolby Atmos support and enough channels let you add speakers later.

Plan placement and cables. Place front speakers at ear level and angled towards the seating position, with rear speakers beside or behind, and tidy the cables.

## Calibration and Setup

Run the receiver's auto room calibration with the supplied microphone, then fine tune the subwoofer level and crossover by ear. Adjust the speaker distances and use a test track to check balance.

## Budget Splits That Work

A sensible way to divide a home theatre budget is roughly thirty to forty percent on the receiver, forty to fifty percent on speakers and the rest on the subwoofer and cables. Resist the urge to spend everything on a feature-heavy receiver, because speakers shape the sound far more. You can also start with front speakers and a centre channel, then add surrounds later when your budget allows.

## Who Should Skip This

Skip a separate receiver and speakers if you live in a small rented flat, share walls closely with neighbours or have no patience for cabling and menus. Speaker wires, a rack with ventilation and a calibration routine all demand effort. A soundbar with a wireless subwoofer will deliver most of the enjoyment with far less hassle, and it will move house with you in one carton.

## Recommendation

For most enthusiasts, a Denon or Yamaha receiver with Klipsch, Polk or Wharfedale speakers and a good subwoofer is a strong, upgradeable system. Compare specifications and warranty on each listing.
