## How to Build a Home Theatre System

Start with the room and budget. Measure seating distance and room size, then split your budget between the receiver, speakers and subwoofer, with speakers deserving a large share.

Choose a receiver with room to grow. HDMI 2.1, Dolby Atmos support and enough channels let you add speakers later.

Plan placement and cables. Place front speakers at ear level and angled towards the seating position, with rear speakers beside or behind, and tidy the cables.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Cheap speakers with an expensive receiver.** Balance the budget.

**Ignoring room acoustics.** Soft furnishings help.

**Skipping calibration.** Run the room correction.

**Forgetting HDMI standards.** Check eARC and HDMI 2.1.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A 3BHK living room.** A 5.1 system with bookshelf speakers.

**A dedicated media room.** A 7.1 or 5.1.2 system with tower speakers.

**A gamer.** A receiver with HDMI 2.1 and low latency.

## What to Check on the Listing

Confirm the channels, the power per channel, the HDMI versions, the supported audio formats, the warranty and what is in the box. See whether speakers include stands or brackets.

## Calibration and Setup

Run the receiver's auto room calibration with the supplied microphone, then fine tune the subwoofer level and crossover by ear. Adjust the speaker distances and use a test track to check balance.

## Warranty and Service

Check the warranty and service reach for the receiver and speakers, and ask about parts. Keep the invoice and serial numbers.

## Quick FAQ

**Is an AV receiver better than a soundbar?** For power and flexibility, yes.

**Do I need Atmos?** It is optional, and needs ceiling or up-firing speakers.

**Can I add speakers later?** Yes, within the receiver's channels.

## Budget Splits That Work

A sensible way to divide a home theatre budget is roughly thirty to forty percent on the receiver, forty to fifty percent on speakers and the rest on the subwoofer and cables. Resist the urge to spend everything on a feature-heavy receiver, because speakers shape the sound far more. You can also start with front speakers and a centre channel, then add surrounds later when your budget allows.

## Final Checklist Before You Buy

Before you order, check the connections on your TV or source, such as HDMI eARC, optical or Bluetooth, measure the space for the speakers or soundbar and confirm what is in the box. Read the warranty terms, think about placement and cabling before you buy and, if you can, listen to a similar model so that you know the sound suits your room.
