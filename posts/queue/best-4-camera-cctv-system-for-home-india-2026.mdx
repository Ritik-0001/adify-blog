## How to Choose a 4 Camera CCTV System

Pick the resolution. Five megapixels is a common balance of clarity and storage. Check that the DVR supports the same resolution, and note that higher resolution needs more storage.

Look at storage and night vision. A 1TB drive typically stores a few weeks of footage for four cameras. Infrared night vision of about 20 to 30 metres suits most homes, and colour night vision is a useful upgrade.

Plan the cabling and power. Wired systems give reliable recording, and PoE simplifies cabling. Budget for installation, cable, connectors and a UPS for backup.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Ignoring camera placement.** Cover entry points.

**Skipping the hard drive.** Confirm it is included.

**Weak passwords.** Change defaults.

**No backup power.** Use a UPS.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A flat.** Cover the entrance and a common area.

**A villa.** Cover gates, driveway and perimeter.

**A shop.** Cover the counter and entrance.

## What to Check on the Listing

Confirm the resolution, the hard drive, the cable length, the night vision range and the warranty. See whether installation is included.

## Quick FAQ

**Do I need Wi-Fi?** Wired systems do not, but remote viewing needs internet.

**How long is footage stored?** It depends on resolution and storage.

**Can I view on my phone?** Yes, through the brand's app.

## Installation Costs and Planning

Professional installation typically adds a few thousand rupees for cabling, connectors, drilling and configuration, and it is worth paying for since bad cable runs cause most CCTV problems. Ask for a written scope that includes cables, power supplies, cable conduits and remote viewing setup.

Keep the recorder in a secure, ventilated spot, and use a UPS so recordings continue during power cuts. Set strong passwords and update the firmware regularly.
