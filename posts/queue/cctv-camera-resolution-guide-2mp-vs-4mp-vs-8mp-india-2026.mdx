For most Indian homes a 4MP CCTV camera is the right resolution, because it gives clearly better zoom detail than 2MP while needing far less hard drive and bandwidth than 8MP (4K). Pick 8MP only for wide gates, driveways and shop fronts where you need to read details far from the lens.

This guide is researched from Hikvision, CP Plus and Dahua specifications, not from my own camera testing. Before you buy, check the lens focal length and the video codec (H.265 matters), since both change the real result more than the megapixel number.

## What the numbers mean

2MP is 1920 x 1080 (full HD). 4MP is around 2560 x 1440, and 8MP is 3840 x 2160, usually sold as 4K. Pixel count is only the canvas: a sharp lens and a decent sensor matter as much.

## Detail: where each resolution earns its keep

A 2MP camera recognises a face at a few metres and is fine for a door or corridor. At 10 metres, faces turn into blobs and a number plate is unreadable.

A 4MP camera has roughly double the pixels, so digital zoom on recorded footage stays usable. It is the sweet spot for entrances, living areas and a compound up to about 12 metres.

An 8MP camera has four times the pixels of 2MP. It wins when you must capture a wide scene, such as a 20 metre driveway, and still zoom into one vehicle afterward.

Roughly ₹12,000-20,000 for a four-camera kit with drive. It fits small flats, a single door, or a budget-limited shop. The limit is zoom: stretching a 2MP frame on a big screen shows blur.

## 3. 8MP (4K): for wide, critical views

Roughly ₹30,000-60,000 for a four-camera kit. Use it on one or two key cameras rather than on every channel. It needs a 4K-capable NVR, and playback on older phones can stutter.

The honest cost is storage and bandwidth. A 4K camera may stream 6 to 8 Mbps even with H.265, so a four-camera kit eats a drive fast. See [8MP 4K CCTV camera](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=8mp+4k+cctv+camera+ip&tag=adifystore-21).

## Storage and bandwidth in practice

Bitrate decides storage. As rough H.265 figures, 2MP runs 1 to 2 Mbps, 4MP around 2 to 4 Mbps, and 8MP around 4 to 8 Mbps. One Mbps is about 10 GB per day. So a 4MP camera at 3 Mbps writes roughly 30 GB a day, and four cameras recording continuously fill a 4TB drive in about a month.

For remote viewing on mobile data, bandwidth matters too. If your home upload speed is 10 to 20 Mbps, viewing four 8MP streams at once will lag, so use the sub-stream (a lower-resolution preview) on the app.

## Lens matters more than megapixels

A 2.8 mm lens gives a wide view, roughly 100 degrees, but small objects at a distance. A 6 mm lens narrows the view and doubles reach. For a gate 10 metres away, a 4MP camera with a 4 or 6 mm lens beats an 8MP with a 2.8 mm lens for number plates.

Also check low-light behaviour. Sensors with good night colour (ColorVu type) may preserve faces at night better than a higher megapixel camera with weak infrared. See [ColorVu night colour camera](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=colorvu+4mp+cctv+camera&tag=adifystore-21).

## When higher resolution is wasted

Higher resolution is wasted if the NVR cannot record it, the network cannot move it, or the lens never frames the subject large enough. An 8MP camera looking at a dark corridor from 3 metres adds nothing over 4MP. Likewise, resolution cannot fix glare from a window or a camera mounted too high.

Match the lens to the distance first, then choose the lowest resolution that delivers identifiable detail. ## Recommendation

Pick 4MP for house entrances, living areas and most compounds. Keep 2MP only for short, low-risk spots. Use 8MP selectively for gates, driveways and wide shop fronts, and pair it with a 4K NVR and a bigger drive. Spend on a good lens and night performance before chasing megapixels.
