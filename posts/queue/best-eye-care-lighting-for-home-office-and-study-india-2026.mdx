## What causes eye strain

Most strain comes from contrast, glare and flicker, not from screens themselves. A bright screen in a dark room forces the eyes to constantly adjust. Reflections on a glossy monitor or a lamp shining into your eyes produce glare. Cheap LED lamps may flicker at frequencies the eye does not consciously see but that can trigger headaches in some people.

The fix is balanced lighting: ambient light at roughly a third to half of screen brightness, task light on paper or keyboard, and no direct light on the screen.

## Colour temperature and CRI

For daytime work, around 4000-5000K feels neutral and keeps you alert. For evenings, 2700-3500K is warmer and easier on the eyes. A lamp that lets you change temperature, and brightness separately, is more useful than a fixed one.

CRI, colour rendering index, shows how accurately colours appear. Look for 90 or higher for study and creative work. Cheap lamps may be under 80, which makes colours look flat. Brightness for a desk lamp: roughly 500 lux on the desk is a common target for reading and writing.

## Flicker and driver quality

Flicker-free is a claim worth checking. Better lamps use constant-current drivers and DC dimming, avoiding the PWM dimming that causes flicker at low brightness. A simple test: film the lamp with a phone camera in slow motion at low brightness and look for banding. Also check for certification such as an eye care or low-blue-light label, but treat vague marketing with caution.

## Monitor light bars

A light bar clips on top of your monitor and shines light on the desk without throwing glare on the screen, thanks to an asymmetric optic. This keeps your keyboard and notes lit while leaving the desk free of a lamp. Features to look for: auto dimming with an ambient sensor, a wireless controller and adjustable colour temperature. Check clearance with curved monitors, as some bars do not sit stable on them. ## Desk lamps for study and reading

For a student desk, a lamp with a wide, even beam, an adjustable arm and 3 to 5 brightness levels works. Place it opposite your writing hand to avoid shadows, and keep the light source below eye level. A [study lamp rechargeable](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=study+lamp+led+desk+eye+protection+clamp&tag=adifystore-21) is useful in power cuts, but a mains lamp usually gives more steady brightness.

## Ambient and bias lighting

A bias light, a soft strip behind the monitor, reduces contrast between the bright screen and the dark wall. Use a neutral white, around 6500K, to match most monitors, or a warm tone for relaxation. Ceiling lights should be diffused and not hit the screen; adding a dimmer helps. A [bias lighting strip monitor](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=bias+lighting+led+strip+monitor+usb&tag=adifystore-21) is inexpensive but is best used as a supplement.

## Screen settings

Lighting is half the solution. Set your monitor to a brightness that matches the room, turn on a warm night mode in the evening, keep the screen about an arm's length away with the top at or just below eye level, and follow the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds. Blink consciously, and consider an anti-glare screen if your monitor is glossy.

## Who should skip

Skip expensive lighting if the real problem is a screen that is too bright or too close, because adjusting settings is free. Skip blue-light glasses as a main solution; evidence is mixed. Skip lamps with no stated flicker data or CRI. If your eye symptoms persist, see an optometrist before spending.
