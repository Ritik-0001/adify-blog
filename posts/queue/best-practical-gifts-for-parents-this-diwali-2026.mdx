## How to Choose for Your Parents

Start with what they already complain about. A parent who mentions stiff shoulders will appreciate a massager; one who struggles with TV apps will love a streaming stick; one who checks their pulse at every family gathering will value a home BP monitor.

Keep it simple. Gifts with too many settings or app requirements often go unused. Prefer products with large buttons, clear displays and a manufacturer with local service centres.

Finally, offer setup as part of the gift. Spend twenty minutes pairing the device, saving contacts or showing how to use the remote. A well-explained gift gets used; a boxed one does not.

## Quick FAQ

**Is a blood pressure monitor a suitable Diwali gift?** It can be a very caring one, provided your parent is comfortable with the idea. It does not replace medical advice, so encourage them to share readings with their doctor rather than self-diagnosing.

**What if my parents are not comfortable with technology?** Choose products with a single button or a physical remote, and avoid anything that needs a smartphone app. A steam iron, an induction cooktop or a BP monitor with a clear display are all easy to operate.

**How do I avoid duplicate gifts from relatives?** Ask a sibling or an uncle what others are planning, or choose something specific to a habit your parents have, such as a health device or a kitchen item they use every day.

**Should I buy an appliance without asking first?** For anything large, such as an air purifier or a cooktop, a quick conversation about space and preference saves returns. For small gifts, a surprise is usually fine.
