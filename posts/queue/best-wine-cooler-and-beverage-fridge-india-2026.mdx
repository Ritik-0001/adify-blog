## Compressor vs thermoelectric

Thermoelectric (Peltier) coolers are silent, vibration-free and cheaper, but they cool only to roughly 10-15 degrees below room temperature. In an Indian summer where the room can reach 35 degrees or more, that can mean they hold just 20 degrees at best, which is too warm for wine. They also struggle to keep a steady temperature, so they suit short-term chilling in an air-conditioned room.

Compressor units work like a small refrigerator and can hold 5-18 degrees regardless of the room. They are louder and cost more, but they are the right choice for storing wine for months. Anything in the Rs 15,000 and above range that is genuinely a compressor model is a safer buy than a cheap thermoelectric one.

## Single zone vs dual zone

Wine is stored at around 12-14 degrees C for reds in long storage, and whites and sparkling are served cooler at 6-10 degrees. A dual zone cooler has two independently set compartments, so you can store reds and whites at their proper temperatures. If you drink mostly one type, a single zone is fine and cheaper. For a mixed collection, dual zone is worth the extra Rs 8,000-15,000.

## Capacity and what bottle counts mean

Bottle counts in listings usually assume standard Bordeaux bottles with the shelves in an ideal arrangement. Real bottles such as champagne, magnums or wide-base Indian wine bottles take more room, so a 24-bottle cooler might hold 18-20 in practice. Choose one size up from what you plan to store. Typical sizes for homes are 12, 18, 24 and 33 bottles, with larger units for collectors.

## Beverage fridge vs wine cooler

A beverage fridge is designed for cans, soft drinks, water, juices and beer at around 2-8 degrees, with wire shelves and a glass door. It is useful for parties, bars, home offices and gaming dens. A wine cooler is gentler and holds a higher temperature, with wooden shelves and UV-protected glass. Do not store wine in a beverage fridge for months, because it runs too cold and has too much vibration for aging.

## Placement and ventilation

Wine coolers need breathing room. Leave 5-10 cm at the back and sides for freestanding models, and a minimum of the manufacturer's stated clearance. Built-in or under-counter models are designed to vent through the front grille, so you can place them in a kitchen cabinet, but check that.

Avoid direct sun, a spot next to an oven or a hot wall. Place on a firm, level floor, because compressor vibration can disturb wine sediments. For a thermoelectric unit, an air-conditioned room is non-negotiable.

## Running cost and noise

Compressor coolers use around 0.5-1 kWh a day, so a typical home adds a few hundred rupees a month to the bill. They cycle on and off quietly, though a bedroom placement may bother light sleepers. Check the noise rating in dB if it will sit in a living room.

## Who should skip

If you open a bottle a month, a regular fridge or a cool, dark cupboard is enough. A wine cooler pays off when you buy wine to age, entertain often or want proper serving temperatures. For party drinks, a good beverage cooler is more useful than a wine cabinet.
