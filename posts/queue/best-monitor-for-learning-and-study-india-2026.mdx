## Eye Care Features That Actually Matter for Study

**Flicker-free backlight:** Traditional monitors flicker at 50–60Hz when dimmed — invisible but causing headaches and eye fatigue over hours. Flicker-free monitors use DC dimming, eliminating the strobe effect. All recommended monitors below use flicker-free technology.

**Blue light filter:** Blue wavelength light (400–500nm) is linked to sleep disruption and, at sustained exposure, contributes to eye fatigue. Study monitors with Low Blue Light modes reduce blue output by 30–70% while maintaining white balance. Best used for late-evening study sessions.

**Brightness Intelligence:** BenQ's exclusive feature — an ambient light sensor adjusts screen brightness to match room conditions automatically. Prevents the "screen too bright at night" or "screen too dim in daylight" problems that cause squinting or glare.

**High resolution:** Sharper text requires less eye effort to read. 1080p at 24 inches (94 PPI) is optimal for text at desk distance. Going below this (768p) noticeably increases reading effort.

## Monitor Placement for Study Ergonomics

Correct placement reduces neck and eye strain regardless of monitor quality:

**Distance:** Monitor should be 50–70cm from your eyes (arm's length). Too close causes eye strain; too far requires squinting.

**Height:** Top of screen at eye level or slightly below. Avoid looking up at the screen — neck strain accumulates over hours.

**Angle:** Tilt monitor slightly backward (5–10 degrees) so the screen face is perpendicular to your sight line.

**Lighting:** Avoid a window directly behind or directly facing the monitor — room should be lit from the side.
