## How to Choose a Heat Pump Water Heater

Check your hot water use. Heat pumps pay back most in homes with heavy daily hot water use, and for a couple a storage geyser may be simpler.

Look at efficiency and warranty. Check the coefficient of performance (COP) claim, the compressor warranty and the tank warranty.

Plan installation. Confirm space, ventilation, plumbing and power with the seller before you buy.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Buying for a small family.** The payback may be long.

**Ignoring installation.** Plan space and plumbing.

**Skipping the warranty details.** Read the terms.

**Assuming instant hot water.** Heat pumps heat more slowly.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A large family in a villa.** A 200 litre heat pump water heater.

**A hostel or small guest house.** A larger commercial-grade system.

**A couple in a flat.** A storage geyser is usually enough.

## What to Check on the Listing

Confirm the tank size, the COP, the compressor and tank warranty, the installation terms, the electrical requirement and the service availability. See what the seller includes.

## How a Heat Pump Saves Energy

A heat pump moves heat rather than creating it, so it can deliver several units of heat for each unit of electricity. The savings are largest where hot water use is high and the climate is warm.

## Quick FAQ

**Is it worth it?** For high use, often yes over several years.

**Does it work in winter?** Many models do, but efficiency drops in cold weather.

**Is service available?** Check with the brand.

## Maintenance and Service

Heat pumps have compressors, fans and refrigerant circuits, so regular servicing keeps them efficient. Ask the seller about an annual service plan, who handles breakdowns and how quickly technicians respond. Keep air filters and vents clean, and check the anode in the tank as advised.

## Payback Thinking

Compare the extra upfront cost of a heat pump with the yearly electricity saved over a storage geyser, using your own hot water use and tariff. For a large family with heavy use, payback can be a few years, while for smaller households a geyser may remain the more sensible choice.
