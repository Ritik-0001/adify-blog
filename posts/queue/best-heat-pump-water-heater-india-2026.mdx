## How to Choose a Heat Pump Water Heater

Check your hot water use. Heat pumps pay back most in homes with heavy daily hot water use, and for a couple a storage geyser may be simpler.

Look at efficiency and warranty. Check the coefficient of performance (COP) claim, the compressor warranty and the tank warranty.

Plan installation. Confirm space, ventilation, plumbing and power with the seller before you buy.

## How a Heat Pump Saves Energy

A heat pump moves heat rather than creating it, so it can deliver several units of heat for each unit of electricity. The savings are largest where hot water use is high and the climate is warm. Heat pumps also heat more slowly than a geyser, so they suit a tank that is topped up through the day rather than an instant heater.

## Maintenance and Payback

Heat pumps have compressors, fans and refrigerant circuits, so regular servicing keeps them efficient. Ask about an annual service plan, keep air filters and vents clean and have the tank anode checked as advised. To judge payback, compare the extra upfront cost over a storage geyser with the yearly electricity saved at your own tariff. A large family with heavy use may recover it in a few years, while smaller households often will not.

## Who Should Skip This

A couple or small family in a flat with one or two bathrooms will rarely recover the heat pump premium, so a good storage geyser is the sensible buy. Skip it as well if you have no ventilated spot for the unit or a tank, or if your city has no authorised service for heat pumps, since a compressor fault with no technician makes the savings meaningless.
