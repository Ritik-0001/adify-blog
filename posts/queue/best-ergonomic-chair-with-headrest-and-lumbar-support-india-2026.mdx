## How to Choose a Chair With Headrest and Lumbar Support

Set the lumbar height first. The support should sit at the curve of your lower back, roughly at belt level. A chair with adjustable height lets you fine-tune the position.

Position the headrest for support, not for pushing your head forward. It should meet the base of your skull when you lean back slightly, and it is most useful during calls or reading.

Check that the tilt mechanism has a lock. Being able to recline and lock the position keeps the headrest and back support working together.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Setting the headrest too high or low.** It should support the neck, not push it forward.

**Ignoring seat depth.** The seat should not press behind the knees.

**Skipping adjustments.** Spend time setting up the chair.

**Assuming more adjustments means better.** Choose the ones you will use.

## What to Check on the Listing

Confirm the adjustment ranges, the weight limit, the warranty and assembly terms. Read reviews about the durability of the headrest mechanism.

## Setting Up Headrest and Lumbar Support

Adjust the lumbar first, then the seat, then the headrest. Sit back fully, and raise or lower the lumbar until it presses gently into the hollow of your lower back. Set the seat depth so that your thighs are supported without pressing behind your knees. Finally, set the headrest so it cradles the base of your skull when you recline a few degrees. Recheck the settings after a week, since your body adapts and you may want small changes.

## Quick FAQ

**Do I need a headrest?** It helps when you lean back, but is optional.

**Is adaptive lumbar worth it?** It suits people who shift posture often.

**How often should I adjust?** Adjust when your posture changes.
