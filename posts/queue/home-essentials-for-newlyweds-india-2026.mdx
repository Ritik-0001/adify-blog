Newlyweds should start with a refrigerator, a washing machine, a water purifier, a geyser and a few kitchen basics, then add comfort items like an AC or a TV as the budget allows. A sensible first set of big appliances can cost roughly ₹60,000 to ₹1,50,000 depending on size and brand, and you can spread purchases over a few months.

This checklist is researched from Amazon India listings, brand information and buyer feedback, not hands-on testing. Every home is different, so adjust the list for a flat or a house, rented or owned, and a small or large family.

Roughly ₹20,000–40,000. A 250 to 350 litre double-door refrigerator suits a couple and leaves room for guests. Choose a good energy rating and a long compressor warranty.

Why it works: a daily essential. The trade-off is size and electricity use.

## How to Plan Your First Purchases

Start with needs, not wants. A refrigerator, washing machine, purifier and geyser come first, and a TV or AC can follow.

Check space and wiring. Measure doorways, kitchen counters and balcony space, and ask an electrician about the load before ordering heavy appliances.

Stage the spending. Buying over a few months lets you compare live prices and avoid overspending.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Buying everything at once.** Spread purchases and compare prices.

**Choosing the wrong size.** Measure first.

**Ignoring warranty and service.** Read the terms.

**Skipping energy ratings.** They affect running cost.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A couple in a rented 1BHK.** A refrigerator, washing machine and purifier first.

**A couple in their own 2BHK.** Add a geyser and a TV soon after.

**A couple who cooks a lot.** Prioritise the kitchen set.

## Quick FAQ

**Should we buy a dishwasher?** Only if you cook daily and have space.

**Is a robot vacuum an essential?** It is a nice extra, not a first-year need.

**Should we buy online?** Yes if delivery and installation are clear.

## What to Check on the Listing

Look at the seller rating and the warranty, and compare the exact model number you shortlisted. Read the delivery and installation terms, since large appliances often need a technician visit, and check the return policy for damaged deliveries.

## Setting a Realistic First-Year Budget

List the items in order of priority, add approximate prices and decide how much you can spend before the wedding and in the first year. Leave a margin for installation, stabilisers, stands and annual servicing. Many couples find that a phased plan keeps debt away and lets them choose better models.

## Buying for a Rented Home

If you rent, think about portability. Compact, freestanding appliances move easily, while built-in items stay behind. Check the landlord's rules on drilling, and keep the installation paperwork so that you can reinstall in the next home.

## Buying Smart Without Overspending

Resist the urge to buy premium versions of everything. A mid-range refrigerator or washing machine from a trusted brand serves most couples for a decade, and the money saved can go toward an AC or a better mattress. Compare two or three models per appliance, read the warranty terms, and keep a spreadsheet of live prices so you can buy at a number you are comfortable with.

## Final Checklist Before You Buy

Before you order, make a shortlist of two or three models per item, compare live prices over several days and check delivery and installation timelines, which can be longer near festivals and winter. Read the warranty and return terms, confirm the exact model on the listing and keep the invoices together in one folder.

## Recommendation

Start with a refrigerator, a washing machine, a water purifier and a geyser, then add kitchen items and comfort appliances. Always check the live price and warranty on each listing.
