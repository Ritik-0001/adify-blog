## How to Choose a Travel Camera

Think about size and weight. A smaller camera gets used more, and a light kit with one zoom is easier to carry on long days.

Look at battery and weather. Good battery life and some weather sealing help on trips, and a spare battery and a USB charger add flexibility.

Consider video and stabilisation. In-body stabilisation helps with handheld shots in low light and with video, which suits travel diaries.

## APS-C or Full-Frame on the Road

APS-C bodies and lenses are lighter, so the whole kit fits in a small sling bag and is easier to carry through a day of walking. Full-frame gives cleaner images at night and in temples, markets and dim interiors, but the lenses add weight. Decide which of those two annoyances you can live with before you compare models.

## Packing and Protecting Your Camera

Use a padded sling or a small camera bag, a silica gel pack for humid climates and a lens cloth. Carry a clear protective filter for dust and sand. Bring at least one spare battery and fast memory cards, and back up photos each evening to a phone, laptop or drive.

## Who Should Skip This

If your travel photos are mostly for social media and you rarely print or crop, a recent flagship phone may do the job without the lens bag, cards and charging routine. Skip a full-frame body such as the a7C II too if you pack light and mostly shoot in daylight, since APS-C quality will look the same on a phone screen.

## Memory Cards, Backups and Charging on Trips

A long trip can produce hundreds of photos a day, so carry more than one fast memory card instead of a single big one. Back up each evening, and charge batteries overnight from a power bank or a hotel socket with a small multi-port charger. Check airline rules for spare lithium batteries, which normally go in carry-on bags rather than checked luggage.
