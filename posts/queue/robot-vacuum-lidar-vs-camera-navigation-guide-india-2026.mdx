For most Indian homes, LiDAR navigation is the better base choice for a robot vacuum because it maps accurately even in the dark, while camera or structured-light sensors add recognition of small obstacles like socks and cables. The best robots in roughly the ₹30,000-70,000 range combine both.

## How each system works

LiDAR uses a spinning laser on top of the robot to measure distance to walls and furniture. It builds a map quickly, in any lighting, and plans efficient straight rows.

Camera navigation, often called vSLAM, uses a lens to recognise ceiling features and landmarks. It needs light. Structured-light or ToF sensors project patterns to see obstacles at floor level, and AI cameras identify objects such as shoes and pet waste.

## Mapping speed and accuracy

LiDAR robots map a flat in minutes with clean room outlines, and rarely lose their place. They allow multi-floor maps, no-go zones, and room-by-room cleaning that you can trust.

Camera-only robots can be good in bright rooms but may get lost when lighting changes, for example after sunset or with curtains drawn. Maps are sometimes less precise, and large open flats can confuse them.

## Dark rooms and night cleaning

Many Indian families run robots at night or in shuttered bedrooms. LiDAR works with the lights off, which makes it the safe default. Camera and structured-light obstacle sensors often need a small LED to see, so check whether the robot has a light and whether it is bright enough.

Robots that rely on a plain camera for navigation may avoid dark areas or bump more often at night.

## Privacy: what the camera sees

A robot with a front camera can capture images of your floor and rooms. Check how the images are handled: processed on the robot or uploaded to the cloud. Some brands process obstacle recognition locally. Look for an option to disable image upload or to switch off the camera.

Cameras can also record video if you use a remote view or patrol feature. Disable such features in bedrooms or use no-go zones. Choose brands with clear privacy policies and enable app security such as two-factor login.

## Obstacles in Indian homes

Typical hazards include charger cables, slippers, dupattas, floor mats, pet bowls and loose curtains. Structured-light sensors detect many of these, but dark objects such as black cables can fool simple sensors. No robot is perfect; prepare the floor before running.

Thresholds and raised door sills are another real obstacle. Check the climbing height, usually 2 to 4 cm. ## Who should skip fancy navigation

If your home is a small studio with minimal clutter, a simple LiDAR model without obstacle AI will do. If you do not want cameras in your home, avoid camera-based models and choose LiDAR with a bumper and keep floors tidy.

## Recommendation

Choose a LiDAR robot with 3D obstacle sensing for most homes, especially with pets or kids. Choose a plain LiDAR robot if you keep floors tidy and want to save money. Choose a camera-led robot only if you value its object recognition and your home is bright. Review the privacy settings before setup.
