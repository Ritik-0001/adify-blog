## How to Choose a Portrait Camera

Look at autofocus. Reliable eye detection makes portraits easier, especially for moving subjects and children.

Think about the lens. A portrait lens often makes more difference than the camera body, so budget for a good 50mm or 85mm.

Consider the sensor. Full-frame gives shallower depth of field and better low light, and high-resolution APS-C gives fine detail in good light.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Spending everything on the body.** Save for a good lens.

**Shooting wide open without checking focus.** Stop down a little.

**Ignoring light.** Use window light or a reflector.

**Skipping practice.** Learn posing and direction.

## Real-World Scenarios

## Getting Better Portraits

Use eye autofocus, place your subject near a window, shoot at around f/2 to f/2.8 for soft backgrounds and keep the shutter speed high enough for sharp images. Direct your subject and take many frames.

## Warranty and Service

Check that warranty is valid in India and that service is available in your city. Keep the invoice and serial numbers.

## Quick FAQ

**Is full-frame necessary?** Not required, but helpful for shallow depth of field.

**Which lens first?** A 50mm f/1.8.

**Do I need two card slots?** For paid work, it is a useful safety feature.

## Lenses by Budget

A 50mm f/1.8 is the most affordable route to soft backgrounds, an 85mm f/1.8 gives a flattering look from a comfortable distance and a 35mm f/1.8 suits environmental portraits indoors. Zoom lenses with a constant f/2.8 aperture are versatile but heavier and costlier. Choose one prime first, learn it well and add another only when you feel limited.

## Posing and Direction Basics

Even the best camera cannot rescue a stiff pose. Ask your subject to turn their shoulders slightly, tilt the chin down a little and look just past the lens for a natural expression. Shoot from eye level or slightly above, use window light at an angle and take plenty of frames so that you can pick the best moments.
