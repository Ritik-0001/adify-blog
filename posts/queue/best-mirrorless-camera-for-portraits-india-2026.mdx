## How to Choose a Portrait Camera

Look at autofocus. Reliable eye detection makes portraits easier, especially for moving subjects and children.

Think about the lens. A portrait lens often makes more difference than the camera body, so budget for a good 50mm or 85mm.

Consider the sensor. Full-frame gives shallower depth of field and better low light, and high-resolution APS-C gives fine detail in good light.

## Lenses by Budget

A 50mm f/1.8 is the most affordable route to soft backgrounds, an 85mm f/1.8 gives a flattering look from a comfortable distance and a 35mm f/1.8 suits environmental portraits indoors. Zoom lenses with a constant f/2.8 aperture are versatile but heavier and costlier. Choose one prime first, learn it well and add another only when you feel limited.

## Light and Direction

Use eye autofocus, place your subject near a window, shoot at around f/2 to f/2.8 for soft backgrounds and keep the shutter speed high enough for sharp images. Even the best camera cannot rescue a stiff pose, so ask your subject to turn their shoulders slightly, tilt the chin down a little and look just past the lens. Shoot from eye level or slightly above and take plenty of frames.

## Who Should Skip This

If your portraits are mostly casual family snaps on a phone screen, a modern phone's portrait mode will cover most of it, and a body plus lens at this price is hard to justify. Anyone doing paid work should look past single-card bodies such as the R8 and favour two card slots for safety.

## Eye Autofocus in Real Use

Eye autofocus helps most when your subject moves, such as children, pets or a bride walking down an aisle. It is less useful for a seated portrait where you can focus once and recompose. Before buying, search reviews for how each body behaves with sunglasses, side profiles and two faces close together, since those are the moments when tracking can wander.

## Recommendation

For most buyers, the Canon EOS R8 with a 50mm lens is a good value start, the Sony a7 IV suits professionals, and the Fujifilm X-T5 suits colour-focused shooters.
