## How to Choose a Treadmill Under ₹40,000

Check the belt size. A belt of at least 42 cm by 120 cm is comfortable for most people when jogging, while narrower belts are fine for walking. Taller users need longer belts.

Look at the motor and warranty. A continuous rating of 1.5 to 2 HP suits regular jogging, and a motor warranty of a few years gives peace of mind.

Check the weight limit. Choose a machine with a weight rating well above your own for a stronger frame.

## Treadmill Safety Basics

Always clip the safety key to your clothing before starting, keep the belt clear of loose items and use shoes with a good grip. Begin at a slow speed, hold the handrails while stepping on and avoid jumping on at high speed. Keep children and pets away from a running treadmill, and unplug the machine when it is not in use for long periods.

## Making the Most of a Mid-Range Machine

Mix steady walks with short interval blocks, such as one minute of jogging and two minutes of walking, and gradually shift the ratio as your fitness improves. Track your weekly total rather than a single session, and use the preset programs on your console to avoid boredom. Consistency matters more than intensity in the first few months.

## Small Extras Worth Budgeting For

Set aside a little extra for a thick rubber mat and for a stabiliser if your home has voltage swings. Compare the warranty on the motor separately from the warranty on parts, and prefer a seller that arranges installation. Spending a little more on a longer belt and a better motor warranty usually costs less over several years than replacing a weak machine.

## Who Should Skip This Price Band

If you plan to run hard five days a week, or the heaviest user is near the top of the weight limit, a machine under ₹40,000 will be stretched. Saving for a sturdier model in the next price tier is wiser than pushing a lighter motor to its limit. Walkers and light joggers, on the other hand, get all they need from this band.
