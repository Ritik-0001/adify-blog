## The throw-ratio maths in one paragraph

Throw ratio is the distance from lens to screen divided by the image width. A 100 inch 16:9 screen is around 2.21 metres wide. At a throw ratio of 1.5 the projector needs about 3.3 metres; at 1.0 it needs about 2.2 metres; at 0.5, about 1.1 metres. So a bedroom 3 metres deep can fit a 100 inch picture with a ratio of 1.2 or less, with room for the projector on a shelf behind the bed.

## 1. Short throw (ratio 0.5 to 1.0)

These projectors sit on a coffee table or shelf and can project a 100 inch image from 1.2 to 2.2 metres. They suit flats and bedrooms. Epson, BenQ and ViewSonic sell these, typically with 1080p resolution and 2,000 to 3,500 lumens. Many corporate-focused short-throw models are bright but lack the colour tuning of home cinema models, so check which type you are buying. [Browse BenQ short throw](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=BenQ+short+throw+projector&tag=adifystore-21).

## 2. Ultra-short-throw (ratio under 0.4)

UST projectors sit right under the wall, only 10 to 40 centimetres away, and throw a very large image upward. They are the closest thing to a TV replacement, often with built-in speakers and a smart TV interface. Prices in India start around ₹1,00,000 and exceed ₹3,00,000 for laser 4K models. They need an ambient-light-rejecting screen if you watch in daylight. [Search ultra short throw projectors](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=ultra+short+throw+laser+projector&tag=adifystore-21). Brands include Hisense, Samsung and Epson.

## Brightness for small rooms

In a small room you do not need the highest lumens, because the picture is smaller than in a big hall. For a dark or curtain-controlled room, 1,000 to 1,500 ANSI lumens is workable. For living rooms with daylight, aim for 2,500 lumens and above, and use a daytime-friendly screen. Beware of listings quoting "LED lumens" or "supported lumens", which are not ANSI lumens and read much higher than real brightness.

## Resolution and sharpness

At 100 inches, 1080p is acceptable, and native 4K is noticeable on text. Many cheaper "4K supported" projectors only accept a 4K signal and display it at lower resolution, so look for native resolution or pixel-shifting 4K (XPR). [Search 4K projectors](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=4K+home+theatre+projector&tag=adifystore-21).

## Screen choice

A white wall works, but a proper screen helps. A fixed-frame or roll-up screen of 100 inches costs roughly ₹5,000-25,000. For ultra-short-throw, you need a special ALR (ambient light rejecting) screen, which costs more. [Browse projector screens](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=100+inch+projector+screen&tag=adifystore-21).

## Installation realities

- Short throw projectors project at steep angles, so wall flatness matters. A bumpy wall shows distortion more clearly.
- Mounting on a table is easy but tolerates little movement. Keep it away from fans and pedestals that wobble.
- Fans are louder on bright models. Check the noise rating, ideally under 30 dB in eco mode, if you will use it in a bedroom.
- Lamp life on lamp-based models is roughly 3,000 to 5,000 hours, and replacements cost several thousand rupees. LED and laser models avoid this.

## Sound

Built-in projector speakers are weak. Plan for a Bluetooth speaker or a small soundbar. [See compact soundbars](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=compact+soundbar+HDMI+ARC&tag=adifystore-21).

## Who should skip a short throw projector

If your room is only about 2 metres deep or you cannot dim it in the evening, a TV may serve better than a projector. If you only watch cricket in a bright hall, a big 65 inch TV outperforms a projector without heavy curtains.

## Recommendation

For a small bedroom or flat, buy a short throw model with a ratio around 0.8 to 1.0, roughly 2,000 ANSI lumens and 1080p or native 4K, in the ₹40,000-90,000 band, plus a proper screen. Choose ultra-short-throw only if you want a TV-like setup and budget above ₹1,00,000. Always check the ratio, ANSI lumens and lamp life on the listing before ordering.
