## How to Choose a MacBook for Programming

Prioritise memory. 16GB is a comfortable minimum for most development, and 24GB or 32GB helps with Docker, emulators and machine learning, since memory cannot be upgraded later.

Choose storage wisely. 512GB suits many developers, and 1TB is better for large projects, since storage upgrades from Apple are expensive.

Pick the right model. The Air suits most, and the Pro suits sustained heavy loads, many ports and an excellent display.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Buying 8GB memory.** It may limit you quickly.

**Skimping on storage.** Large projects fill space.

**Ignoring the chip generation.** Check the listing.

**Buying from unauthorised sellers.** Verify warranty.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A web developer.** A MacBook Air with 16GB.

**A mobile developer.** A MacBook Pro with 24GB or more.

**A student on a budget.** An older generation Air with 16GB.

## What to Check on the Listing

Confirm the chip, the memory, the storage, the screen size, the colour and the warranty. See that the seller is authorised and that the model number matches.

## Making a MacBook Work for Development

Use Homebrew for tools, keep Xcode command line tools updated and use an external monitor for more screen space. Many developers run Docker, so allow memory headroom.

## Warranty and Service

Check the warranty and consider AppleCare for longer coverage. Keep the invoice and serial number.

## Quick FAQ

**Is the Air enough for programming?** For most work, yes.

**Do I need a Pro?** For heavy builds, emulators or ML.

**Is 16GB enough?** For most developers.

## Chips, Memory and Longevity

Apple silicon chips differ in performance cores, graphics cores and memory bandwidth, and higher chips matter mostly for builds, video and machine learning. Because memory and storage cannot be upgraded later, buy slightly more than you need today. A well-chosen MacBook commonly serves developers for many years, which can justify the premium over cheaper laptops.
