## Sound character

Bose designs its soundbars for voices and clean mids. Its Smart Soundbar 600 and 900 use dedicated dialogue drivers and, on the larger model, up-firing Atmos speakers. The sound is polished, and speech stays clear at lower volumes, which suits late-night viewing.

Sonos tunes for balance and room adaptation. The Beam (Gen 2) is a compact bar with Dolby Atmos processing, while the Arc is a larger, more spacious Atmos bar. Sonos's Trueplay tuning measures your room using an iPhone microphone, which can improve sound noticeably in a difficult room. Android users should check the current status of room tuning, as support differs.

Neither brand includes a subwoofer. Both sell one separately (Bose Bass Module, Sonos Sub), which adds a large amount to the price.

## Apps and daily use

This is where the two diverge.

Bose's app handles setup, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and Alexa or Google Assistant control on supported models. It is a simple tool with fewer features, which most buyers find enough.

Sonos has a richer platform. You can group speakers across rooms, play different music in different places and control everything from one app. However, Sonos revamped its app in 2024, and the transition drew widespread complaints about missing features. Check recent reviews of the app's stability before deciding, because it is the main source of dissatisfaction among owners.

## Ecosystem and expansion

Sonos shines if you intend to buy more than one speaker. Add a pair of rear speakers to a Beam or Arc for true surround, add a Sub for bass, then place a smaller speaker in the bedroom. They all work together as a system. [Search Sonos speakers](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=Sonos+Beam+Arc&tag=adifystore-21).

Bose also supports some expansion, such as adding rear speakers and a bass module, but its multi-room features are narrower. If you only want one great bar, this advantage does not matter.

## Streaming features

Both brands support AirPlay 2 and Wi-Fi streaming on their main smart soundbars, so iPhone users can send music directly. Check Bluetooth: Bose generally supports it, while some Sonos models focus on Wi-Fi and do not offer Bluetooth audio streaming, so confirm the exact model.

## Prices and availability in India

Bose has an official presence in India through authorised retailers, so warranty is generally straightforward for the Smart Soundbar range. Sonos has had a more limited and changing retail presence, so confirm that the listing is from an authorised seller and that warranty is valid in India. This is the single biggest risk with Sonos here, since an expensive speaker without service backup is a poor deal. ## Rear speakers and subwoofers

Both brands sell wireless surround add-ons that cost nearly as much as the bar. [Browse wireless surround speaker add-ons](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=wireless+surround+speakers+soundbar&tag=adifystore-21) if you plan to build up over time.

## TV connection

Both use HDMI eARC. Check your TV's eARC port and use the supplied cable. Bose and Sonos both pass through Atmos from supported streaming apps when connected this way. If your TV lacks eARC, the Sonos Beam can still use ARC, but Atmos then depends on the source. [See HDMI eARC cables](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=HDMI+eARC+cable+8K&tag=adifystore-21).

## Who should skip each

- **Skip Bose** if you plan a multi-room setup and want maximum flexibility.
- **Skip Sonos** if you want the least fuss, an Android-friendly experience, or reliable local warranty without researching sellers.
- **Skip both** if value matters more than refinement. Samsung, Sony and JBL offer a subwoofer and surround speakers in the same budget.

## Recommendation

For a single, great-sounding bar for dialogue and everyday TV, buy Bose and confirm the Indian warranty. For a system that grows over years and works in several rooms, consider Sonos, but only from an authorised seller and after reading recent app feedback. If your budget is under ₹40,000, neither is the best value, and a bar with a subwoofer from another brand deserves a look first.
