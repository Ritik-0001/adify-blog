## 1. Refrigerators

Roughly ₹20,000–60,000 for good double-door models. A refrigerator runs all day, so energy rating and capacity matter more than extra features. Decide on size first, then compare star ratings and compressor warranties.

Why it works: a long-lasting, high-use item. The trade-off is that a large model needs space and sometimes a higher electricity draw.

## 2. Washing Machines

Roughly ₹20,000–50,000 for front-load and premium top-load models. Pick capacity by family size, check motor warranty and consider water and power use.

Why it works: saves daily effort. The trade-off is installation requirements and water supply.

## 3. Smart TVs

Roughly ₹20,000–70,000. A 43 to 65 inch 4K smart TV is a common year-end purchase. Compare panel type, sound and app support, and keep the room size in mind.

Why it works: long use and many options. The trade-off is that models change quickly, so check the current lineup.

## How to Plan a Year-End Purchase

Set a total budget. List the appliances you need, rank them by urgency and allocate money to the top two or three.

Compare models, not prices alone. Check energy rating, warranty and service availability for each shortlisted model.

Track live prices. Prices on the same model can move, so check the live price over several days and decide on a number you are happy to pay.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Buying something you do not need because it looks cheap.** A bargain on an unneeded item is wasted money.

**Ignoring installation and delivery.** Check lead times, especially for ACs and geysers.

**Skipping warranty details.** Read compressor, motor and panel terms.

**Not comparing across sellers.** Check seller ratings and return terms.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A family replacing an old fridge.** Shortlist three double-door models and compare for a week.

**A new homeowner.** Prioritise the refrigerator, washing machine and geyser first.

**A couple planning for summer.** Compare ACs now and buy closer to the season.

## Quick FAQ

**Is year end a good time to buy?** It can be, but check live prices and do not assume a discount.

**Should I wait for a sale?** Only if your need is not urgent, and compare real prices.

**How do I avoid fake bargains?** Compare the live price across a few days and check the seller.

## Checking Energy Ratings and Running Costs

A lower purchase price can hide a higher running cost. Look at the star rating on refrigerators, ACs and washing machines, and compare the annual energy figures where the brand publishes them. A five-star appliance costs more upfront but usually costs less to run over the years you own it.

## Delivery and Installation Planning

Large appliances need delivery, unpacking and often installation by a technician. Check whether installation is included, how long the booking takes, and what extra charges apply for things like copper piping on ACs or a stand for a washing machine. Booking installation early avoids waiting at the busiest time.

## What to Check on the Listing

Compare the seller, the warranty on the product and on key parts, the return policy and the model number. Confirm the exact model on the listing matches the one you researched, since brands sometimes sell slightly different variants for different channels.

## Recommendation

Buy what you need now after a calm comparison, and hold off on appliances you will not use for months. Always check the live price and the warranty on each listing.
