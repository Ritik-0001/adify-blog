## Why battery doorbells suit Indian flats

Western doorbells often tap into a low-voltage transformer. Indian homes typically have a mains-powered bell switch with a loud chime, and no suitable transformer. A battery doorbell sidesteps that: it mounts on the wall or door frame, connects to Wi-Fi, and sends a notification to your phone.

The catch is that you must charge or replace the battery, and Wi-Fi must reach your door, which in a corridor-facing flat can be tricky.

## Battery life reality

Brands claim months of battery life. In practice, busy doors with delivery visits, neighbours and constant motion alerts shorten that to a few weeks to two months. A quieter door lasts longer.

Cold, heat and very frequent live-view checks drain batteries faster. Choose a model with a removable or rechargeable battery and consider a spare pack. Setting motion zones and sensitivity reduces needless recordings.

## The chime: do not forget it

A phone notification is not a doorbell. If you are in the kitchen with your phone elsewhere, you will miss visitors. Buy a plug-in wireless chime, or use a base station with a built-in speaker, or both.

Check whether your existing Indian bell can be wired in parallel. Do not attempt to connect a low-voltage doorbell to mains; if you need a wired solution, get an electrician. See [plug-in wireless doorbell chime](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=wireless+doorbell+chime+plug+in&tag=adifystore-21).

## Storage: cloud versus local

Cloud storage costs a monthly fee, but footage is safe if the doorbell is stolen. Local storage on a microSD card or base station is cheaper, but a thief could take the device. Many owners use local storage and rely on app alerts. A high-endurance card matters. ## Wi-Fi and range

Battery doorbells use 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi. If your router is deep inside the flat, signal at the door may be weak. A mesh Wi-Fi node or extender near the entrance solves most problems.

## Who should skip a battery doorbell

Skip it if your door has no Wi-Fi coverage and you cannot add a node, or if you want continuous recording. A wired doorbell with a camera, or a small Wi-Fi camera pointing at the door, may serve you better.

Also skip it if you live in a gated society that already has an intercom and a guard; the doorbell adds less.
