## Sizing by litres per hour

Estimate consumption at roughly 2 to 3 litres per person per day for drinking and tea, more in summer. A 30-person office therefore uses 60-90 litres daily. A 25 LPH unit makes that in four hours, and a storage tank handles peaks. Do not size to the average; a Monday-morning rush or a client visit with ten guests draws far more than the daily mean.

Output ratings are measured under ideal conditions. Real flow drops with low pressure, cold water and old membranes, so leave a 30 percent margin. A [50 LPH commercial RO](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=50+lph+commercial+ro+water+purifier&tag=adifystore-21) suits offices of 50-100 people or any setup with a canteen.

## Commercial RO or dispenser

A commercial RO plant is built around a larger membrane, a stronger pump and a stainless steel tank, and it feeds taps through a pipe. A water dispenser takes purified water (from a built-in purifier or a separate one) and offers hot, normal and cold outputs.

Many offices combine both: the commercial RO treats the incoming water, and a [hot and cold water dispenser](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=hot+and+cold+water+dispenser+with+ro&tag=adifystore-21) serves it at the pantry. Dispensers with built-in RO are simpler but have limited output, typically 10-25 LPH, so check whether the model can handle the number of people.

Bottle-top dispensers using 20-litre jars are cheaper to install, but jar water quality depends on the supplier, and storing jars adds hygiene risk. Skip jar dispensers if you want control over purity.

## Water quality and TDS

Test the incoming supply. Borewell or tanker water with TDS above roughly 1,000 may need a pre-treatment stage such as a softener or a larger membrane, as a standard commercial RO is designed for up to certain limits. Check the maximum input TDS listed by the manufacturer.

Commercial units often run with higher recovery settings, which means less reject water, but also faster scaling in hard water areas. A water softener before the RO costs extra but extends membrane life.

## Servicing and annual contracts

Commercial usage wears filters faster than domestic use. Plan for quarterly pre-filter changes and an annual membrane check, with a written service contract. Ask what is covered, such as labour, the membrane, the UV lamp and visit frequency. Offices in smaller towns should confirm that the brand has a local technician.

Keep a log sheet next to the unit. Record every service date and TDS reading, so that missed maintenance does not go unnoticed.

## Hygiene practices

Wipe taps and drip trays daily, since bacteria spread from dirty taps even when the water is clean. Flush hot and cold tanks as the manual advises, and replace the UV lamp on schedule. Stagnant water in a long-unused tank, for example after a holiday weekend, should be flushed before use.

Clinics and food businesses should check whether local rules require periodic water testing and keep the reports. A cheap TDS meter does not show bacteria, so send a sample to a lab at least annually.

## Power, space and plumbing

Commercial RO units draw more power than domestic ones and typically need a dedicated socket. Check the mounting: wall-mount models suit 10-25 LPH, while larger units are floor-standing and need a strong base and drain. Plan for the reject water drain; a 25 LPH unit can drain well over 25 litres an hour while running.

## Who should skip a commercial plant

Tiny offices, co-working desks and shops with a few staff do not need a commercial RO; its installation, power and service costs are not worth it. A coworking space that already has building-supplied filtered water should check that supply before buying anything.
