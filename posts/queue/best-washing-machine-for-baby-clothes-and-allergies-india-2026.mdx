## Why babies and allergies need different settings

Newborn skin reacts to detergent residue, and dust mites, pollen and pet dander cling to fabric. A normal cold wash removes dirt but leaves much of the allergen load behind. The three levers are heat, steam and rinsing. Higher temperature kills mites and bacteria, steam relaxes fibres and loosens allergens, and extra rinse removes the detergent that would otherwise sit in the cloth.

## Features that matter, ranked

1. **Hot wash at 60 degrees C or more.** Many mite-reducing guidelines refer to washing bedding at around 60 degrees. Check the highest temperature the machine reaches; some go up to 90 degrees.
2. **Steam cycle.** A steam program sanitises and refreshes fabrics without a long hot soak. Useful for bedding, soft toys and baby clothes that cannot take heat.
3. **Extra rinse or rinse hold.** Choose a model where you can add one or two rinses, because baby clothes need minimal detergent residue.
4. **Drum clean or tub clean.** A high-temperature self-clean program prevents the mould and smell that can accumulate in a front loader and trigger allergies.
5. **Gentle or baby care program.** Lower spin speed, longer soak and mild agitation. Check it by name.
6. **Door seal and drum design.** A removable or wipeable seal helps you clean lint and moisture.

## Front load or top load

For allergies, front load is the stronger choice because it heats water inside and has better steam and rinse control. Top loaders generally do not heat water, so you depend on hot tap water or a geyser, and fewer have steam. If you must buy a top load, look for models with a built-in heater, which are rarer. ## Detergent and routine

A machine does not make up for the wrong detergent. Use a fragrance-free, dermatologically gentle liquid detergent in a small dose, skip fabric softener (which leaves residue), and add an extra rinse. Wash baby clothes separately from adults' laundry, and run a hot cycle on towels and bedding weekly. Before first use of newly bought clothes, wash them once.

## Keep the drum itself clean

Every two to four weeks, run the drum clean cycle with the door open afterward. Wipe the rubber seal, clean the detergent drawer and remove the inlet filter for a rinse. A smelly drum defeats the purpose of buying a premium machine, so make this a habit.
