## Technology

Both brands offer RO, UV and UF combinations, and add mineral or TDS control features. Kent markets its RO+UV+UF+TDS Control models heavily, and Aquaguard offers RO+UV+UF and copper or mineral enrichment options on selected models.

The right technology depends on your water source. RO handles high TDS, and UV and UF add microbial protection.

## Range and Choice

Aquaguard offers a wide range from basic to premium models. Kent also has a broad range with countertop, wall-mounted and under-sink options.

Both offer models at most price points, so match features to your water and budget.

## Service and Support

Both have large service networks across India. Kent runs its own service teams, and Aquaguard, from Eureka Forbes, has a long-established network. Check local service availability and response time.

## Running Costs

Both require filter and membrane changes and annual servicing. Costs vary by model, so check annual maintenance contract prices before you buy.

## Design and Convenience

Both offer wall-mounted designs with large tanks. Some models add digital indicators and alerts. Check the tank size and dispensing features.

## Price and Value

Prices overlap at the ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 range. Compare the features included and the servicing cost rather than the purchase price alone.

## How to Choose Between Aquaguard and Kent

Start with your water. Get the TDS tested, and choose RO for high-TDS water. Then compare tank size, stages, service and cost of maintenance.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A home with borewell water.** Prioritise RO strength and a good pre-filter.

**A home with municipal water.** A UF or RO+UV model with TDS control may suffice.

**A family that wants copper.** Look at Aquaguard's copper-enriched models.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Ignoring TDS.** Test your water.

**Skipping servicing.** Change filters on time.

**Buying on brand alone.** Compare models.

## Water Quality Matters More Than the Brand

The most important step is testing your water. High-TDS or hard water calls for RO with a strong pre-filter, while low-TDS municipal water may be well served by UF or RO with a TDS controller. Buying a premium purifier that does not match your water source wastes money and can strip minerals you want.

## Installation and Ongoing Costs

Both brands typically arrange installation and offer annual service plans. Ask what the plan covers, how many visits are included, and whether membranes and UV lamps are covered. Over five years, servicing costs can rival the purchase price, so compare them before you decide.

## Maintenance Habits That Help

Change filters when the indicator or the technician advises, clean the tank periodically and avoid switching the purifier off for long periods with stagnant water inside. Use reject water for cleaning, and keep the surroundings dry.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A city home on municipal supply with moderate TDS.** A model with RO plus a TDS controller and UV suits well, and both brands cover this range.

**A borewell home with high TDS and hardness.** Prioritise a strong pump and membrane, a good pre-filter and a servicing plan.

**A family that stores water in an overhead tank.** Check the input pressure requirements, since some purifiers need a booster.

**A small kitchen with limited space.** Look at compact or under-sink models from either brand.

## Checking Reviews and Service Feedback

Read recent reviews for the exact model, and pay attention to comments about installation quality, technician response times and the cost of filter replacements. Service experience varies by city, so ask neighbours or a local RWA group which brand has responded reliably in your area.
