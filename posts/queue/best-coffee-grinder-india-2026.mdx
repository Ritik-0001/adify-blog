## Burr Grinder vs Blade Grinder — Why It Matters

**Blade grinders (₹500–₂,000):** Spin metal blades like a blender. Produce uneven particle sizes — some powder-fine, some coarse. Uneven grind means uneven extraction: some coffee over-extracts (bitter) while some under-extracts (sour). Also generate heat that degrades coffee flavor.

**Burr grinders (₹2,500–₃₀,000+):** Two abrasive surfaces crush beans to a uniform particle size. Consistent particle size = consistent extraction = balanced, delicious coffee. The foundation of quality home brewing.

**Summary:** Blade grinders are significantly worse for coffee quality. If you're spending money on quality coffee beans, a blade grinder wastes the investment.

## 4. Generic Indian Blade Grinders — What to Avoid

Brands like Philips (HL1643, HL1645), Bosch, and various Indian kitchen mixers with "coffee grinding" jars use blade chopping, not burr grinding. While these work for grinding spices, they produce poor-quality coffee grind.

If your mixer/grinder already has a dry coffee grinding jar, it's better than nothing — but a dedicated burr grinder will produce noticeably better coffee.

**Exception:** If you're using strong South Indian filter coffee powder (Cothas, Leo, Narasu's) which is pre-ground at commercial grade, a grinder isn't necessary — just buy fresh beans when upgrading your coffee game.
