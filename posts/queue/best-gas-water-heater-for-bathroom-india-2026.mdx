## Why safety comes before the shopping list

Gas geysers burn fuel to heat water, and incomplete combustion produces carbon monoxide. Every year there are reports in Indian cities of deaths from bathrooms where an old geyser was placed inside with no ventilation. This is the single most important fact in this article.

A gas heater should never be installed inside a closed bathroom unless it is a sealed, room-sealed or forced-exhaust model with its flue taken outdoors. The simpler, cheaper approach is mounting the heater outside the bathroom, on a balcony or utility wall, and running only the water pipe in.

## LPG or PNG

LPG heaters work with a standard domestic cylinder or a dedicated gas bank. They need an adaptor with pressure regulation and a secure pipe. PNG (piped natural gas, from Mahanagar Gas, Indraprastha Gas and similar networks) gives continuous supply and no cylinder swaps, but the burner and jets are designed for a different gas.

Check the model label for your gas type before you order. Using an LPG-configured heater on PNG, or the reverse, is unsafe. ## Types of gas water heater

### 1. Forced-exhaust or balanced-flue instant heaters

These pull combustion air and push exhaust through a duct to the outside. They are the safer choice for indoor placement and the one to shortlist. Japanese-origin brands such as Rinnai and Paloma are the names usually associated with this category. Expect roughly ₹15,000 to ₹30,000, plus installation and flue kit. See [balanced flue gas water heater](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=balanced+flue+gas+water+heater&tag=adifystore-21).

### 2. Natural-draft instant heaters

These use a chimney effect and rely on room air. They are lighter and cheaper, but they depend entirely on the quality of your ventilation and flue. If you can place the unit outdoors, they are workable. If you cannot, skip them.

### 3. Storage gas geysers

A tank is heated by a gas burner underneath. They deliver a lot of hot water to multiple bathrooms but take floor space and heat slowly. Brands such as Bajaj and Venus lean on these for homes that already have gas lines. Check [storage gas water heater](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=storage+gas+water+heater&tag=adifystore-21).

## Ventilation and installation in practice

- Mount on an exterior wall or balcony wherever possible, so the heater is physically outside the bathroom.
- If a flue is needed, ask for a duct with a rain cap and a straight, short run.
- Keep the heater away from the shower spray and from combustible shelves.
- Use a metal braided gas hose or proper rubber tubing rated for the fuel, and replace it on the schedule the maker gives.
- Do not use a gas geyser in an enclosed attached bathroom with an exhaust fan fighting the flue, because the fan can pull exhaust back in.

A good plumber will also check the water pressure. Many instant gas heaters need minimum flow to ignite, so a low-pressure overhead tank in an old building can cause cold showers.

## Running cost reality

For a family of four, LPG heating usually costs more per month than a 5-star electric storage geyser in cities where PNG is not available, while PNG tends to cost less per shower. The payback depends on your city's gas tariff. Gas heaters also deliver endless hot water, which suits large families with back-to-back bathing.

- Is there a gas shut-off valve you can reach easily?
- Does the manual list a carbon-monoxide warning and ignition safeguards such as flame-failure protection?

## Who should skip a gas geyser

If your bathroom has no outside wall and you do not want a long flue, buy an electric geyser or a heat-pump heater instead. If you are a tenant who cannot drill through a wall, an electric model is simpler. Gas heaters are also not meant for people who cannot reliably service them annually.
