## 1. Recumbent Bike With Backrest

Roughly ₹15,000–40,000. A reclined seat with back support takes weight off the knees and spine and makes getting on and off easy. It is the most common choice for seniors and for people returning from an injury.

Why it works: comfort and stability. The trade-off is a larger footprint than an upright bike.

## How to Choose a Bike for Knee Pain or Age

Choose low step-through and a wide, padded seat. Getting on and off safely is the first test, and a comfortable seat matters for any session beyond a few minutes.

Look for smooth, light resistance. Magnetic resistance with many small steps lets you start very gently and increase slowly.

Check adjustability. The seat should move far enough that your leg is nearly straight at the far end of the stroke, with only a slight bend at the knee.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Starting too hard.** Begin with short, easy sessions.

**Setting the seat too low.** A low seat strains the knees.

**Ignoring pain.** Stop if you feel sharp pain and seek advice.

**Skipping a warm-up.** Spend a few minutes spinning lightly first.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A senior who wants to stay active.** A recumbent bike with a step-through frame.

**Someone recovering from knee surgery.** A recumbent bike after physiotherapy advice.

**A person with back pain.** A recumbent model with lumbar support.

## What to Check on the Listing

Confirm the step-through height, the seat size, the resistance levels, the user weight limit and the warranty. See whether assembly is included and read reviews from older users about seat comfort.

## Building a Gentle Routine

Start with five to ten minutes at the lightest resistance, three or four days a week, and add two minutes each week as you feel comfortable. Keep your cadence steady rather than pushing against heavy resistance, and finish with a few minutes of easy pedalling and gentle stretches.

## Quick FAQ

**Is cycling good for knee pain?** Low-impact cycling helps many people, but ask your doctor first.

**Which type is easiest to get on?** A step-through recumbent bike.

**How often should I ride?** Short sessions several times a week are a common start.
