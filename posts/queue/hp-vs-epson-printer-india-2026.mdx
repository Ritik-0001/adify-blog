## Technology and Range

Epson's strength is its EcoTank ink tank range, which offers refillable bottles that print thousands of pages. HP also sells Smart Tank ink tank printers, but is best known for its wide range of laser and inkjet printers.

If you want laser, HP has the wider selection, and Epson focuses on ink tank and business inkjets.

## Running Costs

Ink tank printers from both brands have low cost per page compared with cartridge printers. Epson EcoTank bottles and HP Smart Tank bottles give thousands of pages per set, according to the brands.

Compare the yield and price of replacement bottles, and factor in the purchase price.

## Print Quality

Both produce good text and decent photos. Epson has a strong reputation for photo and graphics output on its EcoTank range, and HP offers sharp text with its Smart Tank and laser lines.

For everyday documents, both are more than adequate.

## Features and Software

HP's Smart app is well regarded for setup, scanning and printing from phones. Epson's Smart Panel app is also solid. Wi-Fi, mobile printing and cloud services are available on both.

Check duplex, automatic document feeder and scan speed on the model you choose.

## Reliability and Service

Both brands have service networks in India. Ink tank printers require regular use to avoid clogged heads. Check the nearest service centre and warranty terms.

## Price and Value

At the entry of ink tank printers, HP and Epson are similarly priced, around ₹15,000 to ₹22,000. Epson's L6000-series with duplex and ADF costs more, and HP's laser range offers alternatives for text-heavy work.

## How to Choose Between HP and Epson

Start with what you print. For mixed documents and photos, an ink tank printer suits. For heavy mono text, a laser printer may be more economical. Then compare features, yield and service.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A student household.** An ink tank all-in-one from either brand.

**A work-from-home professional.** Epson L3560 or HP Smart Tank with Wi-Fi.

**A small office with high text volume.** HP Laser MFP or Brother laser.

**A photo-focused home.** Epson or Canon for photo output.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Buying on brand alone.** Compare models.

**Ignoring ink or toner costs.** Check yield.

**Leaving the printer idle.** Print regularly.

## Ink Tank vs Laser

Ink tank printers suit mixed documents and occasional photos, with very low cost per page. Laser printers suit high volumes of black-and-white text, with fast output and toner that does not dry out. Choose by what you actually print rather than by brand.

## Print Speed and Duplex

Ink tank models print at moderate speeds and often lack automatic duplex at the entry level, while laser models print faster. If you print double-sided documents daily, look for automatic duplex, which appears on the Epson L6270 and several laser models.

## Warranty and Service

Read the warranty on the printer and whether the print head is covered, and check for service centres in your city. Keep the invoice, and use original ink or toner where the maker recommends it.

## Photo and Colour Printing

If you print family photos or school projects in colour, ink tank printers with four or more colours generally give the best value. Epson's five-colour options can improve photo quality, and HP's Smart Tank printers handle everyday colour documents well. For professional photo work, a dedicated photo printer is a better fit.
