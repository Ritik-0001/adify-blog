## Lens lines: the real decision

**Canon's RF mount** has an extensive range of native lenses, from affordable primes such as the RF 50mm f/1.8 to professional L lenses. Canon also offers special lenses such as the RF 100-500mm and 800mm super-telephotos. A big caveat is that Canon limits third-party autofocus lenses for full-frame RF bodies, so brands like Sigma and Tamron have fewer options than in other mounts. That keeps you mostly buying Canon glass, which tends to cost more.

**Nikon's Z mount** has a wide throat and a short flange distance, which supports sharp optics. Nikon's S-line lenses are well regarded, and the lineup is now broad. Nikon also lets some third-party lens makers participate, giving more choice. Canon's native lens catalogue is somewhat deeper in specialist tele and budget primes, but Nikon's is perfectly capable for most photographers. ## Autofocus

Both brands offer excellent subject-detection autofocus, with human, animal and vehicle tracking. Canon's Dual Pixel autofocus has long been a strong point, especially for video and eye tracking. Nikon's latest bodies such as the Z6III, Z8 and Z5II have caught up and sometimes lead in subject recognition. For sports and wildlife, newer bodies from either brand are capable. The difference matters less than the lens you pair.

## Ergonomics and handling

Nikon bodies generally have deeper grips and a layout that feels familiar to DSLR users, with a clear top display on higher models. Canon bodies have comfortable grips and intuitive menus, and are often praised for ease of use for beginners. Handle both in a store if you can, because grip shape matters in long shoots.

## Price and value

Nikon often prices full-frame bodies competitively, with models such as the Z5II aiming at lower prices than rivals. Canon's entry full-frame models cost more, but its APS-C R10 and R7 offer strong value for crop-sensor shooters. For a budget-limited buyer, an APS-C Canon R10 or Nikon Z50II is cheaper and lighter than full-frame. [Browse Nikon mirrorless](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=Nikon+Z+mirrorless+camera&tag=adifystore-21).

## Video

Canon is respected for colour science and reliable video autofocus, with the EOS R6 Mark II and R5 popular among creators. Nikon's Z6III offers excellent video features, including internal raw. Both brands are fine for YouTube and wedding films. Check for overheating limits on the specific body.

## Used and adapted lenses

If you already own DSLR lenses, both brands offer adapters that keep your old glass working with autofocus. Canon's EF to RF adapter and Nikon's FTZ adapter are well supported. That can reduce your costs.

## Who should pick which

- **Canon:** buyers who want wide lens choice, beginner-friendly handling and strong service.
- **Nikon:** buyers who want full-frame value, familiar DSLR-style grips and sharp S-line lenses.

## Recommendation

For most buyers, Canon is the safer and broader system, particularly if you want to add lenses over time and value service reach. Nikon is the better pick if you want a sharp, good-value full-frame body and enjoy its handling. Before you buy, shortlist the lenses you will own and confirm they exist at an affordable price.
