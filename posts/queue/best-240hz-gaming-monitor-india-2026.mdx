## Who actually needs 240Hz

Competitive players in Valorant, CS2, Apex and Fortnite gain a smoother image and slightly lower latency at 240Hz. The jump from 60 to 144Hz is large; the jump from 144 to 240Hz is much smaller and only matters if you can fill the frames. A casual single-player gamer will barely notice.

If you play AAA games at high settings, you will likely hit 80-120 frames, so a 144Hz or 165Hz monitor costs less and serves better. Skip 240Hz if your budget is tight, or if you mainly watch videos and play story games.

## GPU and CPU needs

To reach 240 frames in esports titles at 1080p, you need a capable CPU and a mid-range GPU. A card in the RTX 4060 class or the RX 7600 class can exceed 240 frames in lighter esports games at 1080p, but CS2 and some others are CPU-bound and need a strong processor such as a recent Ryzen 5 or Core i5.

At 1440p, 240 frames needs a much stronger card, such as an RTX 4070 class, even in esports titles. Check [RTX 4060 graphics card](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=rtx+4060+graphics+card&tag=adifystore-21) listings if your GPU is old. Buying the monitor before the GPU is a common waste.

## Panel types

Fast IPS panels now dominate: good colours, wide viewing angles and response times that suit esports. TN panels are older, very fast but with weaker colours and viewing angles, and they have mostly been displaced. VA panels offer strong contrast but can smear in dark scenes at high refresh rates. OLED panels at 240Hz give near-instant response and deep blacks at roughly ₹60,000 and above, and they carry a risk of burn-in if you leave static images on screen for long periods.

For most buyers, an IPS panel is the safe choice. Check [QD-OLED 240Hz gaming monitor](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=oled+240hz+gaming+monitor&tag=adifystore-21) listings only if you have the budget and play mostly dark, cinematic games.

## Response time claims

Manufacturers quote 1ms response, but that is measured as grey-to-grey under ideal overdrive. Real performance varies with overdrive settings, and aggressive overdrive can cause inverse ghosting. Independent reviews show far more than the spec sheet, so read a detailed review and not just the box number.

Also check for adaptive sync (G-Sync Compatible or FreeSync), which prevents tearing, and a motion-blur-reduction mode if you want a sharper image, though it lowers brightness.

## Size and resolution

At 24 to 25 inches, 1080p looks sharp enough and suits esports, where you want the whole screen in view. At 27 inches, 1080p looks soft, and 1440p is the better choice. A [27-inch 1440p 240Hz gaming monitor](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=27+inch+1440p+240hz+monitor&tag=adifystore-21) costs roughly ₹30,000-45,000 and needs a strong GPU.

## Connections and stand

DisplayPort is the safest way to get 240Hz on a PC, since some monitors limit HDMI to lower refresh rates. Check that the box includes a DisplayPort cable. A stand with height and tilt adjustment helps ergonomics, and a VESA mount lets you add a monitor arm.

Rather than chasing a specific model, compare the same specification: panel type, resolution, response time, adaptive sync, ports, stand and warranty. ## What to skip

Skip 240Hz if you play at 4K, since no mainstream GPU hits those frames in esports at that resolution, and 4K 240Hz monitors cost a lot. Skip it if your mouse, network and aim limit you more than the screen does.
