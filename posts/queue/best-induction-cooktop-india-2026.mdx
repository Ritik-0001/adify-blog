## Indian Vessel Compatibility

**Works with induction:**
- Steel (magnetic flat-bottom) pressure cookers — Hawkins, Prestige induction-compatible models
- Cast iron tawas — flat-bottom
- Induction-compatible non-stick pans — look for induction symbol on base
- Steel kadais (flat-bottom, check with magnet)

**Doesn't work with induction:**
- Traditional aluminium pressure cookers
- Curved-bottom kadais and handis
- Copper and pure aluminium vessels
- Most clay and ceramic cookware

**Transition tip:** Buy an induction-compatible steel kadai (₹500–₁,200) and Prestige induction pressure cooker (₹1,500–₂,500) alongside the cooktop — you can cook 90% of Indian dishes with these two vessels.

## Induction vs Gas for Indian Cooking

**Induction wins on:** Speed (boils water 30–40% faster), energy efficiency, safety (no gas leak risk), apartment suitability.

**Gas wins on:** Works with all existing vessels, flexibility for wok-style tossing, tandoor-style charring, uninterrupted operation (no power cuts issue).

**Best setup:** Keep gas for main cooking + buy an induction cooktop as a second burner for quick tasks (milk, water, reheating). Cost ₹2,000–₄,500 for a second cooking station without installing a third gas burner.

**Recommendation:** Philips HD4938 for primary induction cooking with full Indian cuisine. Prestige PIC 20 for supplemental use or budget constraint. Both work as Diwali kitchen gifts.
