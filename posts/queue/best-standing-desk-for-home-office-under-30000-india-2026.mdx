## How to Choose a Standing Desk

Check the motor and frame. Dual-motor frames lift more weight and are usually more stable, while single-motor frames are cheaper and fine for lighter setups. Look for a steel frame with a wide stance.

Consider the desktop size. For a laptop and one monitor, a desktop around 120 by 60 centimetres is a common starting point. Larger setups need wider tops.

Look at height range and presets. A wide range suits different users, and memory presets save time when switching between sitting and standing.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Buying only the frame.** Check whether a desktop is included.

**Ignoring weight capacity.** Add up your monitor, laptop and accessories.

**Skipping the wobble check.** Read reviews on stability at standing height.

**Not planning cable management.** Leave slack for cables.

## What to Check on the Listing

Confirm the weight capacity, height range, motor count, noise level and warranty. See whether assembly is included and whether delivery covers the desktop.

## Quick FAQ

**Are standing desks worth it?** Many people find that alternating positions helps comfort, but move regularly either way.

**How long should I stand?** Alternate between sitting and standing rather than standing all day.

**Is assembly difficult?** It usually takes an hour or two with two people.

## Setting Up Your Standing Desk

Set the desk to seated height with your forearms parallel to the desk and your wrists straight. For standing, raise it until your elbows sit at about 90 degrees. Place the monitor so the top of the screen is at or just below eye level, and keep a small anti-fatigue mat under your feet if you stand for long periods.

Plan the cables before assembly. Leave enough slack for the full height range, and use a cable tray or clips. Test the full range of motion with everything on the desk before you settle in.
