## Brightness is the main spec, but not the number on the box

Brands love quoting peak brightness in nits, but the useful figure is how bright the TV stays across a large portion of the screen, because daylight washes out whole scenes rather than a single highlight. Mini-LED sets, with thousands of small LEDs and local dimming, keep the whole image bright while still holding some contrast. Direct-lit QLED comes next, and OLED is the one that struggles most in a sunlit room.

Edge-lit entry QLEDs often have quoted brightness that sounds similar but cannot sustain it, and their dimming is weak. In a lit room that does not matter as much as in the dark, so a good direct-lit QLED can still serve well at a lower price.

## Anti-glare and anti-reflection screens

Reflection is as damaging as low brightness. Window light on a glossy screen puts a bright smear across dark areas. Some premium Samsung models use a matte or anti-reflection layer that scatters reflected light, and that finish can make a visible difference. Many cheaper models use a plain glossy surface, which gives deeper-looking blacks in a dark room but shows every window and lamp in a bright one.

When reading listings, look for terms such as anti-reflection, low-reflection, glare-free or matte screen. If the listing says nothing, assume glossy.

## Panel type: VA, IPS and why viewing angle matters

Most mid-range TVs use VA panels, which have strong contrast but fade when you view from an angle. IPS panels hold colour from the side but have lower contrast. In a bright, open living room with seating spread across a wide area, side viewers will notice the fade on a VA panel. Mini-LED backlighting helps, but it does not fix the panel's inherent angle behaviour. If your sofa is wide and people sit at the ends, factor this in.

## Placement matters as much as the TV

You can improve almost any TV with simple positioning:

- Place the screen so windows are beside or behind the viewers, not behind the TV. A bright window directly behind the screen strains the eyes and washes out the dark areas.
- Avoid a spot opposite a window. That position gives the strongest reflections.
- Use sheer curtains. They diffuse light without turning the room dark.
- Mount at eye level; a high wall mount angles the screen toward the ceiling lights and reflects them.

## Who should skip OLED

OLED looks wonderful in a dark room, but its sustained brightness is lower, and many models have glossy screens. If most of your viewing is in daylight, an OLED will look less impressive than a mid-priced Mini-LED. If you only watch at night, ignore this advice and buy OLED. ## HDR and daylight

HDR benefits from brightness, so a bright room is a place where it can work, provided the TV supports HDR10 and ideally Dolby Vision or HDR10 Plus. Many cheaper sets list HDR support but cannot display it properly. Mini-LED sets with real local dimming are the ones where HDR looks different from SDR rather than just dimmer.

## Sound, a note

Brighter rooms tend to be larger, more open spaces, and TV speakers do not fill them. Budget for a soundbar, particularly with a 65 inch set. [See soundbars](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=Dolby+Atmos+soundbar&tag=adifystore-21).

## Recommendation

For a sunlit living room, buy the brightest Mini-LED you can afford at 65 inches, check the screen finish for anti-reflection, and position it away from windows. If budget is limited, a good direct-lit QLED at ₹55,000-75,000 is a reasonable compromise. Skip glossy edge-lit panels, and confirm the service network before ordering.
