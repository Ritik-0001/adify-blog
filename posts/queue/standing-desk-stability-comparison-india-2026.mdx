## What Causes Standing Desk Wobble

### 1. Leg Stage Count (2-Stage vs. 3-Stage)

Standing desk legs telescope — they collapse for minimum height and extend for maximum height. A 2-stage leg has one inner tube inside an outer tube. A 3-stage leg adds a second inner tube.

**2-stage legs:** Simpler, cheaper. At maximum extension, the inner tube extends far out of the outer tube — each extension point is a potential wobble source.

**3-stage legs:** At maximum height, each segment extends less than in a 2-stage design. Less extension per segment = less wobble leverage. The Flexispot E1 uses 3-stage legs.

### 2. Single Motor vs. Dual Motor

A single motor drives one leg. The other leg follows passively through a mechanical linkage. Over time, the passive leg drifts — either moving faster or slower than the driven leg. This creates uneven height on the two sides (one side higher than the other) which introduces a twisting force into the frame. Twisted frames wobble.

Dual motors keep both legs synchronized electronically. No drift, no twist.

### 3. Frame Material and Cross-Bracing

Heavier steel frames with more cross-bracing (the horizontal bars connecting the two leg columns) resist wobble better than lighter frames. Desks above ₹15,000 typically have thicker steel and better cross-bracing.

## Stability vs. Height Tradeoff

All standing desks wobble more at maximum height than at minimum height — this is physics. The question is whether the wobble is within acceptable limits for your use:

**Typing:** Even 15–20mm wobble is acceptable for most typists (the keyboard is on the desk surface, which moves with the frame).

**Mouse use:** 10–15mm wobble affects mouse precision for design and gaming use — a stable desk matters more here.

**Video calls (webcam on desk):** More than 10mm wobble creates visible camera shake during calls. Flex under typing force wobbles a desk-mounted webcam noticeably.

**Dual monitors:** Heavier loads amplify wobble. With 15–20kg of monitors, keyboard, and items, wobble at maximum height increases by 30–50% compared to empty desk measurements.
