A split AC is the better choice for most Indian homes because it is quieter, more efficient and available in more inverter models, while a window AC suits rented rooms or tight budgets where a simple wall opening and easy installation matter more. A good 1.5 ton inverter split roughly costs ₹35,000 to ₹55,000, while window ACs of similar capacity often cost somewhat less and include fewer inverter options.

This comparison is researched from brand information and listings, not hands-on testing. The deciding facts are often about your building rather than the machine, such as whether there is a ledge for an outdoor unit or a window frame strong enough for a window unit, so ask your landlord or society about the rules before you pick a type.

## 1. Split AC: Quiet and Efficient

Roughly ₹35,000–60,000 for 1.5 ton inverter models. The compressor sits outside, so the indoor unit is quiet, and inverter and high star rated models are common. The price of that quiet is installation cost and the need for outdoor space.

## 2. Window AC: Simple and Compact

Roughly ₹30,000–45,000 for 1.5 ton models. A window AC sits in one box in a wall opening or window frame, so it is easy to install and move. Setup cost is lower, but expect more noise and fewer premium features.

## 4. Installation and Society Rules

A split AC needs a place for the outdoor unit and piping, which some societies restrict, while a window AC needs a suitable window or wall opening and sturdy support. Rules vary by building, so finding out early avoids surprises.

## 5. Noise and Comfort

A split AC's indoor unit is much quieter, which suits bedrooms. A window AC's compressor is in the same unit as the fan, so it is louder, which can disturb sleep. Quiet is the thing you pay extra for.

## 6. Moving and Rentals

A window AC is easier to remove and carry to your next home, while a split AC needs a technician to uninstall and reinstall it, which costs money. That flexibility for renters comes with lower efficiency.

## How to Choose Between Split and Window

Start with your home. If you own the home and can install an outdoor unit, a split AC is usually better, and if you rent or have restrictions, a window AC may be the only option.

Think about noise and efficiency. Bedrooms benefit from a quiet split, and heavy users benefit from a high star inverter model.

Compare total cost. Add installation, piping and any stand to the price, since a split's installation cost can add a few thousand rupees.

## Maintenance Differences

Both types need regular filter cleaning, and the split's indoor and outdoor units benefit from a yearly service. Window ACs have the compressor and coil in one box, so servicing is simpler but access to the inside is harder.

## Cost Over Five Years

Add the purchase price, installation, electricity and servicing for each type over five years. Inverter splits usually cost more upfront but less to run, while window ACs can be cheaper to buy and install but use more power. Run the numbers with your own usage hours and tariff, because the winner changes with how long you run the AC.

## Who Services What

Both types come with separate cover on the compressor, the control board and the rest of the unit, so compare all three on each listing. Window ACs are simpler to service because everything is in one box, while splits need a technician who can handle both units.

## Who Should Skip This

If you are a tenant who changes homes every year or two, a split AC can end up costing more in uninstall and reinstall charges than it saves in electricity, so a window unit is the more practical choice. Anyone in a flat with no window or wall opening of the right size should rule out a window AC at the start.

## Recommendation

For most owners, an inverter split AC from a reliable brand is the better long-term choice, and a window AC suits renters or tight spaces. Check the installation terms and your building's rules before you order.
