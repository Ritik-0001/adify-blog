## 1. DVR Systems

Roughly ₹15,000–45,000. A DVR (digital video recorder) works with analogue HD cameras over coaxial cable, with separate power supplies for each camera.

Why it works: low cost and simple parts. The trade-off is lower resolution on older systems and more cables.

## 2. NVR Systems

Roughly ₹25,000–80,000. An NVR (network video recorder) works with IP cameras over network cables and can supply power through PoE, so one cable carries both.

Why it works: higher resolution, cleaner install and easy scaling. The trade-off is higher cost.

[Search NVR CCTV kits on Amazon](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=nvr+camera+kit+poe&tag=adifystore-21)

## 3. Hybrid and HVR Recorders

Roughly ₹15,000–50,000. Hybrid recorders can handle analogue and IP cameras together, which helps if you upgrade gradually.

Why it works: flexible upgrades. The trade-off is that features depend on the model.

[Search hybrid CCTV recorders on Amazon](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=hybrid+hvr+cctv+recorder&tag=adifystore-21)

## 4. Image Quality and Features

IP cameras on NVR systems often offer higher resolution, better low-light performance and smart features such as person and vehicle detection. Analogue HD can be good for general use at lower cost.

Why it works: better image for identifying details. The trade-off is that you pay more.

[Search 4MP IP cameras on Amazon](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=4mp+ip+camera+poe&tag=adifystore-21)

## 5. Cabling and Installation

DVR systems use coaxial cable and separate power, while NVR PoE uses one network cable per camera. If you already have coax in place, a DVR upgrade can reuse it, and if you are starting fresh, PoE is simpler.

Why it works: reduces cost and mess. The trade-off is planning.

## 6. Expansion and Remote Access

NVR systems usually scale more easily and support modern apps, while DVRs can be limited by recorder channels. Both support phone viewing when connected to the internet.

Why it works: future-proofing. The trade-off is cost.

## How to Choose Between DVR and NVR

Start with cabling. If your property already has coax cables, a DVR may save money, and if you are cabling from scratch, an NVR with PoE is neater.

Think about resolution. If you need to read number plates or recognise faces at a distance, IP cameras give better detail.

Consider the future. If you may add cameras or upgrade, an NVR gives more headroom.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Buying on camera count alone.** Check resolution and storage.

**Ignoring storage.** Plan hard drive capacity.

**Using default passwords.** Change them.

**Skipping professional installation.** Poor cabling causes trouble.

## Real-World Scenarios

**An existing home with coaxial cables.** A DVR upgrade.

**A new villa.** An NVR with PoE.

**A shop that wants better detail.** IP cameras with an NVR.

## What to Check on the Listing

Confirm the recorder type, the number of channels, the camera resolution, the included hard drive, the cables and the warranty. See whether installation is included.

## Storage and Power Planning

Calculate storage by camera count, resolution and days of retention, and use a surveillance-grade drive. Put the recorder on a UPS and in a ventilated, secure spot.

## Warranty and Service

Ask about warranty on the recorder, cameras and drive, and who services them in your city. Keep the invoice and login details safe.

## Quick FAQ

**Is NVR always better?** It offers more features, but a DVR can be fine for budgets.

**Can I mix both?** A hybrid recorder can handle both.

**Do I need internet?** Only for remote viewing.

## Upgrading an Existing System

If you already have a DVR system, you do not always need to replace everything. Some recorders support higher-resolution analogue cameras on the same cables, and hybrid recorders accept IP cameras too. Ask an installer to test your cabling before you spend, because reusing good cable can save a large part of the cost of a new system.

## Recommendation

For new installs, an NVR with PoE is usually the better long-term choice, and a DVR is sensible when you reuse existing cables on a budget. Compare specifications and installation terms on each listing.
