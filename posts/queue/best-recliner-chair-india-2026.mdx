## How to Choose a Recliner

Check the mechanism. Manual recliners use a lever or push-back action and are simpler and cheaper. Motorised recliners use a motor and remote and add convenience, but more parts can fail.

Consider the space. Recliners need clearance behind and in front when fully reclined. Wall-hugger designs need less space behind.

Look at frame and foam. A solid frame and high-density foam make a recliner last longer, and the upholstery should suit your climate.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Ignoring clearance.** Measure the space needed when reclined.

**Choosing the cheapest mechanism.** It is the part most likely to fail.

**Overlooking weight capacity.** Check the limit.

**Skipping the trial.** Sit in one if possible.

## What to Check on the Listing

Confirm the dimensions, weight capacity, mechanism type and warranty. See whether assembly is included and whether the price covers delivery.

## Quick FAQ

**Manual or motorised?** Manual is simpler and cheaper. Motorised is more convenient.

**How long do they last?** Five to ten years with good care.

**Are they good for the back?** They can be comfortable, but sit with good posture.

## Sizing and Placement

Measure the space with the recliner fully extended. Standard recliners need extra room behind to lean back, while wall-hugger designs slide forward and need only a few inches of clearance. Leave room in front for the footrest, and place the chair away from heaters and direct sunlight, which dry out upholstery.

Consider the height of the user. Taller users need a longer seat and a higher backrest, and shorter users need a seat depth that lets their feet reach the floor when sitting upright.

## Care and Longevity

Vacuum the seams regularly, wipe leatherette gently, and avoid sitting on the arms, which strains the frame. Lubricate the mechanism if the manual advises it, and stop using a motorised recliner if it makes grinding noises, since motors and gears are best serviced early.

## Who Should Skip a Recliner

If your living room is small, or you already have a sofa and a tight walkway, a recliner may crowd the space. People with hip or knee problems should also check the seat height, since a low recliner can be hard to get out of. In those cases, a chair with a higher seat and a firm base is more practical than a deep, soft one.
