Buying an RO water purifier in India in 2026 correctly means checking your water's TDS level first, choosing between RO alone versus RO+UV+UF combination based on that reading, verifying storage tank capacity matches household size, and confirming service network access in your city — skip any of these steps and you risk overpaying for unnecessary technology or buying a purifier genuinely inadequate for your water source.

RO purifiers get purchased far more often than genuinely needed in India — many buyers default to RO out of brand familiarity without checking whether their specific water source actually requires it, when a simpler and cheaper UV-only purifier would serve equally well.

## Step 1: Test Your TDS Level First

This single step, costing ₹200-₸00 for a TDS meter, determines whether you need RO at all — water under 200 TDS doesn't benefit from RO and loses beneficial minerals unnecessarily.

## Step 2: Choose RO Combination Based on TDS

**200-500 TDS:** RO+UV combination.

**500+ TDS:** RO+UV+UF is essential — common in bore well and hard water regions.

## Step 3: Verify Storage Tank Capacity

**Family of 3-4:** 7-10L tank is typically sufficient.

**Family of 5+:** 15L+ tank reduces the frequency of running dry during peak usage or power outages (RO purification requires electricity).

## Step 4: Confirm Service Network Access

RO purifiers require periodic filter and membrane replacement (roughly every 6-12 months) — verify the brand has accessible service technicians in your city before purchasing.

---

## Common RO Buying Mistakes

**Buying RO for already-low-TDS water:** Wastes money and unnecessarily strips beneficial minerals.

**Ignoring the mineralizer feature:** Pure RO water can taste "flat" — models with a TDS controller or mineralizer address this.

**Underestimating tank size for larger families:** Leads to frequent running dry, especially during power outages when the purifier can't actively process water.
