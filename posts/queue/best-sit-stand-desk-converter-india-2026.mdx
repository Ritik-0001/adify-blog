## Converter or full desk

A converter is a platform that sits on your existing desk and rises. A full standing desk replaces the desk entirely. Converters win on price, on portability, and on renting flexibility: you can take it when you move out. Full desks win on stability, cleaner cable routing, and a bigger work area.

The honest rule: if your existing desk is solid and you have 70 cm or more of depth, a converter is a reasonable stepping stone. If your desk is wobbly, under 60 cm deep or an L-shape, a converter will feel cramped, and you should consider a [standing desk electric](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=electric+height+adjustable+standing+desk&tag=adifystore-21) instead.

## Gas spring vs electric

Gas-spring converters rise with a lever or handle and are cheaper, lighter and need no power; the downside is that they can feel heavy to lower and their springs may weaken over several years. Electric ones raise at the press of a button, remember heights, and handle heavier loads, but cost more and need a socket.

For anyone switching posture every 30 to 45 minutes, electric is more likely to be used, which matters more than any spec. A [motorized desk riser](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=electric+desk+riser+converter&tag=adifystore-21) search compares the options.

## Monitor height: the main ergonomic catch

Many converters have a single flat platform, so the monitor and keyboard rise together. That puts the keyboard higher than your elbows when seated, or the monitor too low when you stand, forcing you to hunch. Better models have a two-tier design: a lower tray for the keyboard that sits about 15 cm below the monitor platform.

Even then, you often need a [monitor arm](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=monitor+arm+single+gas+spring&tag=adifystore-21) or a separate stand to raise the screen so the top is at eye level. Laptop users should add an external keyboard and mouse, since a laptop screen and keyboard cannot both be at ergonomic height.

## Stability and capacity

Converters shake more than full desks because the top is built on a scissor or parallelogram arm. Look for a solid base, wide stance, and a clearly stated weight capacity of 15 kg or more; anything under 10 kg will struggle with two monitors. A heavier base around 12 to 18 kg adds stability, though it also makes it harder to move.

Check the minimum and maximum height: a converter that rises 40 cm or more above the desktop is needed for taller users, and typically a 75 cm desk plus a 45 cm lift brings the keyboard to about 120 cm, enough for people up to around 190 cm.

## Size and what fits

Common platform widths are 80, 90 and 100 cm; for dual monitors, choose 100 cm or more. Remember, a converter takes space on the desk even when lowered, so a desk with a 60 cm depth becomes unworkable. Also check clearance below: some designs need a few centimetres of space behind the desk so the tray can swing back.

## Desk mats and standing comfort

Standing for long stretches is tiring, so alternate every 30 to 60 minutes. An [anti-fatigue mat](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=anti+fatigue+standing+mat+desk&tag=adifystore-21) reduces foot and leg fatigue, and a pair of supportive shoes helps more than a more expensive converter. Start with short standing blocks, around 20 to 30 minutes, and build gradually.

## Who should skip a converter

Skip it if you share a desk of unusual size, such as an L-shaped or corner desk, where a rectangular riser cannot fit. Skip it if your desk is lightweight or has a glass top; the converter will mark or crack it. Skip it if your budget is tight; at around ₹15,000 and up, you are paying close to the cost of a basic electric desk frame, so compare before deciding. And skip it altogether if you never plan to stand, because a [laptop stand adjustable](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=laptop+stand+adjustable+aluminium+ergonomic&tag=adifystore-21) costs far less and fixes most posture problems.
