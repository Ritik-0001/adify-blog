## How much suction a small kitchen needs

Suction is quoted in m3/hr. Bigger is not better in a compact kitchen: a very powerful motor on a short duct is loud and drags more air out of your home than you can replace. For a two-burner hob and typical Indian tadka cooking, 700 to 1,000 m3/hr is comfortable. A 3-burner hob in a small kitchen needs closer to 1,000 to 1,200 m3/hr, and heavy deep-frying households can go a little higher.

Indian cooking produces oily smoke, so the effective suction after the duct bends matters more than the label figure. Each bend can cut airflow noticeably, so a small kitchen with a short straight duct performs better than a large kitchen with a long snaking one.

## 1. Baffle filter wall-mount

Baffle filters use curved steel layers that trap oil, and you wash them in warm soapy water every few weeks. They are the standard for Indian cooking because they do not clog like mesh. Brands such as Faber, Elica, Glen and Hindware sell a range in this size, usually roughly ₹10,000 to ₹18,000. ## 2. Filterless auto-clean models

A filterless chimney uses a spinning or slanted baffle plate with an oil collector, plus an auto-clean cycle where heat melts oil into the tray. This keeps the motor protected without filter washing. The trade-off is price, usually ₹14,000 to ₹25,000 for a 60 cm, and the oil tray must still be emptied. ## Noise in a small space

In a small kitchen, you are standing close to the motor. Noise ratings are rarely reliable, but a few clues help: a two- or three-speed switch lets you run low during simmering, and a chimney with a larger, slower fan tends to sound deeper rather than shrill. Aim for a model that you can run at the low or medium speed for most cooking.

## Ducting in tight spaces

Small flats often have the kitchen on an inner wall with a shaft or an exhaust outlet nearby. Plan the route first.

- Use the shortest, straightest run possible, with as few elbows as you can.
- A 6-inch (150 mm) duct is common; a smaller one chokes the motor.
- Corrugated aluminium duct creates more resistance than smooth PVC or rigid pipe.
- If you cannot vent outside, a ductless recirculating kit with charcoal filters is a poor compromise because it only removes some smoke and no heat. Consider it only for rentals.
- Check for a non-return valve at the outlet so insects and cooking smells do not enter from outside.

## Mounting height

Fit the chimney roughly 65 to 75 cm above the hob. Too high and smoke escapes; too low and you risk heat damage and head clearance issues. A detailed height guide is a topic for a separate guide, but do measure before you commit.

## Who should skip a 60 cm chimney

If your hob is 75 or 90 cm wide, or you have a three- or four-burner gas stove, step up in width so that the hood covers the burners. A narrow hood over a wide hob is one of the most common small-kitchen mistakes. Also skip it if your landlord forbids drilling and you cannot vent outside.
