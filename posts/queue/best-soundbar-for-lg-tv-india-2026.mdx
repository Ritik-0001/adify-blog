## Connection first: HDMI eARC

The most important thing for any soundbar and LG TV is the connection. Look for the HDMI port labelled eARC/ARC on the back of the TV. On most recent LG sets, only one HDMI port has this label. Plug the soundbar's HDMI OUT (eARC) port into it using a good-quality HDMI cable. eARC can carry Dolby Atmos from the TV's own apps, as well as lossless audio from devices plugged into the TV.

If you use an optical cable instead, you lose Atmos and the bar falls back to compressed surround. That is acceptable for news and soaps but not what you pay Atmos prices for. Also turn on the TV's audio output setting to HDMI ARC or eARC, otherwise the TV may keep playing sound from its own speakers.

## LG soundbars and the "pairing" idea

LG markets a feature, usually described as WOW Orchestra on supported combinations, that lets the TV speakers work together with a compatible LG soundbar for a wider soundstage. It is not available on every TV and bar, so read the compatibility list carefully on LG's page for your model rather than assuming. Even without that feature, LG soundbars typically offer wireless links, automatic input switching and remote control integration, so the TV remote adjusts volume.

LG's range spans compact 2.1 bars at around ₹15,000-25,000, mid-range 3.1.2 and 5.1.2 Atmos models at roughly ₹30,000-55,000, and flagship systems with wireless rear speakers above ₹60,000. ## Size matching

A soundbar should roughly match the TV width. For a 55 inch LG, a bar of around 90 to 100 cm looks right. For 65 inch and above, bigger bars between 110 and 130 cm look more balanced. If your TV sits on a stand with a tall central leg, measure the clearance before buying, because some LG stands raise the screen and the bar's front face may block the infrared receiver or bottom of the screen.

If you wall-mount the TV, many soundbars include mounting brackets, and the LG remote's IR signal usually still reaches the bar, but confirm a sensor placement note in the manual.

## Atmos reality check

Dolby Atmos in a compact soundbar uses up-firing speakers that bounce sound off the ceiling. That works best in rooms with flat ceilings around 2.4 to 3 metres. In rooms with very high, sloped or heavily textured ceilings, the effect fades and you hear a wide stereo or surround image instead. In a small room, a simple 2.1 bar with a strong subwoofer may feel better than a pricey Atmos model.

## Who should skip a soundbar

If you want real surround from the sides and back, a full AV receiver with separate speakers beats any single bar, though it costs more and demands more space and cables. If you live in a flat with sleeping neighbours, a large wireless subwoofer may cause complaints; choose a bar with a night mode. For a simple, clean setup on a mid-to-high LG OLED, buy an LG Atmos bar around ₹35,000-55,000 and connect it through HDMI eARC.
