## Why cold inlet water changes everything

Geyser specifications assume tap water around 20 to 25 degrees. In winter in places like Shimla, Manali, Mussoorie, Darjeeling, Nainital or Ooty, water can arrive at 3 to 8 degrees. A 25-litre geyser rated to heat in about 30 minutes in the plains may need an hour or more. An instant geyser of 3 kW may barely warm a thin stream to bearable, never mind a shower. Storage tanks heat a volume once and hold it, which suits mountain homes where you want a bucket or a full shower ready.

## Tank size: bigger than you think

Plains households use 15 litres per person comfortably. In cold climates, people take hotter, longer baths and mix less cold water, so add margin. A family of four fits a 35 to 50 litre tank, while a couple does fine with 25 litres. Bigger tanks take longer to heat, which matters when power cuts are common.

A vertical storage geyser with a 2 kW element is the standard choice. Look for a 5 or 4-star BEE rating, a glass-lined or stainless steel tank, magnesium anode and a multifunction safety valve. In very cold areas, a 3 kW option heats faster but needs a heavier wiring circuit. Brands such as Racold, AO Smith, Bajaj and Havells are common. ## 2. 3 kW storage geysers: best for fast recovery

If you have 15-ampere wiring, a 3 kW storage geyser reheats faster after a family's baths and is helpful when electricity is intermittent. Check your wiring capacity and use an MCB. Look at [3 kW storage geyser](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=3000+watt+storage+water+heater+geyser&tag=adifystore-21).

## 3. Gas geysers: best where LPG is reliable

An LPG geyser heats instantly regardless of inlet water temperature and ignores power cuts, which makes it useful in mountain homes. It needs a well-ventilated space, never a closed bathroom, because carbon monoxide poisoning is a real danger. Install outside the bathroom with an exhaust. ## Freezing and pipe protection

Pipes and the geyser inlet can freeze overnight, cracking joints. Insulate exposed pipes with foam, keep the geyser in a sheltered room, and drain the tank if you leave a house empty for winter. Use CPVC or insulated pipes. A [pipe insulation foam tube](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=pipe+insulation+foam+tube+water+pipe&tag=adifystore-21) costs little and can prevent expensive damage.

## Anti-corrosion and water quality

Hill water is often soft and can be mineral-rich or slightly acidic, which attacks tanks. Choose a tank with a glass-lined coating or stainless steel and a magnesium anode, and replace the anode every one to two years. Brackish or hard borewell water needs more care. A [geyser anode rod](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=geyser+magnesium+anode+rod&tag=adifystore-21) is cheap compared with a ruined tank.

## Power and voltage

Hill areas see frequent voltage drops. A geyser working at low voltage heats slowly and can fail. Use a [voltage stabiliser](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=voltage+stabilizer+for+geyser+5+kVA&tag=adifystore-21) if the supply is unstable, and make sure wiring is thick enough for the load. Fit the geyser with a proper MCB and earthing.

## Who should skip a bigger geyser

Skip a large tank if you live alone or only visit seasonally. Skip a cheap instant geyser for full showers. Skip very cheap unbranded models, which often have poor insulation and tank coatings.
