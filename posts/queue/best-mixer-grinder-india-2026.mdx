## Motor Power Guide

**500W:** For occasional light grinding — smoothies, small quantities of spices. Not suitable for regular Indian cooking involving hard spices or wet grinding.

**750W:** The sweet spot for most Indian families. Handles all typical Indian grinding tasks — coconut, wet batter, dry masala — without overheating in normal use.

**1000W+:** For heavy-duty use: commercial quantity preparation, hard dry spices, or grinding multiple batches daily. Most home cooks don't need this.

## What Indian Cooks Need from a Mixer

**Jar quality:** Stainless steel over plastic. Steel doesn't stain with turmeric, retains sharpness longer, and doesn't leach into food.

**Blade design:** Multiple-angled blades handle both wet and dry grinding better than single-plane blades.

**Chutney jar:** The small chutney jar (0.3–0.5L) is essential for Indian cooking — grinding small quantities of chutney or paste without air pockets.

**Warranty:** Indian cooking wears out motors quickly. A 5-year motor warranty is the strongest signal of manufacturer confidence.

**Recommendation:** Preethi Zodiac for most Indian families. It's been the market leader for decades because it handles the full range of Indian cooking tasks reliably.
