## How to Choose a Laptop in Sale Season

Set your budget and usage first. Office work, coding and browsing need a fast SSD and 16GB RAM more than a powerful GPU. Gaming and video editing need a dedicated graphics card and better cooling.

Compare like with like. Sale listings often show a low headline price for a base model with 8GB RAM or 256GB storage, while the version you actually want costs more. Match the configuration before you compare prices.

Check warranty, return window and after-sales support. A laptop is a five-year purchase, and service reach matters more than a small discount.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Comparing different configurations.** A laptop advertised at a low price often has 8GB RAM or a 256GB drive. The version with 16GB and 512GB can cost noticeably more, so compare like for like.

**Ignoring the display.** A dim, low-resolution panel is hard to live with for years. Look for at least Full HD, and check brightness and colour coverage where listed.

**Skipping the warranty check.** Confirm the warranty length, on-site service and whether accidental damage cover is offered.

## Quick FAQ

**MacBook or Windows laptop?** If you want long battery life, quiet operation and strong resale value, a MacBook is a solid pick. If you need specific Windows software, gaming or a lower entry price, a Windows laptop is more flexible.

**How much RAM do I need?** Sixteen gigabytes is a comfortable target for multitasking and will keep a laptop useful for longer. Eight gigabytes is enough for basic use but can feel limiting later.

**Is a gaming laptop a good choice for students?** It can serve both roles, but it is heavier, has shorter battery life and often runs hotter. Choose it if gaming is a real priority.

## A Note on Apple Chip Generations
