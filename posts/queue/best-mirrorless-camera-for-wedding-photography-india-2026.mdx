## Why dual card slots matter

A wedding cannot be re-shot. Dual slots let you write every photo to two cards at once, so a corrupt card does not ruin the event. For professionals this is non-negotiable. Check that both slots are fast enough for burst shooting; some bodies pair a fast slot with a slower SD slot. Whatever you buy, format both cards in the camera before the day and carry spares.

## Low-light performance

Indian weddings include dark mandaps, candlelit sangeets, indoor halls with mixed lighting and late-night receptions. A full-frame sensor with strong high-ISO performance and reliable autofocus in dim conditions matters more than megapixels. Bodies in the 24 to 33 megapixel range, such as the A7 IV (33 MP), R6 Mark II (24 MP) and Z6III (24 MP), balance low-light noise and file size. High-resolution bodies such as the A7R series suit portraits and studio work but produce larger files and noisier shadows at high ISO.

## Autofocus for moving people

Eye detection, face tracking and subject recognition help you capture varied moments: a bride walking, children running, a groom's entry. All three bodies listed offer reliable human eye-detection. Test continuous autofocus at weddings by shooting a few fast-moving moments during an event you know.

## Flash and lighting

On-camera flash helps in dark halls, and bounce flash gives softer light. A reliable external flash, such as Godox or brand-specific units, costs from roughly ₹10,000 upward and a few models exceed ₹25,000. Wireless triggers allow off-camera flash. ## Battery and cards planning

Weddings run for many hours across several days. Carry at least four batteries per body, and a charger for each night. Electronic viewfinders and screens drain power faster than DSLRs. Bring multiple high-speed cards, ideally 64 GB or larger, and back up nightly to two drives. [Browse spare camera batteries](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=mirrorless+camera+spare+battery&tag=adifystore-21).

## Who should skip

If you shoot only a few weddings a year, renting gear may cost less than buying a full kit. If your budget is under ₹1,50,000, a crop-sensor body may work, but you will give up low-light performance and dual-slot options.

## Recommendation

Buy a full-frame body with dual card slots, a 24-70 mm f/2.8 and an 85 mm prime, with spare batteries and a reliable flash. Choose Canon, Sony or Nikon based on the lenses and handling you prefer. Confirm slot configuration and warranty before buying.
