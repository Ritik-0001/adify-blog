## The Specific Challenges Indian Utensils Present

**Curved kadai bottoms:** Standard racks designed for flat Western pots don't securely hold a kadai's rounded shape — it can tip, trapping food and blocking water spray to items nearby.

**Tall tiffin boxes and steel tumblers:** Standard rack heights are often calibrated for shorter Western dishware — taller Indian containers may not fit properly or block spray arms.

**Turmeric staining:** Turmeric can stain plastic dishwasher components over repeated washes if not addressed with appropriate wash temperature and pre-treatment.

**Heavy oil/ghee residue:** Indian cooking typically uses more oil than average Western cooking, requiring stronger degreasing wash cycles for proper cleaning.

## Tips for Washing Indian Utensils Effectively

**Pre-scrape heavy oil residue:** Even the best intensive wash cycle performs better when large food/oil deposits are scraped off before loading.

**Use the intensive/pots-and-pans cycle for kadais:** Standard/eco cycles are calibrated for lighter soiling and may not fully clean heavily used cookware.

**Load kadais and curved items in dedicated bottom rack space:** Most Indian-market dishwashers include adjustable tines specifically for this purpose — use them rather than forcing curved items into flat-item slots.
