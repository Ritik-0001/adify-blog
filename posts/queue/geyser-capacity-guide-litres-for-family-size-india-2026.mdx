## 1. 15 Litre Geysers: One or Two People

Roughly ₹8,000–15,000 for good brands. A 15 litre tank is enough for one or two people who bathe quickly, or for a kitchen-plus-washbasin setup. It heats fast and uses little space.

Why it works: low cost and quick heat-up. The trade-off is that a long shower for two people can run out, and most models in this size sit below the premium bracket.

## 2. 25 Litre Geysers: The Default for Families of Three to Four

Roughly ₹10,000–22,000. This is the most common size in Indian homes. With the thermostat set high and cold water mixed in at the tap, 25 litres usually covers short showers for three or four people in sequence, with a short recovery gap.

Why it works: it balances recovery time, power draw and cost. Look for a five-star rating and a glass-lined or stainless steel tank.

## 3. 35 Litre Geysers: Five People or Cold Mornings

Roughly ₹14,000–26,000. In winter, when inlet water is colder, you mix less cold water, so each shower uses more of the stored hot water. A 35 litre tank gives headroom for five people or for a household that bathes back to back.

Why it works: fewer cold surprises. The trade-off is a slower first heat-up and a heavier wall load.

## How to Choose the Right Capacity

Count peak use, not average use. If three people bathe in the same hour on a winter morning, you need tank size for three showers plus a little margin. Add kitchen use if the same heater feeds the kitchen.

Check the tank material. Glass-lined and stainless steel tanks are common, and the anode, warranty on the tank and thermostat quality matter more than the capacity number alone.

Match power and wiring. Most storage geysers draw around 2,000 watts, so a dedicated 16 amp point and good wiring are important.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Buying too small to save money.** A tank that runs out in winter costs more in frustration than the saving.

**Ignoring the star rating.** A five-star storage geyser loses less heat while standing.

**Skipping a pressure check.** Storage geysers have pressure limits, so check if you live on a high floor with a booster pump.

**Using the wrong installation.** Vertical and horizontal models are not interchangeable on the same bracket.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A couple in a 1BHK.** A 15 litre geyser, or a 25 litre one if you want comfort in winter.

**A family of four in a 2BHK.** A 25 litre five-star geyser per bathroom, or one 35 litre unit if the bathrooms are adjacent.

**A joint family of six.** One 50 litre unit per bathroom, or a 100 litre system planned by a plumber.

## Quick FAQ

**Is a bigger geyser always better?** No. It costs more to heat and to run, so match it to your use.

**Can one geyser serve two bathrooms?** Yes, with correct piping and a larger tank, but distance causes heat loss.

**Do I need a pressure valve?** Yes, a safety valve is standard and should be checked by the installer.
