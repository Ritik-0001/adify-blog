## How to Choose a Laptop for Coding

Prioritise RAM and storage. Sixteen gigabytes is a comfortable target for modern development with browsers, editors and containers, and a fast NVMe SSD keeps builds and startups quick.

Look at the keyboard and display. You will type for hours, so key travel, layout and a bright, sharp screen matter. A 16:10 display gives more vertical space for code.

Check processor generation and battery. Recent Ryzen 7 or Intel Core i5 or i7 chips are plenty for most coding, and long battery life helps when you work away from a socket.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Buying 8GB RAM.** It limits multitasking.

**Paying for a GPU you do not need.** Unless you do ML or games.

**Ignoring the keyboard.** Test if possible.

**Comparing different configurations.** Match RAM and storage.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A web developer.** A 16GB laptop with a good display handles editors, browsers and containers.

**A data science learner.** Consider more RAM or a laptop with a GPU if you train models.

**A student learning to code.** A reliable everyday laptop from Lenovo or Dell is enough.

## What to Check on the Listing

Confirm the RAM, the SSD size, the processor generation, the display resolution and the warranty. Check whether RAM is upgradeable.

## Quick FAQ

**Is a GPU necessary for coding?** No, unless you work on machine learning or games.

**Windows or Mac?** Both work. Choose based on your tools and budget.

**How much storage do I need?** 512GB is a comfortable minimum.
