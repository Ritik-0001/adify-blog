## 1. Built-in Gas Hobs

Roughly ₹10,000–40,000. Gas hobs offer instant flame control and work with any cookware, including tawa and direct flame for roasting.

Why it works: familiar and flexible. The trade-off is cleaning and heat in the kitchen.

## 2. Built-in Induction Hobs

Roughly ₹15,000–60,000. Induction hobs heat the pan directly with a magnetic field, so they are fast and the surface stays cooler.

Why it works: speed and easy cleaning. The trade-off is the need for induction-compatible cookware.

[Search built-in induction hobs on Amazon](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=built+in+induction+hob&tag=adifystore-21)

## 3. Hybrid Hobs With Gas and Induction

Roughly ₹20,000–50,000. Some hobs combine gas burners with an induction zone, giving both options in one cutout.

Why it works: flexibility. The trade-off is that you give up some burners.

[Search hybrid hobs on Amazon](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=hybrid+gas+induction+built+in+hob&tag=adifystore-21)

## 4. Cookware for Induction

Priced separately from the hob. Induction needs magnetic cookware such as stainless steel with a ferrous base or cast iron. Aluminium and copper without a magnetic base will not work.

Why it works: enables induction cooking. The trade-off is replacing some pans.

## 5. Safety and Heat

Gas hobs have an open flame and need flame failure protection and good ventilation, while induction hobs have cooler surfaces and auto shut-off, which suits homes with children.

Why it works: safety planning. The trade-off is that each has its own cautions.

## 6. Power Cuts and Load

Induction needs steady electricity and a good electrical connection, since hobs draw high power. Gas works in power cuts but needs a cylinder or piped gas.

Why it works: matches your infrastructure. The trade-off is that power cuts affect induction.

## How to Choose Between Gas and Induction

Start with your cooking style. If you roast rotis directly on flame or do heavy tadka, gas feels natural, and if you boil, simmer and fry gently, induction works well.

Think about infrastructure. Check your electrical load, the power supply and the gas connection before deciding.

Consider cleaning and safety. Induction has a flat, cool surface that is easy to wipe, while gas has grates and burners to clean.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Switching to induction without checking cookware.** Test with a magnet.

**Ignoring electrical load.** Ask an electrician.

**Skipping ventilation.** Gas still needs a chimney.

**Choosing on trend alone.** Match your cooking.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A family that makes rotis daily.** A gas hob, possibly with an induction zone.

**A busy couple.** An induction hob for speed and easy cleaning.

**A home with frequent power cuts.** A gas hob.

## What to Check on the Listing

Confirm the burner or zone type, the power rating, the glass or surface, the safety features, the cutout size and the warranty. See whether cookware compatibility is explained.

## Running Costs

Induction is often described as more efficient at transferring heat to the pan, while gas costs depend on cylinder or piped gas prices in your city. Compare your own monthly spend rather than relying on generic figures.

## Quick FAQ

**Can I use both?** Yes with a hybrid hob or a separate induction cooktop.

**Is induction safe?** Yes with correct cookware and care.

**Does gas need a chimney?** Yes for ventilation.

## A Practical Middle Path

If you cannot decide, install a built-in gas hob and keep a portable induction cooktop for boiling and simmering. That lets you test induction cooking at low cost before committing to a built-in induction hob. If you later switch, you will know which cookware works and how it fits your routine.

## Kitchen Planning Notes

Whichever you choose, plan a chimney of suitable suction and a clear cutout. Gas needs a proper connection and ventilation, and induction needs a correctly rated electrical circuit. Check clearances to cabinets and walls, and keep the instruction manual for service.

## Final Checklist Before You Buy

Before you order, measure the counter cutout or wall space and compare it with the dimensions on the listing, ask your carpenter and electrician to confirm clearances, power points and gas or water connections, and check who handles installation. Read the warranty terms on motors and electronics, keep the invoice and manual, and confirm that spare parts and service exist in your city.

## Recommendation

For most Indian kitchens that make rotis daily, a built-in gas hob remains the practical choice, while induction suits those who want speed and easy cleaning. Compare specifications and cutout sizes on each listing.
