## 1. Panel Type — The Most Important Decision

**IPS (In-Plane Switching):**
- Wide viewing angles (178°/178°)
- Accurate, consistent colours
- Good brightness uniformity
- Recommended for: everyone except hardcore gamers

**TN (Twisted Nematic):**
- Fastest response times (1ms)
- Narrow viewing angles (colours shift when viewed from sides)
- Lower price per inch
- Recommended for: competitive gaming only

**VA (Vertical Alignment):**
- High contrast ratios (3000:1 vs IPS's 1000:1)
- Better for dark scenes (movies, games)
- Slower response than TN
- Recommended for: home theatre, cinematic gaming

**OLED:**
- Perfect black levels, infinite contrast
- Best display technology available
- Available under ₹25,000 only in TVs, not desktop monitors in India
- Not relevant for Indian buyers under ₹30,000

**For Indian home use: buy IPS.** The viewing angle advantage alone justifies the slight premium over TN.

## 2. Resolution — The Second Decision

**Full HD (1920x1080):**
- Standard for 22–24 inch monitors
- 94 PPI at 24" — adequate for text reading
- Budget: ₹5,000–₁₅,000
- Suitable for: most users

**QHD / 2K (2560x1440):**
- 25% more vertical resolution = 25% more content visible
- 109 PPI at 27" — noticeably sharper than 1080p
- Budget: ₹14,000–₂₅,000
- Suitable for: developers, designers, heavy multitaskers

**4K UHD (3840x2160):**
- Four times 1080p pixels — extremely sharp
- Requires powerful GPU
- Budget: ₹25,000+ for monitors
- Suitable for: professional creators, photo/video editors

**For Indian buyers under ₹15,000: Full HD is the only practical choice.** QHD monitors below ₹14,000 from reliable brands are rare.

## 3. Refresh Rate — Matters Only for Gaming

**60Hz:** Standard. Every displayed frame is held for 1/60th of a second. Adequate for all productivity, learning, and casual use.

**75Hz:** Slightly smoother scrolling than 60Hz. Minimal improvement for non-gaming use.

**144Hz / 165Hz:** Gaming monitors. Video gaming at 144+ FPS appears dramatically smoother. Zero benefit for productivity work.

**For Indian home and office users: 60Hz is sufficient.** 75Hz is a nice-to-have bonus. 144Hz is unnecessary unless you play competitive games.

---

## 4. Response Time — Mostly Marketing

Response time (1ms, 4ms, 5ms) measures how quickly a pixel changes colour. At anything below 8ms, the difference is invisible in non-gaming use. The "1ms" claimed on many monitors is measured at grey-to-grey transitions in overdrive mode — not representative of typical use.

**For Indian buyers: ignore response time unless buying a gaming monitor.**

---

## 5. Eye Care Features

**Flicker-free:** Eliminates backlight pulse at low brightness. Genuinely reduces eye fatigue in extended use. Present on most monitors above ₹7,000.

**Low Blue Light / Reader Mode:** Shifts colour temperature warmer, reducing high-energy blue light. Useful for late-evening use when blue light disrupts sleep.

**Brightness Intelligence (BenQ exclusive):** Sensor-based automatic brightness adjustment. Most useful feature for study and WFH use in varying lighting.

---

## 6. Connectivity

**HDMI:** Standard for laptops and consoles. All monitors should have at least one HDMI.

**DisplayPort:** Preferred for high refresh rate gaming and desktop GPUs. Better bandwidth than HDMI for QHD+ resolutions.

**VGA:** Legacy. Fine for desktop PCs with dedicated GPU. Unavailable on MacBooks and modern thin laptops without adapter.

**USB-C:** The cleanest connection — video + data + charging over one cable. Available on monitors above ₹13,000. Ideal for MacBook and modern laptop users.
