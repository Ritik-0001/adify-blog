## OLED vs QLED in Indian Homes — The Honest Take

Before spending ₹80,000 on an OLED, understand this: OLED panels have the best blacks, contrast, and viewing angles of any TV technology. They're also vulnerable to **image retention** (temporary burn-in from static content) and peak **brightness that tops out at 700–1000 nits** — significantly below good QLED panels that hit 1500–2000 nits.

In Indian drawing rooms with windows and afternoon sun: a Samsung QN85B at ₹80,000 will look brighter and more "wow" than an LG C3 in daylight. At night with the lights dimmed, the LG C3 destroys it.

OLED is the right choice if:
- You watch in a dark or controlled-light room
- You play console games (PS5/Xbox — 4K 120Hz HDMI 2.1 matters)
- You're a serious movie watcher who cares about blacks and colour accuracy

## Burn-In: Should You Worry?

Short answer: **not in 2026 with normal watching habits**.

Burn-in happens when a static image is displayed for hundreds of hours — like a news channel bug or a game HUD. Modern OLED panels have pixel refreshing, logo dimming, and screen shift features that essentially eliminate burn-in risk for normal TV use.

Where you should be careful: leaving a paused game running for hours, watching a news channel for 8 hours a day, or using it as a PC monitor full-time. Normal TV watching, movies, cricket, OTT — zero risk.
