## Closed-back versus open-back

**Closed-back** headphones seal the cups, so sound does not leak into the microphone while you record, and outside noise stays out. They are essential for tracking vocals or live instruments. Their downside is a more boxy soundstage and bass that can be overstated by the sealed cavity.

**Open-back** headphones let sound pass through the cups. They sound wider, more natural and are generally better for judging balance, panning and reverb during mixing. The downside is that they leak, so you cannot use them for recording and they let room noise in. Many producers in small Indian flats with fans and traffic find open-backs impractical for daytime use.

## Flat response, and what that really means

Studio headphones aim for a neutral response, so you hear mistakes rather than flattery. No headphone is perfectly flat, and each has quirks, such as boosted bass or a treble peak. The practical answer is to learn one pair well and check mixes on speakers and phone speakers. Some producers use correction software; it helps but cannot rescue a poor pair.

## Impedance and your interface

Low impedance headphones (32 ohm) run from phones and laptops. Mid (80 ohm) suit most interface outputs. High (250 to 300 ohm) need stronger amplification. Check your interface's headphone output before buying a high-impedance pair, or add a small headphone amp. [Browse audio interfaces](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=audio+interface+USB&tag=adifystore-21).

## Cables, pads and durability

Choose detachable cables so a broken cable costs a few hundred rupees instead of the pair. A coiled cable suits tracking, a straight one suits mixing. Replacement pads are available for the Beyerdynamic and Sennheiser models, and in the Indian climate pads wear out from sweat in a couple of years.

## Avoid wireless for production

Bluetooth adds delay and compresses audio, so it is not suitable for tracking or mixing, however good the noise cancelling.

## Recommendation

If you buy one pair, choose a closed-back such as the DT 770 Pro or ATH-M50x class for recording and general use. Add a Sennheiser open-back when you start serious mixing in a quiet room. Always confirm the impedance, detachable cable and genuine seller before ordering, and check your mixes on other speakers.
