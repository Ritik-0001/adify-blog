## How to Choose a Wi-Fi Smart Lock

Check what works offline. A good lock still opens with fingerprint, PIN or key if Wi-Fi is down, and the app should be an extra layer rather than the only way in.

Look at app features. Time-limited guest codes, access logs, low battery alerts and multi-user control are the features most households use.

Think about privacy and security. Choose reputable brands, use a strong account password and enable two-step verification where offered.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Relying only on the app.** Keep a key and a code.

**Ignoring Wi-Fi range.** Check signal at the door.

**Skipping updates.** Keep firmware current.

**Sharing one master code widely.** Use individual codes.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A working couple.** Remote unlock for deliveries and a guest code for the cook.

**A rental home.** A removable lock with app control.

**A family with elders.** A lock with simple fingerprint entry and app alerts for you.

## What to Check on the Listing

Confirm the Wi-Fi or bridge requirement, the unlocking methods, the app compatibility, the battery type, the door fit, the warranty and the installation terms. See what happens during power or internet outages.

## Battery and Network Notes

Wi-Fi radios drain batteries faster than offline locks, so check expected battery life and the low-battery alert. Place your router so that it reaches the front door, or use a bridge near the door to keep the connection stable.

## Warranty and Service

Ask what the warranty covers, whether electronics are included and who installs and services the lock in your city. Keep the invoice, the app login details and the installation record safe.

## Quick FAQ

**Can I unlock from anywhere?** On locks with Wi-Fi or a bridge, yes.

**What if the internet is down?** Use fingerprint, PIN or key.

**Is it secure?** Reputable brands use encryption, but follow good account hygiene.

## Setting Up Guest and Staff Access

Create a separate code or app account for each person who needs entry, such as a cook, a cleaner or a visiting relative, and set start and end times so that access ends automatically. Review the log every week, delete unused codes and change the master code if someone leaves your service. This habit makes a connected lock far safer than a hidden spare key.

## Final Checklist Before You Buy

Before you order, confirm that the product fits your door or wiring, check power and internet needs and what still works during an outage and ask who installs it. Change default passwords on first use, keep a mechanical or manual backup, read the warranty on electronics and keep the invoice and the installer contact details together.
