## How to Choose a Wi-Fi Smart Lock

Check what works offline. A good lock still opens with fingerprint, PIN or key if Wi-Fi is down, and the app should be an extra layer rather than the only way in.

Look at app features. Time-limited guest codes, access logs, low battery alerts and multi-user control are the features most households use.

Think about privacy and security. Choose reputable brands, use a strong account password and enable two-step verification where offered.

## Setting Up Guest and Staff Access

Create a separate code or app account for each person who needs entry, such as a cook, a cleaner or a visiting relative, and set start and end times so that access ends automatically. Review the log every week, delete unused codes and change the master code if someone leaves your service. This habit makes a connected lock far safer than a hidden spare key.

## Signal and Battery Realities

Wi-Fi radios drain batteries faster than offline locks, so check expected battery life and keep spare cells at home. Test the signal at the front door itself, as thick walls and metal door frames weaken Wi-Fi. If the router sits far inside, a bridge placed near the door usually steadies the connection.

## Who Should Skip This

If your phone and home internet are patchy, or your household includes members who dislike apps, an app-first lock adds a failure point without much benefit, and a plain fingerprint or PIN lock is simpler. Skip it too if you are renting and cannot leave a bridge plugged in near the door.

## Privacy and Data on a Connected Lock

An app-connected lock keeps a record of when your family comes and goes. Read what the app stores and whether you can delete the history, and use a separate email and a strong password for the account. If the app offers features you will not use, such as automatic unlock by phone location, leave them off until you are sure you want them.
