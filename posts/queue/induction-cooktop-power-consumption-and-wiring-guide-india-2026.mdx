A typical 1,800-2,100 W induction cooktop used for about an hour a day adds roughly 40 to 60 units to your monthly electricity bill, which is around ₹300-500 in most Indian cities, and it needs a 16 A socket, a 2.5 sq mm copper wire and a matching MCB to run safely. ## From watts to units

An electricity unit (kWh) is 1,000 watts used for one hour. A 2,000 W cooktop at full power uses 2 units in an hour. In reality the cooktop cycles on and off at lower power levels, so a family that boils milk, cooks rice and makes a sabzi might average 1,000 to 1,400 W over a 60-minute cooking session.

A simple monthly estimate: average watts times hours per day times 30, divided by 1,000. At 1,200 W for an hour a day, that is 36 units a month. At ₹7 to ₹9 per unit including taxes, the bill impact is roughly ₹250-325. Cooking two meals a day for a larger family pushes this to 60 units or more. Check your own tariff slab, because the last slab for heavy consumers can cost much more per unit.

## Is induction cheaper than LPG

Induction is efficient, wasting less heat than gas, so cooking times are shorter. If a domestic cylinder in your city costs somewhere around ₹800-900 and lasts a month or two, the electricity cost of induction is often comparable, and sometimes lower, but only when you stay within a lower tariff slab. Do not buy induction expecting to save a large amount of money. Buy it for speed, cleanliness and the end of gas leaks. ## Socket, plug and wire

Standard Indian 6 A sockets are meant for lights, fans and chargers. A 2,000 W appliance draws roughly 9 A, so use a 16 A socket and plug. The wire from the distribution board should be a copper conductor of at least 2.5 sq mm, and aluminium wiring in older buildings is a red flag. A loose plug is a frequent cause of overheating, so check that the plug fits tight and does not warm up after 20 minutes of cooking.

Never run a cooktop from a multi-plug extension board. If the cooktop's power cord is short, move the socket closer to the counter instead of extending the cord. [16 A sockets and plugs](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=16+amp+industrial+plug+socket&tag=adifystore-21).

## MCB rating and separate lines

A dedicated 16 A MCB for the kitchen cooking circuit is the standard recommendation for a 2,000 W cooktop. A C-curve MCB tolerates the brief current surge when the cooktop switches on. Do not simply swap an MCB for a higher rating to stop it tripping, because that leaves the wire as the weak link. If it trips repeatedly, the answer is a load calculation, not a larger breaker.

If your kitchen shares a line with a microwave, kettle and mixer grinder, an induction cooktop on the same circuit will trip the breaker whenever two are on. Ask the electrician to run a separate line, and to fit an RCCB or RCBO for earth-leakage protection as well. ## Voltage fluctuation and stabilisers

Low voltage makes induction cooktops run weaker or show error codes, and large voltage swings can damage the control board. Many cooktops sold in India have built-in voltage protection, but a separate stabiliser rated for the load is a sensible investment in areas with regular dips. The stabiliser must be able to handle the full 2,000 W and more, so a small stabiliser meant for a TV will not do. ## Running induction on inverter backup

This is where many buyers get stuck. A 2,000 W cooktop on an inverter needs an inverter with enough capacity, at least 2.5 to 3 kVA, and a battery bank to match. Standard 1 kVA home inverters that run fans and lights will overload and shut down. Pure sine wave output is strongly preferred, because some induction cooktops refuse to start or display errors on a modified sine wave supply.

Even with a large inverter, the battery drains quickly. An hour of cooking at 1,200 W takes around 1.2 kWh from the battery, which is a big part of a typical home battery's usable capacity. If long power cuts are common where you live, keep a gas stove as backup rather than buying a much bigger inverter system just for induction. ## What to check on the cooktop itself

Look at the rated wattage on the sticker, not the headline "2,100 W peak" on the box. A cooktop with adjustable power levels and a fan that stays on after cooking cools down safely and lasts longer. Check for a flat glass top, a decent length of heat-resistant cord, and an inbuilt overheat cut-off.

## Recommendation

For most homes, a 1,800-2,100 W cooktop, a dedicated 16 A copper line and a C-curve MCB is the right setup. Add a stabiliser if your supply dips, and treat inverter use as an extra project with a proper kVA calculation. Ask a licensed electrician to put the circuit in writing before you buy the cooktop.
