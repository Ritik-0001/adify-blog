## Why only convection can bake

A solo microwave heats food by agitating water molecules. A cake batter placed in one will heat unevenly, steam rather than set a crust, and finish rubbery with no browning. A grill microwave adds a top heating element that browns, but it cannot bake the inside properly. A convection microwave adds a heating element and a fan that circulates hot air, so it behaves like a small oven, and that is the mode that bakes cakes, cookies and breads.

So the buying rule is simple: if the listing does not say convection, it cannot bake a proper cake.

## Capacity and cake size

Cavity size dictates the tin you can use.

- **20-23 litres:** fits a 6 inch cake tin or a small loaf. Tight for a round cake with a rack.
- **25-28 litres:** fits a 7-8 inch round tin and most loaf tins.
- **30-32 litres:** fits an 8-9 inch tin and a bit more headroom for taller cakes.

Check the turntable diameter (usually around 28-32 cm) and the interior height, since many convection microwaves have a low roof. A tall cake with a dome can touch the top element, so leave a gap.

## What to look for

- **Convection temperature range:** up to around 200-220 degrees C is enough for cakes, which bake at 160-180.
- **Preheat function:** cakes need a preheated chamber. Check that the oven has one.
- **Metal rack or baking rack:** it lifts the tin into the hot air. Look for a high and a low rack.
- **Crisp plate:** helps with pizza bases rather than cakes, but is a useful extra.
- **Bake presets:** auto cake or bake menus can help beginners.
- **Even heating design:** inner dimensions and fan placement affect uneven baking.
- **Stainless steel or ceramic enamel cavity:** easier to clean after baking.

## Baking tips for a convection microwave

1. **Use the right tin.** Metal or silicone tins work in convection mode, but not in microwave mode. Glass and ceramic dishes are fine for both.
2. **Preheat for 5-10 minutes** to the set temperature before putting in the batter.
3. **Lower the temperature slightly.** Convection microwaves can brown faster, so many recipes use around 10 degrees less than a standard oven.
4. **Use the rack and the middle position,** so the cake sits clear of the top element.
5. **Rotate or check halfway,** since hotspots exist.
6. **Do not use microwave-only mode** for the same recipe unless the recipe is designed for it.
7. **Let the oven cool** before cleaning.

## What a microwave cannot bake well

- Large or multi-layer cakes: the cavity cannot fit two tins comfortably.
- Bread with a thick crust: convection can bake loaves, but the crust is softer than in a proper oven.
- Cakes needing long, slow baking at low temperature.
- Several trays at once: a convection microwave is a single-rack appliance.

If you bake large cakes weekly, a 45-60 litre OTG gives more space and control, and some home bakers buy both.

## An honest trade-off

A convection microwave handles everyday baking with reheating in the same box, which is ideal for smaller kitchens. But the results will not match a proper oven for large cakes or breads, and learning the quirks of your own oven takes a few tries.
