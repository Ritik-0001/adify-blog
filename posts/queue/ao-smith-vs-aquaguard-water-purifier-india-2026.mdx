## Who they are

Aquaguard, from Eureka Forbes, is one of the best-known purifier brands in India and has a large dedicated service network and a long product list covering RO, UV and combination units. AO Smith is a global water-heating and purification company whose Indian range tends to be built around RO with a clean, compact design, often sold through dealers and online. The names are familiar, so the real comparison is about execution.

## Technology on offer

Aquaguard sells RO, UV and multi-stage combinations, and some ranges add mineral enrichment cartridges or copper-related stages. AO Smith's RO range stresses mineral-retaining designs and often adds stage indicators and filter-life displays. The marketing names differ, but a good comparison checks four things in each listing: stages (RO, UV, UF), storage litres, purification capacity per hour, and recovery percentage.

For TDS below roughly 200, a plain UV or UF unit saves water and minerals, and the cheaper versions cost less than this post's price floor, so they only serve as a "skip RO" note. Above roughly 500 TDS, you need a proper RO with a decent membrane.

## Service and parts

Aquaguard's biggest edge is reach: many small towns have an authorised technician, and parts and consumables are easy to find. AO Smith has a smaller but generally well-regarded network that is strongest in metros and tier-two cities. Parts availability for AO Smith can take longer in smaller towns, so ask the dealer about response times.

Whichever you pick, check what the annual service plan covers, since consumables like the RO membrane and UV lamp can make the second-year bill larger than expected. Typically a yearly plan costs a few thousand rupees.

## Build and daily use

AO Smith units usually feel solid with a slim body that fits a wall-mount comfortably, and the filter-change indicators help owners remember maintenance. Aquaguard models vary more by range; its premium units feel well finished, while lower variants are plainer. Noise from the booster pump is similar in both, a steady hum while filling.

Tank size matters more than brand: 7-10 litres suits a family of four, and a bigger tank helps in areas with intermittent supply.

## Choosing by situation

- Small town or tier-three city: Aquaguard, mainly for technician availability.
- Metro flat, compact kitchen, want a clean look: AO Smith.
- Hard borewell water: either brand, but buy a model with a TDS controller and a large membrane. Compare [RO purifier with TDS controller](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=ro+purifier+with+tds+controller&tag=adifystore-21).
- Rental home: neither brand's wall-mount design is ideal if drilling is restricted, so check a [countertop RO purifier](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=countertop+water+purifier+ro&tag=adifystore-21) instead.

## Mineral claims and what to verify

Mineral-enrichment cartridges and copper stages are marketed heavily by both brands. They add taste and some mineral content but are not a substitute for a good membrane. Verify how often the cartridge must be replaced and what it costs, because some need replacing every 6 months.

Also check the purifier's rejection rate, meaning how much water goes to the drain. In water-scarce cities, a high-recovery model saves money on the water bill over time, and some units let you reuse reject water for cleaning.

## Installation reality

Both brands usually include a basic installation, but plumbing extras (a longer pipe, a separate inlet tap or a wall plug) are often charged separately. Confirm what is included before the technician arrives and keep a copy of the quotation.

If you want the least service worry, buy Aquaguard. If you want a compact, premium RO and your local dealer is dependable, buy AO Smith.
