## How to Choose a Smartwatch to Gift

Start with the phone. Apple Watch needs an iPhone; Galaxy Watch works best with Samsung; brands like Amazfit, Fitbit, Noise and Fire-Boltt work across platforms.

Decide what matters: fitness tracking, notifications, call answering or style. A premium watch adds better sensors and build; a budget watch covers the basics.

Think about comfort. Check strap options and case size, since a watch that is too large or heavy will not get worn.

## Quick FAQ

**Are smartwatches medically accurate?** Consumer watches give useful trends but are not medical devices. Encourage recipients to treat readings as a guide and consult a doctor for health concerns.

**How long does the battery last?** It ranges from about a day on Apple Watch to over a week on some Amazfit and Fitbit models.

**Can it replace a phone?** Cellular versions can make calls independently, but most buyers use the watch as a companion to a phone.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Ignoring phone compatibility.** An Apple Watch will not work with an Android phone, and some Galaxy Watch features need a Samsung handset. Confirm the recipient's phone before you buy.

**Buying on looks alone.** A large, heavy watch can be uncomfortable on a slim wrist. Check case size and strap options, and consider a watch that comes in more than one size.

**Expecting medical accuracy.** Heart rate, sleep and oxygen readings on consumer watches are useful trends, not clinical measurements. Present the gift as a wellness tool, not a diagnostic device.

**Forgetting the charger and strap.** Some watches use proprietary chargers, and a spare strap is a small extra that makes the gift feel finished.
