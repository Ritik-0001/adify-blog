## What Engineering Students Actually Need

Before the list: most laptop reviews for students ignore the real workload. Here's what engineering software demands:

**AutoCAD, SolidWorks, CATIA** — need 8GB minimum RAM (16GB comfortable), a dedicated GPU for SolidWorks/CATIA (entry-level Nvidia GeForce MX or RTX 3050 is enough), and a Ryzen 5 or Core i5 CPU.

**Android Studio / IntelliJ** — RAM-hungry. 8GB will feel slow with emulators. 16GB is the floor for comfortable use.

**Python, MATLAB, Scilab** — CPU-bound. More cores and higher sustained clock speeds matter. Ryzen 5 wins here over Intel at this price.

**Spreadsheets, reports, presentations** — any modern laptop handles this.

## What to Ignore in Spec Sheets

**"Thin and light" labels** — thin laptops (under 1.5 kg) at this price point throttle under load. They're designed for office work, not sustained engineering workloads. The thermal mass isn't there for sustained computation.

**Windows 11 Home vs Pro** — irrelevant for most students. Pro adds BitLocker and remote desktop features, not performance.

**HDD + SSD combos** — if a laptop at this price still has an HDD alongside the SSD, avoid it. Pure SSD storage (even if less total) makes the entire system feel faster.
