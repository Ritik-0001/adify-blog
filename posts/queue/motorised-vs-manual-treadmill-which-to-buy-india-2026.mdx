Buy a motorised treadmill, roughly ₹25,000-60,000 for good home models, if you want to walk or jog at a steady pace; choose a manual treadmill only if you specifically want a harder, motor-free workout and have decided that the lower electricity bill and simplicity matter more than comfort. For most Indian homes, motorised is the right default.

This comparison is researched from listings and specifications, not from running on every type. The thing to verify on any manual treadmill is the flywheel and belt design, because cheap flat manual belts feel very different from a curved one.

## How each type works

A motorised treadmill uses an electric motor to turn the belt, and you set the speed and incline from a console. A manual treadmill has no motor. You push the belt backwards with your own feet. Flat manual models are cheap and light but awkward. Curved manual models, with a slatted belt on a curved frame, are smoother and let the belt follow your stride, but they are heavy and expensive.

## Cost, honestly

Motorised treadmills in India start near ₹15,000 for walking-only machines and climb past ₹1,00,000 for heavy, long-warranty ones. Manual flat treadmills often cost ₹8,000-15,000, which is why they tempt first-time buyers. Curved manual treadmills from specialist brands run roughly ₹40,000-90,000 and are mostly sold for gyms.

Running cost is the one place manual wins. A motorised unit at moderate load uses roughly 0.5 to 1 unit per hour, which is around ₹4-9 per hour at typical tariffs. That is small, so do not choose manual to save on electricity alone. [See manual and curved treadmills](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=manual+curved+treadmill&tag=adifystore-21).

## Workout feel

On a motorised treadmill the belt sets the pace, so you can zone out, watch a show and hold 5 km per hour for 40 minutes. It is excellent for beginners, seniors and rehab walks, because the speed is predictable and handrails help.

On a manual treadmill you set the pace yourself, and the belt resists. Calorie burn at the same speed is higher, and the effort feels closer to walking uphill. Many people find manual flat treadmills tiring within 15 minutes, and the stride feels short and jerky. Curved models feel natural but make it hard to walk slowly, since the belt wants you to move at a faster tempo.

## Durability and upkeep

Motorised units have more parts that wear: motor brushes, drive belts, rollers, the walking belt and the control board. Most issues are about belt lubrication and dust inside the motor cover. A silicone lubricant every few months and a clean deck extend life considerably.

Manual treadmills have far fewer failure points, which is a real advantage for someone far from a service centre. But cheap flat models use thin rollers and plastic bearings that can seize. [Treadmill belt lubricant](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=treadmill+belt+lubricant+silicone&tag=adifystore-21).

## Joints, safety and incline

Motorised treadmills with cushioned decks reduce impact, which helps knees. They have safety clips that stop the belt if you slip. Manual flat treadmills have no such feature, and the belt can run away from you if you stop pushing, which is a concern for older users.

Incline is available on better motorised models, either manual or electric, up to 10 or 15 percent. Manual flat treadmills usually have a fixed angle or crude adjustment.

## Space and noise

A manual treadmill is quiet, apart from belt sound, and often lighter to move. Foldable motorised treadmills compress to roughly 60 to 80 cm wide when stored upright. The motor is the noisiest part, and thin floors in flats transmit footfall noise, so a rubber mat underneath helps. ## Who should pick which

Pick motorised if you are a beginner, are over 45, are recovering from injury, want to track speed and distance, or plan to run. Pick manual if you already train regularly, want a short and intense workout, live where power cuts are frequent, and can afford a curved model rather than a flat one.

Who should skip both: if you walk outside easily and only want a rainy-day option, a compact walking pad at ₹15,000 or more may be a smaller commitment than a full treadmill. [Compact walking pads](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=under+desk+walking+pad+treadmill&tag=adifystore-21).
