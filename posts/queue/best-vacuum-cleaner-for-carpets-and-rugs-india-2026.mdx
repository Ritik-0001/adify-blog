## Pile height decides everything

Low-pile flatweave dhurries and thin office-style carpets release dust easily, so almost any decent vacuum copes. High-pile shag, wool Kashmiri rugs and thick wall-to-wall carpets are different: dust and pet hair sit deep at the base of the fibres. For these you need an agitating head plus enough airflow to pull the loosened dirt up through a long pile.

A rough test before you buy: if you can bury a coin in the pile, treat it as high-pile and prioritise brush-bar torque over battery life. Many cordless sticks have a hard-floor soft roller and a separate carpet head, and the carpet head is the one that matters here.

## 1. Cordless stick with a motorised bar

Brands like Dyson and Shark sell the stick designs most owners like for daily carpet work. The motorised head is lighter than a canister to push, and the same body converts to a handheld for sofas and car seats. Expect roughly ₹25,000 to ₹45,000.

The honest trade-off is runtime. On the strongest mode the battery on many sticks lasts only 10 to 15 minutes, and a full-size 1,200 sq ft flat with two carpets can take longer than that. If you have more than 800 sq ft of carpet, a stick should be a secondary machine. Browse [Dyson cordless vacuum cleaner](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=dyson+cordless+vacuum+cleaner&tag=adifystore-21) and [Shark cordless vacuum](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=shark+cordless+vacuum+cleaner&tag=adifystore-21) listings and read the runtime on the carpet head specifically.

## 2. Canister with a powered turbo head

A canister cleaner keeps the heavy motor on wheels while you push a light wand, and the power comes from the wall socket, so suction does not fade. Premium German-engineered canisters, in the roughly ₹20,000 to ₹50,000 band, usually include a motorised floor head for carpets and a bag filtration system that suits allergy sufferers.

This is the strongest choice for wall-to-wall carpet in a bedroom floor or a large rug-heavy drawing room. The downsides are storage and stairs: a canister is awkward to carry up a duplex staircase. Check [canister vacuum cleaner with turbo brush](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=canister+vacuum+cleaner+turbo+brush&tag=adifystore-21) results for a power-adjustable dial, which lets you drop suction for lighter rugs.

## 3. Upright vacuums

Uprights are the traditional carpet machine abroad: wide head, big roller, big dust bin. In India they are a niche product and service support is thinner. If you have large expanses of carpet, such as a furnished hotel-style rental, an upright cleans faster per square foot than any stick.

Skip an upright if you mostly have tiles and just two rugs. They are bulky, loud and hard to manoeuvre under low furniture. Search [upright vacuum cleaner for carpet](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=upright+vacuum+cleaner+for+carpet&tag=adifystore-21) only if you can confirm local service in your city.

## 4. Wet-and-dry and carpet-shampoo machines

Spills on rugs are common in Indian homes, from chai to children's colours. A wet-and-dry vacuum from a brand such as Karcher or Eureka Forbes, in the roughly ₹15,000 to ₹30,000 range, lifts liquids and can handle post-monsoon dampness. Some include shampoo tanks for deep cleaning.

These are not great everyday vacuums, since they are heavy and need emptying and drying after use. Think of one as a second machine for stains. See [wet and dry vacuum cleaner for carpet](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=wet+and+dry+vacuum+cleaner+carpet&tag=adifystore-21).

## 5. Pet owners and long hair

Pet hair wraps around brush bars. Look for anti-tangle designs or a comb-style cutter on the roller, and a bin that opens without touching the dirt. If your rugs shed wool too, a bagless bin you can empty is more practical than buying bags. ## Features that matter on carpet

- Dedicated carpet mode that raises suction and bar speed
- Sealed filtration, since carpets hold dust that escapes through cheap filters
- Adjustable head height so the roller does not stall on thick pile
- A bin capacity of at least 0.5 litre for stick models

Robot vacuums usually struggle with thick rugs, since many stall or push the rug edges, so do not buy one to replace a carpet vacuum.

## Recommendation

If your carpets are limited to a living room rug and a bedroom runner, buy a cordless stick with a motorised head and a spare battery if the brand sells one. If carpet covers most of your floor, buy a corded canister with a turbo head and keep a small cordless for quick jobs. Always switch off the roller on silk, jute or fringed handmade rugs, and vacuum those from the back occasionally.
