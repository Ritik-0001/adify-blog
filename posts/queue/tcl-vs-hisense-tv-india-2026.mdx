TCL and Hisense are the two Chinese TV brands most worth comparing in India, and for roughly ₹25,000 to ₹1,20,000 you get a bigger and brighter panel from either of them than a Japanese or Korean label would give you at the same price. TCL tends to undercut on price and pushes QLED and QD-Mini LED hard, while Hisense often counters with stronger local dimming and brightness on its ULED and Mini-LED ranges.

This comparison is researched from brand lineups and listing details, not bench-tested in a lab, so treat series names as a starting point. The one thing you must verify before ordering is whether the brand has an authorised service centre in your pin code, because that is where both brands are weakest.

## Where the two brands actually differ

Both brands are huge globally, and both source panels from large Chinese panel makers, so a quick spec-sheet read makes them look like twins. The differences show up in tuning, pricing strategy and after-sales.

TCL builds its identity around value. Its Indian catalogue runs from budget 4K Google TV sets in the 43 to 55 inch range up to QD-Mini LED models with hundreds of dimming zones. TCL is also unusually quick to cut prices on larger sizes, so a 65 inch set often costs only a little more than a 55 inch from a legacy brand.

Hisense positions itself a notch more premium on picture engineering. Its ULED label covers local dimming and quantum-dot colour, and its higher Mini-LED series chase peak brightness. Hisense also sells very large screens, including 75 inch and above, plus laser projection products abroad, which signals where its engineering attention goes.

## Panels, brightness and picture character

At the entry level both use VA-type LCD panels in most sizes, which means good contrast in a dark room but narrower viewing angles than IPS. A few smaller sizes may use different panels, so check the listing for panel type if you sit off to the side.

Moving up, the real separator is local dimming. A Mini-LED backlight with several hundred zones can lift highlights and keep blacks deeper than an edge-lit set. Both brands sell these, and the cheaper edge-lit "QLED" labels on either side look very similar to each other. Do not pay a premium for the QLED badge alone; it only means a quantum-dot film that improves colour volume.

In practice, TCL's panels tend to look punchy and slightly cool out of the box, while Hisense often ships with a warmer, more restrained mode. Switch to Movie or Filmmaker mode on either brand and the gap narrows.

## Software and apps

Both sell Google TV models in India, so Netflix, Prime Video, Disney+ Hotstar and YouTube are one tap away. Check the listing for Google TV or Android TV rather than a proprietary OS, and look for Dolby Vision and HDMI 2.1 on the ports if you own a PS5 or a gaming PC. Hisense has also used its own VIDAA platform on some markets, so confirm the operating system on the exact model before you buy.

Remote voice assistant, Chromecast built in and Wi-Fi 5 versus Wi-Fi 6 vary across ranges, and the cheapest models in both brands carry modest processors that can feel slow after a year of app updates.

## Service network: the honest weak spot

Neither brand has the service reach of Samsung, LG or Sony. In metros you will usually find a service partner, but in smaller towns repair visits can take days. Panel damage in transit is also not unusual on very large sizes, so open the box with the delivery person present and photograph it.

Before buying, search the brand's support page for your pin code, and read recent reviews specific to your city. If you are in a Tier 3 town and want zero risk, a Samsung or LG set at slightly higher cost may be the more sensible answer. If you are in a major metro, the service gap matters far less.

## Sizes worth considering

For a living room with a seating distance of around 8 to 10 feet, a 55 inch is the minimum and a 65 inch is the sweet spot. Both brands price 65 inch sets aggressively, so the jump from 55 to 65 often costs less than expected. [Check 65 inch 4K TVs](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=65+inch+4K+Google+TV&tag=adifystore-21). For a bedroom 43 inch is plenty, and there the price floor drops below what this post covers, so buy on warranty rather than panel.

If you want a bigger step up, 75 inch models from both brands sit in the roughly ₹80,000 to ₹1,50,000 zone, and they are among the best-value large screens in India. [Browse 75 inch 4K TVs](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=75+inch+4K+TV&tag=adifystore-21).

## Sound and gaming

Built-in speakers on both brands are adequate for news and streaming, not for movies. Budget for a soundbar if you buy above 55 inches. Gaming is a place where Hisense and TCL both advertise 120Hz or 144Hz panels on upper ranges; read the specification carefully, since some native 60Hz models use motion processing that is not true refresh rate. ## Recommendation

Choose TCL when price per inch decides everything and you live in a city with a service centre. Choose Hisense when you want slightly better HDR headroom and can spend more. Whichever you buy, confirm the model's panel type, HDMI version and operating system, check your pin code on the service page, and keep the invoice, since both brands rely on proof of purchase for warranty claims.
