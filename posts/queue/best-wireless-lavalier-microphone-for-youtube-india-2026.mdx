## Why dual transmitters matter

A single-transmitter kit works for a solo creator. But many Indian YouTubers do interviews, podcasts or two-person walk-and-talks, and then a second transmitter is not a luxury. Two transmitters feed a single receiver, so you record both voices on separate channels or on a combined mono track. You can mix them later in editing.

The price jump from a single to a dual kit is modest compared with buying a second system, so for most creators the dual kit is the smarter spend.

## 1. DJI Mic 2, best all-round kit

Roughly ₹25,000-30,000 for the two-transmitter kit with charging case. ## Range, and what the number really means

Manufacturers claim 200 to 250 metres of range in open air. In real Indian filming conditions, such as a crowded market, apartment building, or lots of Wi-Fi, expect something closer to 20 to 50 metres before dropouts begin. That is plenty for a YouTube talking head, but not for filming a subject far across a street.

Walls, bodies and 2.4 GHz interference cut range quickly. If you shoot in markets or malls, position the receiver high and in clear view of the transmitter.

## Camera and phone connection

For mirrorless cameras or DSLRs, you typically use a 3.5 mm TRS cable from the receiver to the camera's mic input. Check whether your camera uses a TRRS or TRS jack, because the wrong cable gives distorted or no audio.

For phones, many kits now include a USB-C connector for Android and iPhone 15 or newer, plus a Lightning cable for older iPhones. Check the exact adapter included; buying extras is cheap but easy to forget. ## Lavalier placement and wind

The best-sounding clip position is the centre of the chest, around 15 to 20 cm below the chin. Hide the clip under a collar or shirt placket. A foam or fur windscreen helps outdoors, and a tiny pop of cloth tape stops cable rustle if you use a wired lavalier plugged into the transmitter.

If you want a discreet look, add a separate small lavalier microphone rather than relying on the transmitter's built-in mic. Search [lavalier microphone for wireless transmitter](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=lavalier+lapel+microphone+3.5mm+for+wireless+transmitter&tag=adifystore-21), and pick up a few [windscreens for lavalier mics](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=lavalier+mic+windscreen+furry&tag=adifystore-21).

## Onboard recording is a backup, not a luxury

Wireless links can drop in crowds, and a lost take is expensive. Kits that record inside each transmitter let you recover audio later. Check the specs for internal storage and format, and remember to sync the track in editing using a clap or a waveform match.

## Who should skip a wireless lav

If you record at a desk in a quiet room, a USB or XLR microphone usually sounds better and costs less per quality level. A wireless lav is the right tool when you move around, record outdoors, or need to be far from the camera. For podcasting at home, look at a dynamic microphone and interface instead.

Also skip the cheapest no-name Rs 2,000 kits: they tend to have poor noise floors and unreliable pairing, and they are below the range this guide covers.
