## How to Choose a Premium Mixer Grinder

Check the motor. Seven hundred and fifty watts is common, and 900 to 1000 watts suits very heavy use. Look for a copper winding and overload protection.

Look at the jars and blades. Stainless steel jars with strong blades and a tight fit with the motor hold up longer. A chutney jar and a wet grinding jar cover Indian needs.

Consider noise and balance. Heavier mixers vibrate less, and rubber feet reduce movement.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Overloading the jar.** Fill to the recommended level.

**Running too long.** Use short pulses.

**Skipping maintenance.** Clean blades and check rubber seals.

**Ignoring warranty.** Check the motor warranty.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A family that grinds idli batter weekly.** A powerful wet-grinding jar helps.

**A cook who makes masalas at home.** Look for a strong dry jar.

**A small kitchen.** Check size and weight.

## Care and Longevity

Never run the mixer dry, and let the motor rest between long grinding sessions. Wash the jars and lids by hand, dry them well, and check the rubber gaskets for wear. Keep the motor base clear of water, and service the machine if you hear grinding or smell burning.

## Jars, Blades and What You Actually Use

Most Indian cooks use three jars regularly: a large wet-grinding jar for batter, a dry jar for masalas and a small chutney jar. Extra jars, such as a juicer or a blender jar, are convenient but often sit unused. Check the jar capacities and the blade design on the listing, and make sure spare jars and blades are sold separately, since they are the parts most likely to need replacing.
