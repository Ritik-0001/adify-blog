## How to Choose for Municipal Water

Test your TDS. A TDS meter gives a quick reading, and many municipal supplies are low enough that UV and UF suffice, but not always, so also consider local advice.

Think about microbial safety. UV and UF handle bacteria and viruses, and RO adds dissolved salt removal if needed.

Check running costs. UV and UF models usually have lower service costs and no reject water, while RO models use more water.

## Keeping Water Safe

Clean overhead tanks every few months, change filters on schedule and use treated water fresh. Test TDS now and then, especially after the monsoon.

## Seasonal Water Changes

Municipal water quality often changes after heavy rain, when turbidity and microbial loads can rise, and in summer when supply is limited. Having a UV or UF purifier with a good pre-filter gives some protection through these shifts. Boil water during unusual events such as pipe repairs or floods, and clean storage tanks before and after the monsoon.

## Who Should Skip This

If your tap reading comes back high, or your supply is a borewell or tanker with hard water, a UV plus UF purifier will not fix the dissolved salts, and you should look at RO instead. Skip RO if the TDS is already low and you do not want reject water. People who lose power often should also check that a UV unit is not their only line of defence.

## Recommendation

For most low TDS municipal supplies, a UV plus UF purifier is a sound choice, and an RO plus UV purifier with TDS control suits variable supplies.
