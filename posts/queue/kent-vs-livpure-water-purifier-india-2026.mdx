## Purification technology

Both brands sell RO plus UV plus UF combination purifiers, which handle dissolved salts, bacteria and viruses. Kent's selling point is its mineral-retaining RO and a TDS controller that blends a measured amount of raw water back in so that water does not taste flat. Livpure sells a similar multi-stage lineup, often highlighting extra stages and larger storage tanks in its mid-range.

On paper the stages overlap heavily. What differs is the membrane quality, pre-filter design and how well the manufacturer's service team replaces parts. Look at the recovery rate in the listing: a higher percentage means less reject water, which matters in water-stressed cities.

Where your tap TDS is already low, around 200 or under, skip RO from either brand: a [UV plus UF purifier](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=uv+uf+water+purifier&tag=adifystore-21) keeps the minerals and wastes no water. The entry-level versions sit below this site's price floor, so consider them only as the cheaper answer for soft water.

## Service and annual cost

This is where Kent usually pulls ahead. Kent has a long-established direct service network in many cities, and technician visits for filter and membrane changes are generally easier to schedule. Livpure's service coverage is patchier outside large cities, so check whether your pin code is covered before ordering.

Plan for annual maintenance regardless of brand. A yearly service contract with filter changes typically costs a few thousand rupees, and the RO membrane lasts roughly 2-3 years depending on water. Skipping service is the most common reason a purifier tastes bad within a year.

If you can, ask the dealer which one has a registered local technician, since a good local technician matters more than any spec sheet.

## Taste, storage and daily use

Storage capacity usually ranges from 7 to 10 litres in family models. A larger tank is helpful in homes with erratic water pressure because the purifier fills when it can. Kent's blended TDS approach tends to give a more natural taste, though the actual result depends on settings and source water. Livpure units usually offer comparable taste when set properly.

Noise comes from the booster pump and the solenoid valve. Both brands produce a pump hum while filling, so mount the unit away from a bedroom wall.

## Which one wins in different homes

- Hard borewell water, large family, wants dependable servicing: Kent.
- Budget-conscious buyer in a metro with confirmed Livpure service: Livpure.
- Rental home: neither is ideal if you cannot drill, so see a [wall-mount RO with a stand option](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=ro+water+purifier+wall+mount+stand&tag=adifystore-21) or a no-installation model instead.
- Low-TDS water: skip RO from both and choose a UV plus UF model.

## What a service plan should include

Ask what the annual plan covers: sediment pre-filter, carbon filter, UV lamp, RO membrane and a pump check. Some plans cover labour only and charge separately for the membrane, which is the expensive part. Confirm what is included before assuming the plan is complete.

Ask about warranty on the RO membrane and pump separately, because the main warranty period is often shorter on consumable items.
