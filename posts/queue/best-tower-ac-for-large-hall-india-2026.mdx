## How to Choose a Tower AC for a Hall

Start with the room size and layout. A hall of 300 to 450 square feet may need 2 to 3 ton of cooling, depending on sun exposure, height and the number of people.

Check airflow and placement. A tower AC throws air higher and farther, so place it where air can circulate and avoid obstructions.

Plan installation and power. Tower units need space, a drain and a strong electrical supply, so involve an installer and an electrician.

## Splits Versus a Single Tower

Two smaller splits cost about the same as one tower and give redundancy, since one keeps working if the other fails. A tower suits spaces where wall mounting is not possible, while splits are often neater and quieter.

## Electricity and Power Planning

Large ACs draw significant current, so check the rated power of the unit and the capacity of your wiring and MCB. A dedicated line is usually advised, and a voltage stabiliser may be needed if your area has fluctuations. Use an electrician to check the load before ordering.

## Service Cover for Big Units

Ask about warranty on the compressor and the whole unit, and whether the brand offers an annual maintenance contract for large systems. For commercial or large-hall use, how fast a technician can reach you in peak summer matters more than the headline brand name.

## Who Should Skip This

If your hall is under about 250 square feet, a single tower is overkill, and a well-sized 1.5 or 2 ton inverter split will cool it more quietly and cheaply. Skip a tower too if the only free floor space sits in a walkway or behind furniture, since a floor-standing unit with blocked airflow cools badly.

## Recommendation

For most large halls, compare a 2 ton tower AC with two inverter splits, and check current availability. Confirm the stock status, electrical load and installation terms on the exact model before ordering.
