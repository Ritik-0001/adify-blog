## How it differs from a stovetop cooker

A stovetop pressure cooker counts whistles; an electric one runs a timed program at a set pressure and then releases. You press a button, walk away, and the cooker holds temperature and shuts off. The learning curve is understanding natural versus quick release and how long it takes to come up to pressure, which is typically 10 to 15 minutes before cooking begins.

For Indian cooking, the time saved is small, but attention saved is large: no standing by the stove for whistles, and no risk of burning the base.

## Sizes

- 3 litre: one or two people, small batches
- 5 to 6 litre: a family of three to five, the most useful size
- 8 litre: joint families, big batches for guests

An electric cooker can only be filled to about two-thirds, or half for foamy foods like dal, so a 6 litre model gives around 4 litres of usable cooking volume. For a large Indian family, a stovetop cooker remains handy.

## Functions that matter

### 1. Pressure cook with high and low settings

High suits dal, rajma, chole and meat. Low suits delicate foods like fish or vegetables.

### 2. Rice and porridge

Many models include a rice program. Check whether it handles basmati and short-grain varieties well.

### 3. Saute

A saute mode lets you do tadka or onion browning in the same pot before pressure cooking, reducing washing.

### 4. Slow cook, steam and yogurt

These are bonuses. A yogurt function is useful for dahi, while steam suits idli and dhokla with the right insert. Search [electric cooker with yogurt function](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=electric+cooker+with+yogurt+function&tag=adifystore-21).

## Indian cooking realities

Dal and legumes cook well, and tough cuts of mutton soften quickly. Biryani works in one pot, though texture differs from dum cooking. Frying is limited, so papad and puris still need a pan. Foamy foods like dal can clog the vent, so use less water and add a drop of oil.

Heavy tempering and strong spices can leave smell in the silicone sealing ring. Keep a spare ring for sweet dishes, or consider [silicone sealing ring for electric pressure cooker](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=silicone+sealing+ring+electric+pressure+cooker&tag=adifystore-21).

## Safety features

Look for lid-lock, overpressure protection, overheat protection and a pressure-release valve that is easy to operate. Electric cookers have multiple safeguards, but you should never force the lid, and always clear the vent of food residue. Do not leave it unattended during the first few uses until you understand its behaviour.

## Inner pot

Stainless steel pots are durable and non-reactive, while non-stick pots are easier to clean but can scratch. A stainless inner pot is preferable for tomato-heavy Indian gravies. ## Power use

Most electric cookers draw roughly 1,000 watts while heating, and cut back when they hold pressure. Cooking time is short, so energy use per meal is usually modest, especially compared with long-simmered dishes on a gas stove.

## Who should skip an electric cooker

- People who like the speed and sound of a stovetop cooker and cook large batches
- Households with limited counter space
- Cooks who dislike a learning curve
- Anyone who rarely cooks dal or rice at home
