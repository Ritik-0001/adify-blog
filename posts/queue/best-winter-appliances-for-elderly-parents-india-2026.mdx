## Why winter is harder for older people

Seniors lose body heat faster, take longer to warm up and often have slower reflexes. Cold bathrooms raise blood-pressure spikes, a known risk during winter mornings. Burns from scalding water or a touched heater are more serious in thin skin, and smoky or polluted air aggravates asthma and breathing problems. Appliances for them should favour safety and simplicity over power.

## 1. Storage geyser: the first priority

A storage geyser keeps water hot in an insulated tank, avoiding the cold shock of an instant geyser that struggles in winter. Look for a 4 or 5-star rating, a thermostat with a clear temperature display or a selected maximum, a multi-function safety valve, glass-lined or stainless steel tank and ELCB-style or earth leakage protection if offered. Set the thermostat to around 45 to 50 degrees to prevent scalds. Brands such as Racold and AO Smith offer these. Browse [AO Smith storage geyser](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=AO+Smith+storage+geyser+25+litre&tag=adifystore-21).

## 2. Oil-filled radiator: the safest room heater

An oil-filled heater has no glowing element, no fan noise and a surface that warms gradually. Choose a model with a thermostat, a tip-over switch and an overheat cut-off. A timer is valuable so it switches off after the parents sleep. Premium models, roughly ₹15,000 to ₹25,000, add a child lock that also prevents accidental changes. Avoid halogen and exposed rod heaters; a sari or blanket can catch fire. Compare [oil-filled radiator with timer](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=oil+filled+radiator+timer+thermostat&tag=adifystore-21).

## 3. Air purifier: the overlooked winter essential

In many northern cities winter brings dense smog, and indoor air can be harmful for older lungs. A HEPA air purifier suited to the room size helps, with a CADR (clean air delivery rate) matched to the room, a filter-change indicator and quiet night operation. Models from Philips, Honeywell, Coway and Dyson range from roughly ₹15,000 to ₹40,000. Replacement filters cost ₹2,500 to ₹6,000 yearly, so check availability. ## 5. Heated bedding and comfort extras

Electric blankets and heated mattress pads are cheap but need cut-off safety. Choose an ISI-marked blanket with automatic shut-off and never fold it while on. Warm bedding allows lower room heater settings, which saves money and reduces dry air. A [heated electric blanket with auto shut-off](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=electric+blanket+auto+shut+off+double+bed&tag=adifystore-21) and a [non-slip bathroom mat](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=anti+slip+bathroom+mat+elderly&tag=adifystore-21) matter more for safety than a more powerful heater.

## Placement and wiring: where accidents happen

A heater beside a bed, near curtains or in a corridor is a hazard. Keep it at least a metre from fabrics and out of walking paths. Use a dedicated 16-ampere socket, never a loose extension board, and have a qualified electrician check wiring for geyser and heater loads. Install an MCB or RCCB on geyser lines. A [bathroom exhaust fan](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=bathroom+exhaust+fan+6+inch&tag=adifystore-21) helps with steam. Mark switches in large print.

## Simple routines that reduce risk

Warm the bathroom before your parents enter by running the geyser for 15 minutes beforehand and letting hot water run. Place a stool and a grab bar in the shower. Switch off heaters at bedtime unless they have a timer and thermostat. Keep windows slightly open when using any gas appliances, and avoid sigri or angithi indoors altogether because carbon monoxide kills silently every winter.

## Who should skip some of these

Skip an air purifier if your parents live in a low-pollution area. Skip a hot-cold AC if winters last only two weeks. Skip any heater without tip-over and overheat protection regardless of price.
