## 6. TDS Control vs Alkaline

TDS control keeps a controlled level of minerals in RO water, while alkaline cartridges target pH. Many families are best served by good RO plus UV with sensible TDS control, with alkaline as optional.

Marketing often blurs these two features, but they do different jobs. TDS control manages dissolved salt levels, and an alkaline stage only adjusts pH.

## How to Choose an Alkaline Purifier

Start with water safety. Choose RO plus UV or UF based on your water source and TDS, since a purifier that does not treat your water properly is not worth the extras.

Check the claim. Look for the stated pH range, the mineral cartridge life and replacement cost, and be cautious about large health claims.

Look at service and cost. Mineral cartridges add running costs, so check replacement prices and availability.

## Getting the Most From Your Purifier

Service filters on time, keep the tank clean and use the water fresh. Test your TDS now and then to check that the purifier is working as expected.

## Reading Marketing Claims Carefully

Terms such as alkaline, mineral and ionised are used loosely in marketing. Look for a stated pH range, the specific minerals added and an independent test or certification if one is mentioned. Avoid sellers who promise medical benefits. If a feature is not clearly explained in the specifications, treat it as a marketing line rather than a reason to pay more.

## Who Should Skip This

If your water is already low in TDS or you are on a tight budget, a mineral or alkaline stage mostly adds a recurring cartridge cost without changing whether the water is safe. Anyone with a medical condition should take advice from a doctor and not choose a purifier on health claims. A dependable RO plus UV unit with sensible TDS control covers the core need for most households.

## Recommendation

For most families, a reliable RO plus UV purifier with TDS control from AO Smith, Kent or Aquaguard is the priority, and an alkaline or mineral cartridge is an optional extra.
