## How to Choose a Chair With Footrest

Check the footrest design. A smooth, sturdy retractable footrest that locks in place is better than a flimsy one, and it should support the whole foot or calf.

Look at recline and lumbar support. A deep recline with a locking mechanism and adjustable lumbar support protects your back when you lean back.

Think about how you will use it. A footrest is best for short breaks, calls and reading, and it should not encourage sitting in one position for hours.

## Using a Footrest Properly

Recline gently, support your lower back and keep your knees slightly bent. Switch between upright and reclined positions through the day, and stand and stretch every hour.

## Trying Before Buying and Return Policy

Chairs feel very different from person to person, so check the return window and, if possible, try a similar model in a store. Look for sellers with a clear replacement policy for damaged parts, because gas lifts and footrest mechanisms are common failure points. Keep the box until you are sure the chair suits you.

## Who Should Skip This

If you work at a desk eight hours a day and rarely lean back, you may not use the footrest at all, and the money would be better spent on a chair with stronger lumbar and seat adjustment. A footrest also adds bulk, so skip it if your workspace is cramped or if you often tuck the chair under a low desk. Anyone with a back condition should ask a physiotherapist before relying on a reclined posture.
