## What the standard installation includes

On Amazon India, installation for a new split AC is handled by the brand or its authorised partner, not the seller. The job usually covers mounting the indoor unit on a wall plate, placing the outdoor unit on a bracket or ledge, connecting a short length of copper pipe, joining the drain hose, doing basic power connection, vacuuming the line and a test run.

Most brands cover a short run of copper pipe, typically around 3 meters, a short drain pipe and basic electrical connection in the base installation. Everything beyond that is chargeable. The exact free length and what counts as "standard" differs by brand, so read the installation terms on the listing and keep the confirmation message you receive.

## Typical extra charges to expect

These are rough ranges that vary by city and technician, so treat them as a negotiation guide.

- **Extra copper piping:** beyond the free length, around Rs 600-1,000 per foot is common because copper is expensive. A hall AC with a 10-foot extra run can add Rs 6,000 or more.
- **Outdoor unit stand or bracket:** Rs 800-2,500 depending on the size and metal. A 1.5 ton unit needs a sturdier stand than a 1 ton.
- **Drain pipe extension:** roughly Rs 50-100 per foot. A flat with the unit far from a drain point can cost a few hundred rupees extra.
- **Wiring and MCB:** a dedicated power line from the meter or a new MCB can cost Rs 500-2,500 depending on distance. Some societies require a separate meter or approved electrician.
- **Core cutting or wall drilling:** if there is no existing hole, expect an added fee, with a bigger charge for reinforced concrete.
- **High-floor or crane work:** a flat on a high floor with no ledge may need rope work or extra staff, adding Rs 1,000-3,000.
- **Gas charging:** needed only if the unit loses gas or the piping run is long. Typically Rs 2,000-4,000 per top-up, and a long piping run may need extra.

## Copper piping: the biggest swing factor

Most overcharging happens here. The pipe is sold by the foot and the technician measures it at the end. Some points to remember:

- Ask for pipe with proper insulation, not thin foam that will sweat and drip.
- Request that the measurement is shown to you, and that the unused length is not billed.
- Aluminium piping is cheaper but not recommended for inverter splits, and most brands' warranties expect copper.
- A shorter route is cheaper and cools better, so choose the wall that keeps the outdoor unit close. See our [1.5 ton inverter AC](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=1.5+ton+inverter+ac+installation&tag=adifystore-21) listings and note the free-pipe length before you decide where to mount the indoor unit.

## Stand, drain and wiring: the small items that add up

An outdoor stand should be galvanised or powder-coated, not bare iron that rusts in monsoon, and the unit should not hang from loose bolts. In ground-floor houses, a cement platform is often a better answer than a stand. A split AC's drain needs a downward slope; flat or upward drain runs lead to leaking in the first rains. Insist on a sloping route and a drain that discharges away from walls and neighbours' balconies.

Electrical work is where safety matters most. A 1.5 ton AC draws roughly 7-9 amps and a 2 ton draws more. Use a proper AC power plug and a 16 amp MCB, with correct wire gauge from the board. If the technician suggests using an old shared socket, refuse and pay for a proper line.

## How to avoid overpaying

1. **Book through the brand's installation service** from your order, instead of calling a random local technician for the first job. The free service has defined terms.
2. **Get a written quote** with each extra line and rate before work starts. Photograph the final pipe length.
3. **Ask the brand what is "free"** by calling the toll-free number or checking the installation terms in the listing.
4. **Do not pay for a "demo" or "commissioning" fee** if the brand states it is included.
5. **Pay after the AC runs**, not before. Run it for 15 minutes, check cooling at the vent, look for water at the drain and listen for rattling.

Be wary of a technician who tells you the free installation "does not apply to this model" or insists on a higher-priced stand and pipe kit. Ask for the brand's printed rate card and decline anything not on it.

## When to hire a private installer

A private technician can help for relocation, refilling or when a society has strict rules, but be ready for variable quality. Check reviews for the individual, ask whether they use vacuum pumps and nitrogen flushing, and avoid people who just "blow out" the line with refrigerant. Poor installation causes gas leaks and compressor failure, and often voids the warranty.
