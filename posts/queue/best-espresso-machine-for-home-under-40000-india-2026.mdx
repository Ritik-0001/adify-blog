## How to Choose an Espresso Machine

Decide how much involvement you want. Semi-automatic machines require grinding, tamping and pulling shots yourself, while capsule or automatic machines require less skill.

Invest in a grinder. Fresh, consistent grinding matters more than the machine for flavour. A burr grinder is worth budgeting for.

Look at the steam wand. If you want lattes, a real steam wand lets you texture milk. Practice takes time.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Skipping the grinder.** Pre-ground coffee gives weaker shots.

**Using tap water without care.** Hard water causes scale.

**Not purging.** Purge before and after steaming.

**Ignoring descaling.** Scale shortens machine life.

**Is a 15 bar pump necessary?** It is standard, but pressure is regulated by design.

**Can I use pods?** Only if the machine supports them.

**How long do they last?** Five to ten years with descaling.

## Learning Curve and Basics

Espresso is a skill. A typical shot uses about 18 grams of ground coffee to produce roughly 36 grams of liquid in 25 to 30 seconds. If the shot runs too fast, grind finer; if it chokes, grind coarser. Weigh your coffee and time your shots with a simple scale and a timer for consistent results.

Tamp evenly, keep the portafilter clean and purge the group head before brewing. For milk drinks, start with cold milk in a cold jug, and stop steaming when the jug becomes too hot to hold comfortably.

## Ongoing Costs

Budget for a grinder, a tamper, a milk jug, descaler and beans. A decent grinder can cost as much as the machine, but it improves results the most. Check that spare parts such as gaskets and portafilters are available in India.
