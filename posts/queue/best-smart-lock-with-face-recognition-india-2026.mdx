## How face unlock behaves at a real door

Indoors in controlled light, face unlock feels magical: you walk up and the bolt clicks. At an Indian front door, conditions are less friendly. Strong afternoon sun behind you, a dark corridor, a dupatta or helmet, or a face covered with a mask can all trip it up.

Good locks use infrared or 3D structured light, so they work in the dark and resist photo spoofing. Locks that rely on a plain 2D camera can be fooled by a printed photo and perform badly at night.

## What to look for in the sensor

Ask for specifics: 3D structured light, infrared illumination, liveness detection (blink or depth checks), and the number of faces it can store. Families usually need 10 to 50 faces, including relatives and house-help.

Check recognition time as well; a good lock opens in around a second or two. Slower ones frustrate you at the door with shopping bags.

## Privacy: where your face data lives

A face lock stores a mathematical template of your face, not necessarily a photo. Ask whether data is stored on the lock itself or uploaded to a cloud. Local storage is safer. Some models send entry snapshots to an app, which may be useful for security but raises privacy questions.

If you use house-help or tenants, think about consent and deletion: you should be able to remove a person's face data when they leave. Choose a lock that lets you delete profiles from the lock and the app.

## Fallback methods are not optional

Face unlock fails sometimes: after an injury, in harsh light, or if the battery is low. Make sure you have at least three other ways in: fingerprint, PIN code, and a mechanical key. Look for an external emergency power port, such as USB-C or a 9V terminal.

Battery life: face recognition drains batteries faster than a basic lock, typically giving a few months. Keep spare batteries or a rechargeable pack.

## Installation and door fit

Face locks are heavier and need a door cut-out suited to the lock body. Check door thickness and handing, and whether your main door is metal or wood. ## Who should skip face recognition

Skip it if you rent, because it is not removable, if your entrance faces strong sunlight, or if you worry about biometric data. A fingerprint lock is cheaper, more mature, and usually faster.
