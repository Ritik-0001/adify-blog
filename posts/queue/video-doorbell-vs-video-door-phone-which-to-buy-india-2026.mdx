## 1. Video Door Phone Kits

Roughly ₹15,000–40,000. A wired door phone has an outdoor camera unit and an indoor monitor, often with night vision, two-way talk and a door-release option. It keeps working without Wi-Fi, but you pay with wiring and installation effort.

## 2. Wi-Fi Video Doorbells

Roughly ₹15,000–20,000 for premium models. A Wi-Fi doorbell sends alerts to your phone when someone rings or moves in front of the camera, and records clips to a card or cloud. It retrofits easily, though it depends on Wi-Fi and, on some models, a subscription.

Roughly ₹18,000–45,000. Some door phone systems add Wi-Fi so you can see visitors on your phone as well as the indoor monitor. You get both views, at a higher price and with more setup.

## 4. Power and Wiring

Door phones usually need low-voltage cabling between the outdoor and indoor units, while doorbells may run on batteries or on existing bell wiring. Wiring in a finished flat is harder than in a new build, so plan the cable route before you pick a type.

## 5. Storage and Privacy

Door phones often record to a card in the monitor, while doorbells record to a card or cloud. Check where footage is stored, who can see it and what subscription is needed, since cloud storage may cost extra.

In apartments, a door phone may connect with the society's intercom or be easier to fit inside the door frame, while some societies restrict external cameras. Check the rules first to avoid a dispute after you have paid.

## How to Choose Between Them

Start with your home. In a new build or during renovation, a wired door phone is neat and dependable, and in an existing home, a doorbell is quicker to fit.

Think about how you want to see visitors. If you want an indoor screen for everyone at home, choose a door phone, and if you want alerts when you are away, choose a doorbell or a hybrid.

Compare running costs. Door phones have no subscription, and some doorbells have optional cloud plans.

## Picture Quality and Night Performance

Look for at least 1080p video, a wide field of view of around 120 degrees or more and infrared or colour night vision. Backlight from bright corridors can wash out faces, so features like wide dynamic range help. Check sample footage in reviews, because spec sheets rarely show how a camera handles glare and shadows at the actual door.

## Fitting and Servicing

Ask a technician to check the cable route and mount the camera at about 1.4 metres height. Use a weatherproof enclosure outdoors and test the picture at night. Compare the warranty and the service reach, especially for wired systems that need on-site help, and keep the invoice and the installation record.

## Who Should Skip This

If your door already has a peephole and you are rarely away, neither device may be worth the money, since the extra security is modest. Renters who cannot drill or run cable should skip a wired door phone and go for a battery doorbell, or skip both and ask the landlord about shared building intercom.

## Two Devices for Different Moments

A door phone answers who is at the door when you are home, and a doorbell answers it when you are out. Some households run both: a wired monitor for elders in the living room and a doorbell camera for parcels during office hours. If you can only afford one, pick the one that matches the hours when most visitors arrive.

## Recommendation

For new homes, a wired door phone is the dependable choice, and for retrofit and phone alerts, a Wi-Fi doorbell is easier. Compare specifications and installation terms on each listing.
