For Indian cooking, choose a kitchen chimney with suction of about 800 cubic metres per hour for light cooking, 1,000 to 1,200 for regular cooking with tadka and frying, and 1,200 to 1,500 or more for heavy frying, big hobs and long ducts. Chimneys that meet these figures with good build roughly cost ₹10,000 to ₹35,000, with premium models going higher.

This guide is researched from published specifications and listings rather than hands-on testing. The suction number on a box is a best-case maximum, so what you actually get falls with each metre of duct, each elbow and the size of your kitchen, which is why this page sorts chimneys by cooking style and not by brand.

## How to Choose the Right Suction

Start with your cooking style. Frying, grilling and heavy tadka need more suction than boiling and steaming.

Consider the duct. Every metre of duct and every bend cuts effective suction, so a long run calls for a stronger motor, and short, straight ducts are best.

Match the width. A 90 cm chimney captures more than a 60 cm one over a 4-burner hob.

## Matching Suction to Hob Size

A two-burner hob in a small kitchen is well served by 800 to 1,000 cubic metres per hour. Three-burner and four-burner hobs call for 1,000 to 1,400, and wide hobs, wok burners or island layouts push you towards 1,400 to 1,500 or more. Remember that a duct with several bends can cut effective suction substantially.

## Noise and Suction

Higher suction usually brings higher noise, so check the decibel claim and look for models with insulated motors. Using a lower speed for light cooking keeps noise down while still removing smoke.

## Reading the Spec Sheet

Look for the stated maximum suction, then the noise level at full speed and whether the figure is measured with a duct. Check the motor warranty, the filter type and whether the chimney has speed settings, so that you can run it quietly for everyday cooking and raise it only for heavy frying.

## Who Should Skip This

If you cook mostly boiled or steamed meals, chasing a 1,500 rating buys you noise and a bigger bill with no benefit. Likewise, if your flat has a vent shaft that forces a very long, bent duct, no suction figure on a box will fix that, and fixing the duct route first is a better use of money.

## Recommendation

For most Indian kitchens, a chimney rated at 1,200 cubic metres per hour is a sound target. Go higher for long ducts or heavy frying, and work out your duct route before you settle on a model.
