## How to Choose a 90 cm Chimney

Match suction to your cooking. Heavy Indian cooking with tadka and frying calls for around 1,200 cubic metres per hour or more, especially with a long duct or many bends.

Pick the filter type. Baffle filters and filterless designs handle oil better than mesh filters, and auto-clean reduces the need for manual cleaning.

Match the width to the hob. A 90 cm chimney suits a 4-burner hob and gives better capture than a 60 cm model.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Choosing on suction alone.** Duct length and bends reduce actual suction.

**Ignoring noise.** Check the decibel claim.

**Skipping installation planning.** Plan the duct route.

**Mounting too high.** Follow the recommended height above the hob.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A kitchen with daily frying.** An auto-clean or filterless 90 cm chimney.

**A large modular kitchen.** A premium, quiet model.

**A rented flat.** A moderately priced model with a simple install.

## What to Check on the Listing

Confirm the suction, the filter type, the auto-clean feature, the noise claim, the duct size, the warranty and the installation terms. See whether the motor warranty is long.

## Installation and Duct Planning

A short, straight duct with as few bends as possible keeps suction high. Mount the chimney at the height the maker recommends above the hob, usually around 65 to 75 cm, and get an installer to confirm the duct route before you buy.

## Quick FAQ

**Is 90 cm better than 60 cm?** For 4-burner hobs, yes.

## Auto-Clean vs Manual Clean

Auto-clean chimneys heat the collected oil and drain it into a cup, which cuts down on scrubbing, though you still need to wipe the hood and empty the cup. Manual-clean models rely on baffle or mesh filters that you remove and wash. For heavy Indian cooking, auto-clean saves time, and many people consider it worth the extra cost.

## Sizing and Placement

A 90 cm chimney suits hobs of 75 cm or wider and four-burner layouts. Mount the hood at the height the maker recommends, ensure the duct is straight and as short as possible and avoid strong cross-breezes that pull smoke away from the hood. A good installer will test suction after fitting.

## Final Checklist Before You Buy

Before you order, measure the counter cutout or wall space and compare it with the dimensions on the listing, ask your carpenter and electrician to confirm clearances, power points and gas or water connections, and check who handles installation. Read the warranty terms on motors and electronics, keep the invoice and manual, and confirm that spare parts and service exist in your city.
