## How to Choose a 90 cm Chimney

Match suction to your cooking. Heavy Indian cooking with tadka and frying calls for around 1,200 cubic metres per hour or more, especially with a long duct or many bends.

Pick the filter type. Baffle filters and filterless designs handle oil better than mesh filters, and auto-clean reduces the need for manual cleaning.

Match the width to the hob. A 90 cm chimney suits a 4-burner hob and gives better capture than a 60 cm model.

## Installation and Duct Planning

A short, straight duct with as few bends as possible keeps suction high. Mount the chimney at the height the maker recommends above the hob, usually around 65 to 75 cm, and get an installer to confirm the duct route before you buy.

## Auto-Clean vs Manual Clean

Auto-clean chimneys heat the collected oil and drain it into a cup, which cuts down on scrubbing, though you still need to wipe the hood and empty the cup. Manual-clean models rely on baffle or mesh filters that you remove and wash. For heavy Indian cooking, auto-clean saves time, and many people consider it worth the extra cost.

## Sizing and Placement

A 90 cm chimney suits hobs of 75 cm or wider and four-burner layouts. Avoid strong cross-breezes that pull smoke away from the hood, and ask the installer to test suction after fitting.

## Who Should Skip This

If your kitchen is compact, your hob is 60 cm or narrower, or you mostly boil and steam, a 90 cm hood is wasted money and wall space, and a good 60 cm model covers you. Also pause if your balcony or wall cannot take a long duct, because a weak exhaust path undoes the benefit of the bigger motor.

## Recommendation

For most large Indian kitchens, an auto-clean 90 cm chimney from Elica or Faber with strong suction is a safe choice. Confirm suction, filter type and motor warranty on the exact model before ordering.
