## Who really needs a deep freezer

A deep freezer is a separate appliance for long-term frozen storage at around minus 18 to minus 24 degrees, deeper and more stable than the freezer compartment of a refrigerator. It makes sense if you:

- Buy meat, fish or seafood in bulk.
- Run a home kitchen or catering side business.
- Freeze seasonal produce such as peas, mangoes or curry bases.
- Live in a joint family that makes large batches of food.
- Live in a town where fresh supply is irregular.

If your frozen needs are a tray of ice, a few packets of peas and ice cream, the freezer in a modern double-door fridge is enough, and a separate unit would waste power.

## Sizes in litres

- **100-150 litres:** a small chest freezer for a couple or a small family, roughly Rs 15,000-22,000. It fits a utility corner.
- **200-300 litres:** the family size, holding a month or two of bulk food, roughly Rs 20,000-38,000.
- **400-500 litres:** for large families, home-based food businesses and heavy bulk buyers, roughly Rs 35,000-60,000. It needs real floor space.

A useful rule is around 25-30 litres per person for a household that stocks a month ahead. Remember that a chest freezer's depth makes it hard to reach the bottom, so choose baskets and dividers.

## Types

**Chest (hard top) freezers** have a top lid. Cold air stays inside when you open the lid, so they hold temperature better and last longer in power cuts. They use less energy than upright freezers of the same size, but cost floor space.

**Convertible models** let you set the temperature to run as a freezer or a fridge, which can be handy for festive seasons, weddings or when you need to chill a lot of beverages. Some have a deep freeze setting and a mild setting.

**Glass-top freezers** are usually commercial, for shops, and use more power because of the glass. They are not suited for most homes.

## Power use and power cuts

A 300 litre chest freezer typically uses around 1-2 units a day depending on the model, ambient temperature and how often you open the lid. At Rs 7-9 per unit, that is roughly Rs 250-500 a month. Opening the lid often, a hot room and a full-of-warm-items load raise that.

In a power cut, a full chest freezer with the lid closed holds safe temperature for many hours, often most of a day, because cold air does not spill. A half-empty freezer warms faster, so fill spaces with water bottles or ice packs. Some models advertise a long cooling retention period, which is worth checking on the listing.

## Placement and setup

Place the chest freezer on a level floor, with at least 10 cm of gap at the back and sides for air to flow. Keep it away from direct sun and cooking heat. Use a dedicated socket with a proper earth, and use a good voltage stabiliser if your supply swings. Do not use thin extension cords.

Let the unit stand upright for several hours after delivery before switching on, so the compressor oil settles. Defrost it once or twice a year; a manual defrost chest freezer builds up frost, so choose a model with a drain plug.

## Practical tips

- Use baskets and label packs to find food quickly.
- Pack in flat bags to freeze faster and stack neatly.
- Do not refreeze thawed meat.
- Keep the lid closed and the gasket clean.
