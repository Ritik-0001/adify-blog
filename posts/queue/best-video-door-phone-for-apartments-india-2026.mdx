## How to Choose a Video Door Phone

Check the monitor and camera. A 7 inch monitor is easy to see, and a wide-angle camera with night vision helps in dim corridors.

Look at door release and extras. A door release lets you unlock an electric strike from inside, and picture memory records visitors when you are away.

Plan wiring. Door phones need cables between the outdoor camera and the indoor monitor, so ask the installer about conduit routes, especially in finished flats.

## Placement, Society Approval and Second Monitors

Ask the installer to set the camera at about 1.4 metres, avoid strong light behind the visitor and test night vision. Some societies need approval before anything is fixed outside the flat, so ask the committee first. If the flat has two entrances or a large living space, check whether the kit supports a second monitor, and keep the main monitor within reach of the living area.

## Power Cuts and Reliability

Wired door phones usually run on low-voltage power from a small adapter, so they stop working during power cuts unless the supply is backed up. A small UPS or an inverter-backed socket keeps the system alive, and many people place the adapter on the same backup circuit as their router. Ask the installer to plan this, since a door phone that fails during an outage defeats its purpose.

## Who Should Skip This

If your building already has a society-managed intercom with video, or your flat has a short entrance passage and a good peephole, a separate kit may add little. Skip it as well if the walls are finished and the installer cannot hide a cable, because surface wiring along a clean wall can look worse than the problem it solves.

## Matching Monitor Size to the Flat

A 7 inch screen is comfortable on a living room wall, but an elderly family member may find a smaller bedroom monitor hard to read. Decide where people will actually answer the door, then choose the monitor size and mounting point for that spot. A monitor that sits behind a sofa or beside a bright window will be used far less.
