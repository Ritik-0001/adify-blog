## What actually matters for drawing

Three things shape a drawing tablet: pen latency (how quickly the line follows your hand), the screen surface (how the nib feels) and the software. Apple Pencil latency is among the lowest in tablets, and Samsung's S Pen is close behind. Software is the bigger divider: Procreate is iPad-only and loved for its brushes and speed, while Samsung tablets run Clip Studio Paint, Infinite Painter, Autodesk Sketchbook and Krita, good for comics and manga work.

## 1. iPad Air: best overall for illustration

The iPad Air with an M-series chip, roughly ₹60,000 to ₹80,000 depending on screen size, gives a laminated display, tight pen response and full Procreate with plenty of headroom. Add an Apple Pencil Pro or the Pencil USB-C, roughly ₹8,000 to ₹12,000. The trade-off is a glossy screen, so a matte film helps, and 128 GB or 256 GB fills quickly with large canvases, so budget for 256 GB. See [iPad Air](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=iPad+Air+M3+256GB&tag=adifystore-21).

## 2. iPad (standard): best entry into Procreate

The base iPad, roughly ₹34,000 to ₹45,000, supports the Apple Pencil USB-C and runs Procreate well. Its display is not laminated, so there is a visible gap between nib and image, and colours are less rich. For a student starting out, it is fine; for a professional, the Air is worth the jump. Compare [iPad 10th generation](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=iPad+10th+generation+64GB&tag=adifystore-21).

## 3. Samsung Galaxy Tab S9 FE and S10 series: best with pen included

Samsung includes the S Pen in the box, which is a real saving of ₹8,000 or more. The Tab S9 FE, roughly ₹30,000 to ₹40,000, is a value pick, and the S10+ and Ultra with AMOLED screens, roughly ₹70,000 to ₹1,10,000, suit larger canvases. The S Pen attaches magnetically and needs no charging. Weaknesses: fewer professional-grade painting apps than iPad and lighter brush engines. See [Samsung Galaxy Tab S](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=Samsung+Galaxy+Tab+S9+FE+S+Pen&tag=adifystore-21).

## 4. iPad Pro: only for specific needs

The iPad Pro costs roughly ₹1,00,000 and up and adds a 120 Hz OLED and a faster chip. Illustrators rarely need it unless they paint 100-layer 4K canvases or want the best colour. For most people, the Air is nearly the same drawing experience. Check [iPad Pro](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=iPad+Pro+11+inch&tag=adifystore-21) only if you also edit video.

## Screen finish: glossy glass versus paper feel

Glass is slippery, and many artists find it harder to control lines. A matte film adds friction that resembles paper, with a slight loss in sharpness and nib wear. A [paper-like screen protector](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=paperlike+screen+protector+iPad&tag=adifystore-21) costs roughly ₹1,500 to ₹3,000, and replacement [pen nibs](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=Apple+Pencil+replacement+nib&tag=adifystore-21) are cheap, but matte films wear nibs faster, so stock a spare set.

## Storage and RAM realities

A 4K canvas with 100 layers can use a gigabyte or more. Procreate's layer count depends on RAM and canvas size, so a tablet with 8 GB gives noticeably more layers than one with 4 GB. Pick 256 GB if you work on large files and back up to cloud or an external drive. A [USB-C hub](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=USB+C+hub+for+tablet+SD+card&tag=adifystore-21) helps for transferring files and adding an SD card.

## Who should skip a tablet for art

Skip tablets if you mostly produce print-grade work on desktop software like Photoshop with plugins; a screen drawing monitor or a pen display may serve you better. Skip a budget Android tablet under ₹15,000: pen lag and poor palm rejection will frustrate you. Skip cheap unbranded styluses, which lack pressure sensitivity.
