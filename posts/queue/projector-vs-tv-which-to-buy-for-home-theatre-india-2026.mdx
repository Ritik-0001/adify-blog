Buy a projector if you have a dim or curtain-controlled room and want a 100 inch or larger picture for roughly ₹40,000 to ₹1,50,000 including a screen, and buy a TV if the room gets daylight or the set doubles as the family's everyday screen. For most Indian living rooms, a 65 to 75 inch TV is the less frustrating answer.

This comparison is researched from product categories and typical pricing, not bench-tested. The thing to verify before choosing a projector is your real room: measure the throw distance and check whether you can darken it at the hours you actually watch.

## Room light decides most of it

A TV makes its own light and fights ambient light easily. A projector only reflects light off a screen, so any daylight or lamp spill on the screen turns blacks grey. A good projector in a closed room at night is a cinema; in a sunlit hall it looks washed out unless you pay for a bright laser model and an ambient-light-rejecting screen.

If you can only watch after dark, or you can fit blackout curtains, a projector is viable. If your family watches serials at 4 pm, buy the TV.

## Screen size per rupee

This is the projector's one clear win. A 100 inch TV is rare and costs several lakhs, while a 100 inch projector setup with screen can be under ₹1,00,000. A big picture changes how films feel, especially for a few people seated at 3 to 4 metres. [See 100 inch projector screens](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=100+inch+projector+screen&tag=adifystore-21).

Below roughly 80 inches the maths flips and a television gives more brightness per rupee. [See 75 inch TVs](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=75+inch+4K+TV&tag=adifystore-21).

## Picture quality

Even a mid-priced TV beats most projectors on contrast, brightness and HDR. Mini-LED and OLED TVs deliver deep blacks with highlights a projector cannot match. Projectors compensate with size and a filmic look, but few under ₹1,50,000 render HDR well. If you chase picture quality at normal sizes, a TV wins.

## Cost and upkeep

- **Projector:** lamp-based models need a lamp replacement every 3,000 to 5,000 hours at several thousand rupees. LED and laser models avoid this but cost more upfront. Dust on the lens or filter needs cleaning, and a screen, mount, HDMI cable and speakers add to the cost.
- **TV:** nothing to maintain. A wall mount and a soundbar are the usual add-ons. [Browse soundbars](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=Dolby+Atmos+soundbar&tag=adifystore-21).

## Setup and space

A projector needs a fixed shelf or ceiling mount, a cable run, and a flat wall or screen. Throw distance is a hard limit: a standard projector needs roughly 3 to 4 metres for a 100 inch picture, while short throw models need under 2.5 metres. A TV only needs a stand or wall. [Browse short throw projectors](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=short+throw+projector&tag=adifystore-21).

## Sound

Neither has useful built-in sound for a home theatre. Projectors are worse, with tiny speakers. Budget for a soundbar or a 5.1 package, especially for a projector.

## Eye comfort and daily use

Projected light is reflected rather than emitted, which many find gentler for long viewing. On the other hand, a projector requires a switch-on routine, has fan noise and takes time to warm up. A TV turns on instantly, supports casual viewing and works for gaming without fuss. If you or your family prefer instant access, that routine matters more than specs.

## Who should skip each

- **Skip a projector** if your room is bright by day, if your wall is textured or coloured, or if ceiling height and distance do not suit a mount.
- **Skip a TV** if you want truly huge pictures above 100 inches for a modest budget and you can control light.

## Recommendation

Buy a 65 to 75 inch Mini-LED or OLED TV when the room is shared and bright; it is simpler and delivers better picture quality. Buy a 4K projector plus a 100 inch screen when you have a dedicated dark room and the throw distance fits. If you cannot decide, remember that a TV is easy to live with every day, while a projector is a special-occasion cinema.
