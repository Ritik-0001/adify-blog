## The three categories in plain terms

A built-in oven is a fixed unit installed in a cabinet cutout, wired in permanently. An OTG (oven toaster griller) is a freestanding box with heating rods that sits on the counter and plugs in. A countertop oven is the broader term for freestanding ovens, including premium convection models with digital controls, and sometimes air fry modes.

In practice, OTG and countertop ovens overlap, so the real choice is between fitted and freestanding.

## Cutout and installation

A built-in oven needs a planned cabinet cutout, typically 56 to 60 cm wide and 55 to 60 cm deep, with ventilation and a dedicated power line. It is easiest to plan during kitchen design, and hard to retrofit into an existing modular kitchen without carpentry. An OTG simply needs a flat counter and a plug, plus clearance around it for heat.

If you do not own your home, a built-in is a poor idea, since it stays behind when you move.

## Cost

Built-in ovens usually start around ₹25,000 and go well beyond ₹80,000, with installation and carpentry on top. OTGs and countertop ovens cost roughly ₹5,000 to ₹20,000 for decent models. ## Performance

Built-in ovens usually have better insulation, more precise thermostats, multiple heating modes and larger cavities, around 60 to 70 litres. They heat more evenly across racks and are quieter. Many have telescopic rails and cool-touch doors.

OTGs vary widely. Cheaper ones have uneven hot spots and slow recovery after opening the door. A good convection OTG with a fan comes close to a built-in for home baking, especially for cakes and cookies. ## Capacity

A typical OTG holds 25 to 60 litres, with 35 to 45 litres suiting most families. A built-in's 60 to 70 litres fits larger trays and multi-rack baking. If you bake for a big family or entertain often, the larger cavity helps, though a 60 litre countertop is available too.

## Safety and heat

Freestanding ovens get hot on the outside, so leave clearance on all sides, avoid placing them under shelves and keep children away. Built-ins are insulated within cabinetry and are safer around a busy kitchen, though they too heat up the surrounding cabinet if poorly ventilated.

## Rental homes and flexibility

For renters, the OTG is the practical choice. You can move it, sell it or upgrade easily. A countertop oven with air fry modes can double as an air fryer, adding value. Browse [air fry convection oven](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=air+fry+convection+oven&tag=adifystore-21).

## Counter space

OTGs take up counter space, and a 40 litre unit is bulky. In a small kitchen this is a real cost, which is a main reason people go for built-in. If your counter is already crowded, factor in what you give up.

## Power and wiring

Built-ins often need a dedicated 16 or 20 amp circuit with proper cabling. OTGs usually plug into a standard 16 amp socket but still need an appropriate MCB. An electrician should check before installation. Do not overload shared kitchen sockets.

## Who should buy a built-in

- Homeowners remodelling or building a kitchen
- Frequent bakers who want precision and capacity
- Buyers who prefer a clean look with no appliance on the counter

## Who should buy an OTG or countertop oven

- Renters and first-time buyers
- Occasional bakers and families who grill, toast and reheat
- Anyone on a budget

## Who should skip an oven altogether

If you rarely bake and mostly use the stove, a microwave with convection or a simple air fryer may be enough. See [microwave oven with convection](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=microwave+oven+with+convection&tag=adifystore-21).

## Recommendation

For most buyers, a good 35 to 45 litre convection OTG delivers the best value. Choose a built-in only if you own the home, are planning the kitchen and want premium performance. Check wattage, wiring and counter space before you decide.
