## How to Choose a PoE CCTV System

Plan the coverage. Walk the property, list entrances, driveways and corners and decide how many cameras you need, with a few spare NVR ports for later.

Size the storage. More cameras and higher resolution use more space, so choose a surveillance-grade drive, such as 2TB to 4TB, and consider motion recording to extend retention.

Think about cabling. PoE uses network cables, with a limit of about 100 metres per run, so plan routes and use quality cable.

## Cabling, Placement and Power Cuts

Poor cable is the usual cause of cameras dropping out, so avoid the cheapest rolls and have a professional pull the runs neatly. Mount cameras away from glare and strong backlight, and test each one at night. Put the NVR on a UPS so that recording survives power cuts, and keep it in a secure, ventilated spot.

## Network Safety for Your Cameras

Keep cameras and the NVR on a separate network or VLAN if your router allows it, change default passwords, disable unused remote access features and update firmware regularly. Use strong, unique passwords for the NVR and the phone app, and avoid exposing the recorder directly to the internet. Good habits protect your privacy as much as the best hardware.

## Who Should Skip This

If you rent a flat with no permission to run cable through walls, a PoE system is a poor fit, and Wi-Fi or battery cameras are easier to take away when you leave. A one-door apartment with a single entrance also rarely needs eight cameras, so a smaller kit or a video door phone will cover the risk.

## How Much Storage You Really Need

Storage depends on camera count, resolution and how many days of footage you want. Four 4MP cameras recording nonstop will fill a drive much faster than the same cameras recording only on motion. Decide the retention you want, such as two weeks, then ask the seller or installer to size the drive for it instead of accepting whatever disk comes in the kit.
