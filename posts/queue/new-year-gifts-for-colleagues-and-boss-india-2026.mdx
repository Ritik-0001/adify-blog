## Rules that keep office gifts comfortable

Keep gifts proportionate to the relationship and stay within a budget everyone can match. Giving a boss something much costlier than a peer's gift looks like flattery, while a thoughtless token feels cold. Avoid perfumes, clothing, alcohol and anything personal or religious. A group gift from the team is often better than individual presents for a manager.

## How to hand it over

Give gifts discreetly and in private, ideally with a short note thanking them for guidance or collaboration. Avoid giving a gift in front of others if you are not giving to everyone. Keep the original invoice off the packaging. A card with a specific line, such as thanking someone for helping with a project, matters more than the item.

## Gifts to avoid

Avoid alcohol, cash, perfume, clothing, jewellery, religious items and very expensive gadgets. Avoid gifts that look like bribes, especially to clients or senior officials in government or procurement roles.

## Who should skip gifting

Skip individual gifts if your company discourages them, or if the budget would stress you. A sincere message can do the job. Skip gifting a boss alone when the team can pool.
