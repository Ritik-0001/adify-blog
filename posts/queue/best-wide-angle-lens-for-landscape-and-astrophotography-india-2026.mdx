## Focal lengths: how wide is wide

On full-frame, 24 mm is wide, 16 to 20 mm is very wide and 14 mm is ultra-wide with dramatic perspective. For landscapes, 16 to 24 mm covers most scenes. For the Milky Way, 14 to 20 mm captures a large chunk of sky in one frame. Beyond that, 12 mm and wider can cause stretched corners and distortion.

On APS-C, a 10-18 mm or 11 mm lens delivers a similar field.

## Aperture for astrophotography

Stars are faint, so the lens needs to gather light. An f/2.8 aperture works for the Milky Way with a high ISO, and f/1.8 or f/1.4 primes help even more, giving shorter exposures and less star trailing. For daytime landscapes, f/8 to f/11 is the sweet spot, so aperture matters little. If you will only shoot daytime, an f/4 zoom is cheaper and lighter.

## Coma and star shapes

Coma is an optical flaw that turns stars near the frame edges into little wings instead of points. It matters for astrophotography. Premium lenses such as the Sigma 14-24mm f/2.8 DG DN Art, the Sony 14mm f/1.8 GM and Nikon Z 14-24mm f/2.8 S are known for good control, while cheaper or older designs show more coma. ## Filters

Landscape photographers use graduated and neutral-density filters to balance skies and slow water. Many 14-24 mm f/2.8 lenses have a bulbous front, so they need special filter holders or none at all. Lenses such as 14-30 mm f/4 or 16-35 mm f/4 accept screw-in filters, which is simpler. If you plan to use long-exposure filters, check the filter system on the listing. [Browse landscape filters](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=ND+graduated+filter+kit+landscape&tag=adifystore-21).

## Weight and portability

A big f/2.8 ultra-wide weighs roughly 800 grams to over a kilogram, a burden on a trek to the Himalayas. A lighter f/4 zoom is easier to carry but gives up some astro capability. If you hike far, check the weight.

## Support gear

Astrophotography needs a sturdy tripod, a remote shutter and a headlamp. A star tracker allows longer exposures without trailing. A planning app helps you choose dark sites away from city lights. [Browse camera tripods](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=carbon+fiber+camera+tripod&tag=adifystore-21).

## The 500 rule and exposure

A common guide divides 500 by the focal length in millimetres to estimate the longest exposure before stars trail, though high-resolution sensors need shorter times. At 14 mm, a roughly 20 second exposure is workable. Wider lenses allow longer exposures, which is another reason to choose a 14 mm.

## Who should skip

If you mostly shoot daytime landscapes, an f/4 wide zoom is cheaper, lighter and filter-friendly. If you shoot only astro, a fast prime may serve better than a zoom.

## Recommendation

For flexibility, buy a 14-24 mm f/2.8 zoom with good coma control. For astro on a budget, a fast 14 mm to 20 mm prime is better value. For hiking and filters, consider a lighter f/4 zoom. Confirm the mount, filter plan and warranty before ordering.
