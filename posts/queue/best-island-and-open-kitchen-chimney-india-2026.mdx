## Why island hoods are a different product

A wall chimney sits against a wall, which helps funnel smoke upward. An island hood floats in open air, and smoke can drift sideways, especially with ceiling fans, AC airflow or an open balcony door. That is why island models need more suction and a larger capture area than a wall unit rated at the same m3/hr.

Indian cooking adds oil-laden smoke from tadka and frying, so oil management matters too: baffle filters or filterless designs are better than mesh.

## 1. Ceiling-mounted island chimney with a telescopic flue

This is the standard design. A hood hangs from a steel chimney section that extends upward to the ceiling, with ductwork running inside or above a false ceiling. Prices vary widely: roughly ₹25,000 to ₹80,000 depending on brand, glass or steel, and suction. Mounting at roughly 70 to 80 cm above the hob is typical for open kitchens, but follow the manual since island models often sit a little higher for head clearance. ## 3. Downdraft and pop-up hoods

Some premium kitchens use a downdraft system that rises from the counter behind the hob. These are expensive, often ₹60,000 or more with installation, and less effective with tall pots because smoke has to be pulled sideways and down. They suit minimalist design where a hanging hood would block sightlines. ## Suction guidance for open layouts

- A 4-burner hob on an island usually needs 1,200 to 1,500 m3/hr.
- Add roughly 10 to 20 percent if the kitchen is open to a large living area or has ceiling fans nearby.
- A wide hood that overhangs the hob by a few centimetres captures more than a narrow one.
- If you deep fry or do heavy Indian cooking, aim for the higher end.

Remember that airflow drops with duct length and bends, so an island on the far side of a flat with a long duct needs more motor, not less.

## Ceiling and duct planning

Island hoods are difficult to retrofit. The duct must go through the ceiling and out to an exterior wall or shaft, which may mean a false ceiling box. In an apartment, check society rules on external wall penetration before you commit. Heavier glass and steel hoods need a firm ceiling anchor; hollow false ceilings need a steel support frame, not only gypsum board.

## Noise and cooking comfort

Island hoods sit closer to your head and above an open area, so noise travels further into the living space. Look for multi-speed controls and a fan with a good low-speed range. Running on a lower speed while chatting is more important than the maximum rating.

## Who should skip an island chimney

If your hob is against a wall, a wall-mount chimney is cheaper, more efficient and easier to install. Do not buy an island hood just for looks if you do not have a ceiling duct route. Also skip if your ceiling is below 9 feet, since the hood may sit too low for tall cooks.

## Recommendation

Pick an island chimney with at least 1,200 m3/hr, a baffle or filterless oil system and a brand with strong local service, and plan the duct with your interior designer before the false ceiling is finished. Budget for professional installation and a quality duct, because poor ducting is the main reason open kitchens still smell of smoke.
