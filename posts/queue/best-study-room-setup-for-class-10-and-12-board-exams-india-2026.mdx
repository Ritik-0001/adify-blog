The best board-exam study room has a stable desk at the right height, an ergonomic chair (roughly ₹10,000-15,000), lighting that does not cast shadows across the page, and a way to cut noise, with a tablet or laptop only if the student actually studies from it. Most of the gain comes from posture and distraction control, not from expensive gadgets.

This setup is researched from ergonomics guidance and common Indian family situations, not from testing in a student's home. Before spending, measure the student's height and the corner of the room, because a desk that is too high or a chair that does not fit under it ruins the whole arrangement.

## Start with the desk and the chair together

A Class 10 or 12 student studies for long stretches, so the desk and chair must work as a pair. The desk surface should be around elbow height when seated with feet flat on the floor, and wide enough for a notebook, a textbook and a laptop side by side. Roughly 4 feet by 2 feet is a workable minimum; smaller desks lead to books piled on the bed.

A fixed-height dining chair or a bed edge is the usual culprit behind back and neck complaints in exam season. Look for a chair with a height-adjustable seat, a back that supports the lower spine, and armrests that do not hit the desk edge. Mesh backs breathe better through summer, which matters in non-AC rooms. If the budget is tight, a [study table with a shelf and drawer](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=study+table+with+shelf+and+drawer&tag=adifystore-21) in the roughly ₹8,000-15,000 range is still better than a flimsy fold-up, provided it does not wobble when the student writes.

A footrest helps a shorter student whose feet do not reach the floor; it is a small purchase, but it fixes a posture problem the chair alone cannot.

## Lighting: aim for the page, not the room

Eyes tire from glare and shadow more than from brightness alone. Place the main light so that it comes from the side opposite the writing hand, for example from the left for a right-handed student, so the hand does not cast a shadow on the page. A neutral to slightly warm LED lamp is easier to read under for hours than a harsh cool-white tube.

Do not spend heavily here. An adjustable [LED study lamp with eye comfort](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=led+study+lamp+eye+comfort&tag=adifystore-21) costs far below the ₹15,000 mark for this site's other picks, and a lamp in the ₹1,000-2,500 range is plenty. Also keep the room's ceiling light on while the lamp is on so that the page is not the only bright spot in a dark room, which strains the eyes.

## Noise and the phone problem

Exam preparation in a Indian joint-family flat means TVs, cooking, visitors and street noise. Noise-cancelling headphones are the most effective single fix for those who work from a noisy living area, and a model from Sony or Bose in the roughly ₹20,000-30,000 range lasts several years. Over-ear headphones are more comfortable than earbuds for 3-hour stretches. Compare options at [noise cancelling over-ear headphones](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=noise+cancelling+over+ear+headphones&tag=adifystore-21).

Cheaper foam earplugs help too, but the harder problem is the phone. Keep it outside the study room or in a drawer during sessions. A phone on the desk, even face down, pulls attention more often than any noise outside.

If a separate room is not possible, face the desk toward a wall rather than the door, so people walking by do not constantly break concentration.

## Tablet or laptop: only if it replaces something

A board-exam student does not need a laptop for most subjects. The case for one is video lessons, digital notes, previous-year question papers and for a Class 12 science student learning with a coaching app. A tablet in the roughly ₹18,000-35,000 range with a stylus is better than a laptop for handwritten notes and lets a student annotate PDFs of NCERT books. Look at [Android tablets with stylus support](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=tablet+with+stylus+for+students&tag=adifystore-21) if coaching content is digital.

A laptop makes sense if the student will go on to engineering, design or programming soon after boards, and the family would otherwise buy one in a year anyway. A [student laptop with an SSD](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=student+laptop+16gb+ram+ssd&tag=adifystore-21) at roughly ₹40,000-55,000 is sensible then. If the main need is only reading PDFs, a tablet is cheaper and less distracting.

Skip both if the student already has a smartphone with a good screen and free access to a family PC. A new gadget can become a distraction, so buy only when it replaces a paid-for habit like printing notes.

## Air, temperature and the fan

A hot, stuffy room slows concentration quickly. Keep a working fan on the desk side, and a window open for cross-ventilation if outside noise allows. In summer boards (March-April), a room that touches 35 degrees without cooling is hard to study in, so check that a ceiling fan or cooler is serviced before February.

## Keep it simple

Add a wall clock, a small whiteboard for formulae, a bookshelf within arm's reach and a bin. Avoid hanging posters directly in front of the desk. A cluttered view makes the room feel crowded even when the desk is tidy.

## Recommendation

Spend first on the chair and the desk fit, then on a quiet corner, then on a simple lamp. Buy noise-cancelling headphones only if the home is truly noisy, and choose a tablet over a laptop unless a laptop is needed soon for other reasons. The total sensible spend for chair, desk and a lamp is roughly ₹20,000-30,000, and everything beyond that is optional.
