## How the brands differ

Dyson built its reputation on cyclonic suction and engineered airflow, and its current cordless range adds laser or sensor-assisted dust detection on higher models, particle counters on flagship units and a large set of tools. Philips has a broader household range and concentrates on practical designs, such as the SpeedPro line, with lighter bodies, simple controls and lower prices.

## Suction and cleaning performance

Dyson's higher-end models produce strong suction with a motorised head that grips carpet and picks up fine dust, which suits rugs, pet hair and deep cleaning of bedrooms. On hard floors, the soft roller heads work well on fine dust.

Philips cordless models offer solid suction for tiled and marble floors, which are the norm in Indian homes, and handle light carpets. They struggle more than Dyson's top models with thick rugs and ground-in pet hair. Marketing figures for air watts or pascals are not directly comparable between brands, so rely on real use cases rather than numbers.

## Run time and charging

Claimed run times are measured on the lowest power mode without a motorised head. In practice:

- Dyson models run around 20-60 minutes depending on mode and model, with some offering a swappable battery on higher units.
- Philips cordless models claim around 40-65 minutes at low power, and often less at higher power settings.

For a typical 2BHK, a single charge covers cleaning if you use medium power. A 3BHK or a home with pets may need top-up charges or a swappable battery. Charging takes around 3-5 hours on most models.

## Filtration

Dyson's flagship models use sealed filtration systems that trap fine particles, and advertise capturing very small particles. Philips offers multi-layer filtration on its premium cordless vacuums, which is fine for typical dust but less rigorous than Dyson's high-end sealed systems. For allergy sufferers, check that the filter is washable or replaceable, and look at how the dust bin is emptied to limit dust release.

## Tools and flexibility

Dyson typically includes more attachments: crevice tool, combination brush, mini motorised tool and a soft roller. Some also have a wall dock. Philips usually bundles a smaller set, though enough for day-to-day work, and often adds a mini brush for sofas. Both convert into a handheld for sofas, cars and stairs.

## Service and parts

Dyson has an authorised service network in major Indian cities, and parts such as filters and batteries are widely available, though they are expensive. Philips has broader retail presence and service, and consumables are cheaper. If you live outside a metro, check whether the brand has a service point or a doorstep pick-up option.

## Price gap and value

The price gap is roughly Rs 10,000-30,000 for similarly positioned models. Dyson charges for engineering, smart features and resale value; Philips charges less for a leaner feature set. If your home is mostly hard floors and you vacuum a few times a week, the Philips has enough power. If your home has many rugs, pets or a child with dust allergies, the Dyson is worth the premium.

## An honest trade-off

Dyson's vacuum is better at the hard cases, but you pay a lot for that, and a Rs 40,000 vacuum is overkill for a small flat with no pets. Philips gives you 80 percent of the practical cleaning for less than half the price, but may disappoint on thick carpets and as the battery ages.

## Cost of ownership

Batteries last roughly 2-4 years of regular use, and replacement costs range from a few thousand rupees for Philips to higher for Dyson. Filters should be washed monthly and replaced per the manual. Check warranty length (often 2 years on the body, shorter on the battery) before buying.

## Recommendation

For the average Indian flat, buy a Philips cordless vacuum at around Rs 20,000-30,000. For carpets, pets, allergies or a large home, spend on a Dyson. Check both brands' service coverage for your city before ordering.
