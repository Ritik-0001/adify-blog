Buy a line-interactive UPS for a home PC, router and monitor, and buy an online double-conversion UPS (roughly ₹15,000-30,000 at 1-2 kVA) only if you run a server, NAS, medical or lab equipment that cannot tolerate even a millisecond of interruption. For most homes, line-interactive does the job at a fraction of the cost.

This comparison is researched from manufacturer specifications and standard UPS topology, not from bench measurements. Before choosing, note the actual wattage of your equipment and how sensitive it is to voltage swings, since those two facts decide everything.

## How each works

A **line-interactive UPS** passes mains power to your equipment through a transformer that boosts or trims voltage (called AVR), and switches to battery when the mains fails. The switchover takes a few milliseconds, which most PCs, routers and TVs ride through without noticing.

An **online double-conversion UPS** continuously converts incoming AC to DC and back to clean AC, with the battery always in the loop. If mains drops, the battery simply continues supplying the inverter, so there is zero transfer time. The output is clean regardless of what the grid is doing.

## Switchover and protection

For a desktop, that few-millisecond gap is generally harmless, because power supplies have holdup capacitors that bridge it. A few very sensitive devices, like some servers, medical monitors or lab instruments, are less tolerant. Those are the real reason online exists.

An online UPS also isolates the load from spikes, sags and frequency drift. In areas where voltage swings dramatically, such as 160 to 270 volts, a line-interactive's AVR may struggle to correct, while an online unit gives stable output.

## Price and running cost

Line-interactive units at 1 kVA cost far less, often well under this site's price floor for small sizes, so treat them as a skip-the-online note. At the 2 kVA to 3 kVA level, a [line interactive UPS 2kVA](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=line+interactive+ups+2kva&tag=adifystore-21) costs roughly ₹15,000-30,000 and powers a home office with several devices. An online 1 kVA unit costs roughly ₹15,000-25,000, and 2 kVA online roughly ₹25,000-45,000.

Online units run hot because the conversion wastes some power, with efficiency typically around 90-94 percent versus 95-98 percent for line-interactive. Over a year of 24-hour use, the difference in electricity adds up to a noticeable amount.

## Noise and heat

Online UPS units have fans that run continuously, which produces constant noise, usually noticeable in a quiet room. Line-interactive units are silent in normal mode and only make a beep or a transformer click when switching. For a bedroom or living room setup, noise argues for line-interactive.

Online UPS units also generate more heat, needing ventilation. Do not shove one in a closed cabinet.

## Loads and what runs well

- Desktop PC, monitor, router, modem: line-interactive.
- NAS, home server, networking rack: online preferred.
- CCTV and NVR: either, depending on voltage stability; see our [UPS for CCTV](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=ups+for+cctv+nvr&tag=adifystore-21) guide for load details.
- Laser printers: avoid on any UPS, since they draw heavy surges.
- Inkjet printers, small TVs and set-top boxes: either.

Check the wattage rating, not just kVA. UPS units are usually rated for around 0.6 to 0.9 power factor, so a 1 kVA UPS may deliver 600-900 watts. Check the real wattage in the listing.

## Battery and backup time

Both types typically use sealed lead-acid batteries inside. Standard units give 5-15 minutes of backup at full load, enough for a clean shutdown. For longer backup, an online UPS with an external battery pack is common, as is a [long backup online UPS](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=long+backup+online+ups+with+external+battery&tag=adifystore-21), which costs more because of the larger charger and external batteries.

Replace internal batteries every 3-4 years. Sealed batteries age even if idle.

## Who should skip online

Most home users should skip online. A home PC, a TV and a router do not need zero transfer time, and an online unit's fan noise, heat and price are hard to justify. Skip it too if you only need an inverter-style backup for fans and lights, where an inverter and battery are the correct tool.

## Who should skip line-interactive

Skip it if your voltage swings badly, if your equipment is sensitive, or if power quality directly affects revenue, such as a small recording studio or a clinic.

## Recommendation

For a home office, buy line-interactive at 1-2 kVA, with a true sine wave output if you run any compressor-type devices. For servers, NAS or any loss-sensitive equipment, buy an online UPS at roughly ₹15,000-30,000 for 1 kVA and accept the noise and running cost. Match the wattage, not the kVA label, and replace the batteries on time.
