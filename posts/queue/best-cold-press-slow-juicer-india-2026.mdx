## What cold press actually means

"Cold press" in home juicers usually refers to slow masticating juicers that spin at roughly 40 to 80 rpm, grinding and pressing produce rather than shredding at thousands of rpm. The claimed benefit is less heat and oxidation, so juice stays fresher for a few hours and separates less. True cold pressing uses a hydraulic press and bags, and is a commercial format; home "cold press" models are slow juicers.

## Yield and pulp

A slow juicer typically extracts more juice from leafy greens, wheatgrass, carrots and soft fruits, leaving drier pulp. Centrifugal juicers are faster but leave wetter pulp and more foam. If you juice daily, the extra yield from a slow machine can offset its higher price over months.

Look at the pulp: if it is dry and crumbly, the machine is efficient. Wet, slimy pulp means more of your produce is wasted.

## Fruits versus vegetables

- Hard produce such as carrot, beetroot and apple needs a strong motor and torque.
- Leafy greens, wheatgrass and herbs need a fine screen and slow auger.
- Soft fruits like orange, pomegranate and grapes are easy but need a fine strainer to prevent clogging.
- Very fibrous produce such as sugarcane or ginger may need a robust model.

## Cleaning, the real decider

A slow juicer has more parts than a centrifugal one: bowl, screw, brush, strainer and lid. Rinse immediately after use, since pulp dries hard in the screen. Use the supplied brush and avoid scrubbing the fine mesh with abrasive scourers.

A good model should come apart in under a minute and rinse in two. Look for a self-clean function with water where the juicer is run with water for quick cleaning. Browse [juicer cleaning brush](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=juicer+cleaning+brush&tag=adifystore-21).

## Chute size and prep time

A wide chute of 70 mm or more takes whole apples and small oranges. A narrow 40 mm chute means you will cut everything, which adds prep time and puts people off daily juicing. If you are short on time, chute size is more important than rpm.

## Noise and running time

Slow juicers are much quieter than centrifugal ones, which is useful for early mornings. They take longer, though: a glass of carrot juice may take a few minutes, not seconds.

## Who should skip a slow juicer

- Households that juice once a week, since a cheap centrifugal may be enough
- People who dislike cleaning
- Anyone who wants fast juice in seconds for a large family

A good blender with a strainer can also serve occasional users.

## Warranty and service

Motor warranties are often longer than product warranties, so check the terms. Spare screws and strainers wear out, so confirm that replacements are available in India.

## Recommendation

If you juice several times a week, buy a vertical slow juicer with a wide chute and a short list of parts to wash, and choose a brand with local service. If you juice rarely, skip it and use a blender. Fresh produce quality will matter more to the taste than the juicer model.
