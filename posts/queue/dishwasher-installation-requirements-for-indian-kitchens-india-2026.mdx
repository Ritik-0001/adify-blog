## The four requirements

### 1. Water inlet

The dishwasher fills through a hose connected to a cold water tap, usually a spare tap under the sink or a T-junction on the existing line. Many Indian kitchens have a single tap, so the plumber adds a [dishwasher inlet connector](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=dishwasher+tap+inlet+connector+t+joint&tag=adifystore-21) or a dedicated angle valve. Most machines need a minimum water pressure, roughly 0.5-1 bar. If your kitchen gets water from an overhead tank with low pressure, you may need a small booster pump. Many models also accept a hot-water feed, but cold-only connection is the safest in India.

### 2. Drain

The drain hose must go to a sink waste pipe, standpipe or a floor trap. It should loop up to a height of 40-80 cm, as the manual specifies, to stop dirty water siphoning back. The drain hose length is usually 1.5-2 m, so the machine should sit close to the sink. Do not put the drain hose into a blocked or narrow pipe. If the sink waste has no spigot, the plumber can fit a tee with a dishwasher connection.

### 3. Power

Dishwashers draw around 1.8-2.2 kW when heating water. Use a dedicated 15 amp socket with a proper earth, a suitable MCB and wiring of adequate gauge. Do not use a multi-plug extension, and avoid sharing the point with a geyser or microwave. Check your sanctioned load, especially if you also run ACs and a water heater at the same time. A voltage stabiliser is a good idea if your supply is erratic.

### 4. Space

Most dishwashers are 60 cm wide, roughly 60 cm deep and 82-85 cm high. Leave a few centimetres on each side and room at the back for hoses, which is why a small cut-out in the adjacent cabinet is often helpful. Also keep a clear 60-70 cm of floor in front for the door to open and the racks to slide out. A slim 45 cm model exists, but options and capacity are limited.

## Freestanding vs built-in

**Freestanding** dishwashers have finished sides and a top, so they can stand on their own or slide under a counter if the height allows. They suit rentals, as you can take them with you, and need no cabinet changes. Plumbing is exposed behind the machine, which a plumber can tidy up.

**Built-in** dishwashers are designed to fit inside a kitchen cabinet cut-out, with a matching panel on the door for a seamless look. They need planning at the kitchen design stage, a precise cut-out and a suitable plinth. They cost more and are fixed to the kitchen, so they suit a home you own and are renovating. ## Rented flats

In a rental, aim for the least invasive set-up. Choose a freestanding unit, place it next to the sink, and use a quick-connect inlet adapter on a tap that can be returned to its original state. Ask the landlord for permission before drilling the sink waste pipe, and use a clamp-on drain connector where possible. Keep the original fittings and a few photos so you can restore the kitchen when you move out. A compact [table top dishwasher](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=table+top+dishwasher+6+place+setting&tag=adifystore-21) can also be an option if space is limited, though capacity is smaller.

## Delivery and entry

Measure doors and lift entrances. A 12-14 place dishwasher weighs 40-55 kg. Check that your kitchen door and any narrow turns allow the carton through. Bosch, IFB, LG and Whirlpool provide installation after delivery, but plumbing extras, electrical points and cabinet changes are usually charged separately.

## What the installer will check

- Water pressure and tap position.
- Drain height and route.
- Power point, earthing and MCB.
- Levelling of the machine on a firm floor.
- A trial cycle to check for leaks.

Be present during the first run, and look under the machine and at hose joints for drips.

## Costs to expect
