## How each one fits a daily routine

A cordless vacuum is a tool you pick up. Cleaning a 2BHK takes 15 to 25 minutes, you decide where it goes, and it handles crumbs under the dining table, the sofa cushions, the car and the stairs. It only works if you are willing to do it.

A robot is an appliance you schedule. Run it at 11 am while you are at work and the dust never builds up, which suits households that sweep once a day. But the robot only cleans what it can reach, and you still need to pre-clean: cables, socks, dog toys and loose curtains have to be off the floor.

## Stairs and multi-level homes

Robots cannot climb stairs. A duplex or independent house needs either one robot per floor or a cordless that carries easily. In a flat on a single level, this point disappears. Cordless sticks weigh roughly 1.5 to 2.5 kg and carry easily; check the weight on [lightweight cordless vacuum cleaner](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=lightweight+cordless+vacuum+cleaner&tag=adifystore-21) listings.

## Pets and shedding

Dog and cat hair is where cordless sticks with a motorised brush win, because they pull hair out of fabric and fringe edges. Robots do a decent job on tile, but hair wraps around side brushes and the main roller, and you must cut it free every few days. If your pet is also prone to accidents, a robot may smear a mess rather than avoid it, although higher-end models have obstacle recognition. ## Floors and carpets

On tile, marble and wood, robots with vacuum-plus-mop are genuinely useful, and some self-empty into a dock. On thick carpet, many robots stall, drift or drag rug edges. Cordless sticks with a carpet-rated roller handle pile much better. If more than a third of your floor is rugs, pick the cordless.

Robots with mopping suit vitrified tile, though wet pads leave water marks on laminate. Check [robot vacuum with self empty dock](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=robot+vacuum+self+empty+dock&tag=adifystore-21) for models that reduce emptying chores.

## Running cost and upkeep

Both use little electricity, a few rupees a month. The real running costs are consumables and batteries.

- Cordless: replacement battery in roughly three to five years, filters washed or replaced.
- Robot: side brushes, mop pads, filters and dock bags; budget some thousands of rupees a year for replacements.

A cheap robot with weak navigation can also bump furniture and get stuck on door thresholds, so consider models with laser or camera mapping, available from brands such as Roborock, Ecovacs, Dreame and Xiaomi. ## Who should skip a cordless

- Anyone who will not make time to vacuum manually
- Elderly households where pushing and carrying is tiring
- Homes where the battery will be flat when guests arrive, because there is no schedule

## Who should skip a robot

- Homes with many thresholds, steps between rooms or deep rugs
- Rooms with heavy clutter, dangling wires or floor-level plants
- Anyone expecting a robot to fully replace manual cleaning, because corners and edges still need a handheld pass

## The combination that works

Many households end up buying both: a robot for daily maintenance on the main living floor, and a cordless for edges, stairs, cars, sofas and spot messes. If you only have money for one, consider whether you will actually unpack and use a stick every day. Habit matters more than spec sheet.

Battery runtime on a cordless drops sharply on the highest mode, so check the real figure on the standard mode before you assume a full-house clean.

## Recommendation

Buy a cordless stick if you have stairs, rugs, pets or a car to clean, and spend your money on a good motorised head. Buy a robot if your floors are tile, your furniture sits high off the ground and your main goal is a quick daily dust-free finish. If budget allows, get a mid-range robot for the daily run and add a cordless later for corners and upholstery.
