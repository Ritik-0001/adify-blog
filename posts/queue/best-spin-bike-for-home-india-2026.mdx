## How to Choose a Spin Bike

Look at the flywheel. A heavier flywheel, in the 10 to 20 kg range, gives a smoother ride, though weights are not directly comparable between brands.

Check the resistance type. Friction resistance is simple and cheap, while magnetic resistance is quieter with less wear.

Look at adjustability and the weight limit. A fully adjustable seat and handlebars help you fit the bike to your body, and a higher user weight rating means a stronger frame. Check the pedal type too, and whether it takes clip-in shoes.

## Setting Up Your Bike Fit

Set the saddle at hip height when standing next to the bike, so your knee has a slight bend at the bottom of the pedal stroke. Slide the saddle forward or back so your knee sits above the pedal axle when the cranks are horizontal. Set the handlebars at or slightly above saddle height for comfort, and adjust them lower later if you want a more aggressive position. If you feel knee pain, recheck the fit and lower the resistance.

## Beginner Workout Ideas

Start with a twenty minute ride at a moderate resistance, keeping a steady cadence of around 70 to 90 rpm. Add short intervals once a week, such as 30 seconds of faster pedalling followed by 90 seconds of easy spinning, repeated eight times. Stand up for short climbs only after your core and legs feel comfortable, and always cool down for five minutes.

## Delivery and Assembly

Spin bikes arrive in a carton and usually need basic assembly of the pedals, handlebars and saddle. Check that the seller provides tools and a manual, and ask whether a technician visit is included. Place the bike on a level floor with a mat underneath, and tighten the bolts again after the first week, since vibration can loosen them.

## Who Should Skip a Spin Bike

If you have lower back trouble or dislike leaning forward, the aggressive posture of a spin bike may be uncomfortable for long sessions. A recumbent or upright exercise bike with a backrest is kinder in that case. Spin bikes also suit people who enjoy standing climbs and intervals, so if you only want gentle pedalling while watching TV, a simpler bike costs less and feels better.
