## How to Choose a Built-in Oven

Check the capacity and modes. Around 60 to 70 litres suits most families, and modes such as convection, grill and fan assist cover baking and roasting.

Look at cleaning. Catalytic liners, pyrolytic cleaning or easy-clean enamel save time, and pyrolytic models are usually pricier.

Confirm the cutout. Standard 60 cm width is common, but check the height and depth against your cabinet and the electrical supply.

## Choosing Between Convection and Pyrolytic Cleaning

Convection ovens circulate hot air for even baking and roasting. Cleaning varies: catalytic liners absorb some grease, easy-clean enamel wipes down and pyrolytic cleaning burns residue to ash at very high temperature. Pyrolytic models cost more but save effort, and they are worth considering if you roast and grill often.

## Everyday Use

Preheat as the recipe advises, use oven-safe trays and let the oven cool before cleaning. Keep the door glass clean and avoid slamming the door.

## Installation and Safety Checks

Ask your carpenter for a ventilated tall unit that matches the cutout, and have an electrician confirm the circuit rating and socket. Ovens often need a dedicated socket. Leave the clearance that the maker specifies, avoid placing the oven directly above a dishwasher and use oven gloves when handling hot trays.

## What the Warranty Should Cover

Built-in ovens have heating elements, fans and electronic controls, so confirm which of these the warranty covers and for how long. Ask whether service is on-site, how quickly technicians respond in your city and whether spares such as racks and door seals are easy to get.

## Who Should Skip This

If you bake only a few times a year, a good countertop convection oven gives you most of the capability for far less, and it can move with you if you relocate. A built-in oven also makes little sense in a kitchen where the tall unit is not yet planned, since the cabinet and electrical point should be designed around the oven rather than the other way round.

## Recommendation

For most modular kitchens, a 60 cm convection built-in oven from Bosch or IFB is a good choice. Check the cutout and the cleaning method on the exact model before you buy.
