## How to Choose a Built-in Oven

Check the capacity and modes. Around 60 to 70 litres suits most families, and modes such as convection, grill and fan assist cover baking and roasting.

Look at cleaning. Catalytic liners, pyrolytic cleaning or easy-clean enamel save time, and pyrolytic models are usually pricier.

Confirm the cutout. Standard 60 cm width is common, but check the height and depth against your cabinet and the electrical supply.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Ignoring the cutout size.** Measure carefully.

**Skipping electrical planning.** Ovens often need a dedicated socket.

**Choosing on mode count.** Choose what you will use.

**Neglecting ventilation.** Leave the gap the maker specifies.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A baking enthusiast.** A convection oven with a good rack and fan.

**A family that grills and roasts.** A multi-mode oven.

**A small kitchen.** A combination oven and microwave.

## What to Check on the Listing

## Using a Built-in Oven

Preheat as the recipe advises, use oven-safe trays and let the oven cool before cleaning. Keep the door glass clean and avoid slamming the door.

## Quick FAQ

**Is a built-in better than a countertop?** It is cleaner and more integrated, but needs planning.

**Do I need a separate microwave?** Many homes keep one.

**Are they hard to install?** They need a proper cutout and electrical point.

## Choosing Between Convection and Pyrolytic Cleaning

Convection ovens circulate hot air for even baking and roasting. Cleaning varies: catalytic liners absorb some grease, easy-clean enamel wipes down and pyrolytic cleaning burns residue to ash at very high temperature. Pyrolytic models cost more but save effort, and they are worth considering if you roast and grill often.

## Installation and Safety Checks

Ask your carpenter for a ventilated tall unit that matches the cutout, and have an electrician confirm the circuit rating and socket. Leave the clearance that the maker specifies, avoid placing the oven directly above a dishwasher and use oven gloves when handling hot trays.

## Warranty, Service and Spares

Built-in ovens have heating elements, fans and electronic controls, so confirm what the warranty covers and for how long. Ask whether service is on-site, how quickly technicians respond in your city and whether spares such as racks and door seals are easy to get.
