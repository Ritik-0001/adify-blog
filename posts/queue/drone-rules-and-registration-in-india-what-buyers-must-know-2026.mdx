## Weight classes matter

India's rules, set out in the Drone Rules 2021 and later amendments, classify drones by weight. Commonly described classes are nano (up to 250 grams), micro (above 250 grams to 2 kilograms), small, medium and large. Requirements for registration, licences and permissions differ by class, with nano drones generally facing the lightest requirements for non-commercial use. Always read the current text, because thresholds and exemptions can be revised.

For a buyer, this means weight on the spec sheet is not a trivia detail. A drone at 249 grams and one at 300 grams can fall into different categories.

## Airspace zones: green, yellow and red

The Digital Sky platform publishes an airspace map with colour zones.

- **Green zones:** airspace where flying is allowed up to a set height, typically 400 feet (about 120 metres), without special permission, subject to the rules.
- **Yellow zones:** controlled airspace, often near airports, where permission is needed before flying.
- **Red zones:** no-fly areas where flying is prohibited without special permission.

Areas around airports, defence installations, international borders, government buildings and some other sensitive sites are restricted. Check the map for your location every time, because you cannot assume your neighbourhood is green.

## Registration and identification

Depending on the class and use, drones may need to be registered and carry a unique identification number issued through the platform. Remote pilots may need a certificate for certain categories or commercial work. Check whether your drone needs registration, and keep documents ready if asked. This part of the rules has changed over time, so rely on official sources.

## Buying and import implications

India has restricted the import of ready-made drones for some purposes, and the market has changed since. Some well-known foreign brands have had limited official presence or support in India. This affects what is on sale, warranty coverage and spare parts. Before buying, check whether the seller is authorised, whether warranty is valid in India and how repairs are handled. ## Practical flying rules to know

- Keep the drone within visual line of sight.
- Stay below the permitted altitude for your zone.
- Do not fly over crowds, public gatherings, airports, government and defence areas or near moving vehicles.
- Avoid flying at night unless allowed.
- Respect privacy and avoid filming people without consent.
- Check state or local restrictions and the rules at tourist places, forts and heritage sites, which often ban drones.

Using a drone for paid work, such as wedding shoots, real estate or events, often brings extra requirements, including a remote pilot certificate and a registered drone, depending on class. ## Who should think twice

If you live next to an airport, a military area or in a dense city centre, you may have few places to legally fly. In that case a drone may sit unused. If you only want aerial footage once or twice a year, hiring a licensed operator may cost less.
