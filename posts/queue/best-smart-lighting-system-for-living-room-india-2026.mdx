## How the main systems differ

Zigbee-based systems such as Philips Hue use a bridge that connects to your router. Bulbs talk to the bridge over a low-power mesh, so they are fast and stay controllable even if your Wi-Fi is down. Wi-Fi bulbs from brands such as Wipro, Syska, Philips WiZ and others talk straight to your router; they are cheaper and need no hub but can strain a crowded network and may lag.

Matter-compatible lights are emerging in India and aim to work across Alexa, Google Home and Apple Home. If you want future flexibility, check Matter or Thread support in the listing, but do not pay a premium until your other devices need it.

## Hub or no hub

A hub costs more upfront, roughly ₹5,000-7,000, but makes sense once you have eight to ten lights. It gives faster scenes, local control and supports accessories like dimmer switches and motion sensors. Without a hub, a handful of Wi-Fi bulbs is simpler and cheaper, but every bulb counts against your router device limit.

If you are in a rented flat, a Wi-Fi system is easier to take with you; a hub-based system also moves, but needs a spare Ethernet port on the router.

## What a full living room costs

A realistic count: four to six ceiling bulbs or downlights, one or two lamps, a TV backlight and a strip along a cove. Using Hue-style pricing, that is roughly ₹25,000-40,000. A Wi-Fi alternative covering the same room can cost around ₹8,000-15,000, though it has less polish. Premium-priced items like colour gradient strips and light panels can add ₹10,000-20,000.

Count your sockets first, because buying too many bulbs is the most common mistake, and downlights in false ceilings may need smart drivers rather than bulbs.

## Scenes and routines that you will actually use

The value is not colour; it is scenes: a warm 2700K "evening" setting, a bright 4000K "cleaning" setting, a dim "movie" scene with a TV backlight, and a sunrise or sunset schedule. Routines tied to a phone or voice assistant, and motion sensors in a passage, make the system feel automatic. Look at a [smart dimmer switch](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=smart+dimmer+switch+zigbee+wireless&tag=adifystore-21) so guests can use normal switches without turning off power to the bulbs.

## Common limits in Indian homes

Many homes use wall switches that cut power to bulbs, which makes smart bulbs go offline. Leave the switch on, or use a smart relay switch instead. Voltage fluctuations can shorten LED driver life, so buy from brands with local service. Check that your router can handle extra devices; older ISP routers can falter with 20 or more.

## Strips and TV backlighting

Light strips behind the TV reduce eye strain and add depth. Pick a strip with an adhesive that suits your wall finish, a power supply with the right length and an addressable option if you want gradient effects. A [TV backlight kit](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=tv+backlight+led+strip+camera+sync&tag=adifystore-21) with a camera is costly but unusual in a small living room.

## Who should skip a premium system

Skip a Hue-style kit if you only want a few colourful lights and have no plan for scenes; a few Wi-Fi bulbs and a [smart bulb 9w](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=wifi+smart+bulb+b22+9w+rgb&tag=adifystore-21) are enough. Skip it if your building has frequent power cuts and you lack a backup, since lights will reset. Skip colour bulbs for main lighting; white-tuneable ones are better for everyday use.

## Recommendation

For a complete living room with scenes and reliability: Hue-style hub, four to six white-and-colour bulbs, one strip and a dimmer switch. For a budget route: three to five Wi-Fi bulbs plus one strip from a recognised brand with local support. Check base type and voice assistant support first.

Add a [smart plug wifi](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=smart+plug+wifi+16a+alexa&tag=adifystore-21) for lamps that have no smart bulb, so every light joins the same scenes.
