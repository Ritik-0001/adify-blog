## How to Choose an Adjustable Setup

Choose a weight range that lets you progress. Beginners can start with 10 to 20 kg per hand, and many outgrow it within months, so a higher range avoids a second purchase.

Check the bench rating. A bench rated 200 kg or more, with sturdy legs and a firm pad, is safer for heavy presses.

Look at the locking system. Collars and selector pins should lock securely so that plates do not slide during a lift.

## Sizing Your Weight Range

Most beginners need two to three pairs of weights across the week: lighter dumbbells for shoulders and arms, medium for rows and presses and heavier for squats and deadlifts. A set that goes up to 30 or 40 kg per hand usually lasts a year or more, while a lighter set can be outgrown quickly. Check that the plates are available in small increments so that you can progress smoothly.

## A Simple Beginner Plan

Train three days a week with full-body sessions: a press, a row, a squat or lunge, a shoulder press and a hinge such as a Romanian deadlift. Start with eight to twelve reps per set, add a little weight when it feels easy and rest a minute or two between sets. Avoid heavy presses alone without a spotter.

## Floor and Storage Tips

Place a rubber mat under the bench and dumbbells to protect the floor and reduce noise. Store plates on a rack or in a corner box, and keep the collars tight when lifting. Wipe the bars and bench pad after workouts, and check the bench bolts every month, because regular use can loosen them.

## Who Should Skip Adjustable Dumbbells

If you already own, or plan to buy, a full set of fixed rubber dumbbells, adjustable ones add little. Someone who trains very heavy, with single dumbbells beyond 40 kg, will outgrow most adjustable sets. People who dislike fiddling with plates between exercises are happier with fixed pairs, since changing weights mid-workout breaks the rhythm.
