## 1. What the Star Rating Measures

Star ratings are based on an efficiency measure called ISEER, which looks at cooling delivered per unit of electricity across a typical season. More stars mean better seasonal efficiency, and the exact thresholds are set by the energy authority and can change over time.

Why it works: a standard way to compare. The trade-off is that real use differs from the test.

## How to Decide

Estimate your usage. Count the hours per day and months per year you really run the AC, and be honest about shoulder seasons.

Check your tariff. Slab-based electricity rates make heavy use more expensive per unit, which improves the case for 5-star.

Compare real quotes. Look at the live price of the 3-star and 5-star versions of the same model and compute your own payback.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Assuming 5-star always wins.** It depends on use.

**Ignoring warranty.** A longer warranty can matter more.

**Skipping installation quality.** Poor installation hurts efficiency.

**Not cleaning filters.** Dirty filters waste power.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A work-from-home user in a hot city.** A 5-star AC running long hours.

**A family using the AC at night for a few months.** A 3-star may be enough.

**A rental home.** A 3-star with a good warranty.

## What to Check on the Listing

Confirm the star rating, the capacity, the ISEER figure where given, the warranty on the compressor and PCB and the installation terms. See whether the label matches the model number.

## Keeping Running Costs Down

Use a thermostat setting of around 24 to 26 degrees, use sleep or eco modes, close doors and curtains and service the unit yearly. Small habits often save as much as moving up a star.

## Quick FAQ

**Is 5-star much better?** It is more efficient, but the saving depends on use.

**Does 5-star cool faster?** No, cooling capacity depends on tonnage.

**Is inverter more important than stars?** Both matter, and inverters are the standard.
