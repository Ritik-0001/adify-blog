## What these systems do

An air pump inflates chambers around your legs in sequence, usually from the feet up toward the thigh, squeezing and releasing in waves. The idea is to move fluid, reduce a heavy feeling after hard sessions and help legs feel fresher. Evidence for faster muscle repair is mixed, but many athletes find them relaxing and helpful for the sensation of tired legs.

## Boots versus sleeves

Boots cover the foot to the thigh and let air work up the whole leg. They suit long-run recovery, but they are bulkier to store and take longer to put on. Sleeves cover the calf and foot only and are easier to use daily. For marathon training, full boots are better. For standing jobs, sleeves are enough.

## Pressure zones and settings

Look for at least 3 pressure levels, and ideally a maximum around 150 to 200 mmHg for sports use, though gentler settings are more comfortable for beginners. Programmes should allow different sequencing, with a cool-down mode. The unit should have an auto-stop timer of 20 to 30 minutes. Start at the lowest pressure and increase slowly.

## Fit and size

Check the calf and thigh circumference ranges, particularly for muscular legs. A zip that does not close is useless. Boots usually come in sizes or adjustable zips. Look for a length that matches your inseam, as short users can end up with a boot that goes too high.

## Safety and when to ask a doctor

Do not use compression without a doctor's advice if you have deep vein thrombosis, suspected blood clots, severe varicose veins, heart failure, open wounds, skin infections, advanced diabetes with neuropathy or recent surgery. Pregnant women should also ask first. Stop if you feel pain, numbness or tingling. Pumps are not a substitute for treating persistent swelling.

## Who should skip it

Occasional walkers rarely need one. If your legs are sore only after the occasional long walk, a warm shower, gentle stretching and a foam roller cost far less. [Foam roller for legs](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=foam+roller+for+legs+recovery&tag=adifystore-21).

## When to use it

Many runners use compression in the evening after a long run or a heavy leg day, with legs slightly raised on a cushion. Sessions of 20 to 30 minutes are typical. Using it before a race is a personal choice, and nothing replaces sleep, food and easy days in a training week.

## Recommendation

For runners and gym-goers, a mid-range multi-chamber boot system with adjustable pressure is the practical buy. For office workers, a calf sleeve is enough. Premium imports suit athletes who train daily and want app control. Ask your doctor if you have any circulation concerns.
