## How to Choose a Bottom Freezer Refrigerator

Check capacity and layout. 300 to 400 litres suits most families of four to five, and a deep freezer drawer holds more bulk items.

Look at the compressor and warranty. An inverter compressor with a long warranty matters, since the compressor is the most expensive part.

Measure your space. Check door swing, the width including hinges and the clearance behind and above.

## Who Benefits Most From This Layout

An elderly household or anyone with back trouble gains the most, since the daily fridge shelves sit between waist and shoulder height. The trade-off is that you bend down for frozen items, and a drawer freezer is harder to scan than a shelved one, so use bins to group packs. Households that live on frozen parathas, ice and meat will not enjoy a small drawer.

## Delivery and Door Planning

Bottom-mount refrigerators can be tall and heavy, so measure door widths, lifts and turns on the way to the kitchen. Check whether the doors can be removed for delivery, and leave enough space for the doors to open fully against nearby walls and cabinets. Keep air vents clear and the fridge about three quarters full so cooling stays even.

## Who Should Skip This

If you stock a large amount of frozen food, a classic top-freezer or side-by-side with a bigger freezer may suit you better than a bottom drawer. Skip bottom-mount too if your budget stays near the low end of the range, where a plain frost free double-door covers the basics for less.

## Kitchen Planning Before You Order

Bottom-mount models are deeper and wider than a basic double-door, and the pull-out freezer drawer needs floor space in front of it to open fully. Mark the footprint on the floor with tape, open an imaginary door and drawer, and check you can still walk past. If the fridge will sit in a recess, leave a small gap on each side and above for ventilation.

## Recommendation

For most families, a 300 to 400 litre bottom-mount inverter refrigerator from LG or Samsung is a strong pick.
