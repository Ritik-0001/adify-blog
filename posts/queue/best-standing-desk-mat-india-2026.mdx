## Why Indian Floor Types Need Anti-Fatigue Mats

Most Indian homes and offices have hard tile, marble, or stone floors — harder than the carpeted wooden floors in European and North American offices where standing desk culture originated. These surfaces offer zero cushioning or flexibility, making fatigue onset faster than on softer floors.

Wood floors have some natural flex. Tile and marble have none. If you're standing on tile or marble for your standing desk work, an anti-fatigue mat is more important than in Western office settings, not less.

## Anti-Fatigue Mat Buying Guide

**Thickness:** 15–25mm is the ideal range. Thinner mats (under 10mm) don't provide adequate cushioning. Thicker mats (over 30mm) can cause ankle instability.

**Edges:** Bevelled or chamfered edges are mandatory. Perpendicular 90° edges catch feet and cause stumbling — a genuine safety hazard.

**Material:** EVA foam (ethylene-vinyl acetate) is more durable than PVC foam. Memory foam is comfortable but compresses permanently over 12–18 months of daily use. EVA retains its cushioning shape longer.

**Dimensions:** 50cm × 80cm covers one person's natural standing footprint with room for shifting weight. 50cm × 100cm provides more movement area (better for active standing with weight shifting).

**Cleaning:** On Indian office floors, dust and food particles settle on mats. Smooth-top EVA mats wipe clean; textured mats accumulate dust in grooves.

**Recommendation:** Kantek MT2100 for most home office users. Topo for dedicated standing desk users who stand 3+ hours daily and want maximum ergonomic benefit.
