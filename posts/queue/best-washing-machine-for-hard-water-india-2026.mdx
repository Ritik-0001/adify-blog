## What hard water does to a washer

Hard water carries dissolved calcium and magnesium. When heated, these form limescale on the heating element, the drum, the door seal and the hoses. Over months, scale insulates the heater (raising power use), clogs the inlet filter and stiffens clothes, which feel rough and dull because detergent works less well. Whites go grey, and you need more detergent for the same wash.

Front loaders use a heater for hot washes, so they collect more scale on the element. Top loaders usually do not heat water, so scale builds as white crust on the tub and pulsator, and in the drain hose.

## Features that help

- **Aqua or hard water wash programs:** some brands include a cycle or setting for hard water, with extra water or longer rinse. Confirm it by name on the listing.
- **Tub clean or drum clean cycle:** high-temperature cycle that loosens scale and detergent residue.
- **Stainless steel drum:** resists corrosion better than plastic-coated tubs.
- **Easy-access inlet filter:** a small mesh at the tap hose that catches scale flakes. You should be able to remove and clean it yourself.
- **Heater type:** ceramic or coated heaters shed scale better. Ask the service centre what the model uses.
- **Extra rinse:** helps remove detergent that clumps in hard water.

## Treat the water, not just the machine

If your water is very hard (visible white deposits on taps, soap that does not lather), a machine alone will not fix it. Consider:

1. **A softener at the inlet:** a salt-based water softener cartridge or a whole-house system protects the machine, geyser and pipes. This is the most effective step.
2. **A scale inhibitor or inline filter:** cheaper, but works partially.
3. **Washing soda or a water softening additive in the wash:** helps cleaning, not scale.

A [water softener for washing machine](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=water+softener+for+washing+machine+inlet&tag=adifystore-21) costs far less than replacing a heater, so it is worth considering if your TDS is high.

## Descaling routine

- Run a drum clean or hot cycle with a descaling powder or citric acid every one to two months.
- Clean the inlet filter every month.
- Wipe the door seal on a front loader and leave the door ajar to dry.
- Do not overload detergent, since extra detergent traps minerals.
- Use hot wash sparingly if you do not need it, because heating drives scale formation.

Citric acid works for light scale. For heavier build-up, use a branded descaler recommended by the manufacturer, and check that it is safe for the model.

## Top load or front load for hard water

A top loader is simpler: no heater, a pulsator that is easy to clean, and cheaper parts. A front loader cleans better and uses less water, but the heater will need a descale and eventually a replacement if you ignore the water. If you choose front load, add the softener.
