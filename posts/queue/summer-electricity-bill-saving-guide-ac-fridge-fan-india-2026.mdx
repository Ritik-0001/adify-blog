## The AC: where most of the bill hides

An AC is the largest single load in an Indian summer home, so its settings matter more than everything else combined. Set the thermostat to 24 or 25 degrees, not 18. Each degree you raise the setpoint reduces compressor effort noticeably; the commonly cited BEE rule of thumb is roughly 6 percent per degree. The room will still feel cool with a fan pushing air, and it will cool faster than it takes to reach 18 anyway.

Avoid turning the AC off and on every few minutes. Fixed-speed units draw their biggest surge at each start. Instead, let the unit run on a steady setpoint and use sleep mode at night, which gradually raises the setpoint by a degree or two.

Close the room properly. Gaps under a door or an open balcony slider dump cooled air outdoors. Heavy curtains on west-facing windows also cut the heat load, and cleaning the filters monthly keeps airflow healthy, which is free.

## Reading the star label honestly

The star rating is a snapshot of efficiency measured as an ISEER value, and a 5-star split AC today needs roughly 5.0 or higher. A 3-star unit costs less upfront but uses more electricity each hour, so the extra purchase price for 5-star pays back only if you run the AC many hours a day. For a bedroom used 5 hours a night, a [3-star inverter split AC](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=1.5+ton+3+star+inverter+split+ac&tag=adifystore-21) at roughly ₹36,000-42,000 can be the smarter buy. For a home office or a hall that runs 10-plus hours daily, pay for 5-star.

Inverter matters more than stars alone. A fixed-speed 5-star unit still switches fully on and off, while an inverter compressor slows down at the setpoint and sips power.

Also check tonnage. An oversized 2 ton AC in a small room reaches the setpoint fast, short-cycles and often wastes electricity, while an undersized unit runs flat out all day. A rough guide is 1 ton for up to 120 sq ft, 1.5 ton for around 150-180 sq ft.

## The refrigerator never rests

A fridge runs 24 hours, so small inefficiencies add up quietly. Keep it at the middle setting, not the coldest one, and leave a few inches of air behind and above it so the condenser can shed heat. Never put hot food inside; let it cool first. Do not open the door repeatedly while deciding what to eat.

Dust on the back or bottom coil is the most common reason an old fridge pulls extra power. Vacuum it twice a year. Check the door gasket with a paper strip, since a leaking seal makes the compressor run constantly.

If your fridge is over 10 years old, replacing it with a [5-star frost-free double door refrigerator](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=5+star+frost+free+double+door+refrigerator&tag=adifystore-21) (roughly ₹25,000-40,000) usually pays off through lower usage over several years, particularly because older single-door direct-cool designs lose efficiency as they age.

Place the fridge away from the oven, window sun and the stabilizer. Heat around it forces more compressor runtime. Consider a [stabilizer made for refrigerators](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=refrigerator+voltage+stabilizer&tag=adifystore-21) only if your supply swings, because a stabilizer itself consumes a small amount of power.

## Fans: cheap to run, but not equal

An ordinary ceiling fan draws roughly 70-80 watts. A 5-star [BLDC ceiling fan](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=bldc+ceiling+fan+28+watt&tag=adifystore-21) draws roughly 28-35 watts for similar air. Ten hours a day across a month, the saving per fan is around 13 units, so a house with five fans saves meaningfully over a year. BLDC fans are a gradual upgrade at roughly ₹3,500-5,000 each, below the price of the appliances above, so replace them one at a time rather than all at once.

A fan is also the best partner for the AC. Moving air makes 26 degrees feel like 24, which is how you actually get the savings on the AC without feeling it.

## Habits that cost nothing

Turn off appliances at the switch, not just the remote. A TV, set-top box and a router left on constantly add steady standby draw; a smart plug or a switch strip fixes this. Run the washing machine and geyser outside the 6-9 p.m. peak where your state offers time-of-day tariffs, since some utilities charge more in the evening.

Do not run the AC while the geyser, oven and washing machine all pull power at once. Staggering them avoids load spikes and, in some slab systems, keeps your monthly total lower.
