## Picture Quality

Sony is widely known for natural colour, smooth motion and strong upscaling of lower-resolution content, which shows most on films and sports. LG's LED and OLED TVs offer strong colour and contrast, and its OLED range is a benchmark for deep blacks.

If your budget is mid-range, Sony often gives more refined processing, while LG gives more panel choices, including OLED at higher prices.

## Smart Platform

Sony TVs use Google TV, which has a wide app catalogue and content recommendations. LG uses webOS, which is simple, remote-driven and quick to navigate.

Both support the major streaming apps. Pick the interface you find easier to use.

## Gaming Features

LG's OLED and higher-end models offer 120Hz, VRR and multiple HDMI 2.1 ports, which suits console gamers. Sony's recent models also support gaming features, and are especially tuned for PlayStation on some ranges.

Check the HDMI port specification on the model, since not all ports support the full feature set.

## Sound

Both brands have decent built-in speakers on mid-range models, and both benefit from a soundbar. Sony has a reputation for slightly richer sound on some models.

## Price and Range

LG covers a wider range, from entry-level LED to OLED. Sony's range is narrower and generally priced higher. For the same size and resolution, LG is often cheaper.

## Service and Availability

Both have wide service networks in India. Check for a service centre in your city and confirm the warranty terms.

## How to Choose Between Sony and LG

Start with your budget and use. If you want OLED or the most for your money, LG is the more flexible choice. If you want refined processing at mid-range and are willing to pay for it, look at Sony.

Read recent reviews on the specific model, since real-world experience varies.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A film lover with a dim room.** LG OLED or Sony OLED suits this, and the choice comes down to budget.

**A cricket-watching family.** Sony's motion handling helps, and LG's colour also works well.

**A console gamer.** LG's HDMI 2.1 ports and gaming features are a strong fit.

**A budget buyer.** LG's value range often has more options.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Choosing by brand alone.** Model matters.

**Ignoring ports.** Check HDMI specifications.

**Skipping the sound.** Plan for a soundbar.

## Quick FAQ

**Which has better picture?** Sony often has more refined processing, and LG OLED has deep contrast.

**Which is better for gaming?** LG has a strong gaming range.

**Which is easier to use?** Both are easy, and the interfaces differ.

## What to Check on the Listing

Compare the panel type, refresh rate, HDMI port specifications and the operating system version. Look at the warranty length and what it covers on the panel, and check whether installation and a wall mount are included. ## Setup and Long-Term Use

Both brands provide software updates for several years, though the policy differs by model. After setup, spend time in the picture settings, choose a film or cinema mode, and calibrate brightness for your room. A soundbar is the most effective upgrade for either brand, since thin TV speakers limit sound quality.

## Sports, Films and Streaming

For cricket and sports, motion handling and brightness matter most, and both brands do well in their mid-range models. For films, OLED from either brand delivers the best contrast, and Sony's processing suits cinephiles. For streaming and everyday viewing, LG's webOS and Sony's Google TV both offer all the major apps, so choose the interface you find easier.

## Warranty and After-Sales

Read what the warranty covers, especially the panel, which is the most expensive part. Ask whether installation and a demo are included, and keep the invoice. For a TV that will sit on your wall for years, the warranty and local service matter as much as the picture.

## Recommendation

If you want a wide range and gaming strength, start with LG. If you want refined picture processing and can pay more, consider Sony. Match the model to your room and budget.
