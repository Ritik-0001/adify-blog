## Mounting height

The bottom of the hood should sit roughly 65 to 75 cm above the hob surface for most wall-mounted chimneys. Many manuals give 65 cm minimum for gas hobs and 60 cm for electric ones, though always follow the manual.

- Too low: heat damages the hood, filters get greasy faster, and tall cooks hit their heads.
- Too high: smoke escapes sideways before the suction can catch it, and efficiency drops sharply.

Use the tallest person who cooks as a reference. If you are above 5 foot 10, aim for the upper part of the range. If you are short, stay on the lower side so capture remains effective. Measure from the top of the burner grate, not from the counter.

## Duct diameter

Most Indian chimneys use a 6-inch (150 mm) outlet, with some 4-inch or 5-inch on smaller models. Match the duct to the outlet size and never reduce it. A narrower pipe forces the motor to work harder, increasing noise and cutting airflow.

Smooth-walled PVC or aluminium rigid pipe offers much less resistance than a ribbed flexible aluminium hose, which looks tidy but traps oil and slows air. If you must use flexible duct, pull it taut. ## Bends and length

Every 90-degree bend acts like adding a metre or more of duct length in terms of resistance. A good rule of thumb for Indian kitchens:

- Keep total length under 3 metres when possible.
- Use no more than two bends, and make them gentle (two 45-degree elbows are better than one sharp 90).
- Slope the duct slightly downward toward the hood so condensed oil does not run back into the motor? No, slope it so condensate drains outward, and ask your installer which way the manual prefers.

Longer runs can work if you step up to a higher-suction chimney, but each extra metre costs noticeable performance. A long run with a weak motor is the classic reason a chimney "does not suck".

## Wall strength and fixing

A chimney can weigh 10 to 25 kg, more for glass models. Fix it to solid brick or concrete with proper wall plugs and screws, not into a hollow partition, tile adhesive or gypsum. Drill the duct hole with a core cutter instead of chipping, which cracks tiles and weakens plaster.

For an exterior wall hole, slope it slightly outward so rain cannot run in, and fit a non-return flap or louvre cap outside to stop birds, insects and backdraft.

## Power and switch position

Install a 5-amp socket close to the hood, ideally above the hood or in a side cabinet, not behind the hob where heat and grease can reach it. Keep it earthed. ## Common installation mistakes

- Using a 4-inch pipe on a 6-inch outlet to fit an existing wall hole
- Running the duct horizontally for a long distance with no slope
- Hiding a flexible duct above a false ceiling with many kinks
- Mounting the chimney too high to fit under a cabinet
- Forgetting a non-return valve, which lets cooking smells, insects and cold air back in
- Venting into a shared shaft or loft space instead of outside

Not following the manual can also void the warranty on the motor.

## Ductless alternatives and when they make sense

If you cannot vent outside, a recirculating setup with carbon filters removes some odours but not heat or humidity. It is a compromise for rentals and not a long-term fix.
