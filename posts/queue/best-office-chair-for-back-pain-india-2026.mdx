## What Back Pain Sufferers Need in a Chair

**Adjustable lumbar support:** Fixed lumbar is useless — people are different heights and the lumbar spine is at different levels. Look for lumbar that adjusts both up/down (height) and in/out (depth/firmness).

**Seat depth adjustment:** For shorter Indian users, many imported chairs are too deep — thighs don't reach the backrest, eliminating lumbar contact. Seat depth should allow 3–5cm gap between seat edge and back of knee.

**Armrest height:** Elbows should rest on armrests at 90°. Armrests too high create shoulder tension; too low cause forward slumping.

**Recline with lumbar follow:** In chairs with synchro-tilt, the seatback reclines while the seat tilts slightly — maintaining lumbar contact during recline. Basic chairs lose lumbar contact when reclined.

## Complementary Back Pain Interventions

A chair alone won't resolve chronic back pain — it's one component:

**Monitor height:** Screen should be at eye level. Looking down for hours strains the cervical and thoracic spine, which refers pain to the lower back. Stack books or buy a monitor arm.

**Movement:** 5-minute walk every 45–60 minutes releases muscle tension that accumulates even in the best chairs.

**Physiotherapy:** For existing back pain, 4–6 sessions with a physiotherapist to address posture habits alongside the ergonomic chair provides faster relief than the chair alone.
