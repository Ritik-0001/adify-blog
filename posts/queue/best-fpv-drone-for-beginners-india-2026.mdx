## What FPV actually is

FPV stands for first-person view. A camera on the drone streams live video to goggles on your face, so you fly as if sitting inside it. You control it with a radio transmitter, usually in acro mode, with no self-levelling. This is very different from a camera drone that hovers by itself. FPV drones are fast, agile and unforgiving, and learning them takes weeks.

## Ready-to-fly versus building

**Ready-to-fly (RTF) or bind-and-fly kits** include the drone, goggles and a controller, so you can start quickly. Some are beginner-friendly with modes that limit speed or add self-levelling. Check that the bundle includes everything, including batteries and a charger.

**Building your own** from a frame, flight controller, motors, a video transmitter and a camera teaches soldering and Betaflight setup. It is cheaper per flight hour in the long run, but costly in time and mistakes, and parts availability in India can be patchy. Choose this only if you enjoy electronics. ## Goggles and video systems

Video systems differ:

- **Analog:** cheapest, low latency, grainy picture. Goggles are inexpensive and common.
- **Digital systems (DJI, Walksnail, HDZero):** much sharper video, higher cost, and different ecosystems that do not mix.

DJI's FPV systems have been popular, but DJI product availability and support in India are limited, so verify before choosing. Beginners on a budget often start with analog, which is easy to repair. [Browse FPV goggles](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=FPV+goggles&tag=adifystore-21).

## Radio controllers

You need a transmitter with a compatible receiver. Popular hobby radios such as the RadioMaster Pocket or TX12 are small and affordable at roughly ₹8,000-15,000, though check availability in India. Make sure the protocol (ELRS, Crossfire or others) matches the drone's receiver. [Browse FPV radio controllers](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=RadioMaster+FPV+radio+controller&tag=adifystore-21).

## Simulators first

Fly a simulator such as Liftoff, VelociDrone or Uncrashed for at least 10 to 20 hours before flying a real drone. Crashes in a simulator are free. A radio controller connected to your computer works the same way. This single habit saves more money than any drone choice.

## Whoops and cinewhoops

A whoop is a tiny drone with propeller guards, around 65 to 75 mm, that is safe indoors and cheap to repair. A cinewhoop is a larger ducted drone designed to carry a camera for smooth shots around people and buildings. For beginners, a whoop is the safer start; a 3.5 to 5 inch freestyle drone follows once you can fly steadily.

## Crash costs

Expect to replace propellers frequently (₹300-1,000 per set), arms, cameras and sometimes motors. Budget at least ₹5,000-10,000 for spares in the first months. Carry spare batteries, a charger and tools. LiPo batteries need careful storage and charging in a fireproof bag. ## Rules in India

Drones fall under India's Drone Rules, and FPV flying near crowds, airports and sensitive areas is restricted. Weight class, registration needs and no-fly zones apply, and rules can change, so check the Digital Sky platform and local regulations before flying. Do not fly near people, airports, government buildings or at night without permission. This post is not legal advice.

## Spotter requirement

When wearing goggles you cannot see the surroundings, so FPV pilots usually fly with a spotter who watches the drone and surroundings. In India, an open field away from people is the right place to practise.

## Who should skip FPV

If you want stable video for travel, a camera drone is a better buy. If you dislike fixing things, FPV will frustrate you. If you have no open flying space nearby, skip it. [See camera drones](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=camera+drone+4K+GPS&tag=adifystore-21).

## Recommendation

Begin with a simulator, then a small whoop or a ready-to-fly kit with analog goggles. Move to a freestyle drone once you can hover and turn confidently. Budget for spares, learn the local rules and check seller support before buying.
