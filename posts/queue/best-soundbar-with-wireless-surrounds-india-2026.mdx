## How to Choose a Soundbar With Surrounds

Think about your room. Rear speakers need a place behind or beside the seating, and power sockets or charging, so check the layout before buying.

Look at the formats. Dolby Atmos and DTS:X add height effects, and eARC lets the TV pass full-quality audio to the soundbar.

Compare setup. Some systems auto-calibrate to your room, which saves time and improves the balance.

## Placement and Calibration

Place the rear speakers roughly at ear height to the sides or behind the main seat, keep the subwoofer in a corner or along a wall and run any automatic calibration. Adjust the levels so that dialogue stays clear.

## Room Size and Seating Position

Rear speakers work best when you sit in the sweet spot between the front bar and the rears, so a sofa pushed against the back wall may place you too close to them. Measure the distances before buying, and check that power sockets or charging docks are near where the rears will sit. Smaller rooms may be better served by a good 3.1 or 5.1 bar without large rears.

## Who Should Skip This

If you mostly watch news, cricket and OTT shows at low volume in a small bedroom, a wireless surround set is overkill. The rear speakers need somewhere sensible to sit, and in a cramped room they end up beside your ears or on a cluttered shelf. A good single bar with a subwoofer gives clearer dialogue for far less money and fewer boxes to place and charge.
