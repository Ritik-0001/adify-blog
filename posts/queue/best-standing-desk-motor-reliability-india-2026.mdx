## Single Motor vs. Dual Motor

**Single motor:** One motor drives one of the two leg columns. The other leg column follows passively. Weakness: uneven loading causes the passive column to drift out of alignment over time, creating wobble and uneven height. Cheaper to manufacture.

**Dual motor:** Each leg column has its own motor. Motors are synchronized electronically. Result: smooth, even lift, minimal wobble at full height. More reliable because each motor handles only half the load.

**For a ₹15,000–₂₅,000 Indian budget:** The Flexispot E1 uses a dual motor — this is why it's recommended over cheaper single-motor competitors at similar price points. The extra ₹2,000–₃,000 for dual motor is worth it for a desk you'll use for 5–10 years.

## Motor Quality Indicators

**Noise level:** Quality motors operate under 50dB. Cheap motors produce grinding or humming sounds. A standing desk motor should be quieter than a refrigerator.

**Speed:** Quality motors move at 25–40mm per second (full stroke in 20–30 seconds). Cheap motors move slowly (15mm/s) and strain under load.

**Stability at full height:** At maximum height, load the desk with your normal equipment (monitor, laptop, items = 15–25kg) and check for wobble. A quality dual-motor desk should be stable; cheap single-motor desks wobble perceptibly.

**Anti-collision detection:** Quality desks include anti-collision sensors that stop the motor if the desk hits a chair, drawer, or person during descent. Budget desks skip this safety feature.
