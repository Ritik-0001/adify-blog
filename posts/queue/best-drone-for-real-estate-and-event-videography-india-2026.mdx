## What the work needs

Real estate shoots want crisp, stable exterior views of buildings and plots, smooth reveals and sometimes interior walk-throughs. Event videography, such as weddings and corporate gatherings, needs steady shots of crowds, venues and processions, often in low light at dusk or evening. In both cases, stable video and reliable operation matter more than speed.

## Sensor size and image quality

Small sensors produce noisy video in dim light. A 1-inch sensor handles dusk and indoor-outdoor transitions better, and 4/3-inch sensors on flagship models go further. For bright daytime real estate, a smaller sensor may be enough, but for wedding events in evening light, a bigger sensor pays off. Look for 4K at 30 or 60 frames per second and a 10-bit colour option with a flat colour profile like D-Log for grading.

## Gimbal and stability

A three-axis mechanical gimbal is essential. Avoid drones that use electronic stabilisation alone. Wind in tall buildings and open plots can shake lightweight drones, so check wind resistance specifications and fly in calm conditions.

## Flight time and battery planning

Quoted flight times of 30 to 45 minutes are measured in ideal conditions; in real shooting with hovering, wind and return reserve, expect around 20 to 30 minutes of usable flying. For an event, buy at least three batteries and a multi-battery charging hub, and charge between sessions. Carry a power bank or a car charger for long shoots. ## Obstacle avoidance

Around buildings, trees and people, obstacle sensing reduces the risk of crashes. Omnidirectional sensing is more reliable than forward-only systems, but no sensor replaces careful flying. Sensors can fail in low light, glass and thin branches.

## Permissions and professional use

Paid shoots can bring additional requirements, such as registration, a remote pilot certificate and permission to fly in certain zones. Many event venues, temples and heritage sites restrict drones, and wedding venues may require permission from owners. For real estate, check that the property is not in a restricted zone and that you have the owner's consent. Rules change, so verify with the official Digital Sky platform. This post is not legal advice.

## Privacy and professionalism

Flying over crowds is risky and often prohibited. Keep a safe distance from guests and fly during moments when the crowd is not underneath. Inform clients about what you can and cannot film.

## Who should skip

If you only occasionally need aerial shots, hiring a licensed operator costs less than buying a drone and managing permissions. If you shoot mostly interiors, a gimbal or slider is more useful.

## Recommendation

Buy a stabilised 4K drone with a 1-inch sensor or larger, at least three batteries and ND filters. Confirm Indian warranty and service, check the rules for commercial flying and always get permission for venues. Practise in open areas before flying on a client job.
