## The dynamic versus condenser decision

This decision matters more than brand. Dynamic microphones, like the SM7B or Rode Procaster, pick up sound mostly from directly in front, within a few centimetres. They are less sensitive, so they reject room echo, AC hum and a noisy street.

Condenser microphones, such as the Rode NT1 or Audio-Technica AT2020, are more sensitive and detailed. In a quiet, treated room they sound brighter and more open. In a typical Indian flat with ceiling fans, hard tiles, and street noise outside, they capture every flaw.

If you record at home without treatment, start with a dynamic. If you have a closet with clothes, a carpeted study, or acoustic panels, a condenser becomes a real option.

## 1. Shure SM7B, the broadcast standard

Roughly ₹38,000-45,000. This is the microphone behind many well-known podcasts. It has a warm, controlled sound and strong rejection of background noise. It demands a lot of clean gain, often around 60 dB, which means a basic interface may sound hissy.

The trade-off is that you may need an in-line booster, which adds around ₹10,000-15,000. Without it, a mid-range interface will sound thin or noisy. See [Shure SM7B](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=shure+sm7b&tag=adifystore-21).

## 2. Rode Procaster, easier on the interface

Roughly ₹20,000-28,000. It is another dynamic broadcast mic, and it needs less gain than the SM7B, so it pairs well with a Scarlett 2i2. The sound is slightly brighter and more present.

The downside is that it sits closer to an average podcast mic in terms of character; it lacks the SM7B's iconic bass warmth. Check [Rode Procaster](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=rode+procaster&tag=adifystore-21).

## 3. Rode NT1, for treated rooms

Roughly ₹20,000-28,000. A quiet, low-noise condenser with a clear sound. It shines for narration, voice-over, and single-voice shows.

Skip it if your room has echo or if you do not own a reflection filter. Every room flaw will be recorded. Browse [Rode NT1](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=rode+nt1+condenser+microphone&tag=adifystore-21).

## Audio interfaces: what you actually need

An interface converts the microphone's signal into digital and gives you a clean preamp. For a solo podcaster, a Focusrite Scarlett Solo is enough. For two hosts, get a Scarlett 2i2 with two XLR inputs.

Roughly ₹15,000-22,000 for a 2i2 class interface. Focus on three specs: number of XLR inputs, clean gain, and whether it provides 48V phantom power for condensers (dynamics do not need it).

For three or four guests, a podcast mixer such as a Rode Rodecaster or Zoom PodTrak is simpler than stacking interfaces. These cost roughly ₹30,000-60,000 and give each host a headphone output. See [Focusrite Scarlett 2i2](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=focusrite+scarlett+2i2&tag=adifystore-21) and [podcast mixer interface](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=rodecaster+podcast+mixer&tag=adifystore-21).

## Room treatment on an Indian budget

Echo is the enemy, and fixing it often matters more than upgrading the mic. Practical steps:

- Record in the smallest, most cluttered room you have; a bedroom with a bed and curtains beats a big hall.
- Hang thick curtains or a heavy blanket behind you and place a duvet on the wall in front.
- Use a reflection filter behind the mic for added dryness; this is cheap and effective for condensers.
- Turn off the fan and AC during recording, and record after midnight if traffic is loud.

## Accessories you will actually need

A boom arm keeps the mic near your mouth without desk vibrations, and a pop filter or foam windscreen softens plosives. A closed-back headphone, like the Audio-Technica ATH-M40x, prevents bleed. Buy XLR cables with decent shielding. Check [boom arm for microphone](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=boom+arm+microphone+stand+heavy+duty&tag=adifystore-21).

## Who should skip XLR

If you record on the go, travel often, or share a small room, a USB microphone or a wireless lavalier is simpler and cheaper, with fewer parts to carry. An XLR system is for a fixed home studio.

## Recommendation

Choose a dynamic microphone and a Scarlett 2i2 if your room is untreated, which describes most Indian homes. Choose the SM7B if you can afford the booster and want a classic sound; choose the Procaster if you want an easier, cheaper pairing. Choose the NT1 only after you have dealt with echo. Spend a little on a boom arm, pop filter and curtains before you buy a more expensive microphone.
