## Autofocus and speed

Sony's autofocus is widely regarded as the benchmark. Real-time tracking, eye detection for humans and animals, and strong subject recognition make bodies such as the A6700, A7 IV and A7R V reliable for sports, children and wildlife. Fujifilm has improved a lot with its X-H2S and X-T5 using subject-detection autofocus, but Sony usually holds the edge for fast, unpredictable subjects.

If you shoot kids, pets or action, this matters more than any other difference.

## Colour and film simulations

This is Fujifilm's strength. Its film simulations, such as Classic Chrome and Velvia, produce pleasing JPEGs straight from the camera. Many photographers shoot JPEG and share without editing, saving hours. Sony's colour is accurate and neutral and responds well to editing, but many users find the straight-out-of-camera look less characterful. If you dislike editing, Fujifilm is a real advantage.

## Sensors and low light

Sony offers full-frame bodies, which gather more light and help in dim wedding halls or night streets. Fujifilm's main X series uses APS-C, which is excellent in good light but behind full-frame in low light. Fujifilm also sells medium-format GFX cameras, which are costly specialist tools. If you shoot indoors or at night often, Sony's full-frame models have the advantage.

## Lens ecosystem

Sony's E-mount has the largest lens catalogue in mirrorless, with excellent third-party support from Sigma, Tamron and Viltrox. That gives you more choice and often lower prices. Fujifilm's X-mount lenses are well regarded, small and sharp, but the catalogue is smaller and Fujifilm lenses can be pricey. ## Handling and design

Fujifilm bodies have physical dials for shutter speed, ISO and exposure compensation, giving a camera-like feel that many enthusiasts love. The retro design is also attractive. Sony bodies are more modern and menu-driven, and some are small and light, though the menus can feel dense. Try both in your hand if you can, as grip comfort varies. Some Sony bodies, particularly small ones, can feel cramped for large hands. [Browse Fujifilm X cameras](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=Fujifilm+X-T+mirrorless+camera&tag=adifystore-21).

## Video

Sony's video features, such as the A7S III and ZV series, are strong, with reliable autofocus and long recording times. Fujifilm's X-H2S and X-S20 offer good video and film simulations in video. For a vlogger, Sony's ZV line is simple. For a filmmaker who loves colour, Fujifilm is attractive. [Search Sony vlogging cameras](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=Sony+ZV+vlog+camera&tag=adifystore-21).

## Availability and service in India

Sony has broad retail and service coverage. Fujifilm has fewer stores and long waits for some popular models, such as the X100 series, which has often been in short supply. Check stock and warranty from an authorised seller.

## Price

Fujifilm's popular bodies can be costly for what you get, since part of the price covers design and colour. Sony's APS-C bodies are often cheaper per feature, and used Sony lenses are easy to find. [Browse mirrorless kits](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=mirrorless+camera+kit+lens&tag=adifystore-21).

## Who should pick which

- **Sony:** action, wildlife, weddings, video creators and anyone who wants maximum autofocus and lens choice.
- **Fujifilm:** street, travel and portrait photographers who value colour, dials and a camera that is a pleasure to hold.
- **Skip both** if you want the lowest price for your first camera; an entry Canon or Nikon APS-C body may be cheaper. [See Canon vs Nikon options](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=Canon+EOS+R10+camera&tag=adifystore-21).

## Recommendation

Pick Sony for tracking performance, full-frame options and lens variety. Pick Fujifilm if colour, JPEGs and the feel of shooting drive your decision. Confirm the sensor size, lens availability and warranty before buying.
