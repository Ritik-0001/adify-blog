## Doorframe Pull-Up Bar: How It Works in Indian Homes

Doorframe pull-up bars use a leverage mechanism — the bar hooks over the door frame on both sides and your bodyweight pushes it into the frame. The harder you pull, the more secure the bar becomes.

**Will it fit Indian door frames?** Standard Indian residential doorframes are 72–82cm inner width. Most doorframe bars adjust from 60–100cm — they fit. Some older homes with 60–65cm doorframes may not accommodate standard pull-up bars; measure before buying.

**Will it damage the door frame?** Quality doorframe bars with foam padding and rubber grips don't mark or damage door frames. Cheaper bars with hard plastic contact points can leave marks on soft wood. Check for foam padding on the door frame contact area.

**Weight limit:** Most doorframe bars are rated 100–150kg. For users above 90kg, verify the specific model's rating. The Strauss bar supports up to 130kg.

## Learning Pull-Ups from Zero

Most beginners cannot do a single pull-up. Progression:

1. **Australian rows / bodyweight rows:** Place bar low, lean body under it at 45°, pull chest to bar. Easier starting point.
2. **Banded pull-ups:** Loop a Kore resistance band over the bar, kneel in it. Band assists 30–50% of bodyweight.
3. **Jumping pull-ups + slow negatives:** Jump to bar level (assisted by jump), lower yourself slowly (3–5 seconds). Builds strength before you can do unassisted reps.
4. **First full pull-up:** Most people achieve this in 4–8 weeks of consistent band-assisted training.
