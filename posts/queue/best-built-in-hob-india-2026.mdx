## How to Choose a Built-In Hob

Check the burners. Brass burners are more durable and spread heat evenly than aluminium ones. A large burner suits big kadhais, and a small one suits simmering.

Look at the glass and controls. Toughened glass of about 8 millimetres is common, and knobs should feel firm. Flame failure devices and auto-ignition add safety and convenience.

Measure the cut-out. Built-in hobs need a precise cut-out in the counter, and dimensions vary by model. Confirm the size, and plan ventilation with a chimney.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Not measuring the cut-out.** Confirm dimensions.

**Ignoring ventilation.** Pair with a chimney.

**Using the wrong cookware.** Match to the burner or induction.

**Skipping installation planning.** Confirm who fits it.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A modular kitchen.** A 4 burner glass hob fits and looks clean.

**A heavy Indian cooking kitchen.** Prioritise brass burners and a strong flame.

**A gas-saving household.** Consider induction with compatible cookware.

## Quick FAQ

**Are glass hobs safe?** Toughened glass is designed for kitchens, but avoid sudden impacts.

**Brass or aluminium burners?** Brass lasts longer.

**Can I replace a freestanding hob?** Yes, if the counter cut-out suits.

## Installation and Safety

Have the hob installed by a qualified technician, and use an ISI-marked gas pipe and a correctly fitted regulator. The counter cut-out should match the manufacturer's dimensions exactly, and there should be adequate clearance from walls and cabinets. Keep a fire extinguisher or a fire blanket in the kitchen, and check for gas leaks with soapy water rather than a flame.

## Cleaning and Care

Wipe the glass after cooking once it has cooled, using a soft cloth and mild cleaner, and avoid abrasive pads that scratch the surface. Remove the burner caps and heads occasionally and clean the ports with a soft brush so the flame stays even. Do not drop heavy pots on the glass, and use pan supports that fit the burner size.

## Choosing Burner Layout

A 4 burner layout usually offers one large, two medium and one small burner. If you cook large kadhais often, look for a hob with a high-output burner of around 4 kilowatts. Check the distance between burners so that two large pots can sit side by side.
