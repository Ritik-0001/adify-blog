## What ₹50,000-75,000 actually buys

Compared with ₹30,000 treadmills, this band typically gives you a wider and longer belt, a thicker multi-layer belt, a heavier flywheel for smoother motion, motorised incline instead of manual, and a better cushioned deck. The motor is also rated more honestly, and the frame carries a higher user weight, often 110 to 130 kg.

You do not get the full commercial feel. Gym-grade treadmills with 4 HP continuous motors cost more than ₹1,20,000. At this band you are buying a very good home machine.

## The specs that matter most

Continuous HP matters more than peak. A 4 HP peak rating may mean around 2 HP continuous, which is fine for walking and jogging but strained at 10 km per hour for long runs. Belt width of 42 to 46 cm gives room to move. A thick deck with elastomer cushioning reduces knee load.

Look at roller diameter, with 40 mm and above preferred, and a belt thickness of around 1.8 to 2 mm for longer life. Auto incline up to 12 or 15 percent lets you simulate hills.

## Install, space and running cost

A 4 HP treadmill is around 80 to 110 kg and needs two people to move. Delivery partners often handle installation, but check the brand's installation policy before ordering. Fully unfolded dimensions are around 150 by 75 cm, plus extra space behind for safety.

The electricity draw is modest, under about 1.2 kW at high load. Use a stabiliser if your voltage dips. [Voltage stabiliser for treadmills](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=voltage+stabilizer+for+treadmill&tag=adifystore-21).

## Who should skip this band

If you mainly walk 30 minutes a day, a ₹25,000-35,000 motorised treadmill is enough, and the extra spend does little. If you plan to run daily at speed above 12 km per hour, jump to a commercial-grade unit rather than stretching a home model.
