## Layouts that fit a small counter

Three burners can sit in a few patterns, and the layout changes how your pots fit.

### 1. Triangle layout

One burner at the front and two at the back, or the reverse. This makes the hob shorter in width, often around 60 to 65 cm, and works in narrow galleys. The risk is that a big kadhai on the front burner can crowd the back ones.

### 2. Straight line layout

Three burners in a row. The hob gets wider, usually 70 to 80 cm, but pots never collide. This suits counters of at least 75 cm. See [3 burner straight line gas hob](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=3+burner+straight+line+gas+hob&tag=adifystore-21).

### 3. Offset or L-shape

One large burner on one side with two small ones stacked. This is a good fit for families that cook with a large kadhai every day, but may sit awkwardly if you have a chimney that is narrower than the hob.

## Burner sizes and what to cook on them

A good 3-burner set typically includes one large burner, one medium and one small, for example roughly 100 mm, 75 mm and 55 mm. Indian cooking needs a strong large burner for dosa tawa, rotis, deep frying and boiling, with a small simmer burner for dal or milk. Check that the large burner has a triple-ring flame, which spreads heat across a wide pan.

Brass burners are usually more durable and resist corrosion better than aluminium. Check [brass burner gas hob 3 burner](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=brass+burner+gas+hob+3+burner&tag=adifystore-21).

## Cutout and counter fit

Every built-in hob needs a hole cut into your counter, and the cutout is smaller than the hob itself. Typical cutouts for a 3-burner glass hob run around 50 to 70 cm wide and 40 to 50 cm deep, but they differ by model, so check the manual. Leave at least 10 to 15 cm of clear counter on either side for safety and working space, and keep the hob a safe distance from walls and cabinets.

Leave room underneath for the gas hose and the cylinder or PNG connection, and keep combustible items such as plastic jars away.

## Glass versus steel

Toughened glass looks sleek and wipes clean in seconds, but a heavy dropped pan can crack it. Stainless steel is tougher and cheaper, but scratches and shows heat marks. If you want a longer comparison, check the dedicated steel versus glass article on this site. For small kitchens that double as a showpiece, glass usually wins on looks.

## Safety features that matter

- Flame failure safety (FFD), which cuts gas if the flame blows out
- Toughened glass at least 6 mm thick
- Auto-ignition with a reliable spark system
- Sturdy pan supports with rubber feet
- A removable spill tray

Gas leaks are a real risk in small kitchens with poor ventilation, so fit a [gas leak detector](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=gas+leak+detector+home&tag=adifystore-21) and a good chimney to clear fumes and heat. A flame failure device adds a few thousand rupees but is worth it.

## Pan supports and cleaning

Cast-iron pan supports last long but are heavy; coated steel ones can rust. Check whether the supports can be lifted off for cleaning, since dal and milk spills run into every gap on a small hob. A tight seal between glass and body prevents oil from creeping underneath.

## LPG and PNG compatibility

Most 3-burner hobs ship as LPG. If your home uses PNG, you need a conversion kit or a hob specifically configured for natural gas, otherwise combustion will be poor and unsafe. Search [PNG conversion kit gas hob](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=png+conversion+kit+gas+hob&tag=adifystore-21) and confirm with your gas supplier.

## Who should skip a 3 burner hob

If you cook for one or two people and rarely use more than two burners, a 2-burner hob saves counter space. If your counter is under 55 cm wide, no 3-burner will fit safely. And if you have a free-standing stove, a built-in hob may require a new counter cutout and a proper installer.
