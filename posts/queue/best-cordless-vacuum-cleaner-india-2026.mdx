## How to Choose a Cordless Vacuum

Check battery and modes. A removable battery lets you swap and extend cleaning time, and many models give around 30 to 60 minutes in low power but much less in boost mode.

Look at suction and filtration. Strong suction and a sealed filter system help with dust and allergies, which matters in Indian cities.

Think about weight and attachments. Lighter models are easier on stairs, and attachments for crevices, furniture and cars add value.

## Cleaning Indian Floors

Indian homes often have a mix of tiles, marble, carpets and rugs, with dust that settles quickly. A cordless vacuum with a soft roller head works well on tiles, and a motorised brush bar helps on rugs. Daily quick passes keep dust from building up, which is easier than a weekly deep clean. Bin size matters too, because a small dust bin on a dusty balcony route needs emptying every session.

## Battery Life and Care

Batteries wear out over time, so check whether the brand sells replacement batteries in India and at what approximate cost. Avoid leaving the vacuum on charge for days, store it away from heat and use lower power modes for longer run time. Empty the bin after each use and wash or replace filters as the manual advises.

## Who Should Skip This

If your home is large, with several rooms of carpet or heavy daily dust, a cordless stick may need recharging halfway through a clean, and a corded or robot vacuum is a better fit. Also skip it if you want one machine for wet spills and balcony debris, since a wet and dry vacuum handles that far better.

## Where to Dock and Charge It

A cordless vacuum gets used more when it is already charged and within reach. Choose a spot near a socket on a wall that can take a dock or bracket, away from direct sun and the kitchen stove. Keeping the attachments in a small box beside it saves the hunt for the crevice tool when you need it most.

## Recommendation
