## How tri-ply is built

Tri-ply means three bonded layers: usually stainless steel on the inside for food contact, an aluminium core in the middle that spreads heat quickly, and a magnetic stainless layer on the outside so the pot works on induction. Stainless alone is a poor conductor and creates hot spots. The aluminium core fixes that, and the steel skin keeps the cooking surface non-reactive.

Some cheaper pans advertise "tri-ply" but have a thin aluminium disc only under the base, sometimes called an encapsulated base. Those heat the bottom evenly but leave the sides cooler, so milk can scorch while the edges stay lukewarm. Full-wall tri-ply costs more but behaves better for rice, dal and gravies.

## Even heating, and what that means for Indian cooking

The real benefit shows up with milk, rice and slow-cooked gravies. Milk heats evenly and is less likely to burn at the base. Dal simmers without a crust forming at one edge. Onions brown at a steadier pace because the heat spreads across the whole base.

It is not a replacement for a pressure cooker or a carbon-steel tawa. Tri-ply tawas can warp if heated empty on high flame, and rotis cook best on a cast iron or hard-anodised surface.

## How to stop food sticking

Stainless sticks if the pan is cold when you add oil, or if the food goes in too early. Heat the dry pan on medium for a minute or two, add oil, wait until it shimmers, then add food. Let meat and paneer release naturally before turning. Eggs remain hard in stainless, which is why many people keep one non-stick pan alongside.

## Cleaning and care

Soak burnt residue in warm water with a little dishwashing liquid, and use a soft scrubber. Avoid steel wool, which scratches the surface and gives food places to stick. Rainbow-coloured stains from overheating can be removed with a spoon of vinegar or lemon. Hard-water white scale responds to the same treatment.

Do not plunge a hot pan into cold water, as sudden temperature change can warp the base. Allow it to cool for a few minutes first.

## Who should skip tri-ply

If you cook mainly eggs, dosas or low-oil meals, non-stick or cast iron serves you better. If you want light cookware for elderly family members, tri-ply is heavy, and a hard-anodised set at lower cost may suit them more. Anyone who does not want to learn pan-preheating will likely find stainless frustrating.
