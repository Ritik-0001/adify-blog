## 1. 43 Inch: Small Rooms and Bedrooms

Roughly ₹25,000–45,000. A 43 inch TV suits a viewing distance of around 4.5 to 6 feet and works well in bedrooms and small living rooms.

At bed distance a bigger screen fills your vision uncomfortably, so this size is often the right call there. In a hall with a sofa across the room it will look small.

Roughly ₹30,000–70,000. A 50 to 55 inch TV suits a distance of around 5.5 to 7.5 feet and is the most popular size for Indian living rooms.

It fits most standard TV units and wall spaces, and there is a wide spread of brands at every budget. Households that watch a lot of cricket may find themselves wishing for the next size up.

## 5. A Simple Distance Rule

As a guide for 4K TVs, sit about 1.2 to 1.6 times the screen diagonal away. For a 55 inch TV, that is about 5.5 to 7.3 feet, and for a 65 inch TV about 6.5 to 8.7 feet. If you like a more immersive view, sit closer.

Take a tape from your sofa to the wall, divide by 1.2 and 1.6, and the diagonal range falls out. Sitting at the near end suits films, and the far end suits long casual viewing.

## 6. Panel Types and Brightness

Larger TVs show differences in panel type, brightness and contrast more clearly, so consider QLED, Mini-LED and OLED options, especially for bright rooms or dark theatre rooms.

Brighter QLED and Mini-LED panels cope better with daytime rooms, while OLED shines in dim rooms. A bigger screen magnifies a weak panel, so do not trade picture quality for inches.

## How to Choose Your TV Size

Measure your seating distance. Use a tape from the sofa to the wall and apply the distance rule, then check available wall or stand width.

Think about content. Movies and sports benefit from larger screens, while casual TV and news are fine on smaller ones.

Consider budget and room. A good 55 inch TV usually beats a cheap 65 inch one for picture quality, so do not stretch to a bigger size at the cost of quality.

## Mounting and Placement

Mount the TV with the centre of the screen at eye level when seated, use a bracket rated for the weight and size and keep cables tidy. Avoid placing the TV opposite a bright window.

## Who Should Skip This

If you rent and cannot drill into the wall, or your TV unit is narrow, going up a size may be more trouble than it is worth. A wide screen on a small stand looks unstable and can overhang. Likewise, if you mostly use the TV as background noise in a modest room, the extra spend on a 65 inch or bigger set will not be noticed day to day.
