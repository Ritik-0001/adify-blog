## Why lithium is different

Lead-acid tubular batteries are cheap and familiar, but they can only be discharged to around 50-60 percent each cycle without shortening their life. LiFePO4 can be discharged to 80-90 percent routinely, so a 100Ah lithium battery gives usable energy comparable to a much larger lead-acid battery.

Lithium also weighs far less, often 12-15 kg for 100Ah, needs no water topping up, charges faster and holds voltage steadier. That steadier voltage keeps lights and fans at full output until the battery is nearly empty.

## Cost versus lead acid

A 12V 100Ah LiFePO4 battery holds roughly 1.28 kWh and costs around ₹35,000-55,000. A pair of 150Ah tubular batteries holds around 3.6 kWh nominal but only around 2 kWh usable, and costs roughly ₹28,000-36,000. Per usable kWh, lithium looks pricier up front.

The case for lithium rests on lifespan: LiFePO4 is commonly rated for 2,000 to 4,000 or more cycles versus around 800-1,200 for tubular. If a lithium battery lasts 8-10 years against 4-5 for lead acid, the lifetime cost can be competitive. Check the warranty period, since claims mean little without a 5-year or longer warranty.

## Compatibility is the big catch

Most older inverters charge using a lead-acid profile. Forcing that onto lithium can overcharge it, trip the battery's protection or damage it. Make sure your inverter explicitly supports lithium, or buy a lithium battery bundled with a matching inverter. A compatible [lithium inverter and battery combo](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=lithium+inverter+battery+combo&tag=adifystore-21) sold together removes the guesswork.

Voltage also matters. A 1500VA inverter uses 24V, so you would need either a 24V lithium battery or two 12V units wired properly. Never mix chemistries or capacities in one battery bank.

## BMS and safety

Every lithium battery needs a battery management system that controls cell balance, over-charge, over-discharge, over-current and temperature. Buy only from brands that mention BMS protection, certification and a clear warranty, because a cheap unbranded pack is a fire risk.

LiFePO4 is the safer lithium chemistry, far less prone to thermal runaway than cobalt-based cells in phones and laptops. Even so, install the battery in a ventilated place away from heat, and do not puncture or crush it.

## Capacity and backup

A 1.28 kWh pack at around 85 percent usable and 85 percent inverter efficiency gives roughly 0.9 kWh delivered. At a 400-watt load that is about 2.3 hours. For more backup, you need a bigger or additional pack, such as 200Ah, which costs proportionally more.

This is why lithium suits those with lighter loads or very frequent cuts, where cycle life matters, and not those wanting 6-hour backup on a tight budget.

## Who should skip lithium

- Homes with rare cuts, where a tubular battery will rarely cycle and the lifespan advantage is wasted.
- Buyers whose inverter does not support lithium and who do not want to replace it.
- Anyone with a very tight budget, since a tubular pair costs less up front.
