## 50 mm versus 85 mm

An 85 mm lens on full-frame compresses features flatteringly, gives a comfortable working distance and isolates the subject from the background. It is a classic for headshots and half-body portraits. Its downside is that you need space; in a small room you may not fit a full-length shot.

A 50 mm lens is more versatile. It suits half and full-body portraits, couples, street shots and general use, and costs less. For indoor Indian homes and weddings with limited space, a 50 mm is often more practical. If you can only buy one portrait lens and shoot mostly outdoors, the 85 is more flattering; if indoors or travelling light, the 50.

On APS-C bodies, the crop makes a 56 mm lens behave like an 85 mm on full-frame and a 35 mm like a 50 mm. [Search 56mm APS-C lenses](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=56mm+f1.4+APS-C+lens&tag=adifystore-21).

## f/1.8 versus f/1.4 and f/1.2

Wider apertures blur backgrounds more and let in more light, but they cost more and weigh more. An f/1.8 lens already gives strong separation for most portraits. An f/1.4 or f/1.2 lens adds a thinner depth of field, which can make it hard to keep both eyes sharp, and costs well above ₹1,00,000 in some brands. Unless you shoot in dark venues or need the look, f/1.8 is the sensible choice.

## Brand versus third-party

Brand lenses tend to have the most reliable autofocus, full feature support and resale value. Third-party lenses often give better value and optical performance, particularly on Sony. Always check compatibility with your body's firmware, and avoid unknown brands without warranty.

## Practical points

- Eye autofocus works best with a native or fully supported lens.
- Lens stabilisation helps in low light for stills; your camera body may include in-body stabilisation.
- A lens hood protects against flare and bumps.
- Buy from an authorised seller to preserve warranty. [Browse lens hoods and filters](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=camera+lens+UV+filter&tag=adifystore-21).

## Who should skip

If you already own a 24-70 mm f/2.8 zoom, a portrait prime may add little for the cost. If you shoot only video, a fast prime may not be needed.

## Recommendation

For most buyers, an 85 mm f/1.8 is the best portrait lens, with a 50 mm f/1.8 as a budget-friendly alternative. Sony owners benefit from third-party choices, while Canon RF owners should stay with Canon glass. Confirm sensor size, mount and warranty before ordering.
