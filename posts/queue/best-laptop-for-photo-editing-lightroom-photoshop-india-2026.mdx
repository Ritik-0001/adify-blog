## Start with the display, not the CPU

A photo edit is a judgement about colour and tone, and your laptop screen is the judge. Lightroom will run on nearly anything modern; what separates a useful editing laptop from a frustrating one is a panel with wide, accurate coverage. Look for 100 percent sRGB as the minimum, 95 percent DCI-P3 if you export for the web and prints, and a factory calibration report if the brand provides one. A 400-nit brightness figure helps in daylight, but a matte finish reduces reflections better than raw brightness.

OLED panels, common in the ASUS Zenbook and Vivobook OLED lines, give superb contrast and near-complete P3 coverage. They also tend to be glossy, and the brightness can fluctuate in dim scenes, so print work still benefits from a calibrated external monitor.

## RAM: the quiet bottleneck

Photoshop with a few large layered PSDs open can eat 12 GB on its own, before Chrome gets involved. Eight gigabytes is not enough for serious work; 16 GB is the working minimum and 32 GB is comfortable if you edit 45-megapixel files or stack panoramas. On Windows laptops, confirm the RAM is not soldered at a lower size with no slots, since you cannot add more later. On Macs, memory is fixed at purchase, so buy the 16 GB configuration even if it costs another ₹20,000.

## Storage: plan for the catalog, not the OS

A Lightroom catalog and its previews live comfortably on an internal SSD, but the actual RAW files rarely should. A busy photographer makes 200 to 500 GB a year. Keep the catalog and current projects on the internal drive, archive finished shoots to a portable SSD and keep a second backup elsewhere. A [portable NVMe SSD](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=portable+SSD+1TB+NVMe&tag=adifystore-21) over USB-C or Thunderbolt is fast enough to edit from directly.

## Do you need a calibrator?

Even a good screen drifts. A hardware colorimeter such as the [Calibrite or Datacolor Spyder](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=Calibrite+colorimeter+monitor+calibration&tag=adifystore-21) costs about ₹10,000 to ₹20,000, yet it is what makes your prints match your screen. If you only share images on Instagram, you can skip it. If you sell prints or work for clients, it pays for itself on the first job.

## Who should skip a dedicated editing laptop

If you edit twice a month, a desktop with a large monitor gives far better value; a laptop only needs to be good enough to carry. If you shoot only on a phone, almost any modern laptop with 16 GB RAM works. Gaming laptops with high-refresh panels are a poor fit unless the panel is verified as wide-gamut, since many cover only sRGB partially.
