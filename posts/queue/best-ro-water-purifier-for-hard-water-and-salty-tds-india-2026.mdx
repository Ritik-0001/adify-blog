## How to Choose for Hard and Salty Water

Understand hardness and TDS together. Hardness comes from calcium and magnesium, and it scales membranes and heating elements. High TDS includes salts, and RO reduces it. Both affect purifier life.

Look at pre-filtration and pump strength. A sediment and carbon pre-filter, and a pump that maintains pressure, protect the membrane and improve output.

Plan for servicing. In hard water areas, replace filters and membranes on a faster schedule than the default, and flush the system if the manual advises it.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Delaying servicing.** Scale builds quickly.

**Skipping a softener in severe hardness.** It protects the system.

**Ignoring water wastage.** Reuse reject water.

**Buying a low-pressure model.** Weak pumps struggle.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A home with hard borewell water.** A softener plus RO protects both.

**A coastal home with salty water.** A strong RO with a good membrane.

**A flat with a shared tank.** Check water quality regularly.

## Quick FAQ

**Does RO remove hardness?** RO reduces dissolved salts, but scale can still form on the membrane.

**Do I need a softener?** In severe hardness, it helps.

**How often should I change filters?** Follow the manual, and consider earlier changes in hard water areas.

## Signs Your Purifier Needs Attention

Watch for falling output, a change in taste, unusual noises from the pump or a stuck indicator light. A drop in flow often means a clogged pre-filter or a scaled membrane, and a salty taste can mean the membrane is nearing the end of its life. Do not wait for the full scheduled interval if you notice these signs in hard water.

## Servicing in Hard Water Areas

Ask the technician to check the pre-filters, the pump pressure and the membrane at every visit, and consider a more frequent schedule than the default. A pre-filter housing and sediment filter before the RO can help extend membrane life in areas with heavy hardness.
