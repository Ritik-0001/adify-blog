## What Research Actually Shows

**Proven benefits of sit-stand alternation:**
- 35% reduction in lower back discomfort (British Journal of Sports Medicine, 2016)
- Reduced afternoon fatigue and energy dips
- Slightly improved mood and focus in some studies
- Long-term: may reduce some metabolic disease risk factors

**Common myths:**
- "Standing burns significant calories" — Standing burns ~8 more calories/hour than sitting. Over a workday, this is trivial.
- "Standing is always healthier than sitting" — Standing all day increases varicose vein risk, knee and hip stress, and foot fatigue.
- "Standing improves productivity" — Research is mixed. Fine motor tasks (typing) may be slightly worse while standing initially.

## The Correct Way to Use a Standing Desk

**Protocol:** Sit for 20–30 min → Stand for 10–15 min → Repeat.

Most standing desk users don't follow this and leave the desk at one height. The electric motor is essential for making the switch friction-free — a crank requires effort and gets ignored.

Apps like Stretchly or simple phone reminders can prompt transitions until the habit is established.

## Is a Standing Desk Right for You?

**Yes, a good investment if:**
- You already have a good ergonomic chair
- You have recurring lower back pain despite good chair
- Your work is primarily monitor-based (not fine drawing, paper-based)
- Budget allows ₹20,000+

**Consider other options first if:**
- You don't have a proper ergonomic chair yet (buy that first)
- Your back pain is acute or medical — see a physiotherapist
- Budget is under ₹15,000 (buy the chair instead)
