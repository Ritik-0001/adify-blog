## 1. Treadmills

Roughly ₹25,000–60,000. A treadmill supports walking, jogging and running, and adding incline raises effort without more speed. It uses your body weight against gravity, so calorie burn per minute is generally higher than gentle cycling.

Why it works: high calorie burn and a natural movement. The trade-off is impact on joints and a larger footprint.

## 2. Spin Bikes

Roughly ₹15,000–40,000. A spin bike with a heavy flywheel supports interval training and hard efforts, which can burn a lot of calories in a short time. It is lower impact than running.

Why it works: intense workouts with low joint stress. The trade-off is saddle comfort and a learning curve for fit.

## 3. Upright Exercise Bikes

Roughly ₹12,000–25,000. Upright bikes are simpler and cheaper, with magnetic resistance and a display. They suit beginners and steady cardio.

Why it works: price and simplicity. The trade-off is a lighter workout at a casual pace.

## 4. Recumbent Bikes

Roughly ₹15,000–40,000. A recumbent bike with back support is very low impact and good for seniors and people with back or knee issues.

Why it works: comfort. The trade-off is that calorie burn depends on effort and resistance.

## 5. Walking Pads

Roughly ₹15,000–35,000. A walking pad is the smallest treadmill option and suits steady walking at home or under a desk. Walking is the easiest habit to keep.

Why it works: easy habit and small footprint. The trade-off is lower top speed.

## 6. Folding Treadmills for Small Homes

Roughly ₹20,000–55,000. If space is tight, a folding treadmill gives you walking and jogging with a smaller footprint.

Why it works: balanced space and workout. The trade-off is that very light frames feel less solid.

## How to Decide

Start with your joints. If you have knee, hip or ankle issues, a bike is usually gentler, and a doctor can advise you on what is safe.

Think about your habits. People who like walking while watching TV often keep to a treadmill, while people who like short, hard workouts often enjoy a spin bike.

Consider space and noise. A treadmill needs more room and can be louder in a flat, while a magnetic-resistance bike is nearly silent.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Buying for the wrong reason.** Choose what you will use.

**Ignoring joints.** Impact matters for some people.

**Expecting quick results.** Diet and consistency decide.

**Skipping a warm-up.** Always ease in.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A beginner with healthy knees.** A treadmill with a gentle walking plan.

**A person with knee pain.** A recumbent or upright bike after medical advice.

**A family in a small flat.** A walking pad or a folding bike.

## What to Check on the Listing

Confirm the motor or flywheel, the weight limit, the warranty, the folded or footprint size and assembly arrangements. Read reviews about noise and comfort.

## Making Either Machine Work

Set a weekly schedule, track your time rather than calories, and combine cardio with some strength work and sensible eating. Keep the machine where you will see it and use it, rather than hidden in a spare room.

## Quick FAQ

**Which burns more calories?** A treadmill generally burns more per minute, though a hard bike session can be close.

**Which is easier on joints?** An exercise bike.

**Which is better for small homes?** A walking pad or a folding bike.

## Final Checklist Before You Buy

Before you order, measure the room, the lift and the doorway, check the maximum user weight against the heaviest person in your home and read the warranty on the motor or frame separately from the warranty on parts. Confirm who delivers and assembles the machine, and ask whether a technician visit is included. If you live in a flat, think about noise and a floor mat before the box arrives.

## Recommendation

If your joints are healthy and you like walking, buy a motorised treadmill. If you want low impact and quiet, buy a spin or recumbent bike. Whatever you choose, compare specifications and warranty on the listing.
