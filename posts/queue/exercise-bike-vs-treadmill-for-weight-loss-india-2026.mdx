A treadmill usually burns more calories per minute for most people and suits those who like walking and running, while an exercise bike is lower impact, quieter and easier on the knees, so the better choice depends on your joints, your space and which one you will actually use daily. This comparison is researched, not hands-on tested, and it leans on general fitness guidance rather than lab figures. Machine displays estimate calories loosely, so judge progress by weekly time and how you feel, and speak to a doctor if you have health concerns.

## 1. Treadmills

Roughly ₹25,000–60,000. A treadmill supports walking, jogging and running, and adding incline raises effort without more speed. It uses your body weight against gravity, so calorie burn per minute is generally higher than gentle cycling. The cost is impact on joints and a larger footprint.

## 2. Spin Bikes

Roughly ₹15,000–40,000. A spin bike with a heavy flywheel supports interval training and hard efforts, which can burn a lot of calories in a short time. It is lower impact than running, though saddle comfort and bike fit take some getting used to.

## How to Decide

Start with your joints. If you have knee, hip or ankle issues, a bike is usually gentler, and a doctor can advise you on what is safe.

Think about your habits. People who like walking while watching TV often keep to a treadmill, while people who like short, hard workouts often enjoy a spin bike.

Consider space and noise. A treadmill needs more room and can be louder in a flat, while a magnetic-resistance bike is nearly silent.

## What Actually Drives Weight Loss

The machine matters less than the routine. Calories burned in a 30 minute session are modest compared with what a snack or a sweet drink adds back, so diet and consistency do most of the work. Set a weekly schedule, track your time rather than calories, add some strength work, and keep the machine where you will see it rather than in a spare room.

## Which One Gets Used

Many treadmills end up as clothes racks because jogging feels like effort, while a bike you can ride in front of a show gets used longer. Be honest about your temperament. If you enjoy walking, buy the treadmill. If you hate impact or want to ride while reading, buy the bike. Used five days a week, the cheaper machine beats the premium one used once a week.

## Who Should Skip Both

If you rarely exercise at home, or your spare room is already crowded, neither machine may be worth buying yet. Try a month of daily brisk walking outdoors or a gym membership first, and buy only if the habit sticks. Home equipment pays off when it matches a routine you already keep.

## Recommendation

If your joints are healthy and you like walking, buy a motorised treadmill. If you want low impact and quiet, buy a spin or recumbent bike. Whatever you choose, compare specifications and warranty on the listing.
