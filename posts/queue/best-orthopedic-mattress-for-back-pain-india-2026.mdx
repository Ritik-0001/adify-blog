## How to Choose an Orthopedic Mattress for Back Pain

Look for even firmness and support. Most people with back discomfort do better on medium-firm to firm surfaces, but a very hard mattress can create pressure points. The ideal is a surface that supports the spine while cushioning the shoulders and hips.

Check the construction. Layered foam, coir and pocket springs each feel different. Foam gives contouring, coir gives firm support and springs give bounce and airflow.

Consider your sleeping position and weight. Heavier sleepers need denser support layers, and side sleepers need a comfort layer for pressure relief.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Assuming harder is always better.** A very hard mattress can be uncomfortable and does not automatically help back pain.

**Skipping the trial.** Your body needs a few weeks to adjust, so use the home trial fully.

**Ignoring your base.** A sagging bed base undermines a good mattress.

**Treating it as a cure.** A mattress may help comfort, but persistent pain needs medical advice.

## What to Check on the Listing

Look at the firmness rating, the layers and the thickness. Confirm the warranty and the trial terms, and read recent reviews for sagging complaints in the first year.

## Quick FAQ

**Is orthopedic just marketing?** The term is not a medical standard. Focus on firmness, support and your own comfort.

**How firm should it be?** Medium-firm suits many people, but personal preference and body weight matter.

**How long should I test it?** At least three to four weeks.
