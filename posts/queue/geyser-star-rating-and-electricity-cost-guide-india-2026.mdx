## What the stars actually measure

The Bureau of Energy Efficiency star label for storage water heaters is based on standby energy loss: how much electricity the tank uses over 24 hours just to hold water at a set temperature, with nobody drawing hot water. The more stars, the lower the standby loss for that tank size.

This means a 5-star geyser does not heat water faster, and it does not reduce the energy needed to raise cold water to 55 degrees. Heating a bucket from 20 to 55 degrees takes the same kilowatt-hours in any element. The saving is in what leaks away afterwards.

## How big is the saving

A 25 litre geyser with 1 star versus 5 stars may differ by a few tenths of a kilowatt-hour per day in standby loss. Across a year that works out to a modest few hundred rupees at typical Indian tariffs, not thousands. A 5-star unit pays back only slowly, so choose one for the better insulation and build quality rather than expecting a huge bill reduction.

## Daily electricity use in plain numbers

Most geysers are rated 2 kW. Heating enough for a family of four's morning baths usually needs about 30 to 45 minutes of element time. That is roughly 1 to 1.5 kWh a day, depending on tank size and incoming water temperature. At ₹7 to ₹9 per unit, a typical month of daily use lands in the low hundreds of rupees, rising in winter.

Larger tanks cost more to keep hot but let you bathe in one go. Instant geysers with 3 to 4.5 kW elements draw much more power, so they need a thick wire, a dedicated MCB and a proper earthing. ## Habits that cut the bill more than stars do

- Use a timer or plug-in smart switch so the geyser heats 20 minutes before bathing, not all night. A [geyser timer switch](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=geyser+timer+switch&tag=adifystore-21) is a small purchase that stops forgotten heating.
- Set the thermostat to 50 to 55 degrees, not maximum. Higher settings mean more standby loss and scald risk.
- Switch off after the last bath. A tank cools slowly, so you rarely need to heat again within an hour.
- Insulate the hot pipe run. A long uninsulated pipe can waste heat before it reaches the shower.
- Do not leave the geyser on permanently, since the standby loss is exactly what the star label measures.

A 15 litre geyser suits one or two people in a small bathroom. A 25 litre unit covers three to four people when used in shifts. ## Features that actually reduce losses

Look for a thick polyurethane foam layer, a glass-lined or stainless steel inner tank, and a thermostat with a cut-off, since these are what determine how well the tank holds heat. Brands such as AO Smith, Racold, Bajaj and Havells publish these details.

## Solar and heat pump alternatives

If your home has roof space and you use a lot of hot water, a solar water heater has high initial cost but very low running cost. Heat pump water heaters use around a third of the electricity of a resistance geyser, at a much higher price. They make sense for big families and for hotel-style use, not for a couple. Search [heat pump water heater](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=heat+pump+water+heater&tag=adifystore-21) to see the price gap.

## Who should skip the 5-star premium

If you shower once a day for a few minutes and your geyser stays off for most of it, a 3 or 4-star unit from a trusted brand costs less up front and wastes almost nothing extra. Spend the difference on better build, such as a thicker tank anode and a proper stainless steel flange.
