## How to Choose a Beginner DSLR

Think about lenses. DSLR lenses are plentiful and cheap on the used and new market, which keeps the total cost low.

Check ease of use. A guide mode, clear menus and a comfortable grip help you learn faster.

Consider longevity. DSLRs are in their later years, so if you want to grow into video or newer features, look at entry mirrorless too.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Buying on megapixels.** Image quality depends on more than numbers.

**Using only the kit lens forever.** A 50mm prime adds a lot.

**Ignoring memory cards.** Buy fast, reliable cards.

**Skipping learning.** Learn aperture, shutter and ISO.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A student learning photography.** A Canon EOS 1500D or Nikon D3500 kit.

**A parent photographing children.** A DSLR with a telephoto zoom.

**A future vlogger.** An entry mirrorless camera.

## What to Check on the Listing

Confirm the model, the kit lens, the warranty, the seller and what is in the box. See whether a bag and memory card are included.

## Learning Tips

Start in aperture priority mode, learn how aperture changes background blur and practise composition. Shoot in good light and review your photos to see what works.

## Warranty and Service

Check that the warranty is valid in India, that service is available and that the seller is authorised. Keep the invoice and serial number.

## Quick FAQ

**Are DSLRs outdated?** Still capable, but brands focus on mirrorless.

**Is a kit lens enough?** For starting, yes.

**Should I buy used?** Only from trusted sources with a warranty.

## Buying New, Used or Refurbished

New cameras from authorised sellers come with warranty and support, which matters for a first camera. Used bodies can be good value, but check the shutter count, the sensor for dust, the lens for fungus and the seller's return policy. Refurbished units from the brand can be a middle path, offering some warranty at a lower price.
