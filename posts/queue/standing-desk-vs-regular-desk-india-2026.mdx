## The Evidence for Standing Desks

**Lower back pain:** Sitting for extended periods loads the lumbar spine unevenly. Studies of office workers show standing desk use reduces reported back pain by 32–54% after 6 months of use. The key is alternating — not standing all day (which causes its own fatigue), but breaking up sitting with 20–30 minute standing intervals.

**Alertness and productivity:** Blood flow to the brain is higher when standing. Users report improved afternoon alertness when standing for 30 minutes after lunch compared to remaining seated.

**Metabolic:** Standing burns 8–10 more calories per hour than sitting. Over a workday with 3 hours of standing, that's approximately 25–30 additional calories burned. Not a weight loss solution, but a meaningful contribution to metabolic activity for sedentary professionals.

## Who Benefits Most from a Standing Desk

**High benefit:**
- People who sit 7–10 hours daily at a desk (developers, analysts, writers, finance professionals)
- People with existing lower back pain that worsens through the day
- WFH professionals with no commute or physical activity built into the day
- People who find they're mentally fatigued by 3–4pm daily

**Moderate benefit:**
- People who sit 5–7 hours daily with regular breaks
- People with mild occasional back discomfort

**Low benefit:**
- People who sit less than 4 hours at a desk daily
- People who already exercise regularly and have no back pain
- Students who have regular class-based breaks from desk work

---

## The Honest Counterargument

A standing desk does not replace the need for exercise. It reduces extended sitting — it doesn't provide cardio, strength, or flexibility benefits. People who use standing desks but otherwise remain sedentary (no walking, no exercise) get partial benefits at best.

Standing all day is also bad for health — causes leg swelling, varicose veins, and joint strain. The benefit is in alternating: 45 minutes sitting, 15 minutes standing, repeat. If you won't actually alternate, you won't extract the full benefit.
