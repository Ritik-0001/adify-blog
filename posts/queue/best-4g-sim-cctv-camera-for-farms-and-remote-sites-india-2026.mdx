## The first test is signal, not the camera

Stand where the camera will mount and check Jio or Airtel on your phone. Two bars of 4G is the minimum; one bar means constant buffering and missed alerts. A camera cannot beat a weak tower, though an external antenna can help on some models.

If your phone shows only 3G or no service, skip 4G cameras altogether. A local DVR with recording on site, checked in person, makes more sense there.

## How 4G cameras are powered

Remote sites rarely have reliable mains. The practical options are:

- Solar kits: a camera with a built-in or separate panel, and an internal battery that carries it through the night and cloudy days.
- Battery only: simple, but you must climb to recharge every few weeks.
- Mains with a 4G SIM: for farmhouses and sheds that have power but no broadband.

Solar needs direct sun for several hours a day. Trees, shed roofs and monsoon weeks can starve the battery, so mount the panel on a south-facing pole away from shade.

## Data use: plan for it

A 4G camera streaming live video uses a lot of data. A rough guide: live viewing at moderate quality eats several hundred MB per hour. Event clips, such as 20-second motion clips uploaded to a cloud, use far less.

For a typical farm camera that sends alerts and a few clips a day, expect roughly 10 to 30 GB a month. Check whether you need a data-only SIM, the validity, and whether the cheaper prepaid plans are throttled. Keep the SD card as the main recorder and use live view sparingly.

## Storage: SD card first

Use a high-endurance microSD card, 128GB to 256GB, so footage survives a lost signal. A 128GB card holds a few days of continuous recording or weeks of motion clips. Cloud storage adds a monthly fee and needs data; use it for alerts and key clips only. ## Mounting and security

Mount at 3 to 4 metres on a pole or wall, angled down, so animals and thieves cannot easily reach it. Use a metal box or cable ties that resist theft, and keep the SIM slot hidden. Wild animals such as monkeys and langurs may knock cameras, so a sturdy bracket helps.

## Who should skip 4G cameras

Skip them if a Wi-Fi router can reach the site (a mesh extender is cheaper and uses no SIM data). Also skip if you need 24x7 continuous recording with remote viewing: that demands a DVR with mains and broadband.
