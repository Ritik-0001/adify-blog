## What actually helps in a power cut

When power goes, the compressor stops and the cabinet starts to warm. How long food stays safe depends on insulation thickness, door seal quality, how full the refrigerator is and whether you open the door. An inverter compressor does not make the fridge hold cold longer, which is a common misunderstanding. What it does is run slower and more steadily, handle voltage fluctuation better and cope with the restart when power returns.

The features that matter, in order:

1. **Insulation and cooling retention.** Thicker walls and a good gasket slow warming. Some brands publish a cooling retention figure, such as hours the temperature stays below a safe level, though the test is done in lab conditions.
2. **Wide voltage operation.** Many Indian models can run on a wide window, such as 130-300 volts, so they do not need an external stabiliser. This protects the compressor from low-voltage damage.
3. **Inverter compressor.** Reduces the sharp current surge on restart and cuts power use, which matters if you run the refrigerator on a home inverter during an outage.
4. **Frost-free or direct-cool design.** Direct-cool models have simpler parts and cool well but need manual defrost. Frost-free models have fans and heaters that can fail, but offer convenience.

## Direct cool vs frost free in an outage-prone home

Direct-cool refrigerators often hold cold longer during short cuts because they chill the cabinet with a coil in direct contact with the walls and the freezer, which retains cold mass. They also tolerate voltage swings well and cost less, roughly Rs 15,000-26,000 for 180-250 litres. The downside is manual defrosting.

Frost-free models are more convenient and keep food uniformly cold, but the fan and defrost heater mean more components. The extra electronics can be sensitive to voltage spikes, so a good stabiliser or a wide-voltage model is important. Most families buying at Rs 28,000 and above choose frost-free.

## Using a home inverter

Many households in outage-prone areas run the refrigerator on a battery inverter. A refrigerator draws roughly 100-200 watts when running, but its compressor start surge can be several times higher, so the home inverter should be sized with headroom, often 900 VA or more with a good battery. An inverter compressor model draws less surge, which is easier on the battery system. Check your home inverter's rating before relying on it.

## Voltage stabiliser

If your supply swings widely, a voltage stabiliser rated for refrigerators protects the compressor. Many modern models claim stabiliser-free operation within a stated range, but if your voltage drops below that or spikes above it, a stabiliser is still safer. Use one with a time delay to avoid restarting the compressor too quickly after power returns.

## Practical habits that matter more than the model

- Do not open the door during an outage. A closed refrigerator can hold safe temperature for around four hours, and a full freezer for longer.
- Keep the freezer full. Frozen water bottles act as ice packs and fill empty space.
- Group items so you can take what you need in one go.
- Keep a thermometer inside. Meat and dairy above 5 degrees for more than a few hours should be discarded.
- Plug into a dedicated socket, not an extension board.
- After a power cut, wait a few minutes before restarting if you have no delay-protected stabiliser.

## An honest trade-off

A direct-cool model costs less and holds cold well, but you defrost by hand. A frost-free inverter model is easier to live with but costs more and has more parts to fail. If outages are long and frequent, a home inverter and a wide-voltage fridge matter more than any single feature.
