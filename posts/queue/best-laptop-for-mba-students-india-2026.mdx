## What MBA Students Need (Different From Engineering)

**Excel with large datasets** — financial modelling, valuation models, supply chain analyses use large spreadsheets. You need enough RAM (16GB ideal, 8GB minimum) and CPU single-core performance for responsive formula recalculation.

**PowerPoint / Keynote** — heavy decks with embedded charts, animations, and images. Both macOS and Windows handle this well, but macOS renders fonts and graphics more crisply on Retina displays.

**Zoom/Teams for 4–5 hours daily** — battery life matters enormously. The MacBook Air M3 delivers 14–16 hours; Windows ultrabooks manage 7–10 hours.

**Business software** — Bloomberg Terminal, Tableau, Tally, SAP — check if your B-school uses software that's Windows-only. Most top Indian B-schools (IIM, XLRI, ISB) work with both platforms.

## MacBook vs Windows for MBA: The Real Answer

**Choose MacBook Air M3 if:** your B-school peer group uses Macs, you want the best battery life, and budget is not the primary constraint.

**Choose Windows if:** your B-school uses Bloomberg, SAP, or Windows-only software, or budget is under ₹70,000.

**The financial model angle:** MBA students who go into consulting/finance use Excel 8 hours a day. Windows Excel still has features (Power Query, certain VBA macros, Bloomberg add-in) that Mac Excel lags on. If you're going into finance, check with current students at your specific program.

**Recommendation:** MacBook Air M3 for most Indian B-school students. ZenBook 14 OLED if budget is under ₹70,000. ThinkPad X1 Carbon if you're going into consulting.
