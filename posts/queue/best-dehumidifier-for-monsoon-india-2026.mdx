## Why monsoon needs one

Indian monsoon humidity often sits at 80 to 90 percent. Indoors that means musty wardrobes, mould on walls and leather goods, warped wood, slow-drying laundry and a damp feel that an AC alone does not always fix. A dehumidifier pulls moisture out and holds the room around 50 to 60 percent.

## 1. Compressor dehumidifiers

These work like a small refrigerator, cooling coils to condense water from the air. They are efficient in warm, humid conditions, which is exactly the monsoon. Capacity runs from 10 to 30 litres a day, priced roughly ₹12,000-30,000. They add a little heat to the room and make a low compressor hum. Brands that have listed them include Midea and Honeywell, among others, so verify availability. [Compressor dehumidifiers](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=compressor+dehumidifier+home+20+litre&tag=adifystore-21).

## 2. Desiccant dehumidifiers

These use a moisture-absorbing wheel and a heater, and work better in cool rooms. They are lighter and quieter, but use more power per litre in warm weather. They suit small closets and cold winter rooms more than a monsoon living room. [Desiccant dehumidifiers](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=desiccant+dehumidifier+portable&tag=adifystore-21).

## 3. Wardrobe and small-room units

Small Peltier units under 1 litre a day are cheap and weak, and not what this guide recommends. For a closet, silica gel boxes or hanging moisture absorbers cost far less. [Silica gel moisture absorber for wardrobes](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=wardrobe+moisture+absorber+silica+gel&tag=adifystore-21).

## Room size and litres per day

A rough guide, in humid conditions: 10 to 12 litres a day for a 150 to 250 sq ft room, 16 to 20 litres for 250 to 400 sq ft, and 25 to 30 litres for large halls or damp basements. Go one size up if the room is leaky, has wet walls, or you dry clothes indoors.

Remember that rated capacity assumes ideal conditions. In normal monsoon use, expect about 60 to 70 percent of that figure.

## Drain options

Most units have a removable tank of 2 to 4 litres. At high humidity, a 12 litre unit can fill the tank in a few hours, which means constant emptying. Look for a continuous drain hose outlet, so water flows into a bathroom drain or bucket without attention. Check that the hose is included and that the outlet sits low enough for gravity drainage. [Dehumidifier drain hose](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=dehumidifier+drain+hose&tag=adifystore-21).

## Running cost

A 12 litre compressor unit draws roughly 200 to 300 W. At 8 hours a day, that is around 2 units, or about ₹14-18 a day at ₹7-9 per unit, so under ₹500 a month if used daily. Use the humidistat to stop it at your target level, which cuts that down.

## Features that matter

An auto-restart function after power cuts, a humidistat with 40 to 80 percent settings, a clean-filter indicator, a full-tank shutoff and castor wheels. Check the noise level, usually 40 to 50 dB. A washable dust filter is helpful. A built-in laundry mode is useful for drying clothes indoors.

## Placement and use

Keep doors and windows closed while it runs. Place it away from walls, with 30 cm of clearance. Do not use in rooms under 18 degrees Celsius, where compressor units ice up. Empty and dry the tank to avoid smells. [Hygrometer to track room humidity](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=hygrometer+humidity+meter&tag=adifystore-21).

## Who should skip it

If damp is limited to one wardrobe, silica gel and ventilation are cheaper. If your AC is in dry mode and you keep rooms shut, you may not need one. If damp comes from a leak, fix the leak first.

## Recommendation

For a bedroom or living room, buy a 12 to 16 litre compressor dehumidifier with a drain hose option and a humidistat, and place it in the room you want to dry. Budget under ₹500 a month for electricity.
