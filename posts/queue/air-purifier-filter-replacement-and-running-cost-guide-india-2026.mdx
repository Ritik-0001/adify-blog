## The filters, in order

Most purifiers stack three layers. A pre-filter catches hair and large dust and is often washable. A HEPA filter captures fine particles such as PM2.5 and dander. An activated carbon layer adsorbs gases and odours. Some also add a formaldehyde or VOC layer.

Each layer wears out differently. Pre-filters last for years if cleaned. HEPA clogs gradually. Carbon saturates silently, so you may still have airflow but lose odour control.

## Typical filter life and what really happens

Brands claim 6 to 12 months, or more, for HEPA filters. Those numbers come from average conditions. In Delhi-NCR winters, with PM2.5 often high, filters can clog in 3 to 6 months. In a clean coastal city, they can last the full year. Smoky kitchens, incense, mosquito coils and pets also shorten life.

Use the purifier's filter indicator, if any, but also check the airflow and the filter colour. A dark, thick coating means replacement is due.

## Filter prices

Replacement sets for mainstream models range roughly ₹2,500-8,000. Premium brands can go higher. Some purifiers sell HEPA and carbon as one combined cartridge, which simplifies replacement but wastes the layer that is not yet worn. Others sell them separately, which is cheaper over time.

## Power use

Most home purifiers draw around 20 to 60 W on medium speeds, and 5 to 10 W on low or sleep mode. High speeds on large units can reach 80 to 100 W. Auto mode usually keeps the average low.

## A worked yearly cost example

Take a purifier drawing 40 W on average, running 10 hours a day for 365 days. That is 0.04 kW times 3,650 hours, or 146 units. At ₹8 per unit, electricity costs about ₹1,170 a year.

Now take a replacement set of ₹4,500 changed every 8 months. That is ₹6,750 a year. Total roughly ₹7,900 a year, or about ₹660 a month. A model with a ₹3,000 set that lasts 12 months and uses the same power costs about ₹4,200 a year. The cheaper-to-run model can save over ₹3,000 a year, which beats a ₹3,000 discount on the purchase price.

## HEPA versus carbon: what to replace and when

Replace HEPA when airflow drops, the indicator says so, or the PM2.5 reading stays high at normal speed. Replace carbon when odours return, usually every 6 to 12 months, sooner with cooking smells or smoke. Wash pre-filters monthly in households with pets or dust.

## Washable and permanent filters

Some purifiers advertise washable HEPA. These are rarely true HEPA performance after washing, so be careful. Permanent filters can save money but check what they actually capture. Electrostatic and ionic designs reduce filter cost but can produce ozone, so look for ozone-safe certification such as CARB.

## Tips to stretch filter life

Keep windows closed when running. Vacuum the pre-filter weekly. Place the unit away from the kitchen. Use sleep mode at night. Do not block the air intake with furniture. [Washable pre-filter sheets](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=air+purifier+pre+filter+sheet+cut+to+size&tag=adifystore-21).

## Who should skip a purifier

If you live in a low-pollution area with open ventilation, a purifier adds yearly costs for little benefit. In that case, put the money into cleaning and keeping dust sources in check.
