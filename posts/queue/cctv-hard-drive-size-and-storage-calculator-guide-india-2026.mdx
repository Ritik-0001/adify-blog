For a typical four-camera 4MP home CCTV system that records for about 30 days, a 4TB to 8TB surveillance hard drive is the right size, and you can work it out with one rule: each Mbps of camera bitrate writes roughly 10 GB per day. Bigger drives cost more but save you from overwritten evidence.

This guide is researched from NVR manuals and drive specifications, not from my own installations. Before buying, read your NVR's maximum supported drive size and its actual bitrate setting per camera, since both change the result.

## The calculator in one line

Storage per day in GB = bitrate in Mbps x 10.8. Multiply by the number of cameras and by the number of days you want to keep footage. Add around 10 percent spare for the file system.

Worked example: four 4MP cameras at 3 Mbps each is 12 Mbps total. That writes about 130 GB a day, so 30 days is around 3.9 TB. A 4TB drive is borderline, so an 8TB drive gives comfortable headroom and extra days.

## Where the bitrate comes from

Bitrate is set in the NVR or camera, and codec matters. H.265 uses roughly half the data of H.264 at the same quality. As rough H.265 numbers: 2MP around 1 to 2 Mbps, 4MP around 2 to 4 Mbps, and 8MP around 4 to 8 Mbps.

Fixed bitrate (CBR) gives predictable storage. Variable bitrate (VBR) saves space when nothing moves but spikes when it does, so calculate with the maximum figure.

## Continuous versus motion recording

Continuous recording captures everything and is the safest for evidence. Motion recording only saves events, which can cut storage by 60 to 90 percent in a quiet house, but it risks missing the first seconds before a trigger.

A hybrid is common: continuous on key cameras such as the main gate, motion on interior ones. Use a pre-record buffer of 5 to 10 seconds if your NVR supports it.

## Sizing examples for Indian homes

- Two cameras, 2MP, motion recording: a 2TB drive lasts well over a month.
- Four cameras, 4MP, continuous: 4TB lasts about 30 days; 8TB lasts about 60.
- Eight cameras, 4MP, continuous: 8TB lasts about 30 days; 10TB or more gives breathing room.
- Four cameras, 8MP, continuous: 8TB lasts around 25 to 35 days, depending on bitrate.

## Mistakes specific to CCTV storage

Using a desktop drive is the most common error; it may overheat and fail within a year. Another is forgetting that the NVR may only support up to 8TB or 10TB per bay; check the maximum. A third is leaving all cameras at maximum bitrate when a lower setting would look nearly identical.

Heat and power matter in India. Keep the NVR ventilated, away from a closed cupboard, and on a small UPS so a power cut does not corrupt the drive. See [UPS for CCTV NVR](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=ups+for+cctv+dvr+nvr&tag=adifystore-21).

## Backups and clips

A single drive is a single point of failure. For key clips, such as incidents, export to a pen drive, a [portable external hard disk](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=portable+external+hard+disk+2tb&tag=adifystore-21) or cloud. Some NVRs support a second drive in a mirrored setup, but that doubles the cost.

## Who should skip a big drive

If your cameras record only on motion and you review weekly, a 2TB drive is usually enough. If you only need a few days of history, do not pay for 8TB.

## Recommendation

Work out total bitrate, multiply by 10.8 GB per Mbps per day, and multiply by your retention target. For most four-camera 4MP homes, an 8TB surveillance drive such as WD Purple or SkyHawk is the safe choice. Use H.265, put the NVR on a UPS, and export key clips to a second location.
