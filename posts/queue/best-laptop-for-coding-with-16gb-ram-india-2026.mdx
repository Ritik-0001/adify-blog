## How to Choose a Coding Laptop

Check CPU and RAM. A recent multi-core processor and 16GB RAM suit most development, and heavy workloads like Android emulators or machine learning benefit from more RAM and a dedicated GPU.

Look at the keyboard and display. You will type and read all day, so a good keyboard and a sharp, bright screen of 14 to 16 inches matter.

Think about battery and weight. A lighter laptop with long battery life is easier to carry, and fast SSD storage of 512GB or more speeds up builds.

## Setting Up for Development

Install your tools through a package manager, keep the OS updated, use version control and back up your projects. Consider a Linux or WSL setup if your workflow needs it.

## What Else Matters for Developers

Beyond RAM, look for a good keyboard with decent key travel, a screen with at least 1920 by 1080 resolution and brightness around 300 nits, and enough ports for peripherals. A webcam and microphone matter for remote work. If you use virtual machines or containers heavily, consider 32GB RAM or a model with upgradeable memory slots.

## Who Should Skip This

If your work is mostly web pages and light scripts, you may be paying for more than you need, and a cheaper 8GB laptop with a good SSD can cope with that for a while. Buyers who do Android emulation, large builds or machine learning should skip a thin 16GB model with no upgrade path and look at more memory or a dedicated GPU. Do not pick purely on weight if the keyboard is poor.

## Recommendation

For most developers, a 16GB RAM laptop from Lenovo, ASUS or HP with a good keyboard and display is a sound choice, and a MacBook Air suits those who want battery life and macOS. Compare configurations and warranty on each listing.
