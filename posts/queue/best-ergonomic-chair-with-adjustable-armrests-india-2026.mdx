## 1D, 2D, 3D and 4D explained

The "D" counts the directions an armrest moves. A 1D armrest goes up and down. A 2D adds forward and backward sliding or inward and outward movement. A 3D adds a second axis, commonly angling or pivoting the pad so your forearms can turn in towards the keyboard. A 4D adds one more, such as moving sideways, which is the most flexible and the most expensive.

Do not assume a bigger number is always better: poorly built 4D armrests can wobble. A well-made 2D armrest beats a rickety 4D one.

## Why armrests matter for the shoulders

If armrests are too low, your arms hang and your shoulders and neck bear their weight. If too high, shoulders hike and you may get tension and trapezius pain. The right height is where your elbow rests at roughly 90 degrees with the shoulder relaxed. At a keyboard, the arms should rest lightly rather than sit heavily.

Armrests that move inward help narrow users or those using a compact keyboard; ones that rotate help for mouse-heavy work. People with neck or shoulder discomfort often benefit most from fine adjustment.

## Matching the desk height

Standard desks sit at around 72-75 cm. Many armrests cannot slide under a desk if they are too tall, and some collide with the desktop edge. If your armrests are too high to fit under the desk, you cannot sit close, and you will slump forward. Look for armrests that fold down or have a low minimum height, and check the underside height of your desk.

For a better desk fit, consider pairing the chair with an [adjustable standing desk](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=height+adjustable+standing+desk+electric&tag=adifystore-21) so the armrests can rest at the correct height when sitting and standing.

## Armrest material and feel

Hard plastic pads are durable but feel cold and can crack, while PU or soft-touch pads are more comfortable but may wear in a few years in humid heat. Fabric-wrapped pads can trap sweat. If you rest your forearms for a long time, softer pads and a wider surface help. Some chairs let you replace the armrest pad, which is useful over a five-year lifespan.

## Other features to pair with armrests

Armrests are only part of the picture. Pair them with adjustable lumbar support, a seat slide, and a tilt lock. A chair with sound lumbar and seat depth but poor armrests can be fixed; a chair with fancy armrests and no lumbar is not a bargain. ## Who should skip

Skip a chair with fixed armrests if you use different desks or have narrow shoulders. Skip a complex 4D system if you will never adjust it; a simple 2D with solid build costs less and works as well. Skip armrests altogether if you work on a high keyboard tray: some people prefer armless chairs that tuck fully under the desk.
