## How each design washes

A front load tumbles clothes through a horizontal drum, lifting them and dropping them into a small pool of water. It uses gravity and friction, which is gentle on fibres but thorough on dirt. A top load uses a pulsator or an agitator at the bottom or centre to churn clothes in a larger volume of water. It is faster and rougher, with more wear on delicate fabrics.

## Water and power

Front loaders typically use a third to half the water of a top loader per cycle, which matters in cities with water shortages and in flats that depend on tankers. They also heat water inside, so hot wash and steam are common. The cost is longer cycles, often 60-90 minutes on a normal wash, against 35-50 for a top load. Power use per wash is similar, but the heater on a hot cycle adds noticeably.

## Fabric care and cleaning

For woollens, silks, jeans and heavily soiled loads, front load wins. The drum motion gives better stain removal at the same temperature, and clothes stay in better shape. Top loads handle everyday cottons well but can tangle sarees and long dupattas, and are harder on delicate items unless you use a wash bag.

## Cost and repairs

Prices overlap at the mid-range. A basic 6.5 kg semi-automatic top loader costs less than a front loader, but fully automatic models in each type price similarly. Front loaders need more care: the door seal can collect lint and develop odour, the drum bearing and shock absorbers can wear, and parts are costlier. Top loaders tend to be cheaper to fix and tolerate rough use better.

## Space and installation

A front loader is usually 60 cm wide and deep with a door that swings open, so you need clearance. You can place a counter on top, which suits a utility area. A top loader needs overhead space to open the lid, so it cannot go under a shelf. Both need a water inlet, a drain and a levelled floor. Front loaders vibrate during spin, so put them on a firm floor and use anti-vibration pads.

## Things people overlook

- **Loading mid-wash:** most top loaders allow you to add clothes. Some front loaders have an add-clothes door, but not all.
- **Hard water:** both suffer from scale, but front loaders show it as limescale on the heater, so check descaling advice.
- **Smell:** front loaders need the door left open after use. If you will not do that, a top load is easier.
- **Capacity:** a 7 kg front load generally fits more than a 7 kg top load because of how the drum sits, but check the drum volume.
- **Drying:** a front load spins faster, often 1,200-1,400 rpm, and clothes come out drier.

## One honest trade-off

The front load cleans better but takes longer and costs more to repair. If your house runs a washing machine daily for clothes that are not particularly dirty, a good top load will serve you well for years. If your main complaint is that clothes do not look clean or colours fade, a front load solves it.
