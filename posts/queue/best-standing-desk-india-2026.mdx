## What Matters in a Standing Desk (India Edition)

**Stability at standing height** is everything. A desk that wobbles when you type at 90 cm ruins the whole point. Look for desks with cross-beam frames or dual motors — single-motor electric desks with no cross-beam sway noticeably.

**Weight capacity** matters if you have multiple monitors. A 24" monitor + laptop + accessories can hit 12–15 kg. Budget desks rated for 50 kg are usually lying — their real comfortable max is closer to 25–30 kg.

**Indian power supply:** Most electric desks now work on 220V/50Hz. Not an issue with any desk listed here, but cheap imports sometimes need a converter.

**Humidity:** Wooden tops in humid Indian cities (Mumbai, Chennai, coastal areas) can warp over time. MDF with laminate finish handles humidity better than raw wood veneer.

## What to Avoid

**No-brand desks under ₹8,000** — these have single motors and no cross-bracing. At full standing height they wobble enough to shake your coffee. The motor rattles after 6 months. Not worth it.

**L-shaped standing desks from unknown brands** — these need very precise assembly and the corners are structurally weak. If you want L-shaped, go Flexispot or nothing.

**Glass tops** — heavy, fingerprint magnets, and shatter risk if the frame motor stutters. Stick with laminate MDF or bamboo.

---

## The Ergonomics Part (Actually Important)

Buying the desk is step one. Using it correctly is step two. Most people buy a standing desk and then stand for 4 hours straight, which is worse than sitting.

The research-backed approach: **25 minutes sitting, 5 minutes standing** in the first month, then gradually work up to 50/50. Standing for 2+ hours continuously causes leg fatigue and lower back load that's nearly as bad as constant sitting.

Set memory presets to your exact sitting height (elbow at 90° when typing) and exact standing height (same rule). The difference should be about 30 cm for most people.
