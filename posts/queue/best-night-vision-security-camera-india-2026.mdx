## IR Night Vision vs Colour Night Vision

**Standard IR night vision:** Camera switches to black-and-white mode at night, uses infrared LEDs to illuminate the scene. Range: 15–60m depending on LED count. Image quality degrades at range edges. All detail visible but no colour.

**Colour night vision (smart illumination):** Uses white LED illuminators alongside IR. Provides colour images at night. Better for identifying clothing colour, vehicle colour, and facial features. Requires external LED or built-in white light. Brighter than standard IR but may cause glare concerns for neighbours.

**Starlight / low light:** Uses a larger sensor to capture available ambient light (street lights, building lights) in colour without additional illumination. Best for areas with some ambient lighting. Expensive in budget cameras.

For most Indian residential uses, standard IR (black-and-white night vision) is sufficient and cost-effective. Colour night vision is better for entrance monitoring where identifying clothing or vehicle colour matters.

## How to Choose: Wired vs Wireless Night Vision Camera

**Choose wired IP cameras (Hikvision, CP Plus, Dahua) if:**
- You want reliable, 24/7 recording
- You're doing a full security installation (4–16 cameras)
- You want professional installation with NVR storage

**Choose wireless cameras (TP-Link Tapo, Wyze) if:**
- You want quick self-installation without cabling
- You need 1–3 cameras for targeted monitoring
- You're renting and can't do permanent installation

**Recommendation:** Hikvision DS-2CD1143G2-I for professional installations. CP Plus 2MP for budget residential installs. TP-Link Tapo C310 for wireless, no-installation-required monitoring.
