## How to Choose an OTG for Baking

Check convection. A fan circulates hot air and bakes more evenly than plain heating elements, which matters for cakes and breads.

Look at capacity and rack layout. Forty-five to 60 litres suits most home bakers, and multiple rack positions help with different dishes. Ensure the tray fits your cake tins.

Check the temperature range and thermostat. A range up to 250 degrees Celsius handles most recipes, and a separate top and bottom heating control gives flexibility.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Skipping preheat.** Always preheat.

**Using dark tins without adjusting.** Lower the temperature if browning too quickly.

**Not checking the thermostat.** Use an oven thermometer if results seem off.

**Overcrowding.** Leave space for air.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A beginner baker.** A 52 litre convection OTG covers cakes, cookies and bread.

**A home baker who sells orders.** Choose 60 litres and a strong convection fan.

**A small kitchen.** Choose 45 litres and check dimensions.

## Baking Tips for OTG Beginners

Use the bottom rack for cakes so the top browns evenly, and rotate the tray halfway if your oven has hot spots. Preheat for at least ten minutes, and use an oven thermometer to check accuracy, since cheaper OTGs can run 10 to 20 degrees off. Choose light-coloured, heavy-gauge tins, which bake more evenly than thin dark ones.

If your cake browns too fast on top, lower the rack or cover it loosely with foil. For breads and pizza, use the convection fan if available, and keep the door closed as much as possible.

## Rotisserie and Grilling

Most OTGs include a rotisserie spit and a grill rack, which lets you cook kebabs and whole chicken. Line the tray under the grill to catch drips, and clean it after each use to avoid smoke.
