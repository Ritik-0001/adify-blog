## How to Choose a Solar Water Heater

Size by family. A rough guide is about 25 to 30 litres per person per day, so a family of four needs about 100 to 150 litres per day.

Check your roof and water. You need a shaded-free roof facing the sun and a strong roof structure, and hard water favours flat plate collectors and regular maintenance.

Look at warranty and installer. A long tank warranty and a reliable local installer matter as much as the brand.

## Roof Space and Structure

A typical home system needs a few square metres of shade-free roof space facing the sun, plus a stable base and a path for pipes to the bathrooms. Check that the roof can bear the weight of a filled tank, and insulate the pipes to reduce heat loss on the way down. In a flat, ask whether the society allows panels on the shared terrace before you pay anything.

## Hard Water and Maintenance

Hard water can scale collector tubes and tanks over time, so choose a system that suits your water and plan periodic descaling or flushing. Clean the collectors every few months, check pipes and insulation, and arrange a yearly visit from the installer, which keeps output steady and extends the life of the system.

## Who Should Skip This

A household that bathes mostly in the evening may find the tank has cooled by then unless the tank is well insulated and a backup element is fitted. Skip solar altogether if your roof is shaded for most of the day, or if you live in an apartment without private roof access, where a geyser or a heat pump is more realistic.

## Monsoon and Winter Expectations

Solar heating output falls on overcast days, so plan for the backup element to run during the rainy season and on cold, hazy mornings. Do not expect the same temperature in July as in April. Setting the backup on a timer, so that it warms the tank before the first bath, usually keeps hot water reliable without leaving the element on all day.
