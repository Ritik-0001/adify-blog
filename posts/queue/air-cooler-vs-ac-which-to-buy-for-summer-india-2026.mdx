## How each one actually cools

An AC removes heat and moisture from the room and throws the heat outside. A cooler works by evaporating water, which drops air temperature a few degrees but adds moisture. That is why a cooler feels wonderful in Jaipur in May and miserable in Mumbai or Kolkata, where the air is already close to saturated and evaporation barely happens.

An AC also needs a closed room. A cooler needs the opposite: a window or door open to let the damp air leave. If you close a room around a cooler, humidity climbs and the room turns clammy within an hour.

## The humidity test

Check your city's typical April-June humidity. Under roughly 40-50 percent, a cooler delivers a real drop. Between 50 and 65 percent, it feels better than a fan but nowhere near an AC. Above that, it is a fan with a water tank. Pre-monsoon weeks in most cities spike humidity, so a cooler that felt great in April can disappoint in June.

## Running cost over a summer

A large desert cooler draws roughly 150-250 watts. A 1.5 ton inverter AC averages somewhere around 700-1,000 watts once it settles, though the label rating is higher at startup. Assume 8 hours daily for 30 days and a tariff near ₹8 per unit: the cooler costs roughly ₹300-500 a month, the AC roughly ₹1,400-1,900. In a house with slab-based billing, the AC can also push you into a higher slab, so the real gap is often wider.

Add the purchase gap. A premium [large desert air cooler](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=desert+air+cooler+90+litre&tag=adifystore-21) sits near ₹15,000-20,000, an AC around ₹36,000-50,000 plus installation, copper piping and a stand, which can add several thousand rupees. So an AC costs two to three times as much up front and several times as much to run.

## Room size and how people live

A cooler can handle a 300-400 sq ft hall in dry weather because it moves a lot of air. An AC cools roughly 150-180 sq ft per 1.5 ton and needs the door shut. If your family drifts between kitchen, balcony and hall, a cooler tolerates open doors, while an AC wastes electricity every time someone walks out.

Cooler upkeep is real. You refill the tank daily, drain it weekly, and replace pads each season or two. Water that sits for days smells and breeds mosquitoes. If you travel a lot, an AC is lower maintenance.

## Who should skip the AC

Skip it if your room has a leaky window, a tin or asbestos roof, or a landlord who forbids drilling. A split AC in a poorly insulated top-floor room never catches up, and a [window AC](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=1.5+ton+window+ac+5+star&tag=adifystore-21) in the roughly ₹30,000-38,000 range is the compromise where drilling is not allowed. Also skip it if your supply is unstable and you will not buy a stabilizer.

## Who should skip the cooler

Skip it if you live in a coastal city, have an allergy to damp air, need a sealed room for a baby or a work-from-home call, or sleep in a small bedroom. People also regret coolers in apartments where noise matters, since the fan and pump can be loud.

If you still want one for a dry-climate hall, look at [tower coolers with ice chambers](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=tower+air+cooler+with+ice+chamber&tag=adifystore-21) for small spaces, but prefer a full desert cooler for anything over 200 sq ft.

## Can you own both?

Many dry-city households do exactly this: a desert cooler for the hall from March to mid-May, and an AC in the bedroom for the humid weeks before monsoon. That split gives the cheapest bill with a cool night, and it is more sensible than buying one large AC to cover a hall you only sit in during the day.

For sleepers who dislike direct airflow, a [1 ton inverter split AC](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=1+ton+inverter+split+ac&tag=adifystore-21) in the roughly ₹32,000-40,000 range is the quiet option. If you want to keep the AC bill down, pair it with a [5-star inverter AC](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=5+star+inverter+ac+split+1+ton&tag=adifystore-21) sized correctly, since an oversized unit short-cycles and wastes power while a 1 ton unit suits a 100-120 sq ft room.

## Verdict

Humid city, bedroom, closed room: buy the inverter AC and size it properly. Dry city, big hall, tight budget and a tolerance for refilling water: buy a large premium desert cooler. In between, if you can afford the AC and have a bedroom to cool, the AC is the purchase you will not regret at 2 a.m. in June.
