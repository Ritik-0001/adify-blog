## Why HDMI 2.1 is the deciding spec

Consoles output only over HDMI, and 4K at 120Hz requires the bandwidth of HDMI 2.1, around 48 Gbps. Some monitors list HDMI 2.1 but implement a reduced bandwidth version that cannot carry 4K 120Hz with full colour, so read the details. Look for phrases like 48Gbps, 4K 120Hz and VRR on the HDMI port, not just a 2.1 label.

Many monitors have only one HDMI 2.1 port, so you can attach just one console at full speed. Check the port count if you own both a PS5 and an Xbox.

## What your console actually supports

Both PS5 and Xbox Series X support 4K 120Hz in games that offer it, and both support variable refresh rate (VRR) over HDMI 2.1. Not every game reaches 4K 120Hz; many run at 60 or use dynamic resolution to hit 120. PS5 also supports 1440p output on compatible displays after a software update, and Xbox supports 1440p at 120Hz as well, which suits cheaper 1440p monitors.

Neither console supports G-Sync branding the way a PC does, but both work with HDMI VRR, so look for VRR support listed on the monitor.

## Input lag and response

Console gamers care about input lag, the delay between pressing a button and seeing the result. A good gaming monitor in game mode has input lag of a few milliseconds, far lower than many TVs. Check independent measurements and avoid models with heavy processing. Turn off motion smoothing and image enhancement modes on the monitor.

Response times quoted at 1ms are best-case grey-to-grey numbers, so do not rely on them alone.

## Panel type and HDR

IPS panels give good colour and viewing angles. VA panels have higher contrast but can smear in fast scenes. OLED monitors offer the best contrast and response but cost more. HDR on a monitor varies enormously: HDR400 is a marketing label that often adds little, while real HDR needs high peak brightness and local dimming, usually found on pricier models.

Do not pay extra for an HDR badge on a basic IPS panel. Look instead at actual measured brightness in reviews.

## Who should skip 4K 120Hz

Skip it if you play mainly fast competitive games where console frame rate, not resolution, matters, since 1080p 120Hz monitors cost less. Skip it if you own a PS4 or older consoles, which cannot use these features.
