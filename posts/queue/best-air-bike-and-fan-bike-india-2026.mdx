## How fan resistance works

A big fan wheel spins as you pedal and push-pull the handles. Air pushes back, and the harder you move, the more resistance you feel. There is no knob to adjust. Your effort sets the difficulty. That makes air bikes popular for intervals, such as 20 seconds hard and 40 seconds easy, and for short 15 to 20 minute full-body sessions.

Because arms and legs both drive the fan, the workout engages upper body as well as lower body, unlike a regular exercise bike where the handlebars stay fixed.

Belt-drive models run quieter than chain-driven models and need less lubrication. They are less common and priced a bit higher. ## Intensity: what to expect

Air bikes feel harder than they look. A beginner may struggle to last 10 minutes at a steady pace. Start with 5 minute sessions and build up. Heart rate climbs fast, so people with heart conditions, high blood pressure or recent illness should check with a doctor first.

## Noise, space and sweat

The fan creates a constant whoosh that rises with effort. It is louder than a magnetic bike and can disturb people in the next room. A typical air bike measures about 120 to 130 cm long and 55 to 65 cm wide, and the front fan throws air at your legs, which is nice in summer and chilly in winter.

Put the bike on a rubber mat to reduce vibration and protect the floor. Sweat drips are common, so keep a towel. [Exercise bike floor mat](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=exercise+bike+mat+floor+protector&tag=adifystore-21).

## Chain, belt and maintenance

Chain-driven bikes need occasional lubrication and tension checks. Belt drives need little but can slip if overtightened or worn. Check the seat and handle adjustments for your height, and that the pedals have straps or cages.

## Who should skip an air bike

If you want gentle rehab cycling, pick a magnetic or recumbent bike. If you share walls with sleeping neighbours early in the morning, the noise may be a problem. If you hate intense workouts, you will not use it. People with shoulder issues should be cautious about the moving handles.
