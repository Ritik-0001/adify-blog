## Why a Monitor Arm is Necessary for Standing Desks

With a fixed monitor stand or raised platform:
- Desk at sitting height (72cm): Monitor at eye level ✓
- Desk at standing height (110cm): Monitor now 38cm too low ✗

Your neck cranes downward when standing — the opposite ergonomic problem of a standing desk. To get correct ergonomics both sitting and standing, the monitor needs independent height adjustment from the desk surface.

A monitor arm gives the monitor its own height range (typically ±40–50cm of adjustment) independent of desk height. One movement of the arm repositions the screen to eye level as you switch between sitting and standing.

**Additional benefit:** Monitor arm reclaims the desk surface where the stand footprint sat — creates 25–30cm of extra depth for a keyboard, notepad, or phone.

## Checking VESA Compatibility Before Buying

Most monitors have a VESA mount pattern on the back — 4 bolt holes in a square arrangement. Standard patterns: 75×75mm (small monitors, up to 24 inches) and 100×100mm (most 24–32 inch monitors). Some ultrawide and premium monitors use 100×200 or larger non-standard patterns.

Check your monitor's manual or look at the back plate for the VESA mount. Verify the arm is compatible before purchasing.

**Monitors without VESA mount:** Some curved monitors and thin-frame designs lack VESA mounts. An VESA adapter bracket (₹500–₁,500) may retrofit VESA capability to monitors that have mounting holes but not VESA pattern.
