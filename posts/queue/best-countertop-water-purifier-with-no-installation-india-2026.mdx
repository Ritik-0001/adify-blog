## Two very different types

A **manual-fill countertop purifier** has a top tank. You pour tap water in, gravity or a pump pushes it through the filters, and the clean water collects below. No plumbing, no power for gravity models. Capacity is limited to the tank, often 8 to 20 litres per fill.

A **plumbed countertop RO** sits on the counter but takes water from a tap through a hose or diverter and sends reject water to the sink. It gives the usual RO quality and automatic refilling but needs a tap fitting, and the fitting must suit your tap's thread.

Most true RO performance comes from the plumbed type. Manual-fill models are typically gravity UF, carbon or UV and do not remove dissolved salts, so they suit only soft municipal water.

## TDS decides the type

If your water is municipal and TDS is under roughly 200, a [gravity-based UV plus UF purifier](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=gravity+water+purifier+uv+uf&tag=adifystore-21) works and needs no electricity or plumbing. The cheaper gravity models cost less than this post's price floor, so they appear only as a "you may not need RO" note. For borewell or tanker water above 500 TDS, a gravity filter will not fix the taste or salts, and you need a plumbed RO.

Test first with a TDS meter. Landlords seldom object to a unit that sits on a counter, but a hose through a cabinet may need permission.

## Capacity and daily use

A family of four drinks and cooks with roughly 15-20 litres a day. A manual-fill unit with a 10-litre purified tank therefore needs a refill once or twice a day, which suits people who are home but not those who leave for long stretches. A plumbed RO with a 7-10 litre storage tank refills on its own, which is better for working households.

Counter space matters. These units are bulky, roughly the footprint of a small microwave, and need clearance above for filling or opening the lid.

## Running costs and maintenance

Gravity filters use cartridges that last a few months, and they are cheap to replace. Plumbed RO needs yearly membrane and pre-filter service like any RO, which costs a few thousand rupees. Carry the filters with you when you move, since many brands sell a [water purifier moving kit](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=water+purifier+installation+kit&tag=adifystore-21) for reinstalling elsewhere.

Reject water from a plumbed unit needs a drain hose into the sink. Make sure the hose does not loop above the outlet, or the drain backs up.

## Safety and hygiene

Always wash the storage container regularly. Manual-fill units collect algae and biofilm if the tank is not cleaned monthly. Keep the lid shut and place the unit away from direct sun.

Do not fill a UV-based unit with tank water that is visibly muddy, since particles shield microbes from UV light. Pre-filter the tank water through a cloth or sediment filter first.

## Who should skip countertop units

Large families with high usage may find a gravity unit tedious. Households with very hard water cannot rely on a gravity filter. Those who live somewhere permanent, and whose landlord allows drilling, will get a better and cheaper long-term result from a [wall-mount RO plus UV purifier](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=wall+mount+ro+uv+water+purifier+10+litre&tag=adifystore-21), which has a larger tank and better service coverage.

Check your tap thread and diverter fit, the power requirement and the reject water ratio. Ask the seller whether installation is truly free or whether the technician charges for a connector. Look for models with a clear tank so that you can see the water level.
