## Loaf size

Bread makers are rated by loaf weight, usually 500 g, 750 g, 1 kg or 1.5 kg. A 750 g loaf yields around 12 to 14 slices, enough for a small family for a day or two. A 1 kg loaf suits four people or more. A 500 g loaf is fine for one or two people who finish bread quickly, since homemade bread without preservatives goes stale faster than shop loaves.

Check whether the machine bakes a horizontal or vertical loaf. Vertical loaves are compact and tall, while horizontal ones look more like shop bread and slice more easily into sandwiches.

## Programs worth having

- Basic white and whole wheat for everyday loaves
- Dough-only, useful for pizza, buns and rotis
- Gluten-free, for people with intolerance
- Jam and cake, as extras
- Rapid bake for shorter cycles

Whole wheat and multigrain bread, popular in India, usually need longer cycles, so check that the machine has a proper whole wheat program. A dough-only mode adds the most flexibility, as you can shape and bake in an oven. See [bread maker with dough function](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=bread+maker+with+dough+function&tag=adifystore-21).

## Delay timer

A delay timer lets you load ingredients at night and wake up to fresh bread. In Indian summers, avoid using perishable ingredients such as milk or eggs with a long delay, since they can spoil before baking starts. Use water and dry ingredients for overnight cycles.

## Noise and kitchen use

Kneading is noisy, often with thumping for 20 to 30 minutes. A delay timer helps you pick when the noise happens. Place the machine on a stable, non-slip surface away from the edge of the counter, since vibration can make it walk.

## Paddle and pan

A single kneading paddle is common. Some better models have a collapsible paddle that folds before baking to leave a smaller hole in the loaf. The pan is usually non-stick, so avoid metal utensils. Replacement pans and paddles should be available through the brand; check before purchasing. ## Indian flour and yeast realities

Indian atta and maida vary in protein content. Bread machines are designed for strong bread flour, so many users get better results with a bread flour or by mixing atta and maida. In humid or hot weather, dough rises faster, so use slightly less yeast. ## Running costs

A bread maker draws roughly 500 to 700 watts while baking, and a loaf takes 2 to 4 hours. Electricity use per loaf is modest, but ingredients such as flour, yeast and sometimes milk or butter cost money. Home bread is not always cheaper than shop bread, but it is fresher and free of preservatives.

## Who should skip a bread maker

- People who bake bread less than once a month
- Kitchens with no spare counter space
- Buyers who want bakery-style crusty bread, since a bread maker yields a uniform soft loaf
- Anyone who prefers to knead dough by hand and bake in an oven

A stand mixer plus oven gives more control for bakers who enjoy the process, while a bread maker gives convenience.

## Honest trade-off

A bread maker is convenient, but the loaf has a uniform shape and a paddle hole, and the machine takes space. If you want pizza dough, rotis and dinner rolls as well, the dough program will earn its place; if you only want sandwich loaves, a good local bakery may suit better.
