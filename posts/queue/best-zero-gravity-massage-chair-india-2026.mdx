## How to Choose a Zero Gravity Chair

Check the recline range. Look for a zero-gravity position and several recline angles so you can find one that feels natural.

Look at the track and airbags. An SL-track follows your spine, and airbags add pressure to arms and legs.

Think about your size. Check the user height range, the seat width and the weight limit before buying.

## What Zero Gravity Does and Does Not Do

The name comes from a recline that puts your knees roughly level with your chest, which takes pressure off the lower back. It is a comfort position, not a medical treatment. If your back or neck feels worse after a session, shorten it, lower the intensity and speak to a doctor rather than pushing through.

## Features That Add to the Zero-Gravity Experience

Heat on the lower back, foot rollers, calf airbags and shoulder airbags all make the reclined position more effective. Bluetooth speakers and app control are nice extras but not essential. Look at the chair's ability to hold several recline angles so that you can find the one that feels best for your body.

## Measuring Your Room for a Reclining Chair

Measure the reclined length, not the upright footprint, and add clearance behind the backrest if the chair does not slide forward. Check the doorway and any turns in the corridor, and keep the chair off damp walls. Wipe the upholstery with a soft cloth and keep the warranty paperwork somewhere easy to find.

## Who Should Skip This

If your main complaint is a stiff neck or sore shoulders, a zero-gravity chair is more than you need, since a well-reviewed shoulder massager or a physiotherapist visit costs far less. Skip it as well if the only place for it is a narrow room, because a chair you cannot fully recline will never deliver the position you paid for.
