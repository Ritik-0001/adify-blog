## How to Choose a Wet and Dry Vacuum

Match the tank size. A 15 to 20 litre tank suits most homes, and larger tanks suit garages and workshops.

Check suction and filters. Strong suction and a washable filter matter, and a foam filter is used for wet pickup.

Look at the accessories. A long hose, crevice tool, floor nozzle and blower port make the machine more useful.

## Using It for Spills and Outdoor Cleaning

A wet and dry vacuum is useful for water spills after a leak, cleaning terraces and balconies after the monsoon, picking up leaves and clearing dust from car interiors. Fit the correct filter before each wet or dry job, and empty the tank promptly so water does not sit and smell.

## Safety, Storage and Care

Do not vacuum flammable liquids or hot embers, switch off the machine before emptying and keep the motor head dry. After wet use, rinse the tank and let the filter dry fully before storing. Keep the cable untangled and avoid leaving the machine in a damp corner.

## Who Should Skip This

A drum vacuum is bulky and louder than a household stick or canister model, so it is a poor daily cleaner for a small flat with limited storage. If your only need is light dust on tiles, a cordless stick or a robot vacuum is easier to live with, and you can keep this category for terrace, garage and spill duty.

## Noise, Cable Reach and Wheels

Wet and dry vacuums are louder than household cleaners, so plan to run them when the family is not on a call or asleep. A drum on small wheels is easy to pull along a hall, but check the cable length against the distance from your nearest socket to the terrace or balcony, as an extension lead near water is best avoided.
