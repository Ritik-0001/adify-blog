## What dual zone means

A dual zone air fryer has two separate cooking compartments, each with its own basket, and usually its own heating element and fan. You can set different temperatures and times for each, or use a sync mode that makes both finish together even if foods need different times. That is the big advantage over a single basket, where you have to cook in batches.

## Sync cook and match cook

Two functions matter most:

- **Sync finish**, where the machine delays the shorter-cook basket so both finish together. Ideal for cooking paneer tikka in one basket and aloo wedges in the other.
- **Match cook**, where both baskets copy the same settings, handy when you want to double a batch of the same food.

If a model lacks sync, you will still be able to run baskets independently, but you manage timing yourself.

## Capacity in real terms

Total capacity is usually split into two baskets of roughly 3.5 to 4.5 litres each, around 7 to 9 litres overall. Each basket fits about 500 to 700 g of fries or a few chicken pieces. ## Why Indian cooks like it

Dual zone suits household routines where one basket handles tandoori-style chicken or tikka while the other crisps potatoes, cauliflower or spring rolls. Because baskets are separate, spices and smells do not cross over, which matters if one dish is vegetarian and the other not.

## Counter space reality

These machines are wide, often 40 cm or more across, and need space behind and above for hot air exhaust. In a small kitchen, you may need to store it in a cabinet and take it out for each use, which discourages daily use. Measure before buying, including the clearance beneath wall cabinets.

## Cleaning

Both baskets and crisper plates are usually non-stick and dishwasher safe, but many people hand wash for longevity. Two baskets mean two sets of parts, so washing takes slightly longer. Avoid abrasive scrubbers on non-stick coatings. Browse [air fryer liners reusable](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=air+fryer+silicone+liners&tag=adifystore-21) to reduce mess.

## Power use

Dual zone models draw roughly 1,700 to 2,400 watts, so check your socket and the MCB rating in the kitchen. Running both baskets at once pulls more power, which could trip older wiring or an inverter with limited capacity. Use a dedicated 16 amp socket if possible.

## Honest trade-off: dual zone versus a big single basket

A single large basket cooks one big batch, while dual zone offers flexibility but with smaller volumes per basket. For big whole items such as a whole chicken, a single large basket or oven-style fryer fits better. For mixed meals, dual zone wins.

## Who should skip a dual zone

- Couples or singles, since a smaller single basket costs less and uses less counter
- Small kitchens with little space
- Buyers who mostly cook large single items, such as whole chicken
- Households with limited wiring capacity

## What to check on the listing

Look for independent temperature control per basket, a clearly stated total capacity in litres, a shake reminder if you want it, and an easy-to-reach crisper plate. A window is rare on dual models, so be prepared to pull baskets out to check food.
