## Who needs 14 place settings

A place setting is a standard set of plate, bowl, glass, cutlery and serving pieces. A 12 place dishwasher suits a family of four to five. A 14 place model suits families of six or more, joint households, homes that cook large batches or those that host guests often. It is the largest standard size sold in India, around 60 cm wide.

If you have a small family and wash only breakfast and dinner dishes, a 12 place is enough, and a 14 place will mean running half loads, wasting water and power.

## Place settings are not vessel capacity

The number tells you how many European-style place settings fit, not how many Indian vessels. Indian cooking uses pressure cooker bodies, kadhais, tawas, steel tiffins, big thalis and tall steel glasses. These rarely sit neatly in racks made for plates.

What you should check:

- **Bottom rack height:** can it hold a 28 cm thali and a pressure cooker lid?
- **Third rack or cutlery tray:** many 14 place models have a top cutlery tray that frees the second rack for glasses.
- **Adjustable or fold-down tines:** let you lay out a large pot or kadhai flat.
- **Removable upper rack:** lets you fit a tall pot at the bottom.
- **Spray arm coverage:** makes sure water reaches the inside of deep vessels.

Some vessels still belong outside: nonstick tawas, cast iron, wooden spoons, thin aluminium and anything with a glued handle will degrade in a dishwasher.

## Cycles that matter

- **Intensive or heavy wash:** at higher temperature and longer, for oily Indian cookware.
- **Eco or normal:** for daily loads, with lower power and water.
- **Quick wash:** a short cycle for lightly soiled plates.
- **Half load:** useful when you cannot fill a 14 place.
- **Pre-rinse or soak:** helps with dried-on dal and rice.

A good cycle uses around 10-14 litres of water, far less than hand washing a similar load.

## Installation and running costs

A 14 place dishwasher needs a water inlet, a drain, a power point and a 60 cm by 60 cm space with a height of around 85 cm. It uses around 1.5-2 kW when heating, so a dedicated 15 amp socket is sensible. A typical eco cycle consumes around 1 kWh. At Rs 7-9 per unit, that is about the cost of a few hours of cooking gas, and usually less than the cost of the extra hot water used in hand washing.

Detergent tablets, rinse aid and dishwasher salt (where water is hard) add ongoing cost, usually a few thousand rupees a year.

## Honest trade-offs

- A 14 place unit is big. If your kitchen is narrow, you may lose cabinet space.
- Large machines need more detergent and water for half loads, so only buy one if you can fill it daily.
- Cleaning pressure cooker bodies and heavy kadhais can be uneven, so you may need to pre-rinse.
- Noise ranges from around 42 to 52 dB, which is quiet in a closed kitchen but audible in an open plan.
