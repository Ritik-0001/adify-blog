## How a retrofit lock works

A retrofit lock mounts on the inside of the door. It replaces or covers your thumb-turn and uses a small motor to turn the existing deadbolt. Outside, your original key still works, and you add a keypad, fingerprint reader or app control.

Installation is usually adhesive or clamp-based, with no drilling. When you move, you peel it off and the door returns to its original state, which is exactly what landlords want.

## The compatibility test before anything else

Retrofit locks suit doors with a standard deadbolt or mortise lock that has a thumb-turn on the inside. They do not suit:

- Multi-point locking doors common in newer flats, where several bolts move at once.
- Locks with an interior key-only cylinder and no thumb-turn.
- Doors that need a hard push to latch, since the motor may stall.

Photograph your inside lock and compare the shape to the maker's compatibility list. Some brands send a trial adapter set.

## Limits you should know

Retrofit locks do not change your door's strength. They add convenience, not security. The original deadbolt remains the main barrier, so if it is weak, upgrading is better than adding a smart layer.

Battery life is typically several months on AA or CR123 cells, depending on usage. Keep a spare set, and know where the manual override key is. Wi-Fi control often needs a hub, and without it you use Bluetooth within range only.

## Door and landlord reality

Check the lease. Many landlords are fine with a removable device, but some forbid altering the lock. Take photos before installing, and keep the original thumb-turn if it gets replaced. ## Power cuts and Wi-Fi outages

The lock itself works on batteries, so a power cut does not lock you out. Remote features need Wi-Fi though, so keep a mechanical key and an offline method such as a keypad. A smart plug-based UPS for the hub is a small extra worth considering. See [mini UPS for Wi-Fi router](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=mini+ups+for+wifi+router&tag=adifystore-21).

## Who should skip a retrofit lock

Skip it if your door uses a multi-point lock, if you do not have a thumb-turn, or if your landlord forbids changes. In those cases, a smart door sensor and camera may be a better rental security upgrade.

Also skip if you only want keyless entry occasionally: a simple key lockbox costs far less.
