## Power sharing is the spec nobody reads

Most 2 zone cooktops quote a total power, say 3,500 W, and then split it between zones. When both are on high, the unit often lowers each zone's output so that the total stays within the limit. If one zone is rated 2,000 W and the other 1,500 W, you may get only one at full strength when both run. That is fine for dal plus sabzi, but if you plan to boil a big vessel of milk beside a wok of fried food, one will run weaker than the listing's headline.

Some premium hobs carry a booster for one zone that borrows from the other. Look for wording such as "power management" or "power sharing" and read the wattage per zone, not just the total.

## Cookware that actually works

Induction heats only magnetic bases. A fridge magnet that sticks firmly to the bottom of the vessel is the quick test. Cast iron, magnetic stainless steel and induction-labelled non-stick pans work. Pure aluminium, copper and glass do not, unless they carry a steel disc on the base.

Round-bottomed kadhais and tawas with a warped base lose contact with the glass and heat unevenly, so pick flat-bottomed induction kadhais. Chapatis on a flat induction tawa work well, but puffing a phulka directly on the glass is not recommended, so keep that for a gas stove if you want the full flame technique. ## Wiring and the electrical reality

A 3,500 W appliance draws around 15 A on a 230 V supply. That is beyond a standard 6 A socket and close to the limit of a 16 A circuit. Ask a licensed electrician to check three things: the copper wire gauge (2.5 sq mm is the usual minimum), a dedicated MCB of the right rating, and a socket and plug that are rated for 16 A. Do not use an extension board.

Society wiring in older apartments can be thin and aluminium. If your flat was wired decades ago, price in a rewiring from the meter or distribution board to the kitchen before you buy the cooktop. ## Who should skip a 2 zone induction cooktop

If your kitchen already has a 4-burner gas hob and you only need a secondary burner for tea, a single-zone portable unit at lower power is enough, and a double burner would only add wiring cost. People who cook large batches in wide pots for events also find 2 zone hobs slow, since the zone diameter limits how well big vessels heat. If power cuts are frequent and you have no inverter that supports high load, keep gas as the primary.
