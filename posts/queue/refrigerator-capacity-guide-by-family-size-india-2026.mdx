## How to Choose a Capacity

Start with family size. A rough guide is about 50 to 60 litres per person, plus extra if you shop in bulk or entertain often.

Think about your habits. If you buy groceries weekly and store leftovers, add some margin, and if you shop daily, you can go smaller.

Check the freezer. If you store a lot of frozen items, look for a larger freezer or a convertible model.

## Buying Too Big and Too Small

An undersized fridge forces extra shopping trips and crammed shelves that block airflow. An oversized one wastes floor space and runs a mostly empty cabinet, which is a poor use of electricity. Use the per-person rule above, then decide whether your cooking style pushes you one step up or down.

## Dimensions, Energy and Warranty

Capacity is only part of the story. Measure the width, depth and height of the space, leave clearance for ventilation and door swing, and check the route from the entrance to the kitchen. A higher star rating means lower running cost, and for an appliance that runs all day for ten years the rating matters nearly as much as the price. Check the compressor warranty and ask about service in your city.

## Who Should Skip This

If you are single, away most of the week or eat out regularly, the bigger sizes in this guide will be wasteful, and a compact model near the bottom of the range fits better. People planning a kitchen renovation should also pause, because the final cabinet cut-out should drive the size choice, not the other way round.

## When Guests and Festivals Change the Maths

Many Indian homes need extra space only a few weeks a year, around festivals or when relatives stay. Buying a bigger fridge for those weeks means paying for empty shelves the rest of the year. A convertible model, or a secondary small freezer, can cover the peak without oversizing the main refrigerator.

## Recommendation

For most families, 250 to 350 litres is the sweet spot. Go larger if you shop in bulk or have a joint family. Compare specifications and warranty on each listing.
