For a 4-8 camera CCTV system, a 1 kVA online UPS (roughly ₹15,000-25,000) or an inverter with a tubular battery gives backup for hours rather than minutes, and a small DC mini UPS suits only a router and a few cameras. Size the UPS by the total watts of the NVR, switch and cameras, not by the number of cameras alone.

This guide is researched from typical CCTV power specs and standard electrical arithmetic, not from installing systems. Before buying, read the label on your NVR, PoE switch and power adapters, since the numbers vary widely between brands.

## Start with the load

Add up watts. An 8-channel NVR with one hard disk draws roughly 15-30 watts. A PoE camera draws roughly 4-12 watts, depending on features like IR and pan-tilt. Eight cameras might add 60-90 watts, and a PoE switch adds its own 10-20 watts of overhead. A typical home system therefore draws roughly 100-150 watts in total.

Analog systems with a DVR use separate 12V adapters for each camera, usually 1 amp each, so eight cameras draw roughly 8 x 12 = 96 watts at maximum, though real use is lower. Add a modem and router at 15-25 watts if you want remote viewing during cuts.

## Runtime arithmetic

Backup hours equal usable battery watt-hours divided by load watts. A typical 1 kVA UPS with a single 12V 9Ah internal battery holds roughly 108 Wh, giving only about 40 minutes at 100 watts at best, usually less. That is why external battery packs matter: a 12V 100Ah battery stores around 1,200 Wh, which at 120 watts runs roughly 6-7 hours after losses.

So a CCTV UPS is really a battery decision. The bigger the battery, the longer the cameras stay alive. For a longer-cut area, consider an inverter such as a [900VA inverter with battery](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=inverter+with+battery+for+cctv&tag=adifystore-21) feeding the CCTV circuit along with a few lights.

## AC UPS or DC UPS

An AC UPS gives standard 230V output, so it can power the NVR, the PoE switch and the router through their normal adapters. Every AC-to-DC conversion wastes some energy though, so efficiency is lower for a small load.

A DC mini UPS takes mains in and gives out 12V (or 9V and 5V) directly to cameras and routers, with fewer conversion losses and long runtimes from a small battery. These cost only a few thousand rupees, which is below this site's price floor, so I list them only as a skip-the-AC-UPS note for homes with two or three analog cameras and a router. ## PoE considerations

PoE cameras are powered by the NVR's PoE ports or a PoE switch, which concentrates the entire load at one point. That is convenient: one UPS powers the NVR, the switch and every camera. But the PoE budget draws more than the label watts under load, so add around 20 percent margin.

Do not put a laser printer or other heavy devices on the same UPS. Keep the CCTV circuit clean, with only the recorder, switch, router and perhaps a monitor.

## Online or line-interactive

For recorders with hard disks, abrupt shutdowns damage the drive and corrupt footage. Voltage dips from a poor supply also stress the drive. An online UPS gives clean power and zero transfer time, which is the better protection for a 24/7 recorder. A line-interactive UPS is acceptable if your supply is stable, and a [line interactive UPS 1kVA](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=line+interactive+ups+1kva+for+cctv&tag=adifystore-21) costs less, though small units fall below this site's price floor.

## Battery care

CCTV runs 24/7, so the UPS is always online. Sealed lead-acid batteries inside a UPS typically last 3-4 years in constant float use. Plan to replace them on that schedule, and keep the UPS in a cool, ventilated spot, since heat shortens battery life dramatically.

Test the system once a month by switching off the mains and watching whether the cameras stay live, then measure the actual runtime.

## Wiring tips

Run the UPS output to a surge-protected extension, and plug in the NVR, switch and router. Label the sockets. Keep the modem and the router on the UPS too, since cameras with remote viewing are useless if the internet is down.

## Who should skip a big setup

A flat with a single camera and a doorbell does not need a 1 kVA online UPS; a small DC unit is enough. Shops with long power cuts, though, need a proper battery bank, since a few minutes of backup does not help when a cut lasts hours.
