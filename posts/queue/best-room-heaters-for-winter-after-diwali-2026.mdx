## How to Choose a Room Heater

Match the heater type to the room. Oil-filled radiators suit bedrooms because they heat evenly and quietly. Fan heaters heat quickly but dry the air. Halogen heaters warm a person, not the room.

Check the wattage. Two thousand watts or more is common for a medium room, and higher wattage means higher electricity use.

Look at safety features such as a thermostat, tip-over switch and overheat protection. Keep heaters away from curtains and bedding.

## Quick FAQ

**Are room heaters expensive to run?** Higher wattage means higher electricity use, so use a thermostat and heat only the room in use.

**Oil-filled or fan heater?** Oil-filled heaters are quieter and heat evenly; fan heaters are cheaper and heat quickly.

**Is it safe to run overnight?** Some oil-filled heaters are designed for it, but check the manufacturer's guidance and use a thermostat.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Using a heater that is too weak for the room.** A small heater in a large hall will barely change the temperature. Match wattage and fin count to the room size.

**Placing it near curtains or bedding.** Keep heaters at least a metre from flammable items, and never use them to dry clothes.

**Leaving fan heaters running unattended.** Fan and halogen heaters get hot quickly and should be switched off when you leave the room or go to sleep, unless the manufacturer states otherwise.

**Ignoring ventilation.** Fully sealed rooms with a heater can feel stuffy. Allow some fresh air, and do not use gas or fuel heaters indoors.

## Running Costs and Safety Tips

Electric heaters draw significant power, so a thermostat is important. Heat the room you are in rather than the whole house, and close doors to keep warmth in. Use a heavy-duty extension cord rated for the load if the socket is far away, and avoid daisy-chaining adapters.

Check the safety marks and tip-over protection on the listing, and keep children and pets away from hot surfaces.

## Sizing a Heater to Your Room

As a rough guide, a bedroom of about 120 to 150 square feet is comfortable with a 2000 to 2500 watt oil-filled radiator, while a small study or bathroom is fine with a smaller fan heater. Large halls with high ceilings are hard to heat with a single portable unit, so consider heating only the area you use.
