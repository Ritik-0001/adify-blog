## How to Choose a Built-in Induction Hob

Check the zones and power. Two to four zones suit most families, and a power boost speeds up boiling, but total power draw matters for your wiring.

Look at the controls and safety. Touch controls, timers, child lock and auto shut-off improve everyday use and safety.

Plan the electrical supply. Built-in induction hobs often need a dedicated, correctly rated circuit, so involve an electrician early.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Skipping the electrical check.** Plan the load.

**Using non-magnetic cookware.** Test with a magnet.

**Ignoring ventilation.** Induction still needs a chimney.

**Choosing a hob without a cutout check.** Measure the counter.

## Real-World Scenarios

**A modular kitchen with a chimney.** A 3 or 4 zone induction hob.

**A small kitchen.** A 2 zone hob.

**A family that wants both.** A gas and induction combination.

## What to Check on the Listing

Confirm the zones, the total power, the controls, the safety features, the cutout size and the warranty. See whether cookware recommendations are included.

## Cookware and Care

Use flat-bottomed, induction-compatible pans, lift rather than slide them to protect the glass and wipe spills quickly with a soft cloth. Do not put hot pans on the control area.

## Quick FAQ

**Is induction faster than gas?** Often for boiling, yes.

**Does it work in power cuts?** No, it needs electricity.

**Can I use any pan?** Only magnetic cookware.

## Power Sharing and Zone Layouts

Many four-zone hobs share total power between zones, so running all four at full power may reduce each zone's output. Check how the hob manages power and whether a bridge or flexible zone is available for large pans. A hob with a dedicated booster for boiling helps with big vessels used for rice, dal or milk.

## Electrical and Installation Notes

Induction hobs often draw 3.5 kilowatts or more in total, so ask an electrician to confirm the wire gauge, the MCB rating and a proper socket or hard-wired connection. Leave the ventilation gap that the maker specifies beneath the hob, and use an installer who can match the cutout exactly.

## Everyday Use and Safety

Use the child lock when the hob is not in use, wipe spills as soon as the surface cools and avoid placing metal items on the glass. Most hobs shut off automatically if no pan is detected, which is a helpful safety feature in busy kitchens.

## Final Checklist Before You Buy

Before you order, measure the counter cutout or wall space and compare it with the dimensions on the listing, ask your carpenter and electrician to confirm clearances, power points and gas or water connections, and check who handles installation. Read the warranty terms on motors and electronics, keep the invoice and manual, and confirm that spare parts and service exist in your city.
