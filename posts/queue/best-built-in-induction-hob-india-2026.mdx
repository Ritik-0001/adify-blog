## How to Choose a Built-in Induction Hob

Check the zones and power. Two to four zones suit most families, and a power boost speeds up boiling, but total power draw matters for your wiring.

Look at the controls and safety. Touch controls, timers, child lock and auto shut-off improve everyday use and safety.

Plan the electrical supply. Built-in induction hobs often need a dedicated, correctly rated circuit, so involve an electrician early.

## Power Sharing and Zone Layouts

Many four-zone hobs share total power between zones, so running all four at full power may reduce each zone's output. Check how the hob manages power and whether a bridge or flexible zone is available for large pans. A hob with a dedicated booster for boiling helps with big vessels used for rice, dal or milk.

## Electrical and Installation Notes

Induction hobs often draw 3.5 kilowatts or more in total, so ask an electrician to confirm the wire gauge, the MCB rating and a proper socket or hard-wired connection. Leave the ventilation gap that the maker specifies beneath the hob, and use an installer who can match the cutout exactly. A chimney is still worth having for frying fumes.

## Cookware, Safety and Care

Use flat-bottomed, induction-compatible pans, and test an old pan with a magnet before assuming it will work. Lift rather than slide pans to protect the glass, wipe spills quickly with a soft cloth and keep hot pans off the control area. Use the child lock when the hob is idle; most hobs shut off automatically if no pan is detected.

## Two Zones or Four, Honestly

A four-zone hob looks complete, but most households use two zones at a time and the other two sit idle while sharing the same power budget. A two-zone model is cheaper, needs less counter and can leave room beside it for a gas burner or a worktop. Choose four zones only if you routinely cook several dishes at once or entertain often.

## Who Should Skip This

If power cuts are frequent in your area and you have no inverter that can carry a high load, a built-in induction hob will leave you without a stove when you need one. Households that cook roti directly on flame every day should also keep gas, since induction cannot char a phulka the way an open burner does.

## Recommendation

For most modular kitchens, a 3 or 4 zone built-in induction hob from Bosch or Elica is a strong pick, provided your wiring is ready. Have an electrician confirm the load and the cutout before you place the order.
