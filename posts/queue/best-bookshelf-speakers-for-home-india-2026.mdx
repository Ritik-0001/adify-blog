## Active versus passive

**Active (powered) speakers** have a built-in amplifier and often Bluetooth, optical, HDMI ARC and a phono input. You plug them into power and a source. One speaker holds the amplifier and connects to the other with a cable. They suit anyone who wants simplicity.

**Passive speakers** need an external amplifier or AV receiver. This is more flexible, since you can upgrade parts separately, but it adds cost, cables and shelf space. A decent stereo amplifier from Yamaha, Denon or Cambridge Audio costs roughly ₹25,000-50,000 and adds a better DAC, phono input or streaming.

For first-time buyers and TV use, active is the practical choice. For people who already own an amplifier or enjoy building a hi-fi chain, passive offers better long-term value. [Browse stereo amplifiers](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=stereo+amplifier+hi-fi&tag=adifystore-21).

## Amplifier needs

Match the amplifier to the speakers. Check the speaker's impedance (usually 4 to 8 ohms) and recommended power range. A speaker that suggests 30 to 100 watts per channel suits a 50 watt amplifier in a normal room. Underpowered amplifiers distort when pushed, and that can damage tweeters more than a bigger amplifier would. A 2-channel stereo amplifier also needs a source, either a TV with optical output, a streamer or a phone via Bluetooth.

## Placement

Placement changes sound more than most upgrades.

- Keep speakers at ear height, on stands or a sturdy shelf, with tweeters aimed at your head.
- Place them roughly 1.5 to 2.5 metres apart, with the listener forming an equilateral triangle.
- Keep them away from walls and corners if the speakers have rear ports, or the bass becomes boomy. A gap of 20 to 30 cm helps.
- Angle them slightly inward, called toe-in, to sharpen the centre image.
- In a bookshelf, front-ported models work better than rear-ported ones.

## Room size

Bookshelf speakers suit rooms up to around 200 to 250 square feet. In larger halls they lack bass, and a subwoofer or floorstanding pair works better. Many powered models include a subwoofer output. [Browse subwoofers](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=powered+subwoofer+home&tag=adifystore-21).

## Connections to check

For TV use, look for HDMI ARC or optical input. For music, Bluetooth with a good codec (aptX is common) and USB or a line input suit laptops. For turntables, a phono input is helpful. A powered pair without a connection your TV supports can be frustrating to set up.

## Who should skip

If you only watch TV and want movie-style effects, a soundbar or a 5.1 system is easier. If you live in a small flat with shared walls, loud bookshelf speakers may annoy neighbours, and good headphones are cheaper. [See soundbars](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=Dolby+Atmos+soundbar&tag=adifystore-21).
